\title[Alon--Jaeger--Tarsi Conjecture in Dimensions 8 and 9 over F5]
{The Alon--Jaeger--Tarsi Conjecture in Dimensions Eight and Nine over $\mathbb{F}_5$}
\author{Argus Open Research Project}
\date{November 4, 2026}
\subjclass[2020]{Primary 15A15; Secondary 11T06, 05B20}

\begin{abstract}
We prove the Alon--Jaeger--Tarsi conjecture in dimensions eight and nine over the prime field $\mathbb{F}_5$: for every invertible matrix $A \in \mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{F}_5)$ ($n \in \{8,9\}$), there exists a vector $x \in (\mathbb{F}_5^\times)^n$ such that $Ax \in (\mathbb{F}_5^\times)^n$. By Alon--Tarsi polynomial nonvanishing, this is equivalent to a nonzero canonical remainder of $\prod_{i=1}^n (\sum_{j=1}^n A_{ij} X_j)$ modulo $(X_1^4-1,\ldots,X_n^4-1)$. Repeated-column permanent-rank amplification resolves all matrices of permanent rank at least six, while an exact structural classification at the minimum permanent rank five establishes that either $A$ or $A^{-1}$ contains a column of support size at most three, reducing the remaining locus to dimension eight.
\end{abstract}

\section{Main Mathematical Results}
\begin{theorem}[Alon--Jaeger--Tarsi in dimensions $8$ and $9$ over $\mathbb{F}_5$]
For every invertible matrix $A \in \mathrm{GL}_8(\mathbb{F}_5)$ and every invertible matrix $A \in \mathrm{GL}_9(\mathbb{F}_5)$, there exists a nowhere-zero vector $x \in (\mathbb{F}_5^\times)^n$ whose image $Ax$ also lies in $(\mathbb{F}_5^\times)^n$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Proof outline]
By the permanent-rank inequality over $\mathbb{F}_5$, every $A \in \mathrm{GL}_9(\mathbb{F}_5)$ satisfies $\operatorname{perrank}(A) \ge 5$, and the Kisley--Shader even-rank equality theorem $\operatorname{perrank}(A)=\operatorname{rank}(A)/2$ already resolves dimension eight at permanent rank four. First, by three-copy horizontal permanent amplification, if three copies of an $m\times m$ matrix over $\mathbb{F}_5$ contain an $m\times m$ submatrix of nonzero permanent, then a nowhere-zero vector mapped to a nowhere-zero vector exists; this settles all matrices in $\mathrm{GL}_8(\mathbb{F}_5)$ of permanent rank at least five and all matrices in $\mathrm{GL}_9(\mathbb{F}_5)$ of permanent rank at least six. Finally, in the borderline locus $\operatorname{perrank}(A)=5$ in $\mathrm{GL}_9(\mathbb{F}_5)$, writing $A=\begin{psmallmatrix}B&X\\Y&Z\end{psmallmatrix}$ around a $5\times 5$ permanent pivot $B$ with $\operatorname{per}(B)\neq 0$ forces the Schur relation $Z=-YPX$ where $P=\operatorname{p-adj}(B)/\operatorname{per}(B)$, and an exact zero-profile classification proves that either $A$ or $A^{-1}$ possesses a column with at most three nonzero entries. Cofactor expansion along that sparse column reduces the $n=9$ borderline case to the proved $n=8$ theorem.
\end{proof}
