# An explicit Hanoi counterexample

## Result and logical dependencies

Let \(X=\{0,1,2\}\), let \(T=X^*\) be the rooted ternary tree, and define
\[
 a=(01)(1,1,a),\qquad b=(02)(1,b,1),\qquad c=(12)(c,1,1).
\]
For the concrete subgroup \(G=\langle a,b,c\rangle\leq\operatorname{Aut}(T)\),
put \(K=G'\) and **\(H=G''\)**. This certificate establishes:
\[
 G\text{ is finitely generated, faithful, level-transitive and branch},\qquad
 |H|=[G:H]=\infty,\qquad g^{-1}Hg=H\quad(g\in G).
\]
Thus \([H:H\cap g^{-1}Hg]=1\) for every \(g\), and the printed unconditional
claim is false.

## Conventions and the concrete action

Actions are on the right: a product \(uv\) first applies \(u\), then \(v\).
Write \(g@x\) for the section at \(x\). Thus
\[
 (uv)@x=(u@x)(v@(x^u)).
\]
The displayed recursions define unique automorphisms by induction on the
length of a finite word. Each generator is an involution: for example
\(a^2=(1,1,a^2)\) with trivial root permutation, so induction on word length
shows that \(a^2\) fixes every vertex. The other two proofs are identical
with the recursive coordinate changed. The action is faithful because \(G\)
is defined as the subgroup of these actual tree automorphisms, not as an
abstract group with a potentially nonfaithful action. It is generated by
the specified three elements, and all sections of elements of \(G\) lie in
\(G\).

To match [BSZ] exactly, put
\[
 A=c,\quad B=b,\quad C=a.
\]
Their source alphabet \(1,2,3\) is our \(0,1,2\), respectively. In source
notation \(A=(A,1,1)(23)\), \(B=(1,B,1)(13)\), \(C=(1,1,C)(12)\).
This changes names, not the subgroup or either derived subgroup.
Use \([u,v]=u^{-1}v^{-1}uv\) and \(u^v=v^{-1}uv\). Set
\[
 d=[A,B],\ e=[B,C],\ f=[C,A],\quad
 g=d^C,\ h=e^A,\ i=f^B.
\]
The lowercase auxiliary \(h\) here is an element, not the subgroup \(H\).

## Level transitivity, with no branching assumption

The root permutations of \(a,b,c\) generate \(S_3\). Furthermore
\[
 A,\quad B^C,\quad C^B
\]
all fix the vertex \(0\), and their sections there are \(A,B,C\),
respectively. The checker verifies these exact identities. Consequently
the section homomorphism
\(\operatorname{Stab}_G(0)\longrightarrow G\) is onto.

Conjugating a first-level vertex stabilizer to another one conjugates its
section image by a section of the conjugating element. Such a section
belongs to \(G\). Root transitivity therefore gives the same surjectivity
at each first-level vertex.

Induct on \(n\) to prove transitivity on \(X^n\). It holds for \(n=1\).
Given two words of length \(n+1\), first move the first letter to the
desired first letter. The section-surjectivity of that vertex stabilizer
then allows any element of \(G\) to act on the remaining suffix; use the
induction hypothesis on those length-\(n\) suffixes. This proves
transitivity at every level. In particular \(G\) is infinite, since its
orbits have the unbounded sizes \(3^n\).

## The precise branching subgroup is \(K=G'\)

For \(s\in\operatorname{Aut}(T)\), let \(\delta_x(s)\) act as \(s\) below
the first-level vertex \(x\) and trivially elsewhere. The exact recursions
give
\[
 \delta_0(d)=h^{-1}de,\qquad
 \delta_0(e)=e^{-1}if^{-1},\qquad
 \delta_0(f)=efh^{-1}.                                      \tag{1}
\]
All three right sides belong to \(K\). In the original generator alphabet
they are `cabacaba`, `abcacbcaca`, and `babcbabc`.
The checker verifies their root permutations and all three sections as
tree automorphisms, not just at a chosen finite depth.

The normal closure of \(d,e,f\) in \(G\) is \(K\): killing these three
commutators makes the generating set \(A,B,C\) pairwise commuting,
whereas all three already lie in \(G'\). If \(s\in G\), choose
\(t\in\operatorname{Stab}_G(0)\) with \(t@0=s\), using the preceding
surjectivity. Then
\[
 \delta_0(d^s)=\delta_0(d)^t\in K,
\]
and the same holds for \(e,f\). Products give \(\delta_0(K)\leq K\).
Conjugation by an element moving \(0\) to \(x\) sends \(\delta_0(K)\)
onto \(\delta_x(K)\), because the resulting section is conjugated by an
element of \(G\) and \(K\lhd G\). Thus \(\delta_x(K)\leq K\) for all
\(x\in X\). Iteration gives
\[
                  \delta_v(K)\leq K\qquad(v\in X^*).         \tag{2}
\]
This specifies the branching subgroup and the embeddings, without any
convention-dependent inference from the phrase "regular branch."

The abelianization of a group generated by three involutions has order
at most eight, so \([G:K]\leq 8\). For each \(n\), set
\[
 L_n=\prod_{v\in X^n}\delta_v(K).
\]
The factors have disjoint supports, and (2) makes \(L_n\) a subgroup of
\(G\). It is contained in the rigid level stabilizer
\(\operatorname{rist}_G(n)\).

On \(\operatorname{Stab}_G(n)\), the map taking all level-\(n\) sections
and then reducing them modulo \(K\) has kernel exactly \(L_n\):
an automorphism fixing level \(n\) is uniquely determined by those
sections, and all the requisite coordinate elements exist by (2).
It follows that
\[
 [\operatorname{Stab}_G(n):L_n]\leq 8^{3^n}.
\]
The action on the finite depth-\(n\) ternary tree gives
\([G:\operatorname{Stab}_G(n)]\leq6^{(3^n-1)/2}\). Therefore, for every
nonnegative \(n\),
\[
 [G:\operatorname{rist}_G(n)]
 \leq [G:L_n]\leq 6^{(3^n-1)/2}\,8^{3^n}<\infty.             \tag{3}
\]
Together with the preceding faithfulness and transitivity proofs, this
is the branch property required in the original question.

## Infinitude of \(H\), independently of every quotient argument

Take \(k=[d,e]\in K'=H\). In the original alphabet its reduced word is
`bcbcababcbcaba`. Direct exact evaluation gives
\[
                         00^k=02,
\]
so \(k\neq1\). By (2), for every vertex \(v\),
\[
 \delta_v(k)=[\delta_v(d),\delta_v(e)]\in K'=H.               \tag{4}
\]
At level \(n\), the \(3^n\) elements \(\delta_v(k)\) have disjoint
supports and are nontrivial. Their products over distinct subsets of
\(X^n\) are distinct: look at a subtree in the symmetric difference of
the two subsets. Hence \(|H|\geq2^{3^n}\) for every \(n\). Thus \(H\)
is infinite. This argument neither assumes an infinite quotient nor
infers kernel infinitude from one.

## The full derived quotient, not a finite presentation truncation

Let \(F=\langle A,B,C\mid A^2,B^2,C^2\rangle\), the free product of
three order-two groups, and let
\[
 \tau(A)=A,\quad \tau(B)=B^C,\quad \tau(C)=C^B.
\]
Using the six words \(d,e,f,g,h,i\) already defined, put
\[
\begin{aligned}
 r_1&=d^{-1}efi^{-1}ge^{-1},&
 r_2&=he^{-1}d^{-1}fdi^{-1},\\
 r_3&=e^{-1}g^{-1}f^{-1}egf,&
 r_4&=e^{-1}dhe^{-2}d^{-1}h^2,\\
 r_5&=hgd^{-2}f^{-1}gfe^{-1}.&&
\end{aligned}
\]
**Imported presentation theorem [BSZ, Proposition 3.14]:**
the natural map \(F\to G\) has kernel
\[
 N=\langle\!\langle \tau^m(r_j):m\geq0,\ 1\leq j\leq5\rangle\!\rangle_F.
                                                               \tag{5}
\]
The primary definitions immediately preceding Lemma 3.13 fix these
five words and the substitution. The executable checks that the five
base words act trivially; the stronger completeness assertion (5) is
the explicitly imported theorem.

Write \(F_0=\ker(F\to(C_2)^3)=F'\), where the map records the parity
of each generator count. Each \(r_j\) belongs to \(F_0\), and \(\tau\)
preserves parity, so \(N\leq F_0\). In particular \(G/G'\cong(C_2)^3\).

The Schreier graph of \(F_0\) in \(F\) is the cube with eight vertices,
twelve undirected edges, and generator-labeled edges flipping the
corresponding bit. The only reductions in \(F\) are backtracking
along an edge. Choosing a spanning tree therefore identifies the
group of based loops with a free group on the five non-tree edges.
This proves \(F_0/F_0'\cong\mathbb Z^5\).

The program uses bits \(A,B,C\) in that order and the sorted-bit
transversal. Its oriented free basis, written in the original
\(a,b,c\) alphabet, is
\[
 \texttt{bcbc},\quad \texttt{acac},\quad \texttt{abab},\quad
 \texttt{cababc},\quad \texttt{bacabc}.
\]
For a parity-zero word, its coordinate is its signed traversal count
on each non-tree edge. Backtracking cancels, and concatenation of
based loops adds the vectors; these are genuine abelianization
coordinates.

The columns of the base-relation matrix in this basis are
\[
 R=\begin{pmatrix}
 2&1&0&0&2\\
 2&1&0&0&2\\
 -1&1&0&3&-1\\
 1&-1&0&-3&1\\
 -2&-1&0&0&-2
 \end{pmatrix}.                                             \tag{6}
\]
Let \(u=(1,1,0,0,-1)^t\), \(v=(0,0,1,-1,0)^t\).
The column lattice is exactly
\[
 L_0=\mathbb Z(2u-v)+\mathbb Z(u+v),
\]
since the remaining nonzero column \(3v=2(u+v)-(2u-v)\).
The checker derives all operators by substituting actual words into
the Schreier basis. On the plane spanned by \(u,v\), conjugation by
each of \(A,B,C\) sends \(u,v\) to \(-u,-v\), while
\[
                      \tau_*u=u-2v,\qquad \tau_*v=-3v.
\]
Consequently \(\tau_*(2u-v)=2u-v\) and
\(\tau_*(u+v)=2(2u-v)-3(u+v)\). The lattice \(L_0\) is invariant
under all three generating conjugations and under \(\tau_*\).

It follows by induction that the images of **all** conjugates of
**all** \(\tau^m(r_j)\) lie in \(L_0\). Conversely the images of the
base relators generate \(L_0\) and lie in the image of \(N\).
Equation (5) therefore gives equality: the image of \(N\) in
\(F_0/F_0'\) is exactly \(L_0\), with no depth or substitution cutoff.
Since \(G'=F_0/N\),
\[
                 G'/G''\cong\mathbb Z^5/L_0.
\]

The vectors \(u,v\) span a direct summand of \(\mathbb Z^5\):
projection to coordinates one and three is a left inverse of their
inclusion. In this summand, the relation matrix is
\(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}2&1\\-1&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\),
whose Smith invariants are \(1,3\). Thus
\[
                         G'/G''\cong\mathbb Z^3\oplus C_3.    \tag{7}
\]
The independently replayed Jacobian certificate supplies the full
unimodular identity \(D=URV\), with
\(D=\operatorname{diag}(1,3,0,0,0)\),
\(\det U=1\), and \(\det V=-1\). The local checker recomputes these
integer identities and lattice invariance rather than trusting the
returned verdict. It also checks that \(d,e,f\) lift a basis of the
free quotient and \([d,C]\) generates the order-three torsion.
This reproduces the precise statement of [BSZ, Proposition 3.17].

Equation (7) implies \([G:H]=\infty\). Notice that \(G'\) itself
has index eight and cannot serve as the witness.

## A second, explicit infinite-quotient mechanism

Delete \(A\) and retain \(B,C\). On \(F\) this is a homomorphism
\(\rho:F\to\langle B,C\mid B^2,C^2\rangle\).
It sends \(d,f,g,i\) to \(1\) and sends \(e,h\) to the same
commutator \([B,C]\); substitution in the five displayed relators
gives \(1\) each time. Moreover
\(\rho\tau=\bar\tau\rho\), where
\(\bar\tau(B)=B^C\), \(\bar\tau(C)=C^B\).
These are homomorphisms since their generator images are
involutions. Thus \(\rho\) kills every relator in (5), not only
the five base ones, and descends to a retraction onto
the infinite dihedral group \(C_2*C_2\).

There is also a direct recursive confirmation that the concrete
subgroup \(\langle B,C\rangle\leq G\) is infinite. For \(t=BC\)
the root permutation is a 3-cycle and
\[
                        t^3=(t^{-1},t,t).                   \tag{8}
\]
If \(t\) had finite order \(m\), then \(3\mid m\) and \(t^3\)
would have order \(m/3\), whereas a section of \(t^3\) has order
\(m\). Sections of a root-fixing element give homomorphisms,
so this is impossible. Two involutions whose product has infinite
order generate \(C_2*C_2\): a reduced alternating even word is a
nonzero power of the product, and a relation of odd length would
force an additional finite-order relation on that product.

This quotient is metabelian, so it kills \(G''\), and is infinite.
Its kernel is independently infinite because it contains the
subgroup proved infinite in (4). This route explicitly supplies
both an infinite quotient and an infinite normal kernel; it does
not infer one infinitude from the other. The deletion endomorphism
is also the result of [BSZ, Proposition 3.16].

## Commensuration and exact replay

A derived subgroup is characteristic, and a characteristic subgroup
of a characteristic subgroup is characteristic. Therefore
\(H=(G')'\) is characteristic in \(G\), in particular normal.
For every \(g\in G\), \(g^{-1}Hg=H\), so the required intersection
index is exactly one. Together with (3), (4), and (7), all original
hypotheses and both infinitude requirements are satisfied.
