## Empty genuine-context operands

Let \(\mathcal G(g,A)\) be **all actual finite simple partial quartic
exteriors** with complementary boundary degrees, boundary edges disjoint
from \(A\), and a width-five decomposition rooted at the specified bag of
at most six vertices. There is no graph-order or boundary-feedback
restriction. An inside genuine graph is *context-empty* if no member of
this class completes it to between one and eleven Hamiltonian edge sets.
This property is not zero routing counts, and it need not hold against
arbitrary formal integer vectors.

**Genuine absorption lemma
(`C3167-MULT-GENUINE-EMPTY-ABSORPTION`).** Every legal primitive image of
a context-empty genuine graph is context-empty; at a join the other
operand can be any genuine partial graph with the prescribed root bag.

**Proof.** Suppose a primitive image has a low genuine exterior \(X\).
Move the primitive to the exterior of the allegedly empty operand.
For an edge introduction, put that actual edge into \(X\). It was absent
from \(X\), since \(X\) was edge-disjoint from the image. Its endpoint
degrees were at most three in \(X\), so this is a legal exterior insertion.
For an inside introduction of an isolated boundary vertex, forget that
vertex from \(X\): its exterior underlying degree is four. For an inside
forget of a degree-four vertex, introduce it as an isolated exterior
boundary vertex. In both cases the new root bag has size at most six.

For a join \(G\oplus H\), absorb \(H\) into \(X\). Its new boundary degree
is
\[
g_H+\bigl(4-g_G-g_H\bigr)=4-g_G .
\]
Rename all private vertices apart. The actual edge sets of \(G,H,X\) are
pairwise disjoint, and the rooted decompositions of \(H\) and \(X\) glue
at their common bag without increasing width. Every private vertex still
has underlying degree four. Relabeling transports the entire construction
and its exterior. In each case associativity of actual gluing preserves
the number of Hamiltonian edge sets exactly. The resulting low genuine
exterior of \(G\) contradicts context-emptiness. QED.

The lemma is deliberately about \(\mathcal G\), not closure of every
nonrealizable signature admitted by `E`. The accepted marked-carrier
argument is needed only for \(\mathcal G\subseteq E\) when a sufficient
unit test is applied. Absorption, including each of the two sequential
join replacements, keeps the actual exterior genuine and rooted-width
five. Fixed degrees and the reviewed future-edge gate still ensure
legality after every replacement. Thus the restriction remains justified
throughout the original contextual induction, without assuming the
twelve-cycle theorem.

## Exact comparison reductions

Let \(b\) be the target completion profile and \(a^r\) the eligible
representative profiles, including all degree-preserving relative orders.
The target-zero exterior coordinates are saturated at twelve, and the
target-overflow coordinates are zero, as in the reviewed normal form.
Any representative positive on a saturated coordinate already overflows and
can be omitted from this comparison. All remaining positive target
coordinates have integer costs \(b_i\) between one and eleven.

The following elementary reductions are used.

**Forced zeros.** Suppose every separator is already known to vanish outside
a remaining set \(I\) of positive target coordinates. For a representative
define
\[
 M_r(B)=\max\left\{\sum_{i\in I}a^r_i x_i:
                 x_i\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0},\quad\sum_{i\in I}b_i x_i\le B\right\}.
\]
This is the ordinary unbounded integer knapsack with budget at most ten.
If \(a^r_j>0\) and
\[
                  a^r_j+M_r(11-b_j)<12,
\]
no separator can have \(w_j>0\): subtracting one unit at \(j\) leaves the
displayed budget, so this representative's total is positive and below
twelve. Thus \(w_j=0\) can be imposed. Repeating this argument in any order
is valid because each new restriction holds for every separator already.
If all positive coordinates are eliminated, collective coverage follows.
This can already happen while visiting a prefix of the eligible family:
coverage by that subfamily suffices. A shared separator, in contrast, is
returned only after every eligible alignment has been accounted for.

**Two-representative forced zeros.** Suppose \(a^r_j>0\) and \(a^s_j>0\).
In a separator with \(w_j>0\), both representatives have positive completed
counts, so both must overflow. Their sum must therefore be at least 24.
Apply the same integer knapsack calculation to the coefficient row
\(a^r+a^s\), still using target costs \(b_i\) and budget \(11-b_j\).
If
\[
 a^r_j+a^s_j+
 \max\left\{\sum_{i\in I}(a^r_i+a^s_i)x_i:
                  x_i\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0},\quad
                  \sum_{i\in I}b_i x_i\le11-b_j\right\}<24,
\]
then \(w_j=0\). The positivity of **both** coefficients at \(j\) is
necessary: a representative allowed to stay zero need not overflow.
Pair sums are not capped at twelve. Individual and pair deductions
are repeated to a fixed point before the unchanged exhaustive integer
enumeration. This changes neither the exterior domain nor the retained
graphs. Literal integer-normal-form comparisons and the two-profile
example \((1,3),(3,1)\) versus target \((2,2)\) exercise the rule; the
disjoint profiles \((0,5),(5,0)\) check that a zero representative is not
incorrectly forced to overflow. The native report separately counts
pair tests and pair-forced coordinates.

**Identical columns.** If two remaining coordinates have the same target
coefficient and the same coefficient in every remaining representative,
their exterior multiplicities can be added and placed on either one.
Every completed count is unchanged. The total is at most eleven because
its target cost is positive, so this substitution also respects the
cap-twelve normal form. Therefore one coordinate per identical column is
enough. This statement concerns the full formal integer domain, not an
additional, unproved class of realizable exteriors.

**Exact subtree pruning.** During enumeration, let \(s_r>0\) be a
representative's partial completed count, \(B\) the remaining target budget,
and \(I_p\) the unassigned coordinates. The earlier implementation bounded
its future contribution by
\[
       \left\lfloor B\max_{i\in I_p}a^r_i/b_i\right\rfloor.
\]
The current implementation uses the tighter exact integer maximum
\(M_{r,p}(B)\) over \(I_p\). With the coordinates in enumeration order,
\[
 M_{r,p}(B)=\max\{M_{r,p+1}(B),\ a^r_p+M_{r,p}(B-b_p)\},
\]
omitting the second term when \(B<b_p\), and with the empty suffix zero.
An optimum either uses no unit at the current coordinate or uses one and
leaves the second displayed subproblem. Budgets increase within each suffix,
so the recurrence is acyclic. If \(s_r+M_{r,p}(B)<12\), this representative
works throughout the subtree, which therefore contains no separator.
All quantities are integers. Otherwise the code enumerates the remaining
integer multiplicities, taking higher target-cost coordinates first.
This only changes their order; every affordable multiplicity remains in
the schedule. Exceeding the declared
node budget returns `unknown_node_budget`; it does not return coverage or
a separator.

These reductions never require a chosen representative to preserve a
particular marker. A returned separator is one shared vector defeating
every eligible representative.

**Same-support column dominance.** A remaining coordinate \(i\) can be
removed if another coordinate \(j\) has \(b_j\le b_i\), has
\(a^r_j\ge a^r_i\) for every relevant representative, and has exactly the
same set of positive representative coefficients as \(i\). Transfer
every unit of \(w_i\) to \(w_j\). The target cost does not increase and
stays positive; every representative that was zero remains zero, and
every overflow remains an overflow. The combined multiplicity is at
most eleven. After identical columns are merged, strict dominance is
acyclic, so every removed column reaches a retained maximal column.
The equal-support condition is essential. This reduces the search
domain without asserting any graph realization of a formal exterior.

### Independent exact integer SMT fallback

The same reviewed finite normal form also gives an exact `QF_LIA`
formula, independent of the native search reductions. For each live
positive-target coordinate introduce an integer \(w_i\ge0\), require
\[
                 1\le\sum_i b_iw_i\le11,

and for every distinct relevant profile require
\[
             \sum_i a^r_iw_i=0\quad\text{or}\quad
             \sum_i a^r_iw_i\ge12.

Target-zero coordinates are saturated at twelve, target-overflow

## Realizability and complete schedules

An operation image is retained only after exact collective comparison
returns a shared separator. Rejection means whole-exterior coverage,
equality of completion profile and future-eligible edges, or absence of
any low-positive completion. All coordinates, not only chosen markers,
are retained.

An optional reduction pass deletes a graph only after an exact collective
query against the **other currently retained graphs** succeeds. The leaf
and required proper-join seed remain protected. This is noncircular:
if a removed graph was covered using a graph removed later, replace that
choice at the later deletion. The deletion time strictly increases, so
the replacement sequence ends in the final retained family. The
future-edge subset gate is transitive, and the full live exterior domain
depends only on the preserved degrees. Thus every such substitution is
still eligible in the original exterior. This also preserves coverage of
operation images processed before the deletion; their cursors need not
be reset. The final verifier does not rely on this chronological argument:
it checks every removed graph directly against the final family.

### Interior-only binary simulation

There is a separate, optional sufficient certificate for the binary
obligations; it does not delete anything from the unary carrier. Let \(U\)
be that fixed carrier and \(B\) a subfamily consisting only of its genuine
graphs with a nonempty interior. Require every interior member of \(U\)
to be collectively covered by \(B\) on the full live integer exterior
domain subject only to \(w_{\mathrm{empty}}=0\). Retain the same degrees,
future-edge subset gate, cap twelve and all relative alignments.

This domain is justified after both sequential replacements, not merely
for an original graph. An interior graph has zero empty selected-routing
count. In the routing join tensor, an empty output can arise only from
two empty inputs: nonempty selected edges cannot disappear under a union.
Consequently, joining any nonnegative formal exterior vector to an
interior operand still gives an exterior vector with empty count zero.
This statement applies to formal integer vectors as well as genuine
graph exteriors. Projection onto live routings loses no completed cycle:
a discarded selected boundary degree cannot be filled to two within the
complementary underlying degree. Capping at twelve also preserves all
low-positive completion tests.

Now fix any legal proper relative join \(P\oplus Q\) of interior members
of \(U\) and any live formal exterior \(w\) giving a low-positive completed
count. The context \(Q\oplus w\) has empty count zero. Replace \(P\) by a
member \(D\) of \(B\) using the stated collective coverage. Since \(D\)
itself has an interior, the updated context \(D\oplus w\) still has empty
count zero; then replace \(Q\) by a member \(D'\) of \(B\). Both choices
preserve low-positive completion sequentially, not necessarily its
numerical count. Degree
preservation retains the isolated boundary vertex of a proper join.
The transitive future-edge gate preserves actual-edge disjointness.
Thus full-domain collective closure of **every** required relative join
of \(B\) suffices for all relative joins of \(U\). Boundary-only operands
still use the unchanged unary reduction. No exterior restriction is
imposed on the final join query or on the global contextual induction.

### Complete Cartesian join certificates

The optional `--join-method blocks` certifies the same obligations,
without listing every row pair individually. A block contains genuine
interior graphs with one fixed degree vector. Let \(L_P\) be the
coordinatewise minimum of their full routing-count vectors, let \(S_P\)
be the zero-one union of their positive coordinates, and let \(E_P\)
be the intersection of their actual boundary-edge sets. For two blocks
and one fixed relative order, form the routing-tensor images
\[
               \ell=T(L_P,L_Q),\qquad u=T(S_P,S_Q).
\]
The edge set attached to \(\ell\) is \(E_P\cup E_Q\). These are formal
bounds, never representatives or newly retained graphs. For every
legal genuine member join \(H\), nonnegative integer tensor arithmetic
and the monotonicity of capping give
\[
 C(\ell)\le C(H),\qquad
 \operatorname{supp}C(H)\subseteq\operatorname{supp}C(u),
\]
where \(C\) is completion pairing. Also
\(\operatorname{supp}C(\ell)\subseteq\operatorname{supp}C(H)\).
All live closed entries are included.

If the lower and upper completion supports agree, then every member
join has that same support. Any exterior giving \(H\) a low-positive
count therefore also gives \(\ell\) a low-positive count. Collective
coverage of \(\ell\) by the **genuine fixed carrier** proves coverage of
every such \(H\). The future-edge subset gate remains sound because
\(E_P\cup E_Q\) is a subset of every actual member union and the output
degrees are fixed. If the common edge sets intersect, every member
pair repeats that actual edge and the entire block is illegal.

An inconclusive or uncovered abstract bound is not a genuine operation
failure: the checker splits its Cartesian rectangle. A self block is
partitioned into the two triangular child blocks and their intervening
rectangle; a rectangle is split on one side. Only a singleton pair may
return a genuine uncovered image. Every required relative order is
visited, or is analytically degree-infeasible, and the final accounted
total must equal the complete factorial-weighted class-pair census.
The bounded native probe for an abstract bound can only cause more
splitting, never acceptance or omission of an operation. A time
interruption never marks the schedule exhausted.

There is a second sufficient block bound which does not require common
completion support. For fixed underlying degrees \(g\), call an input
routing \(q\) completable if its completion-kernel row meets the full live
complementary domain. Let \(m(P)\) be the smallest positive count of \(P\)
on such routings; set it to twelve if there is none. For a block, take
the minimum of its members' values. Every positive completed term of a
legal join \(P\oplus Q\) is a product \(P_qQ_r\), possibly with an
additional positive integer tensor coefficient. Context contraction
shows that both \(q\) and \(r\) are completable: their complementary
contexts are respectively \(Q\oplus w\) and \(P\oplus w\), projected onto
the same full live domains as in the reviewed induction. Therefore each
composite is live: at a completed term \(h_P+h_Q+h_w=2\), and
\((4-g_P)-(h_Q+h_w)=2-(g_P-h_P)\) lies between zero and two.
The same calculation applies with \(P,Q\) exchanged. Thus each
positive completion coefficient is at least
\(\min(12,m(P)m(Q))\). If the two block minima multiply to at least
twelve, no member join has any low-positive integer exterior, regardless
of its support. The whole block/order is covered vacuously.

This ignores uncompletable input coordinates only when calculating that
bound; every original count remains stored. In particular a closed cycle
omitting an isolated boundary vertex cannot contribute to a completion,
but a closed cycle using the whole boundary remains eligible for an
empty selected exterior. Tests explicitly distinguish these cases.
Blocks are ordered by this exact minimum before their full vectors.
This is an additional mathematical certificate, not an empirical speedup
claim.
