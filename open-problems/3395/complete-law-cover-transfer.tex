\section{A cover functional on the unchanged domain and law}
Let $\Omega$ be the full product of original outgoing-choice groups,
and set $p=\Delta$. Write $R(\nu)=R_n(X_\nu)$ and
$h(\nu)=h_Q(\nu)$. Under the original BP1 promise,
\[
 R/\kappa\le h\le\kappa R\quad\hbox{throughout }\Omega,\qquad
 1\le\kappa\le65/64,\qquad
 R\in\{1,2\},\qquad \E_pR=(3+2^{-q})/2\le2 .
                                                        \tag{CT1}
\]
The endpoint $\kappa=1$ is included only to state the exact-target
diagnostic. No rationality of the circuit constants is assumed.

For any nonnegative $f$ and $\epsilon<\E_pf$, let
$\mathcal A_\epsilon(f)$ be all finite nonnegative weighted covers
by balanced-original-source rectangles satisfying
\[
 P=\sum_Bw_B1_B,\qquad
 0\le P\le f\quad\hbox{on all }\Omega,\qquad
 \E_p(f-P)\le\epsilon .
\]
For such a cover put
\[
 Z=\E_pP,\qquad
 C_p(w)=\sum_Bw_Bp(B)\log(1/p(B)),\qquad
 \Phi_\epsilon(f)=\inf_{\mathcal A_\epsilon(f)}C_p(w)/Z .
                                                        \tag{CT2}
\]
A zero-$p(B)$ term in $C_p$ is zero, but all full-domain domination
constraints remain. Also write $\mathcal I_\epsilon(f)$ for the
infimum of the actual selector information over the same covers.
All these classes are nonempty: singleton rectangles give an exact
finite cover. No small number of labels is inferred.

For any constant $a>0$, replacing every coefficient by $aw_B$
and $Z$ by $aZ$ leaves
\[
 \pi(\nu,B)=p_\nu w_B1_B(\nu)/Z,\qquad C_p(w)/Z
                                                        \tag{CT3}
\]
exactly unchanged. This is an equality of the complete joint law,
not a conditional-history identity or a new reference on its labels.
The output does change; its loss must be charged explicitly.

\section{Two-way transfer with the original loss budgets}
\begin{proposition}
For every original BP1 output in (CT1),
\[
 \Phi_{1/8}(R)\le\Phi_{1/16}(h)\le\Phi_{1/2048}(R),
 \qquad
 \mathcal I_{1/8}(R)\le\mathcal I_{1/16}(h)
                         \le\mathcal I_{1/2048}(R).    \tag{CT4}
\]
Every transfer preserves the original rectangle partitions and the
entire normalized selector law. Both sides use the same complete $p$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Given $P_R\in\mathcal A_{1/2048}(R)$, set $P_h=P_R/\kappa$.
Then $P_h\le R/\kappa\le h$ everywhere, and
\[
 \E_p(h-P_h)
 \le(\kappa-\kappa^{-1})\E_pR+(2048\kappa)^{-1}
 \le2(\kappa-\kappa^{-1})+(2048\kappa)^{-1}
 \le1/16 .                                           \tag{CT5}
\]
For the last inequality, multiply its slack by $2048\kappa>0$
and set $t=\kappa-1$. The result is
\[
 127-8064t-4096t^2=(1-64t)(127+64t)\ge0
                  \quad(0\le t\le1/64).              \tag{CT6}
\]
In particular equality at the endpoint in this coarse bound is
permitted. Equation (CT3) proves the upper inequalities in (CT4).

Conversely, given $P_h\in\mathcal A_{1/16}(h)$, set $P_R=P_h/\kappa$.
Full-domain domination follows from $h/\kappa\le R$. Its total loss is
\[
 \E_p(R-P_R)
 \le(1-\kappa^{-2})\E_pR+(16\kappa)^{-1}
 \le2(1-\kappa^{-2})+(16\kappa)^{-1}
 \le\frac{518}{4225}<\frac18.                        \tag{CT7}
\]
The displayed expression is increasing on $[1,65/64]$:
its derivative is $(64-\kappa)/(16\kappa^3)>0$.
Evaluation at $65/64$ gives $518/4225$, with slack $81/33800$.
Equivalently, $16\kappa^2$ times the slack to $1/8$ is
\[
 32-\kappa-30\kappa^2=1-61t-30t^2
                    \ge81/2048>0.                 \tag{CT8}
\]
Apply (CT3) again and take infima to prove the lower inequalities.
The full loss in each direction is charged once, not once per cut.
\end{proof}

These are coefficient-accounted retentions, not zero-loss identities
between $h$ and $R$. Every weight is divided by the same $\kappa$;
the resulting coefficient reduction is precisely what (CT5)--(CT7)
charge. Domination of full-group compatible polynomials is
coefficientwise because each compatible monomial has its unique
one-hot assignment. The construction permits arbitrary nonnegative
real coefficients, original sharing, powers and unrestricted degree.
It does not produce a small circuit computing the retained polynomial.

\section{An admissible universal cover, with the inadequate rate exposed}
Let $\mathcal N=\{\nu:R(\nu)=2\}$ be the non-tree assignments.
Across one fixed balanced original-source partition, take the full
rectangle of weight $1/\kappa$ and, for each $\nu\in\mathcal N$,
its singleton rectangle of weight $1/\kappa$. Their sum is
\[
                         P=R/\kappa\le h.             \tag{CT9}
\]
This is a nonnegative monomial construction of
$F+(F-T)$, not a subtraction gate. Coefficients at tree assignments
are $1/\kappa$ and at non-tree assignments $2/\kappa$.
It is a finite construction on the full domain, including
zero-probability assignments. Its single total loss is at most
\[
 (\kappa-\kappa^{-1})\E_pR
 \le\frac{129}{2080}=\frac1{16}-\frac1{2080}.         \tag{CT10}
\]
The empty assumption that this cover has few labels is not used.

Its normalized entropy cost is
\[
 \frac{C_p(w)}Z
 =\frac{\sum_{\nu\in\mathcal N}p_\nu\log(1/p_\nu)}{\E_pR}
 \le\frac23 H_p(\nu).                                \tag{CT11}
\]
The complete seed of the actual sampler consists of at most $4N$
binary edge marks, one ordered distinct four-tuple in each of $q$
blocks of size $L$, and $2q$ fair gadget bits. The matching, order,
and outgoing assignment are determined from that seed.
Entropy cannot increase under a deterministic map, by the entropy
chain rule. A binary variable has entropy at most $\log2$, and each
ordered tuple has at most $L^4$ values. Therefore
\[
 H_p(\nu)\le4N\log2+q(4\log L+2\log2)
 \le(4+94/L)N\log2.                                 \tag{CT12}
\]
This accounts for the matching and tuple histories; none is
conditioned away. With $L=2^{23}$, $94/L<1/2$ and $\log2<1$ give
\[
                  I_\pi(\nu;B)\le C_p(w)/Z<3N.       \tag{CT13}
\]
RI4 supplies the first inequality. This is a genuine all-BP1,
complete-law, full-domain cover, but its proved rate is $3$, not
$2^{-139}$. Its existence was never the unresolved part of MP9;
WV already supplies an exact zero-loss cover.
The construction has no affordable relation to the original DAG
size, and no LR3 materialization was used.

\section{The complete-law lower diagnostic survives transfer}
The larger loss $1/8$ in (CT4) still leaves a linear lower bound.
This short extension of RI's argument is needed for that new loss
budget; unchanged RI7 itself is not being rechecked.

For $P_R\in\mathcal A_{1/8}(R)$ put $\delta=2^{-q}$ and let
$\chi$ be $1$ on a non-tree assignment and $-1$ on a tree assignment.
The exact original parity law gives
\[
 \frac{\E_p(\chi P_R)}{\E_pP_R}
 \ge\frac{3+12\delta}{4(3+\delta)}\ge\frac14,\qquad
 \frac{P_R}{\E_pP_R}\le\frac{16}{11}<\exp(1).         \tag{CT14}
\]
The first inequality uses
$\E_p(\chi R)=(1+3\delta)/2$, loss at most $1/8$ and
$\E_pP_R\le(3+\delta)/2$; its numerator is positive.
For the second, $\E_pP_R\ge3/2-1/8=11/8$ and $P_R\le2$.
Thus the corresponding input divergence is less than one.

The reviewed balanced-rectangle discrepancy is
$\gamma=3\exp(-N/2^{136})$. At admissible $N\ge2^{142}$,
$128\gamma<1$. If $\theta$ is the selector mass on rectangles
with $p(B)\le128\gamma$, then
\[
 \frac14\le\theta+\frac{1-\theta}{128},
 \qquad \theta\ge\frac{31}{127}>\frac{31}{128}.
                                                        \tag{CT15}
\]
It follows directly from (CT2) and RI4 that
\[
 \begin{split}
 \Phi_{1/8}(R)&\ge
 \frac{31}{127}\left(\frac{N}{2^{136}}-\log384\right)
 >\frac{31N}{2^{143}}-3,\\
 \mathcal I_{1/8}(R)&\ge
 \frac{31}{127}\left(\frac{N}{2^{136}}-\log384\right)
       -\log(16/11)
 >\frac{31N}{2^{143}}-4.                              \tag{CT16}
 \end{split}
\]
Here $\log384<9$ and $279/127<3$. These lower bounds extend
through (CT4) to the entire BP1 band.
They are deliberately weaker than the already reviewed RI7 at
the native loss $1/16$, and do not improve its theorem.

In particular the entropy cost in (CT4) does not approach zero
with $b_Q$: the exact target $Q=R_n$ has zero defect and a finite
positive monomial circuit, and (CT16) still applies.
No small original size is asserted for that circuit.
This excludes only a size-free output-proximity argument, not
the live estimate with its indispensable $\beta\log(s+1)$ term.

\section{Where the MP9 proof attempt stops}
If MP9 held, (CT4) and (CT16) would force
\[
 K_0+a\log(M_n+2)+\beta\log(s+1)
 \ge\frac{121N}{1016\,2^{136}}-\frac{31}{127}\log384.
                                                        \tag{CT17}
\]
The coefficient $121/1016$ is $31/127-1/8$.
This is a necessary consequence of an unproved upper bound,
not a circuit lower bound already obtained here.

Conversely, if a new circuit argument had already proved
$\log(s+1)\ge cN$ for some explicit $c>0$ in the required
positive-defect regime, the universal cover (CT9)--(CT13) would
satisfy MP9 with $K_0=a=0$ and $\beta=\max(1,3/c)$, even
without its linear allowance. That exponential circuit statement
is not known here. Together with GL10--GL13, this explains why
optimizing the output alone has not made the original lower-bound
problem easier.

The attempted route from uniform BP1 output proximity to a small
entropy optimum therefore stops at a precise point: transfer
preserves the selector, while both endpoints still carry linear
information. A circuit-dependent upper construction is missing.
No inequality relates the optimal full-domain pooling in (CT2)
to $s$ at a fixed positive $b_Q$ threshold. CF/LR's exact
normalization and the compulsory cut do not give that inequality;
they preserve the compatible output without controlling the
information of its recombination.

This continuation does not retire semantic pooling, the
original-$\Delta$ raw-cover alternative, or GL9. It also does not
introduce a replacement acceptance boundary. The original task
requires the missing estimate, followed by the rational factor,
threshold and original-DAG conversion in GL10--GL13.
All of those quantitative conclusions remain conditional.
There is a mathematical proof gap, not an external or operator block.
