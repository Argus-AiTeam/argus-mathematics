# The component-conjugation quotient collapses on \(G_{4,3}\)

## Exact result

Let

\[
G=G_{4,3}=F_4/[F_4^3,F_4]
\]

and let \(\mathcal N(G)=K_1(\mathbb ZG/I_G)/T_G\) be the
Lustig--Moriah Nielsen-torsion group.  Fix the standard generating tuple
\(U=(x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4)\).  Define the realizable torsion subgroup

\[
\mathcal R_U=
\left\langle\mathcal N(V,U):
V\text{ is a minimal generating four-tuple of }G\right\rangle
\subseteq\mathcal N(G).
\]

For every such \(V=(v_1,\ldots,v_4)\), every \(i\), and every \(h\in G\),
write

\[
C_i^h(V)=(v_1,\ldots,hv_i h^{-1},\ldots,v_4)
\]

and define the full component-conjugation defect subgroup

\[
\mathcal D_{\mathrm{AC3}}=
\left\langle\mathcal N(C_i^h(V),V):
V\text{ minimal},\ 1\leq i\leq4,\ h\in G\right\rangle.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{\mathcal D_{\mathrm{AC3}}=\mathcal R_U.}
\]

Consequently \(\mathcal R_U/\mathcal D_{\mathrm{AC3}}=0\).  In
particular, no central automorphism of \(G_{4,3}\) with unimodular
abelianization has a nonzero torsion class after quotienting by all AC3
defects.  This covers the entire selected central-automorphism family,
whether or not a member has a balanced normally generating free lift.
The mandatory quotient gate therefore kills the route before any lift
search.

This is an exact universal route exclusion, not a finite search and not an
Andrews--Curtis counterexample.

## Source scope

The definition used here is exactly the already reviewed
Lustig--Moriah definition in arXiv:1910.02759v4, Remark 6.7:
\(\mathcal N(V,U)\) lies in \(K_1(\mathbb ZG/I_G)\) modulo the trivial
units \(T_G\), where \(I_G\) is generated by the images of Fox derivatives
of kernel words, and it depends only on the Nielsen classes of \(U,V\).
The archived source is in
`research/experiments/periodic-extension-n-torsion/source/`.

The central-extension parameter range is the one reported for Moriah and
Shpilrain, *Non-tame automorphisms of extensions of periodic groups*,
DOI `10.1007/BF02761688`: \(F_n/[F_n^p,F_n]\), \(n\geq4\),
\(p\geq3\).  The primary article remains unavailable anonymously, and no
unseen automorphism formula from it is used.

Two additional source propositions are used:

1. W. Specht's contemporary JFM review `58.0125.02` of Levi and van der
   Waerden, *Uber eine besondere Klasse von Gruppen*, states verbatim:
   "\(\mathfrak A_{3,n}\) hat die Ordnung
   \(3^{\binom {n}1 + \binom n2 + \binom n3}\)."  The preceding text defines
   \(\mathfrak A_{3,n}\) as the universal \(n\)-generated group satisfying
   \(s^3=1\), namely \(B(n,3)\).  Therefore
   \[
   |B(4,3)|=3^{\binom41+\binom42+\binom43}
           =3^{4+6+4}=3^{14}.
   \]
   The exact locator is zbMATH document `2549299`,
   `editorial_contributions[0]`, preserved at
   `source/levi-van-der-waerden-zbmath.json`.  The related primary article
   has canonical DOI `10.1007/BF02940639` and appears in volume 9,
   pp. 154--158.  The zbMATH record catalogs it as 1932, whereas the
   preserved Reviewer excerpt cites 1933; this catalog-year discrepancy
   does not affect the quoted formula, DOI, or page span.
2. Aglaia Myropolska, *Andrews--Curtis and Nielsen equivalence relations
   on some infinite groups*, arXiv:1304.2668v2, published as DOI
   `10.1515/jgth-2015-0031`, Theorem 1.3 and proof on pp. 4, 7--9:
   for a finitely generated group in which every maximal subgroup is
   normal, two normally generating \(n\)-tuples, \(n\geq w(G)\), are
   AC-equivalent exactly when their images in the abelianization are.
   The same source states that finitely generated nilpotent groups belong
   to this class.  Its full author manuscript and extracted text are
   archived under `source/`.

## The group \(G_{4,3}\)

Put \(P=F_4^3\).  There is an exact central extension

\[
1\longrightarrow P/[P,F_4]\longrightarrow
F_4/[P,F_4]\longrightarrow F_4/P=B(4,3)\longrightarrow1.
\]

The kernel is central by definition.  The order formula gives

\[
|B(4,3)|=3^{4+6+4}=3^{14},
\]

so the quotient is a finite \(3\)-group and hence nilpotent.  A central
extension of a nilpotent group is nilpotent: if the quotient has class
\(c\), then \(\gamma_{c+1}(G)\) is contained in the central kernel and
\(\gamma_{c+2}(G)=1\).  Thus \(G\) is finitely generated nilpotent.

Moreover \([P,F_4]\subseteq[F_4,F_4]\), so

\[
G_{\mathrm{ab}}
=F_4/([P,F_4][F_4,F_4])
=F_4/[F_4,F_4]\cong\mathbb Z^4.
\]

Therefore \(\operatorname{rank}(G)=w(G)=4\).  A four-tuple with
unimodular exponent matrix generates \(G_{\mathrm{ab}}\), and hence
generates \(G\): if \(H\leq G\) satisfies \(G=HG'\), induction gives
\(\gamma_k(G)\subseteq H\gamma_{k+1}(G)\), ending with \(G=H\).

The rank-four Nielsen graph of \(\mathbb Z^4\) is connected.  By
Myropolska's Theorem 1.3, every normally generating four-tuple of \(G\)
is therefore AC-equivalent to \(U\).  Her convention
\(v_i^s=s^{-1}v_i s\) gives the present move \(hv_i h^{-1}\) by taking
\(s=h^{-1}\).

## The full arbitrary-conjugator defect law

Let \(V=(v_1,\ldots,v_4)\) be an arbitrary minimal generating tuple.
Choose a word \(H(Y_1,\ldots,Y_4)\) representing \(h\) relative to \(V\),
put

\[
z=hv_i h^{-1},\qquad
d_j=p_V\left(\frac{\partial H}{\partial Y_j}\right)
\in\mathbb ZG/I_G,
\]

and apply AC3 to component \(i\).  Fox's product and inverse rules give

\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\partial(HY_iH^{-1})}{\partial Y_j}
&=\frac{\partial H}{\partial Y_j}
  +H\delta_{ij}
  +HY_i\frac{\partial H^{-1}}{\partial Y_j}\\
&=d_j+h\delta_{ij}-hv_i h^{-1}d_j\\
&=h\delta_{ij}+(1-z)d_j.
\end{aligned}
\]

Thus the transition matrix is the identity except in row \(i\), whose
\(j\)-entry is

\[
h\delta_{ij}+(1-z)d_j.
\]

For every \(j\neq i\), the elementary row operation

\[
R_i\longleftarrow R_i-(1-z)d_jR_j
\]

clears column \(j\).  Over the possibly noncommutative quotient ring this
is a left elementary operation, so the multiplication order is exact.
The resulting diagonal entry is

\[
q(V,i,h)=h+(1-hv_i h^{-1})d_i.
\]

Hence the complete defect is

\[
\boxed{\delta(V,i,h)=
\left[q(V,i,h)\right]\in K_1(\mathbb ZG/I_G)/T_G.}
\]

Since \(h\) is a trivial unit, it can equivalently be represented by

\[
\left[1+h^{-1}(1-hv_i h^{-1})d_i\right].
\]

This formula quantifies over every tuple, component, and conjugator.
Changing the word \(H\) does not change \(d_j\) modulo \(I_G\): the Fox
derivative of the intervening kernel word lies in the defining Fox ideal.
Nielsen transports require no extra generators, because \(V\) already
ranges over all minimal tuples.  The subgroup above is therefore the full
arbitrary-component-conjugation defect subgroup in the realizable
\(K_1/T_G\) image.

`matrix.symbolic.determinant.compute` independently checked the

\(h,z,d_1,\ldots,d_4\), it returned exactly

\[
h-zd_1+d_1=h+(1-z)d_1.
\]

## Why the defect subgroup is the whole realizable image

Fox's chain rule gives the cocycle identity

\[
\mathcal N(W,U)
=\mathcal N(W,V)\mathcal N(V,U)
\]

for any three minimal generating tuples.  If \(W=C_i^h(V)\), then

\[
\delta(V,i,h)
=\mathcal N(W,U)\mathcal N(V,U)^{-1}\in\mathcal R_U.
\]

Therefore

\[
\mathcal D_{\mathrm{AC3}}\subseteq\mathcal R_U.
\]

Conversely, take any minimal generating four-tuple \(V\).  The
nilpotent AC theorem supplies a finite AC path

\[
U=V_0,V_1,\ldots,V_m=V.
\]

AC1 and every signed or oriented AC2 move are Nielsen moves, so their
transition torsions are trivial in \(\mathcal N(G)\).  Every remaining
edge is an AC3 move and contributes exactly one
\(\delta(V_k,i_k,h_k)\).  Repeated use of the cocycle identity gives

\[
\mathcal N(V,U)
=\prod_{\text{AC3 edges }k}\delta(V_k,i_k,h_k)
\in\mathcal D_{\mathrm{AC3}}.
\]

Since these classes generate \(\mathcal R_U\),

\[
\mathcal R_U\subseteq\mathcal D_{\mathrm{AC3}},
\]

proving equality.

## Consequence for the proposed lift

A central automorphism sends \(U\) to a generating four-tuple, so its
torsion lies in \(\mathcal R_U=\mathcal D_{\mathrm{AC3}}\) and becomes
zero in the proposed quotient.  More generally this holds for every
four-tuple with unimodular abelianization.  In particular it holds for
the image of every balanced free tuple that normally generates \(F_4\),
because its exponent matrix has determinant \(\pm1\).

Thus the required nonzero endpoint difference cannot occur anywhere in
the declared central-automorphism family.  The route's own stopping rule
requires termination at this point; searching for free lifts would not
repair the already-zero quotient.

This does not say that the original non-tame automorphisms are Nielsen
trivial, and it does not assert that they lack normally generating free
lifts.  It says precisely that quotienting Nielsen torsion by all AC3
defects erases every generating-tuple torsion class in \(G_{4,3}\).
