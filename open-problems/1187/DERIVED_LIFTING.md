# Constructive donor-changing lift through a soluble normal kernel

## Exact scope

Let \(F=\langle a,b\rangle\), let \(\pi:F\to A_5\) be the existing
kernel-1 epimorphism, and let \(K=\ker\pi\). Write
\(K^{(0)}=K\) and \(K^{(j+1)}=[K^{(j)},K^{(j)}]\).
The quotient in this note is
\[
                         G=F/K^{(3)}.
\]
It is not a nilpotent truncation. Its soluble normal subgroup is
\(K/K^{(3)}\), and its quotient is the original nonabelian group \(A_5\).
The accepted first-order paths for literal P0 and P2 end at
\((ac,bd)\), with actual free words \(c,d\in K'\).

The construction below gives finite strict AC programs from these endpoints
to \((a,b)\). No normal-closure membership oracle is used: the three
normal-closure multipliers have finite signed-conjugate programs, and the
only discarded word has an explicit certificate of membership in
\(K^{(3)}\). Independent Reviewer acceptance remains required.

## The metabelian donor step, and why it is not the deliverable

For \(q=bd\), killing \(q\) in \(F/K''\) kills a normal generator of
the \(A_5\) quotient. The resulting group is therefore an image of
\(K/K''\), hence metabelian. Its abelianization is infinite cyclic,
generated by the image of \(a\), since \(q\) has exponent vector
\((0,1)\). An element lifting this generator normally generates: after
killing it the remaining group is both soluble and perfect, hence trivial.

Guyot, *On Andrews-Curtis conjectures for soluble groups*,
arXiv:1612.06912v2, Proposition 1.7(ii), p. 5, and its proof on p. 13,
states that weight elements with the same abelianized image in a finitely
generated metabelian group with cyclic abelianization are conjugate.
The proof writes the derived subgroup as \(A=(1-t)A\).
Thus this fixed-donor weight-conjugacy test cannot separate here at
relative derived length two.

Proposition 1.7(iii) only exhibits a different, three-generated polycyclic
group of soluble length three with nonconjugate weight elements. It says
nothing about these literal rank-two tuples. Neither proposition is
applied to \(G\) itself: \(G\) is not soluble, and \(G_{\rm ab}\cong
\mathbb Z^2\). The argument below is a direct construction, not an
application of a virtually-soluble version of Guyot's theorem.

## Five phases

Use left conjugation \({}^h u=huh^{-1}\) and
\([u,v]=uvu^{-1}v^{-1}\). Regard \(c=C(a,b)\) as its actual free word and
put
\[
       \widehat c=C(a,d^{-1}),\qquad \ell=a\widehat c,\qquad
       u=ab^{-1}.
\]
The phases, with their changing donors displayed, are
\[
\begin{split}
(ac,bd)&\longrightarrow(\ell,bd)
 \longrightarrow(\ell,b\widehat c)\\
 &\longrightarrow(u,b\widehat c)
 \longrightarrow(u,b)\longrightarrow(a,b).
\end{split}                                                    \tag{1}
\]
The first, second, and fourth arrows multiply the receiver by, respectively,
\[
 c^{-1}\widehat c\in\langle\!\langle bd\rangle\!\rangle_F,\qquad
 d^{-1}\widehat c\in\langle\!\langle\ell\rangle\!\rangle_G,\qquad
 \widehat c^{-1}\in\langle\!\langle ab^{-1}\rangle\!\rangle_F.
                                                               \tag{2}
\]
The third arrow is first-times-second-inverse, and the last is
first-times-second. The strict implementation of the fourth arrow uses
the actual current first component, not the ideal word \(ab^{-1}\).
Their difference is tracked by an exact normal-error certificate.

The first inclusion in (2) is obtained by substituting \(b=d^{-1}\)
in \(c^{-1}\). The third is obtained by collecting the word after
substituting \(a=b\); both exponent sums of \(\widehat c\) vanish.
The middle inclusion is the substantive step. Its finite construction
follows.

## A finite certificate for \(a\) in the new donor's normal closure

Let \(A=\langle\!\langle a\rangle\!\rangle_F\). Since the total
\(b\)-exponent of \(d\) is zero, \(d\in A\). Since \(C\in F'\),
\(\widehat c\) is a product of conjugates of \([a,d^{-1}]^{\pm1}\).
Consequently \(\widehat c\in A'\).

Define the endomorphism
\[
                f(a)=\widehat c^{-1},\qquad f(b)=b.
\]
It satisfies \(f(A)\subseteq A'\). Inductively,
\[
                     v=f^3(a)\in A^{(3)}\subseteq F^{(3)}.       \tag{3}
\]
Also \(x=f(x)\) modulo \(\langle\!\langle\ell\rangle\!\rangle_F\)
for each free generator: \(a f(a)^{-1}=\ell\) and \(b f(b)^{-1}=1\).

The image of \(\ell\) is \(\pi(a)\): \(d\in K'\), and the total
\(a\)-exponent of \(C\) is zero. The finite permutation certificates
give, for \(x=a,b\), actual products
\[
 T_x=\prod_j h_{x,j}\ell^{\epsilon_{x,j}}h_{x,j}^{-1},
 \qquad \pi(T_x)=\pi(x),\qquad \epsilon_{x,j}\in\{1,-1\}.
\]
Set
\[
       \rho(a)=T_a^{-1}a\in K,\qquad
       \rho(b)=T_b^{-1}b\in K,\qquad e=\rho(v).
\]
Equation (3) gives the exact, untruncated membership certificate
\[
                              e\in K^{(3)}.                    \tag{4}
\]
Notice that this uses a homomorphism into \(K\), not a centrality
assumption about \(K'\). No identity \([K',K]\subseteq K''\) is asserted.

For a substitution \(\theta\) whose generator differences have specified
donor factorizations, let \(D_\theta(w)\) be the finite donor-factor
program evaluating to \(w\theta(w)^{-1}\). Its exact recurrences are
\[
\begin{split}
D_\theta(xy)&=D_\theta(x)\,{}^{\theta(x)}D_\theta(y),\\
D_\theta(x^{-1})&={}^{\theta(x)^{-1}}D_\theta(x)^{-1}.             \tag{5}
\end{split}
\]
The generator differences for \(f\) are \(\ell,1\); those for
\(\rho\) are \(T_a,T_b\). Thus the explicitly constructed factor program
\[
 P=D_f(a)\,D_f(f(a))\,D_f(f^2(a))\,D_\rho(v)
\]
has the **free-group** value
\[
                         P=ae^{-1},\qquad a=Pe.                \tag{6}
\]
All its factors are signed conjugates of \(\ell\).
This is a finite witness for the middle inclusion in (2): freely collect
\(d^{-1}\widehat c\) as conjugates of \(a^{\pm1}\), then replace every
such donor factor by \(P^{\pm1}\).

## Exact endpoint errors, including donor changes

Every factor program has only identity, atom, product, inverse, and
conjugation nodes. Substituting a new value for its atom leaves the
conjugating words fixed. If its old and new values are \(X,Y\), a
simultaneously constructed error program evaluates to \(XY^{-1}\).
For product, inverse, and conjugation nodes the recurrences are
\[
\begin{split}
\Delta(X_1X_2,Y_1Y_2)
  &=\Delta(X_1,Y_1)\,{}^{Y_1}\Delta(X_2,Y_2),\\
\Delta(X^{-1},Y^{-1})
  &={}^{Y^{-1}}\Delta(X,Y)^{-1},\\
\Delta({}^hX,{}^hY)&={}^{h}\Delta(X,Y).                          \tag{7}
\end{split}
\]
For the second phase the atom error is \(aP^{-1}={}^P e\), by (6).
Consequently every resulting error is an explicit product of conjugates
of \(e^{\pm1}\), not a numerical or finite-quotient equality test.

Let \(W_2\) be the actual second-phase multiplier and let
\[
 B=bdW_2,\quad V=b\widehat c,\quad
 E_2=BV^{-1},\quad p=\ell B^{-1},\quad
 E_1=p u^{-1}={}^uE_2^{-1}.
\]
All errors here are represented by their finite \(e\)-factor programs.
Freely collect \(\widehat c^{-1}\) into \(u\)-factors, and substitute the
**actual** word \(p\) for the donor. If \(W_3\) is the resulting
multiplier and
\(J=\widehat c^{-1}W_3^{-1}\), (7), with atom error
\(up^{-1}=E_1^{-1}\), supplies its exact \(e\)-factor program.
After the last two arrows of (1), the literal endpoints satisfy
\[
\begin{split}
 b_{\rm final}b^{-1}&=E_2\,{}^V J^{-1}=:E_b,\\
 a_{\rm final}a^{-1}&=E_1\,{}^u E_b=:E_a.                       \tag{8}
\end{split}
\]
Equations (4) and (8) are the endpoint certificate: both right sides
belong to the normal subgroup \(K^{(3)}\).

## Finite strict expansion

To multiply a receiver by \(h v h^{-1}\), conjugate the donor by
\(h^{-1}\) in the convention \(v\mapsto g^{-1}vg\), multiply the
receiver by the donor, and conjugate the donor back by \(h\).
For \(h v^{-1}h^{-1}\), invert the donor before these three moves and
invert it again afterwards. These use three and five strict moves.
The signed Nielsen move in (1) uses donor inversion, multiplication,
and donor inversion. There is no stabilization.

The acyclic word and factor programs specify actual finite words over
\(a,b\). Product and inverse nodes expand by concatenation and by
reversing order with opposite signs. Conjugation prepends the fixed
conjugating word to every factor. These operations have finite recursive
strict expansions even when their uncompressed lengths are large.
Conjugating words refer to frozen free words, never mutable tuple slots.

## Comparison with the accepted result

The accepted certificate only kills the class-two error
\([d,a^{-1}t]\in[K',K]\) in \(F/\gamma_3(K)\).
Here that error is not killed or reused. The new donor is changed,
and the endomorphisms \(f,\rho\) manufacture an error in \(K^{(3)}\)
instead. Since \(K^{(3)}\subseteq\gamma_3(K)\), equality in the present
quotient is strictly stronger in scope than the accepted class-two
conclusion. It is still quotient non-separation, not AC equivalence in
\(F\), a counterexample, or exhaustion of the original conjecture.

`replay.py` does not import the construction code. It treats the stored
DAGs as frozen: a purported recurrence cannot create or repair a missing
node. It binds the literal inputs and the kernel-1 permutation images,
executes their word prefixes, and checks that both residuals have zero
integral flow on the full 60-vertex Cayley graph. This is the exact
first-order \(K/K'\) condition, not a modular approximation.
The complete base identities for \(C^{-1}\) as normal commutator factors
and for \(d^{-1}\) as normal \(a\)-factors are freely reduced.

The higher membership certificate is explicit. Set
\[
 a_0=\rho(a),\quad b_0=\rho(b),\quad
 d_j=d^{-1}(a_j,b_0),\quad
 a_{j+1}=C^{-1}(a_j,d_j)\quad(0\leq j<3).
\]
At each layer the stored normal-\(a\) factorization of \(d^{-1}\) is
mapped to a factorization of \(d_j\) by conjugates of \(a_j^{\pm1}\).
The stored normal-\([a,b]\) factorization of \(C^{-1}\) is mapped to
one of \(a_{j+1}\) by conjugates of \([a_j,d_j]^{\pm1}\).
The replayer checks these homomorphic images and all their actual word
substitutions. Starting from \(a_0,b_0\in K\), induction gives
\[
             a_j,d_j\in K^{(j)},\qquad a_3=e\in K^{(3)}.
\]
This argument uses normality of each \(K^{(j)}\) in \(F\); it does not
assume that the kernel action is central.

All substitution and normal-error recurrences are replayed independently.
Their underlying group identities are checked by free reduction on
indeterminates, so they hold under every substitution in every group.
The final free equations (8) are certified by these recurrences and
the completely replayed base identities. The enormous final words are
not expanded letter by letter. This is an exact algebraic certificate,
not a Lean kernel certificate or a numerical endpoint experiment.
