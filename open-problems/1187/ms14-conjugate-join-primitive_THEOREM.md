# An all-conjugator primitive-free boundary

## Statement

Let \(F=F(x,y)\), and let
\[
 R=x^{-1}y^2xy^{-3},\qquad
 S_e=x^{-1}yx^2y^ex^{-2}\quad(e\in\{-1,1\}).
\]
For every \(e\) and every \(h\in F\), the subgroup
\[
 H_{e,h}=\langle R,hS_eh^{-1}\rangle
\]
contains no element primitive in the ambient group \(F\).

In fact, every nonidentity element of this subgroup has a cyclically reduced
representative containing both signs of at least one of \(x,y\).

This is a complete boundary for one unrestricted conjugation-then-Nielsen
block, not a counterexample to the Andrews--Curtis conjecture. Originality and
publication-level significance have not been established. The proof and exact
certificate below await the normal independent Reviewer.

## 1. The rank-two input and its precise use

We use only the following necessary condition: if \(w\) is cyclically reduced
and primitive in \(F(x,y)\), then
\[
 |w|=|\operatorname{exp}_x(w)|+|\operatorname{exp}_y(w)|.
\]
It follows that \(w\) uses at most one sign of each generator. Equivalently,
its letters belong to one of the four sets
\[
 \{x,y\},\quad\{x,y^{-1}\},\quad
 \{x^{-1},y\},\quad\{x^{-1},y^{-1}\}.
\]
This includes primitive words using just one generator. No converse is used:
a sign-coherent word need not be primitive.

Source: Adam Piggott, *Palindromic primitives and palindromic bases in the
free group of rank two*, arXiv:math/0507049v1, Section 1, Remark 1.2, page 4;
notation in Notation 0.1. The remark states the displayed necessary equality
and cites the rank-two classification of Cohen--Metzler--Zimmermann.
The source passage read by a separate research worker is in
`primitive-source.txt`. The ledger records this as the external assumption
`PIGGOTT-R12`, not as a compiler-checked fact. Independent citation attribution
remains part of Reviewer delivery.

## 2. Universal reduction, before the finite atlas

Use the Cayley tree \(T\) with vertices \(F\), and an edge labelled \(a\)
from \(g\) to \(ga\), for \(a\in\{x,x^{-1},y,y^{-1}\}\). Left multiplication
preserves labels. Both displayed relators are cyclically reduced and have
length seven.

For a cyclically reduced word \(u\) of length seven, let \(A_u\) be its
periodic line through the identity. Its vertices are exactly
\[
 u^k p_i(u),\qquad k\in\mathbb Z,\quad 0\leq i<7,
\]
where \(p_i(u)\) is its length-\(i\) prefix. Concatenation of copies of \(u\)
and, backwards, \(u^{-1}\), has no cancellation; it is therefore an embedded
line in the tree. Left multiplication by \(u\) translates this line by seven.
The corresponding line for \(hS_eh^{-1}\) is \(hA_{S_e}\).

Two such lines either have a common vertex or are disjoint. They cannot
intersect only in the interior of an edge, because an intersection containing
an edge contains its endpoints. This dichotomy does not assume a bound on
\(|h|\), on the length of the common segment, or on a subgroup word.

### 2.1 Intersecting lines

Suppose the lines have a common vertex \(z\). For some integers \(k,l\) and
phases \(0\leq i,j<7\),
\[
 z=R^k p_i(R)=hS_e^l p_j(S_e).
\]
Consequently
\[
 h=R^k t S_e^{-l},\qquad
 t=p_i(R)p_j(S_e)^{-1},
\]
and hence
\[
 R^{-k}H_{e,h}R^k=\langle R,tS_et^{-1}\rangle.             \tag{1}
\]
Primitivity is invariant under conjugation: conjugating every member of a
free basis gives another free basis. Thus the intersecting case is covered
by exactly the \(7\cdot7=49\) phase inputs for each sign. Coinciding lines,
arbitrarily long intersections, and either orientation are included by the
same common-vertex argument; no maximal-overlap assertion is needed.

### 2.2 Disjoint lines

Suppose the lines are disjoint. There is a shortest connecting segment
\([a,b]\), with \(a\in A_R\) and \(b\in hA_{S_e}\). One can obtain it by
minimizing their nonempty set of integer vertex distances. In a tree it is
the unique connecting segment meeting each line only at its endpoint.
Its first edge at \(a\) is not either edge of \(A_R\) at \(a\), and its
last edge, directed from \(b\) toward \(a\), is not either edge of
\(hA_{S_e}\) at \(b\); otherwise the connecting segment could be shortened.

Write \(a=R^kp_i(R)\), \(b=hS_e^lp_j(S_e)\), and \(c=a^{-1}b\). The
conjugate \(a^{-1}H_{e,h}a\) is generated by
\[
 r_i=p_i(R)^{-1}Rp_i(R),\qquad
 c\,s_j\,c^{-1},\quad s_j=p_j(S_e)^{-1}S_ep_j(S_e).
\]
As reduced words, \(r_i,s_j\) are cyclic rotations of \(R,S_e\).
Construct a labelled barbell with these two length-seven circles and a path
labelled by the reduced word \(c\) joining their distinguished vertices.
At each circle the two outgoing labels are distinct, because its word is
cyclically reduced. The preceding shortest-segment property makes the
connector's outgoing label distinct from both circle directions at each
endpoint. At an interior connector vertex its two directions are distinct
because \(c\) is reduced. Thus this entire barbell is folded: there is at most
one outgoing edge of any given label at any vertex.

Any nonempty cyclically nonbacktracking circuit in a barbell includes a
complete traversal of at least one circle. A circuit confined to a circle is
a nonzero power of that circle. If it uses the connecting path, it must travel
around a circle before returning across that path: at every other circle
vertex degree two forces continuation, and reversing immediately is
backtracking. A circuit cannot be supported on the connecting path alone.
Both \(R\) and \(S_e\), in either orientation and any cyclic rotation, contain
both \(x\) and \(x^{-1}\). Every such circuit therefore has both signs of
\(x\). Section 4 explains why this suffices for all subgroup elements,
including words whose initial representatives have extensive cancellation.

## 3. Exact certificate for the intersecting case

The alphabet in the code and JSON is `x,y,X,Y`, where capitals denote
inverses:

```
R   = XyyxYYY
S_- = XyxxYXX
S_+ = XyxxyXX
```

`atlas.py` starts from the two based loops
\[
 R,\quad \operatorname{red}(tS_et^{-1}),\qquad
 t=\operatorname{red}(p_i(R)p_j(S_e)^{-1}).
\]
It repeatedly identifies the endpoints of edges with the same initial
vertex and label, carrying inverse edges as well. Each identification reduces
the number of vertex classes, so the procedure terminates without a search
cutoff. No restriction is placed on subgroup words.

The result is stored in `atlas.json`. For each of the 98 indexed inputs it
contains a finite labelled inverse graph, with base vertex zero, accepting
both literal generators. For each of the four sign sets it also contains a
topological ordering of the directed subgraph consisting of the edges with
those labels. The stored order increases strictly along every such edge.
Thus each of these 392 subgraphs is acyclic.

The graphs have 11, 12, or 13 vertices; all have rank two. These measurements
are not the universality argument: equation (1) is. No conjugator-length or
subgroup-word-length search substitutes for that reduction.

The separate standard-library `replay.py` imports no code from `atlas.py`.
It verifies the exact Cartesian product of both signs and all phase pairs;
recomputes the conjugators and generator words from the displayed literals;
checks every inverse edge, determinism, connectivity and rank; follows both
generator loops; and checks every edge inequality in all 392 topological
orders. The proof only needs a folded inverse graph *containing* the two
generator loops. It does not rely on an unverified assertion that a saved
graph accepts no additional words, or on a black-box folding theorem.

As an additional bounded check, the Argus Jacobian adapter is used with
`graph.directed.acyclic_order.compute`. Its input is the disjoint union of the
four sign subgraphs of one maximum-vertex case for each sign, eight components
in total. Its exact payload, typed response, and adapter status are saved as
`jacobian-payload.json`, `jacobian-output.json`, and `jacobian-status.json`.
The separate replay checks that payload against the atlas and verifies the
returned topological order edge by edge. This supporting operation is not
claimed to cover the other phase cases; the full replay covers all of them.
Transport failure, timeout, or any result other than exact acyclicity fails
the replay and provides no mathematical conclusion.

The saved Engineer replay passed 98 phase cases, 196 literal generator loops,
2,552 inverse-edge and determinism checks, 392 topological orders, and 5,104
edge inequalities. Four deliberately invalid controls (a changed literal
generator, a missing inverse edge, a reversed order, and a directed two-cycle)
were rejected. Jacobian version 0.14.0, protocol 2025-11-25, returned exact
acyclicity for its 104-vertex, 112-edge payload, with adapter exit zero, no
timeout, and no error. These are replay results, not independent review of
the universal prose proof or a novelty verdict.

## 4. Cancellation, cyclic reduction, and the conclusion

Here is the elementary graph-to-word argument, used in both cases. In a
folded inverse graph, consecutive inverse *labels* on a path must traverse
the same edge in opposite directions: at their common vertex the reverse
of the first edge is the unique edge with the second label. Deleting a
free-cancellation pair therefore preserves the path endpoints.

Products of the two accepted generator loops and their inverses are closed
paths, so free reduction of every subgroup word remains a closed path.
If the first and last labels of a reduced closed path are inverse, the
folded property likewise says that its first edge is inverse to its last.
Deleting both leaves a closed path based at the next vertex. Repeating gives
a cyclically nonbacktracking circuit whose label is a cyclic reduction of the
element. It is nonempty for a nonidentity element.

In the disjoint case that circuit contains a full mixed-\(x\)-sign circle,
as proved in Section 2.2. In an intersecting phase graph a nonempty
sign-coherent circuit would be a directed closed walk of positive length in
one of the four sign subgraphs. Such a walk would strictly increase the
saved vertex order at every edge yet return to its starting vertex, which is
impossible. Thus its label contains both signs of some generator. Equation
(1) transfers this conclusion to every original intersecting join.

Conjugation and cyclic rotation preserve primitivity and the signs present
in a cyclic word. The rank-two necessary condition from Section 1 now excludes
every nonidentity element in both cases. The identity is not primitive.
This proves the stated theorem for both signs and all \(h\). No bounded
search failure is used as an infinite quantifier.

## 5. Exact implication for the AC route, and what remains open

After arbitrary independent conjugations the tuple has the form
\[
 (gRg^{-1},\,kS_ek^{-1}).
\]
Simultaneous conjugation by \(g^{-1}\) changes it to
\((R,hS_eh^{-1})\), with \(h=g^{-1}k\).
Every finite signed relator Nielsen sequence preserves the subgroup generated
by the two components. Both resulting components therefore remain in a
primitive-free subgroup. Neither can be a member of an ambient free basis.
Additional terminal componentwise conjugations do not help, because
primitivity is conjugacy-invariant.

This strengthens the accepted fixed-current-donor P0 and P2 certificates in
a different direction: the present block permits arbitrary initial
conjugators and unrestricted changes of both donors by any finite relator
Nielsen sequence. It is not asserted to contain either earlier bridge as a
special case; those bridges allow a different subsequent conjugation
interaction. All previously accepted artifacts are preserved.

Interleaving new, independently chosen componentwise conjugations *inside*
the Nielsen sequence can change the subgroup and is not excluded. An initial
Nielsen block before the conjugations is also outside this statement. The
full AC orbits, an exact counterexample certificate, and a verified-new
publishable result remain unresolved.
