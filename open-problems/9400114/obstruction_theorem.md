## 1. Statements

For positive a, write

    F_a(X) = (X-1)^a - X^a + 1.

Fix r>=2 and let c_j(p,r), 0<=j<r, be the integer coefficients of the
remainder of F_p on division by X^r-1. For a prime p not dividing r, put

    G(p,r) = gcd(|c_0(p,r)|,...,|c_(r-1)(p,r)|),
    w(p,r) = v_p(G(p,r)).

The proof below shows that G(p,r)>0 and w(p,r)>=1. In particular these are
finite, effectively computable integers, not conjectural invariants.

**Theorem 1 (target-free prime-power reduction).** Let p be prime, e>=1,
n>=1, p|n, r>=2, and gcd(n,r)=1. Then

    F_n = 0 modulo (p^e, X^r-1)

if and only if both

    F_n = 0 modulo (p, X^r-1),     e <= w(p,r).

There is no hypothesis that n modulo r belongs to the subgroup generated by
p, no restriction on the other prime factors of n, and no restriction on
their multiplicities.

Consequently, if n>=2, gcd(n,r)=1, and n=product p^e, the original Agrawal
identity is equivalent to

    F_n = 0 modulo (rad(n), X^r-1)
    and e <= w(p,r) for every p^e exactly dividing n.

**The exponent in the radical-modulus identity is still n, not rad(n).**
This is not a claim that replacing n by its radical preserves the identity.

**Theorem 2 (uniform repeated-prime obstruction).** Suppose n>=2, r>=2,
gcd(n,r)=1 and F_n=0 modulo (n,X^r-1). Then 4 does not divide n.
If an odd prime p satisfies p^2|n, necessarily r<p. Write

    p-1 = q*r+s,     0<=s<r.

Then

    s <= q,         r-s <= q-1.                         (1)

In particular, with

    B(r) = r+2+(r-1)*ceil((r+1)/2),

every repeated prime satisfies p>=B(r). Explicitly,

    B(r) = (r+1)^2/2 + 1        if r is odd,
    B(r) = (r+1)*(r+2)/2        if r is even.

Thus every nonsquarefree n satisfying the identity obeys

    n >= B(r)^2.                                       (2)

For odd r, this gives the single-modulus squarefreeness criterion

    r > sqrt(2)*n^(1/4)-1  ==>  n is squarefree.         (3)

The exact bound (2) is stronger than the convenient form (3).
These conclusions apply to all cofactors, not a finite prime pool.

**Corollary 3 (all exponents for pure prime powers).** For every prime p
not dividing r and every a>=1,

    F_(p^a) = 0 modulo (p^a,X^r-1)  <==>  a<=w(p,r).

This classifies admissible pure-prime-power exponents for every fixed p,r.
It does not assert that any such p^a violates the conclusion n^2=1 modulo r.

## 2. A finite-ring lemma

Let A_e=(Z/p^e Z)[X]/Psi_r, where

    Psi_r = 1+X+...+X^(r-1).

Because p does not divide r, X-1 and Psi_r are comaximal over Z/p^e Z:
Psi_r(1)=r is a unit. The Chinese remainder decomposition by these two
monic factors identifies the full cyclic ring with

    (Z/p^e Z) x A_e.

Put b=X-1 in A_e. The same comaximality proves that b is a unit.
The identity F_n at X=1 is zero for every positive n, so checking F_n in
the whole cyclic ring is equivalent to checking it in A_e.

For any integer u coprime to r, substitution X -> X^u defines an
automorphism sigma_u of A_e. Indeed, multiplication by u permutes residues
modulo r, so Psi_r(X^u)=Psi_r(X) modulo X^r-1 and hence is zero in A_e.
If uv=1 modulo r, the substitutions for u and v are inverse because X^r=1.

Reduction from A_e to A_1 induces a map on unit groups. Its kernel is

    K = 1+p*A_e,

which is a finite p-group: A_e is free of rank r-1 over Z/p^e Z, and K has
p^((e-1)*(r-1)) elements. Every element 1+p*z is a unit by the finite
geometric series. In particular:

**Lemma.** Two units of orders prime to p, with the same reduction modulo p,
are equal if their quotient also has order prime to p.

The condition on the quotient is automatic here because A_e is commutative:
if the units have orders dividing T with p not dividing T, so does their
quotient. A unit in K with order prime to p must be the identity.

## 3. Proof of Theorem 1

Suppose first that F_n vanishes modulo (p^e,X^r-1). In A_e this says

    b^n = sigma_n(b).

Let t be the order of b in the finite unit group. Automorphisms preserve
orders, whereas the order of b^n is t/gcd(t,n). Equality therefore gives

    gcd(t,n)=1.

Since p|n, the order t is prime to p. The units b^p and sigma_p(b) both
have orders dividing t. They have the same reduction modulo p by the
ordinary characteristic-p binomial identity. The finite-ring lemma yields

    b^p = sigma_p(b).

This is F_p=0 in A_e, hence F_p=0 in the full cyclic ring. It is exactly
e<=w(p,r). Reduction also gives the required identity modulo p.

Conversely, suppose that F_p vanishes modulo (p^e,X^r-1). Let f be the
order of p in the finite group (Z/rZ)^*. Iterating b^p=sigma_p(b) gives

    b^(p^f) = sigma_p^f(b) = b.

Thus t divides p^f-1 and is prime to p. If F_n vanishes modulo p, the
units b^n and sigma_n(b), whose orders divide t, have the same reduction.
Their quotient has order prime to p, so the lemma again gives
b^n=sigma_n(b) in A_e. This proves the claimed lift and both directions.

Applying this argument separately to each prime-power divisor of n and
then using the ordinary integer Chinese remainder theorem proves the
radical-modulus formulation.

For completeness, G(p,r) is not zero. Otherwise the integer polynomial
F_p would vanish in Q[X]/Psi_r. Taking the product over all nonidentity
r-th roots of unity would give

    |product (zeta-1)|^p = |product (zeta^p-1)|.

The product has absolute value r, and p permutes those roots, giving
r^p=r, impossible for r>=2 and p>=2. Characteristic-p Frobenius shows that
p divides every c_j, so w(p,r)>=1.

Over Z/p^e Z, use the determinant of multiplication in the free
rank-(r-1) algebra A_e instead of complex roots. Multiplication by b has
determinant (-1)^(r-1)*r, and an algebra automorphism conjugates
multiplication operators. Applying determinants to b^p=sigma_p(b)
therefore gives r^(p-1)=1 modulo p^e. (The signs cancel: if p=2, r is odd.)
In particular this gives the useful necessary bound

    w(p,r) <= v_p(r^(p-1)-1).

The converse to this norm condition is not asserted and is false in the
explicit p=491531,r=7 control already recorded in this project.

## 4. Proof of Theorem 2

For p=2, F_2=2-2X. If p does not divide r then r is odd and at least 3.
Its degree is less than r, so its cyclic residual does not vanish modulo 4.
Theorem 1 therefore rules out 4|n.

Let p be odd with p^2|n. Theorem 1 gives F_p=0 modulo (p^2,X^r-1).
For 1<=k<p,

    binom(p,k) = p*(-1)^(k-1)/k modulo p^2.

Multiplication by (-1)^(p-k) cancels the sign, because p is odd.
Consequently

    F_p(X)/p = sum_(k=1)^(p-1) X^k/k modulo p.          (4)

If r>=p, the nonzero polynomial on the right has degree p-1<r and
cannot vanish modulo X^r-1. Hence r<p.

Write p-1=qr+s, 0<=s<r. For j=1,...,r, the coefficient in residue j
(with j=r representing residue zero) of the cyclic reduction of (4) is

    H_j = sum 1/(j+t*r),

where t=0,...,q if j<=s and t=0,...,q-1 if j>s.
All these denominators are integers between 1 and p-1 and hence invertible
in F_p. Every H_j must be zero.

For 1<=m<p define in F_p[Y]

    Q_m(Y) = product_(t=0)^(m-1) (Y+t*r).

At each relevant j, Q_m(j) is nonzero and the product rule gives

    Q_m'(j)/Q_m(j) = sum_(t=0)^(m-1) 1/(j+t*r).

For j=1,...,s, use m=q+1. These s distinct field elements are roots of
Q_(q+1)', a nonzero polynomial of degree q: its leading coefficient is
q+1, which is not zero modulo p. Thus s<=q.

For j=s+1,...,r, use m=q. These r-s distinct field elements are roots of
Q_q', a nonzero polynomial of degree q-1. Thus r-s<=q-1.
Here 1<=q and q+1<p, since r>=2 and p is odd, so both degree statements
hold, including q=1. This proves (1).

Adding the two inequalities gives r<=2q-1, hence
q>=ceil((r+1)/2). The second inequality also gives s>=r-q+1. Therefore

    p = qr+s+1 >= q*(r-1)+r+2 >= B(r).

Every nonsquarefree n has a repeated prime; the case p=2 has been excluded.
Thus p^2<=n and p>=B(r), proving (2).
For odd r, (2) implies
sqrt(n)>=B(r)>(r+1)^2/2, which implies (3) by contraposition.

## 5. Proof of Corollary 3

For n=p^a, the identity modulo p is the iterated characteristic-p binomial
identity. Apply Theorem 1 with e=a. No assumption on a modulo the order of
p modulo r is necessary.

## 6. Prior art and the precise unverified novelty question

The proof above is elementary and self-contained. No external theorem is
being used as an unproved mathematical assumption.

Several ingredients are classical and are not presented as new:

* Elbaz-Vincent and Gangl, *On Poly(ana)logs I*, Compositio Mathematica 130
  (2002), 161-214, DOI 10.1023/A:1013757217319, Definition 5.1 and
  Observation 5.5(1), pp.182-183, explicitly give the finite-logarithm
  polynomial (4) and its Witt-vector connection.
* Voloch, *Plane curves and p-adic roots of unity*, Bulletin of the
  Australian Mathematical Society 60 (1999), 479-482,
  DOI 10.1017/S0004972700036637, main theorem and proof pp.479-480,
  uses a Frobenius-defect polynomial as a p^2 torsion obstruction.
* Dobson, *On Lerch's formula for the Fermat quotient*,
  arXiv:1103.3907v6, Section 2, equation (8), p.6, identifies
  residue-class reciprocal sums with Lerch interval sums. Dilcher and
  Skula, *Linear relations between certain sums of reciprocals modulo p*,
  Annales des sciences mathematiques du Quebec 35(1) (2011), 17-29,
  Theorem 1.2, p.18, proves universal linear relations among such sums.
* Popovych, *A note on Agrawal conjecture*, ePrint 2009/008,
  Proposition 3.1, pp.3-4, manuscript December 30, 2008, gives a
  squarefree, Frobenius-targeted sufficient construction. It does not
  state the target-free prime-power equivalence proved here.
* Granville, *It is easy to determine whether a given integer is prime*,
  Bulletin AMS 42 (2005), 3-38, DOI 10.1090/S0273-0979-04-01037-7,
  Proposition 8.6, p.35, gives the norm condition for the Agrawal
  congruence. Its following paragraph reports a different squarefreeness
  result requiring the congruence for every small r.
* Kayal and Saxena, *Towards a deterministic polynomial-time Primality
  Test*, IIT Kanpur project report, April 2002, Section 5, Lemmas 5.2-5.3,
  pp.10-12, already prove the arbitrary-cofactor necessary lifting
  implication at prime-power divisors of n. The archived full primary
  report was obtained in the continuation; Section 6.6 gives the precise
  overlap and the narrower remaining contribution.

### 6.1. Direct comparison with Granville

The actual paper was read again for the materially new originality comparison,
not to repeat the accepted arithmetic. The publisher PDF request returned
HTTP 403; a public copy of the primary text was readable at

<https://raw.githubusercontent.com/dtbinh/OpenCourse/master/AbstractAlgebra/Andrew%20Granville%202004%20It%20is%20easy%20to%20determine%20whether%20a%20given%20integer%20is%20prime.pdf>.

On printed p.35, Proposition 8.6 assumes positive m,n,r, odd n, gcd(n,r)=1
and F_n=0 modulo (m,X^r-1), and concludes r^n=r modulo m.
Taking n=p odd and m=p^e gives r^(p-1)=1 modulo p^e.
Thus the determinant consequence in Section 3, including its application to
w(p,r), is a known consequence and is **not** an original contribution.

The following paragraph reports a squarefreeness theorem with the congruence
assumed at every r<=2*(ln n)^2. Theorem 2 instead uses one specified r and
has the threshold r of order n^(1/4). These hypotheses differ: the
single-length result must not be described as an asymptotic improvement of
the many-length criterion. Proposition 8.6 and that paragraph do not state
the target-free lifting equivalence or the two floor inequalities. This is
a comparison with those actual passages, not a claim that the entire
literature lacks such statements.

The same page also reports Fibonacci congruences at r=5, and pp.35-37 give
equivalent recurrence formulations (Lemma 8.7 and Propositions 8.8-8.9).
Consequently, a reformulation in cyclotomic roots, a norm congruence, or a
recurrence sequence alone would not resolve the originality gap.

### 6.2. Direct comparison with the torsion-obstruction mechanism

The four-page author preprint of Voloch's paper is available at

<https://web.ma.utexas.edu/users/voloch/Preprints/torgm.pdf>.

Its theorem on preprint p.1 assumes integral coefficients in an unramified
extension of Q_p, irreducible nonbinomial reduction of degree d, and
p>d^2+2. It bounds by p*d^2 the number of root-of-unity pairs (also
allowing zero coordinates) with |f(zeta_1,zeta_2)|<p^(-1).
The proof on pp.1-2 encodes the prime-to-p torsion problem modulo p^2
using the reduction of

    (f^sigma(X^p,Y^p)-f(X,Y)^p)/p.

Thus a Frobenius defect as a p^2 torsion obstruction is classical.
The inspected theorem does not state the arbitrary-e equivalence of
Theorem 1 or the arithmetic-progression root counts of Theorem 2.
This identifies the precise additional statements to compare; it does not
establish that they are absent elsewhere in this literature.

### 6.6. Earlier arbitrary-cofactor necessity: Kayal-Saxena 2002

The strongest relevant earlier result read in this continuation is not the
squarefree Popovych construction. It is the original report by Neeraj
Kayal and Nitin Saxena, *Towards a deterministic polynomial-time Primality
Test*, April 2002. The archived institutional page is

<https://web.archive.org/web/20021019215923id_/http://www.cse.iitk.ac.in:80/research/btp2002/primality.html>.

It links the full PostScript report, preserved at

<https://web.archive.org/web/20060312204452id_/http://www.cse.iitk.ac.in:80/research/btp2002/primality.ps.gz>.

Section 5 contains the following actual statements, with notation
transcribed from the report. The report's setting uses odd n; the
substitutions in the proofs require gcd(n,r)=1. Restricting r to the
coprime prime lengths used by the original conjecture is already enough
to establish the overlap below.

* **Lemma 5.2, p.10, proof pp.10-11:** if p is prime, p^a divides n,
  and F_n=0 modulo (n,X^r-1), then
  F_(p^a)=0 modulo (p^a,X^r-1).
* **Lemma 5.3, p.11, proof pp.11-12:** if
  F_(p^a)=0 modulo (p^a,X^r-1), then
  F_p=0 modulo (p^a,X^r-1).

The key equations were read both from the streamed PostScript text and
from separately rendered printed pp.10-11, rather than guessed from
legacy-font superscripts. Lemma 5.2 writes n=m*p^a, performs substitutions
by inverses of p and m modulo r, and imposes no local Frobenius-target
condition or extra restriction on the other prime factors of m.
Its use of the original congruence is only through reduction modulo p^a,
so the argument also applies if that local congruence is the hypothesis
and p^a divides n.

In particular, at every actual prime-power divisor p^e of a full
Agrawal solution, the necessary conclusion F_p=0 modulo
(p^e,X^r-1), hence e<=w(p,r), is **already in this earlier proof**.
The arbitrary cofactor in that necessary implication is not an original
contribution of this manuscript. This corrects the earlier comparison
against only squarefree sufficient constructions.

This does not justify calling the whole of Theorem 1 a quotation from
the report. Its local statement allows an arbitrary e>=1 even when
p^e does not divide n, and it includes the sufficient direction

    F_p=0 modulo (p^e,X^r-1), F_n=0 modulo (p,X^r-1)
        ==> F_n=0 modulo (p^e,X^r-1).

That implication and the full exact equivalence were not located as
statements in the inspected Section 5. They still need a genuine
originality and significance assessment; an unlocated elementary
converse is not automatically a publishable result. In particular,
Corollary 3's necessity is already covered by these lemmas, while its
sufficiency follows by iterating the exponent-p identity. Introducing the
notation w(p,r) is not by itself a new theorem.

**Theorem 5.1, p.12**, says that acceptance by Algorithm B implies n is
squarefree. Algorithm B, Section 2.1, tests a range of ring lengths up
to 4*(log n)^2, not one specified r. Its proof uses Lemmas 5.2-5.3,
the norm consequence, and a count of smooth integers. The report's
introduction, p.3, explicitly distinguishes its empirical observation
for one r>=5 from that proved algorithmic statement. Thus neither the
algorithmic squarefreeness theorem nor its proof should be confused
with Theorem 2's one-length floor inequalities.

For comparison, a separate reader also obtained the original April
2001 Bhattacharjee-Pandey report:

<https://web.archive.org/web/20060117221552id_/http://www.cse.iitk.ac.in:80/research/btp2001/primality.ps.gz>.

Its Theorem 1, p.5, and Theorem 4.8, p.15, give squarefreeness in the

Its related single-polynomial implication in Section 5, p.18, remains a

### 6.7. Contribution and significance boundary

Compared with the project's strongest relevant prior result, this proof
replaces finite first-lift exclusions and a Frobenius-target-dependent
argument by a necessary-and-sufficient lifting theorem for every p,r,e
and arbitrary cofactors. The bound (2) excludes an infinite nonsquarefree
parameter region. Neither conclusion supplies the original counterexample
or resolves squarefree integers.

That is a comparison with the campaign's previous work, not the strongest
literature result. Against Kayal-Saxena's 2002 lemmas, the
cofactor-independent necessary multiplicity obstruction is already known.
The proposed residual contribution is the sufficient local lift and
exact equivalence at arbitrary e, together with the quantitative
single-length floor exclusion. It is not the finite logarithm,
Teichmuller uniqueness, the norm condition, the known necessary
implication, the pure-power restatement, or the computations.
The converse has a short finite-group proof and has not been shown to
give a new algorithmic complexity bound. The floor exclusion constrains
an infinite nonsquarefree region, but no unconditional supply of
admissible primes or classification of squarefree solutions is proved.
These limitations matter to field-level significance, not just wording.

## 8. A strict endpoint refinement at the same ring length

This section is subsequent work, separately accepted for correctness and
statement fidelity in the November 3 endpoint review. Its proof uses the
reviewed floor obstruction but no external theorem. Neither originality
nor publishable significance has been established.

**Theorem 4 (endpoint exclusion).** Under the hypotheses of Theorem 2,
let p be a repeated odd prime and write p-1=qr+s, 0<=s<r. Then

    s <= q-2.

Furthermore, either (p,r)=(11,3), or

    r-s <= q-2.                                       (5)

The same assertion holds with the weaker local hypothesis
F_p=0 modulo (p^2,X^r-1), without an integer n.

Define

    C(r) = r+3+(r-1)*ceil((r+4)/2),
    D(r) = 11 if r=3, and C(r) otherwise.

Every repeated prime of a full Agrawal solution satisfies p>=D(r).
Consequently, every nonsquarefree solution satisfies

    n >= D(r)^2.                                      (6)

This strictly improves the accepted baseline B(r)^2 under identical
hypotheses. It does not improve the order of growth: away from r=3,

    D(r)-B(r) = r             if r is even,
    D(r)-B(r) = 2*r-1         if r is odd.

In particular D(r)/B(r) tends to 1. This lower-order sharpening must not
be called an improved asymptotic exponent or, without independent
assessment, a publishable result.

### 8.1. The critical-point moment

Work in F_p, p>=7, and let 2<=m<p and p not divide r. Put

    Q_m(Y) = product_(t=0)^(m-1) (Y+t*r),
    c = -(m-1)*r/2.

If lambda_1,...,lambda_(m-1) are all the roots of Q_m', counted with
multiplicity in an algebraic closure, then

    sum lambda_i = (m-1)*c,
    sum (lambda_i-c)^2 = (m-2)*(m^2-1)*r^2/12.         (7)

Here 2, 12 and m are units. To prove (7), the roots of Q_m centered at c
are the equally spaced elements (t-(m-1)/2)*r, up to sign. Their sum is
zero and their sum of squares is m*(m^2-1)*r^2/12.
Thus Q_m(Z+c) has zero coefficient of Z^(m-1) and coefficient
-m*(m^2-1)*r^2/24 at Z^(m-2). Differentiate and divide by m.
For m>=3, the first two coefficient identities for the resulting monic
polynomial give (7). When m=2 the derivative has its unique root at c,
so both assertions follow directly. No assumption of distinct critical
points is used.

### 8.2. Excluding the first endpoint

Theorem 2 gives s<=q and r-s<=q-1. It also gives p>=B(r)>=6,
so p>=7; q>=2 and q+1<p. Suppose s=q. Section 4 shows that
Q_(q+1)' has all the q roots 1,...,q, and its degree is exactly q.
Their center is (q+1)/2, which equals -q*r/2 in F_p because

    p = q*r+q+1.

Compare their centered square sum q*(q^2-1)/12 with (7). Cancelling
the nonzero factor q*(q-1)/12 gives

    q+1 = (q+2)*r^2 in F_p.

Since q*r=-(q+1), multiplication by q^2 now gives

    (q+1)*(3*q+2) = 0 in F_p.

But s=q<r implies r>=3, and

    p-(3*q+2) = q*(r-2)-1 > 0.

Both positive factors are smaller than p, a contradiction. Thus s<q.
The next possibility s=q-1 would give p=q*(r+1), impossible for a
prime because q>=2. This proves s<=q-2.

### 8.3. Excluding the second endpoint, with its exact exception

Suppose r-s=q-1. If q=2 then s=r-1 and p=3*r, impossible.
Hence q>=3. The derivative Q_q' has precisely the q-1 roots
s+1,...,r, counted without repetition. Their center (s+r+1)/2
equals -(q-1)*r/2 in F_p, and their centered square sum is
q*(q-1)*(q-2)/12. Equation (7) therefore gives

    q = (q+1)*r^2 in F_p.

Also p=(q+1)*r-q+2, so (q+1)*r=q-2 in F_p. Multiplying the
last displayed moment identity by q+1 yields

    5*q-4 = 0 in F_p.                                (8)

The endpoint hypothesis and s>=0 imply q<=r+1.
For r>=6,

    p-(5*q-4) = q*(r-6)+r+6 > 0,

contradicting (8). For r=5, q<=6 and the same difference is
11-q>0. For r=4, p=3*q+6 is composite. For r=3 the only
possibilities q>=3 are q=3,4: these give p=11,13 respectively;
only p=11 divides 5*q-4. For r=2 only q=3 remains, giving
p=7, which does not divide 11. Thus the sole possible endpoint is
(p,r,q,s)=(11,3,3,1).

That exception really occurs: the already accepted integer cyclic
residual of F_11 at r=3 is [-242,0,242], divisible by 11^2.
This reuses the established control, not a new counterexample claim.
Outside that exception, the old inequality strengthens to (5).

### 8.4. The strengthened uniform bound and what it contributes

Outside the exception, add the two strict endpoint restrictions:
r<=2*q-4, hence q>=ceil((r+4)/2). The second restriction also
gives s>=r-q+2. Therefore

    p = q*r+s+1
      >= q*(r-1)+r+3
      >= C(r).

At r=3 the exceptional prime is 11, and every nonexceptional repeated
prime is at least C(3)=14. Thus p>=D(r) in every case. The exclusion
of p=2 is already Theorem 2, and p^2<=n gives (6).

For comparison, B(5)=19 whereas D(5)=28, and B(7)=33 whereas
D(7)=46. Since p is prime, these particular thresholds force
p>=29 and p>=47 respectively. These improvements use the same local
hypothesis and cannot be attributed to changing the number of tested
ring lengths or to restricting cofactors.

The new mathematical ingredient is the critical-point second moment
that excludes equality cases of the accepted degree bound. Necessary
prime-power lifting remains Kayal-Saxena's known input, and the finite
logarithm and its reciprocal sums remain classical. The result is a
strict but lower-order improvement of the strongest applicable project
baseline, not a classification of squarefree solutions or a new
asymptotic regime. The complete Hegde-Devaraj comparison and an
independent field-significance assessment remain necessary before
claiming a publication-qualified result. No such claim is made here.

### 9.1. Exact size and scope of the quantitative contribution

The surviving mathematical contribution relative to the accepted project
baseline is the universal endpoint exclusion, rather than a larger finite
test. Its improvement can be quantified exactly under identical hypotheses:

    r even:
        B(r) = (r^2+3*r+2)/2,
        D(r) = (r^2+5*r+2)/2,
        D(r)^2-B(r)^2 = r^3+4*r^2+2*r;

    r odd, r>=5:
        B(r) = (r^2+2*r+3)/2,
        D(r) = (r^2+6*r+1)/2,
        D(r)^2-B(r)^2 = 2*r^3+7*r^2-2.

These polynomial identities were checked by exact rational coefficient
arithmetic, not by sampling integers. Both square bounds retain leading
term r^4/4. Solving the inequalities for the allowed ring length gives

    r even:
        old: r <= (sqrt(8*sqrt(n)+1)-3)/2,
        new: r <= (sqrt(8*sqrt(n)+17)-5)/2;

    r odd, r>=5:
        old: r <= sqrt(2*sqrt(n)-2)-1,
        new: r <= sqrt(2*sqrt(n)+8)-3.

Thus the new cutoff has the same leading term sqrt(2)*n^(1/4);
its limiting shift is one ring length in the even formulas and two in
the odd formulas. These real cutoffs need not differ by that exact
integer after rounding and parity restrictions. At r=3, p>=B(3)=9
already forces the odd prime p>=11, so D(3)=11 adds no repeated prime
exclusion after prime rounding, even though its numerical threshold is
larger.

The second-moment proof is an additional argument that rules out
universal parameter families; it is not a finite exclusion or a
restatement of necessary lifting. However, the comparison has not supplied
a new asymptotic exponent, an improved leading constant, a classification
of squarefree solutions, a proof of the original n^2=1 conclusion, or a
new algorithmic complexity result. Calling the theorem publishable solely
because it is correct and excludes infinitely many parameters would not
substantiate field-level significance. Conversely, these limitations are
not a proof that no journal could value the result. Publication
significance remains an assessment not earned by the present evidence.
