# Conditional exact-order subset products

This construction seeks the original r=5 counterexample. No witness has been
found. It does not replace the full-ring certificate by a smoothness test,
subgroup-membership result, or arbitrary-exponent solution.

## Exact finite encoding

The input is a finite set of distinct primitive primes modulo 5. For each p,
the exact order t_p of X-1 in F_p[X]/(Phi_5) is computed, not replaced by the
upper bound 10(p^2-1). Both the annihilating power and every prime-factor
minimality condition are separately replayed in the full cyclic ring, using
its idempotent

    E = 1 - (1/5)(1 + X + X^2 + X^3 + X^4).

Fix s in {2,3}, and put a_p=1 if p=s mod5, and a_p=3 otherwise.
Frobenius and exactness of the order give, for n=s mod5,

    (X-1)^n = X^s-1 modulo (p,Phi_5)
        iff n = p^a_p modulo t_p.

For a squarefree product of selected primes these equivalences, the identity
at X=1, and CRT give the full identity modulo (n,X^5-1). A resulting integer
must nevertheless pass `certificates/verify_candidate.py`.

Let M=lcm(5,{t_p}). The current unit encoding restricts the pool to primes
coprime to M. This is a disclosed construction restriction, not a theorem
excluding nonunits. The smooth-pool recipes guarantee it by taking p>B and
requiring p-1 and p+1 to be B-smooth. They exclude primes with smooth exact
order but nonsmooth upper bound, as well as nonprimitive and repeated factors.

For each odd prime-power factor q^e of M, choose and verify a primitive root.
The logarithms of every pool prime are recomputed by modular exponentiation.
The two-adic part uses the two factors generated by -1 and 5; it is not
incorrectly assumed cyclic. Reduction to a smaller q-power reduces its log
modulo the corresponding smaller group order.

With Boolean variables x_p, the multiplicative subset problem becomes

    n mod5 = s;
    sum(x_p) >= 3;
    x_p implies sum_q x_q log(q) = a_p log(p)
        in every cyclic coordinate required by t_p.

Here the inner q indexes pool primes, not prime-power components. All
variables are genuinely 0 or 1. No local congruence is activated by an unused
prime. Consequently the common M is an encoding device, not an additional
requirement that n meet the targets of the whole pool. A synthetic control
has an admissible subset product 273 that violates an unused prime's
congruence; the encoding permits that subset, while the full-ring verifier
correctly rejects it as a counterexample.

## First smooth-pool obstruction

`coordinated-smooth47-p2000000.pool.json` contains every prime p with

    47 < p <= 2000000,
    p mod5 in {2,3},
    p-1 and p+1 both 47-smooth.

There are 110 primes and 16 unit coordinates; M has 146 bits.
Z3 returns UNSAT for the s=2 conditional Boolean problem.
The independent implementation in
`certificates/replay_coordinated_subset.py` does not use the generator's
polynomial reduction, its discrete-log search, or its SMT encoding. It
reconstructs the prime pool by trial division, checks all 110 exact local
orders in the full cyclic ring, and checks the actual integer product of
every compatible subset for both targets s=2 and s=3.

For either target it visits 269 clique nodes, including 44 triples and
7 four-element subsets. There is no compatible subset with five or more
primes. None of the eligible products is a witness. The incompatibility
graph excludes 5888 of the 5995 possible prime pairs. Thus this is a
product-compatibility obstruction in the stated finite pool, not a solver
timeout or an argument excluding all squarefree integers.

The exact replay is `certificates/coordinated-smooth47-replay.json`.
This is Engineer-run independent code, not an independent Reviewer verdict.
The mathematical improvement over the previous bounded-cardinality searches
is a complete, selection-conditional treatment of every cardinality in a
jointly smooth pool with primes up to two million. It still does not resolve
the original existence claim.

## Residue structure change

The next construction fixes the paired classes {3,27} modulo 80 rather than
mixing incompatible two-adic targets. These are the powers 3 and 3^3 modulo
80. Both have v_2(p^2-1)=3. For either target s, the corresponding p^a_p
values agree modulo 16, so all pairwise two-adic compatibility constraints
are satisfied by construction. This follows directly from the two residues;
it does not assume existence of a counterexample from the source theorem.

The odd-order support is allowed to use primes through 199, still with
p<=2000000. This changes the residue structure and order-factor support,
not merely the coefficient-prime cutoff. Every remaining odd-coordinate
constraint still has to be satisfied by an actual selected product.

The materialized paired pool has 160 primes and 47 unit coordinates, with
a 363-bit common modulus. The recorded 45-second integer-arithmetic solve
returned UNKNOWN, not UNSAT. A second, exact bit-vector encoding also
returned UNKNOWN under its 45-second limit. No speed or solvability
improvement is claimed from that change. The independent product enumerator
reached its two-million-node limit for each target and encountered compatible
subsets with 18 primes. Its `boundary_replayed` field is **false**.
These results are stored in `coordinated-paired80-target2.json`,
`coordinated-paired80-target2-bv.json`, and
`certificates/coordinated-paired80-replay.json`.

This paired, broader-support pool does not have the first pool's
four-factor ceiling, but it has not supplied a successful product.
The paired pool is not excluded and its route must not be retired on
these timeout or node-limit results.

## Exact alternative encodings and disjoint search domains

The bit-vector formulation first splits each required cyclic-group modulus
into relatively prime prime powers. Modulo 2 it uses Boolean XOR. Modulo
2^h it uses an h-bit sum, whose wraparound is precisely the required
congruence. For odd moduli the width represents both the modulus and the
largest possible nonnegative weighted sum, so no arithmetic overflow is
discarded before taking the remainder. The Boolean cardinality constraint
still rejects fewer than three factors.

The disjoint-branch search fixes the least selected prime p_i and excludes
all earlier primes, while leaving all later selections Boolean. Every
permitted nonempty subset has exactly one such least prime. Together with
the two target residues, the 160-prime pool has 320 disjoint branches.
An UNSAT branch excludes only its own stated least-prime/target class;
UNKNOWN leaves that branch open. The batch records every attempted branch
and preserves its exact domain and timeout status, rather than restarting
an undifferentiated search. Its native durable-runner receipt is retained
in `coordinated-paired80-submission.json`.

The durable batch finished normally after 600.4 seconds on one leased CPU.
All 320 branches were attempted. For target 2, 77 returned UNSAT and 83
returned UNKNOWN; for target 3, 71 returned UNSAT and 89 returned UNKNOWN.
There were no SAT models and no candidate. These are exact solver outcomes
on the stated encodings, not independent Reviewer judgments. In particular,
the 172 UNKNOWN branches are not exclusions, and the 160-prime pool has
**not** been exhausted. Every branch is retained in
`coordinated-paired80-branches.json`, whose `exhausted_declared_pool` and
`campaign_complete` fields are both false.

## A sharper paired-class identity

The paired residue structure yields a further exact simplification.
For each prime p in the paired pool write

    u_p = t_p / (2^v_2(t_p) * 5^v_5(t_p)).

Then for a subset of at least three primes from this pool, the entire
counterexample identity and failed conclusion are equivalent to

    the subset has odd cardinality, and
    n/p = 1 (mod u_p) for every selected p.

To see this, write p=3^epsilon_p modulo 80, with epsilon_p in {1,3}.
For the product, n=3^K modulo 80, where K=sum(epsilon_p). Thus n mod5
is primitive exactly when K, equivalently the cardinality, is odd.
With s=n mod5 and a_p chosen as above, a_p*epsilon_p=K modulo 4.
Consequently n=p^a_p modulo 80. Since t_p divides 10(p^2-1),
its 2-primary part divides 16 and its 5-primary part is exactly 5
or a divisor thereof. Those parts of every selected congruence are
therefore automatic.

The remaining factor u_p divides p^2-1. Hence p^3=p modulo u_p,
so n=p^a_p modulo u_p is simply n=p modulo u_p.
The pool's unit condition allows division by p, giving n/p=1 modulo u_p.
Reversing these implications and using the exact-order criterion proves
the stated equivalence. This does not assert that a subset satisfying the
remaining conditions exists. It separates the unresolved product problem
from residue conditions already guaranteed by the construction.
