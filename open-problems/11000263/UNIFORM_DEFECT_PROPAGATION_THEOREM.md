# Exact computer-assisted Schreier-defect propagation and universal finite-dimensionality

## Theorem

Let \(K=\mathbb Q(q)\), let \(Q_n\) be either repaired
twist-compatible Bigelow zipper quotient from `OBJECTIVE.md`, and put

\[
s=(q-1)^2(q+1),\qquad e=s^{-1}X_3,\qquad
P(T)=(T-1)(T+q).
\]

For every \(n\geq4\), define

\[
d_j^{(n)}=\sigma_j\sigma_{j+1}\cdots\sigma_{n-1},
\qquad
\bar d_j^{(n)}=\sigma_{n-1}\cdots\sigma_{j+1}\sigma_j,
\]

with \(d_n^{(n)}=\bar d_n^{(n)}=1\), and recursively define

\[
\begin{aligned}
L_3&=\operatorname{span}_K\{e,\sigma_1e\},&
L_n&=\sum_{j=1}^{n}d_j^{(n)}L_{n-1},\\
R_3&=\operatorname{span}_K\{e,e\sigma_2\},&
R_n&=\sum_{j=1}^{n}R_{n-1}\bar d_j^{(n)}.
\end{aligned}                                                   \tag{1}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{Q_ne=L_n,\qquad eQ_n=R_n}
\]

and therefore

\[
\boxed{\dim_KQ_ne\leq\frac{n!}{3},\qquad
\dim_KeQ_n\leq\frac{n!}{3}.}                                  \tag{2}
\]

Writing \(J_n=Q_nX_3Q_n\), one consequently has

\[
\boxed{\dim_KJ_n\leq\left(\frac{n!}{3}\right)^2,\qquad
\dim_KQ_n\leq(2n-1)!!+\left(\frac{n!}{3}\right)^2.}             \tag{3}
\]

In particular, both universal repaired quotients are finite-dimensional
at every finite rank by an exact computer-assisted theorem.  The only
essential machine lemma is the rank-four left/right closure stated below;
conditional on that finite lemma, the all-rank proof is algebraic and is not
a conclusion from a finite matrix image.

## 1. Exact rank-four base and algebraic tail propagation

The reviewed characteristic-zero \(Q_4\) regular quotient gives

\[
\dim_KQ_4e=\dim_KeQ_4=8.
\]

The eight elements generated by (1) on each side have exact rank eight,
so

\[
Q_4e=L_4,\qquad eQ_4=R_4.                                    \tag{4}
\]

The same exact action reconstructs all six left and all six right
rank-four diagonal defects in these bases.  Thus rank four is a complete
base table, rather than a specialization or a guessed rewrite system.
The source is
`../base-and-candidate-transversals-certificate.json`, which in turn uses
the exact 169-dimensional quotient certified from the cubic, braid,
distant-commutation, zipper, twist, and \(X_4\) rows.

The rank-four right closure includes

\[
eP(\sigma_3)=0.                                               \tag{5}
\]

The five-strand tail identity follows algebraically from (5), so it does not
need an independent rank-five computation.  Put \(z=\sigma_3\) and
\(w=\sigma_4\).  Since \(zwz=wzw\),

\[
wzP(w)=P(z)wz,
\qquad z^{-1}P(w)z=wP(z)w^{-1}.                              \tag{6}
\]

The element \(e\) commutes with \(w\).  Left multiplying (6) by \(e\),
using (5), and cancelling the indicated invertible factors gives

\[
ezP(w)=0,\qquad ez^{-1}P(w)=0.
\]

Expanding \(eP(z)=0\) and multiplying on the right by \(z^{-1}\) gives

\[
ez^{-1}=q^{-1}ez+(q-1)q^{-1}e.
\]

Hence

\[
0=ez^{-1}P(w)
=q^{-1}ezP(w)+(q-1)q^{-1}eP(w)
=(q-1)q^{-1}eP(w),
\]

and therefore \(eP(w)=0\).  Repeating the same two-generator argument and
using that \(e\) commutes with every \(\sigma_i\), \(i\ge4\), gives

\[
eP(\sigma_i)=0\ (i\ge3),\qquad
P(\sigma_i)e=0\ (i\ge4).                                    \tag{7}
\]

Thus the previously computed identity
\(X_3(\sigma_4-1)(\sigma_4+q)=0\) now has a direct derivation.  The exact
rank-five certificates remain useful independent checks, but are no longer
proof inputs for the terminal quadratic.

The rank-four closure in (4) remains computer-assisted.  Its exact
characteristic-zero action tables verify all 24 left and 24 right generator
products.  No readable relation-by-relation derivation of those 48 inclusions
is currently claimed.  Therefore a reader requiring a wholly hand-derived
proof should regard the theorem as conditional on (4).  The theorem recorded
here is explicitly computer-assisted and rests on the supplied exact
certificate.

If \(b=\sigma_{n-1}\) and \(u\) belongs to either principal module on
generators through \(\sigma_{n-3}\), (7) gives the terminal quadratic
rules

\[
b^2u=(1-q)bu+qu,\qquad ub^2=(1-q)ub+qu.                       \tag{8}
\]

Equivalently, on the appropriate side,

\[
b^{-1}u=q^{-1}bu+(q-1)q^{-1}u,\qquad
ub^{-1}=q^{-1}ub+(q-1)q^{-1}u.                               \tag{9}
\]

The coefficients in (9) were also returned by the inspected Jacobian
operation `matrix.symbolic.linear_system.solve`; that call is an
independent algebra check, not an input to the proof.

## 2. Schreier multiplication and the only defects

For \(1\leq i<n\), braid and distant-commutation give

\[
\sigma_i d_j^{(n)}=
\begin{cases}
d_j^{(n)}\sigma_i,&i<j-1,\\
d_{j-1}^{(n)},&i=j-1,\\
(1-q)d_j^{(n)}+qd_{j+1}^{(n)}
 +P(\sigma_j)d_{j+1}^{(n)},&i=j,\\
d_j^{(n)}\sigma_{i-1},&i>j.
\end{cases}                                                   \tag{10}
\]

All terms except the last term in the diagonal case are already in
\(L_n\), provided \(L_{n-1}\) is positively closed.  Define that remaining
left defect by

\[
D_k^{(n)}(v)=P(\sigma_k)d_{k+1}^{(n)}v,
\qquad 1\leq k<n,\quad v\in L_{n-1}.                          \tag{11}
\]

Reversing only the pure braid words in (10) gives

\[
\bar d_j^{(n)}\sigma_i=
\begin{cases}
\sigma_i\bar d_j^{(n)},&i<j-1,\\
\bar d_{j-1}^{(n)},&i=j-1,\\
(1-q)\bar d_j^{(n)}+q\bar d_{j+1}^{(n)}
 +\bar d_{j+1}^{(n)}P(\sigma_j),&i=j,\\
\sigma_{i-1}\bar d_j^{(n)},&i>j.
\end{cases}                                                   \tag{12}
\]

Thus the right defects are

\[
\bar D_k^{(n)}(v)=v\bar d_{k+1}^{(n)}P(\sigma_k),
\qquad v\in R_{n-1}.                                         \tag{13}
\]

No reversal anti-automorphism of \(Q_n\) is asserted or used.  In fact,
the reviewed rank-five computation shows that word reversal does not
preserve the defining `define_y` row.  Formula (12) is only the reversal
of braid-group equalities; the right base defects are independently
certified.

## 3. The finite insertion identities

Fix \(n\geq5\), write \(b=\sigma_{n-1}\), and for
\(0\leq j\leq n-1\) define

\[
C_j^{(n)}(A)=d_{j+1}^{(n)}Ab.                                \tag{14}
\]

If \(A=d_l^{(n-1)}u\), where \(1\leq l\leq n-1\) and
\(u\in L_{n-2}\), then

\[
C_j^{(n)}(d_l^{(n-1)}u)=
\begin{cases}
d_l^{(n)}d_j^{(n-1)}u,&l\leq j,\\[2mm]
(1-q)d_{j+1}^{(n)}d_l^{(n-1)}u
+q\,d_{l+1}^{(n)}d_{j+1}^{(n-1)}u,&l>j.
\end{cases}                                                   \tag{15}
\]

The first branch is a braid identity.  For the second branch, first use

\[
d_{j+1}^{(n)}d_l^{(n-1)}
=d_{l+1}^{(n)}d_{j+1}^{(n)}
\qquad(l>j),
\]

and then apply (8) to the two terminal copies of \(b\).  Every term on
the right side of (15) is visibly in \(L_n\).  The endpoint \(j=0\) is
always in the second branch, and \(j=n-1\) is always in the first branch;
both are included in the formula.

For the right side define

\[
\bar C_j^{(n)}(A)=bA\bar d_{j+1}^{(n)}.                       \tag{16}
\]

If \(A=u\bar d_l^{(n-1)}\), with \(u\in R_{n-2}\), reversing the same
pure braid identities and applying (8) on the left gives

\[
\bar C_j^{(n)}(u\bar d_l^{(n-1)})=
\begin{cases}
u\bar d_j^{(n-1)}\bar d_l^{(n)},&l\leq j,\\[2mm]
(1-q)u\bar d_l^{(n-1)}\bar d_{j+1}^{(n)}
+q\,u\bar d_{j+1}^{(n-1)}\bar d_{l+1}^{(n)},&l>j.
\end{cases}                                                   \tag{17}
\]

Thus both insertion maps land in their respective rank-\(n\) recursive
families.  There are only two indexed insertion branches on either side.

## 4. Uniform left defect propagation

Take \(v=d_j^{(n-1)}u\), where \(1\leq j\leq n-1\) and
\(u\in L_{n-2}\).  Direct braid-polynomial calculation gives

\[
P(\sigma_k)d_{k+1}^{(n)}d_j^{(n-1)}=
\begin{cases}
d_j^{(n)}P(\sigma_{k-1})d_k^{(n-1)}b^{-1},&j<k,\\
d_{k+1}^{(n)}d_k^{(n-1)}P(b),&j=k,\\
d_{j+1}^{(n)}P(\sigma_k)d_{k+1}^{(n-1)}b,&j>k.
\end{cases}                                                   \tag{18}
\]

In the middle branch, \(P(b)u=0\) by (7), because \(u\) uses only
generators through \(\sigma_{n-3}\).  In the first branch put
\(A=D_{k-1}^{(n-1)}(u)\).  Inductively \(A\in Q_{n-1}e\), so
\(AP(b)=0\), and (9) replaces \(Ab^{-1}\).  The last branch is precisely
an insertion.  Consequently

\[
D_k^{(n)}(d_j^{(n-1)}u)=
\begin{cases}
q^{-1}C_{j-1}^{(n)}(D_{k-1}^{(n-1)}(u))
 +(q-1)q^{-1}d_j^{(n)}D_{k-1}^{(n-1)}(u),&j<k,\\[1mm]
0,&j=k,\\[1mm]
C_j^{(n)}(D_k^{(n-1)}(u)),&j>k.
\end{cases}                                                   \tag{19}
\]

These are the promised three uniform branches.  Their index ranges are
\[
1\leq j<k<n,\qquad 1\leq j=k<n,\qquad
1\leq k<j\leq n-1.
\]
The endpoint \(j=1<k\) invokes \(C_0^{(n)}\), already covered by (15).

The rank-four base table puts every \(D_k^{(4)}(u)\) in \(L_4\).
Assuming the statement through rank \(n-1\), every recursive defect on
the right side of (19) is in \(L_{n-1}\), and (15) puts every insertion
in \(L_n\).  Hence every rank-\(n\) defect belongs to \(L_n\).
Equation (10) now proves positive generator closure of \(L_n\).

## 5. Uniform right defect propagation

For \(v=u\bar d_j^{(n-1)}\), the independently oriented braid calculation
is

\[
\bar d_j^{(n-1)}\bar d_{k+1}^{(n)}P(\sigma_k)=
\begin{cases}
b^{-1}\bar d_k^{(n-1)}P(\sigma_{k-1})\bar d_j^{(n)},&j<k,\\
P(b)\bar d_k^{(n-1)}\bar d_{k+1}^{(n)},&j=k,\\
b\bar d_{k+1}^{(n-1)}P(\sigma_k)\bar d_{j+1}^{(n)},&j>k.
\end{cases}                                                   \tag{20}
\]

Here \(uP(b)=0\) in the middle branch.  In the first branch put
\(A=\bar D_{k-1}^{(n-1)}(u)\).  Inductively \(A\in eQ_{n-1}\), hence
\(P(b)A=0\), and use the left version of (9).  This yields

\[
\bar D_k^{(n)}(u\bar d_j^{(n-1)})=
\begin{cases}
q^{-1}\bar C_{j-1}^{(n)}(\bar D_{k-1}^{(n-1)}(u))
 +(q-1)q^{-1}\bar D_{k-1}^{(n-1)}(u)\bar d_j^{(n)},&j<k,\\[1mm]
0,&j=k,\\[1mm]
\bar C_j^{(n)}(\bar D_k^{(n-1)}(u)),&j>k.
\end{cases}                                                   \tag{21}
\]

The independent right rank-four table starts the induction, and (17)
completes it.  Equations (12) and (21) therefore prove positive generator
closure of \(R_n\).

## 6. Strict termination and inverse closure

For one recursive defect call, use

\[
\mu=(r,\epsilon)\in\mathbb N^2,
\]

where \(r\) is its rank and \(\epsilon=1\) records its single unresolved
marked defect.  Every nonzero child call in (19) or (21) has rank \(r-1\);
the insertion maps create no marked defect, and the equal-index branch
has none.  Thus \(\mu\) strictly decreases lexicographically along every
branch and reaches the exact rank-four table after at most \(n-4\)
recursive calls.  For a linear combination, the equivalent global
measure is the multiset of defect ranks with the standard multiset
extension of \(<\): replacing one rank-\(r\) defect by finitely many
lower-rank defects is strict.  This also handles branching without
claiming that support size itself decreases.

The common cubic relation is

\[
(\sigma_i-1)(\sigma_i+q)(\sigma_i+q^3)=0,
\]

so

\[
\sigma_i^{-1}=q^{-4}\left(
\sigma_i^2+(q+q^3-1)\sigma_i+q^4-q-q^3
\right).                                                       \tag{22}
\]

Positive closure therefore implies inverse closure on both sides.
Since \(e\in L_n\cap R_n\), closure gives
\(Q_ne\subseteq L_n\) and \(eQ_n\subseteq R_n\).  The reverse inclusions
are immediate from (1), proving the equalities in the theorem.

The recursive families have at most

\[
2\cdot4\cdot5\cdots n=\frac{n!}{3}
\]

members, which proves (2).  No independence assertion at higher rank is
needed.

## 7. Ideal and quotient bounds

Because \(s\) is invertible in \(K\),

\[
J_n=Q_nX_3Q_n=Q_neQ_n.
\]

Multiplication is a surjection

\[
(Q_ne)\otimes_K(eQ_n)\twoheadrightarrow J_n,
\]

so (2) gives the ideal bound in (3).  The reviewed structural quotient is

\[
Q_n/J_n\cong\operatorname{BMW}_n(q,-q^3),
\qquad \dim_K(Q_n/J_n)=(2n-1)!!.
\]

The exact sequence \(0\to J_n\to Q_n\to Q_n/J_n\to0\) then proves the
second inequality in (3) and universal finite-dimensionality.

The argument first proves all identities in the common truncated
presentation.  The direct-limit terminal-row repair is a further quotient,
so every spanning identity and bound descends.  At the rank-four base its
additional row is already in the ideal generated by \(X_4\), giving the
same exact base.  This proves the theorem separately for both repairs
without assuming a relation-preserving anti-automorphism.

The immutable Burau twist representation has nonzero \(X_3\), so
\(e\ne0\) and \(J_n\ne0\).  The BMW projection survives both repairs and
kills \(X_3\).  These facts show that neither side of the exact sequence
has been collapsed accidentally; they are not used as substitutes for
the spanning proof.
