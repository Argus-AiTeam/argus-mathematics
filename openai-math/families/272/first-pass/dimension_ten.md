# Exact dimension-ten construction

## Statement and scope

Let the four integer matrices \(M_i\) be those in
`sections/certificate.tex`, equation `eq:explicit-matrices`, of the staged
September 27, 2026 manuscript. Set
\(M(x)=\sum_{i=0}^3x_iM_i\) and \(L(E_{ij})=M_i^TM_j\).
The verification target is the specific pair defined in
`sections/geometry.tex`, equations `eq:UV`, `eq:complement`, `eq:SR`, and
`eq:phi-pair`: both maps must be PPT, and the composite's complex Choi
matrix must be nonzero with no nonzero product vector in its range.

This is not a verification of zero distillable secret key. That conclusion
also uses the represented-class transfer, the four-effect inequality, and
the complete-transcript protocol argument. No minimality or novelty claim
is made here.

## Verification theorem

For the staged integer pencil, the following assertions hold over **C**.

1. The maximal-minor scheme in projective three-space consists of twenty
   reduced points, all in the chart \(x_0=1\). Its rational coordinate
   algebra is a degree-twenty field generated by \(t_1\).
2. Every ten of their quadratic-evaluation vectors are independent, even
   for linear relations with arbitrary complex coefficients.
3. The specified maps \(\Phi_1,\Phi_2\) are PPT. Their composite Choi
   matrix \(Z\) is nonzero and has no nonzero complex product vector in
   its range.
4. In addition, \(\operatorname{rank}Z=80\), and its kernel is exactly the
   span of the twenty vectors
   \(\widehat x_j\otimes\widehat x_j\). The rank assertion and an explicit
   rational kernel basis are additional certified invariants, not
   conclusions drawn from the manuscript's printed modular summaries.

This is an exact verification theorem with an additional rank
determination, not a counterexample, numerical experiment, proof of
zero secret key, or assertion of verified novelty. Independent review
and a significance/novelty assessment remain outstanding.

### A regular representation proves the quotient, not just its dimension

Order the three-variable monomials by total degree, then by
\((a,b,d-a-b)\), with \(a=0,\ldots,d\) and \(b=0,\ldots,d-a\).
Let v list the twenty monomials of degree at most three, and w the
fifteen quartics. Direct determinants of the fifteen four-row
submatrices give
\[
 p=(p_\rho)_\rho=Dv+Hw,\qquad C=-H^{-1}D.
\]
The two-sided inverse proves \(H\) invertible over Q; the certificate
retains D, H and C, not just determinant residues.

Construct three operators \(T_i\) on a formal rational vector space with
basis \(e_\alpha\), \(|\alpha|\leq3\). A column is \(e_{\alpha+e_i}\)
when the successor has degree at most three; otherwise it is the
corresponding row of C, transposed. The script verifies
\[
 [T_1,T_2]=[T_1,T_3]=[T_2,T_3]=0,\qquad
 T^\alpha e_0=e_\alpha\quad(|\alpha|\leq3),
\]
and \(T^\gamma e_0=C_{\gamma,*}^{\,T}\) for all fifteen quartics.
Consequently \(p_\rho(T)e_0=0\) for every minor.

Here is the algebraic deduction that makes those tests sufficient.
Commutativity gives an action of Q[t1,t2,t3]. Each \(p_\rho(T)\)
annihilates every \(T^\alpha e_0\), since it commutes with the \(T_i\).
These vectors are a basis, so \(p_\rho(T)=0\) as an operator.
Thus evaluation on \(e_0\) factors through
\(\mathcal A=\mathrm Q[t_1,t_2,t_3]/(p_\rho)\), and is onto the formal
twenty-dimensional space. Conversely, \(w=Cv\) reduces total degree,
so v spans \(\mathcal A\), proving its dimension is at most twenty.
The surjection proves the opposite inequality and independence of v.
It is therefore an isomorphism of vector spaces, intertwining
multiplication with the \(T_i\).

This argument proves a nonzero quotient directly. It does not assume
independence from a spanning list, assume confluence of arbitrary
reductions, infer characteristic-zero dimension from a modular rank,
or invoke the six-bilinear-equations intersection lemma.

### Primitive element and all twenty complex points

Let \(f(z)=\det(zI-T_1)\). Characteristic polynomials are computed
directly over Q and over each finite field, rather than by the supplied
Newton-identity code. Rational reduction of every coefficient is checked
against the independently computed finite-field characteristic
polynomial.

The certificate contains
\[
 d=16424929493204834831157019337052,\qquad
 F(y)=d^{20}f(y/d)=\det(yI-dT_1)\in\mathrm Z[y].
\]
Indeed d is the common denominator of the entries of \(T_1\), and every
displayed coefficient of F is explicitly checked integral. The primes
41, 131 and 139 are prime by trial division and do not divide d.

The modular factorizations are reconstructed by polynomial gcd and
exact division, and every resulting factor is certified irreducible:
for degree s the checker proves \(z^{p^s}=z\) modulo the factor and,
for each prime divisor q of s, proves
\(\gcd(g,z^{p^{s/q}}-z)=1\). These conditions characterize
irreducibility because the irreducible factors of
\(z^{p^k}-z\) have exactly the degrees dividing k. The full products
and the squarefree derivative gcd are checked as well.

| p | det H mod p | Certified factor degrees of f mod p |
|---|---:|---|
| 41 | 40 | 20 |
| 131 | 23 | 1, 2, 17 |
| 139 | 9 | 1, 19 |

In particular f is irreducible over Q. For completeness, monic
factorization over Q respects reduction at a prime where all
coefficients are integral: the minimum p-adic valuation of polynomial
coefficients is additive under multiplication, since the nonzero
reductions after normalization multiply nontrivially. In a monic
factorization both minimum valuations are nonpositive and their sum is
zero, hence both are zero. Reducing such a factorization would
contradict irreducibility modulo 41.

The Krylov matrix
\([e_0,T_1e_0,\ldots,T_1^{19}e_0]\) has an explicitly checked nonzero
determinant 19 modulo 41. Its columns are therefore independent over Q.
The exact vector identity \(f(T_1)e_0=0\) is also checked; commutativity
and cyclicity make it an operator identity. There is no lower-degree
annihilator, by the Krylov independence. Hence
\[
 \mathcal A=\mathrm Q[t_1]\simeq\mathrm Q[z]/(f).
\]
Solving the Krylov system reconstructs and checks the rational
polynomials \(h_2,h_3\) of degree below twenty representing \(t_2,t_3\).
They are serialized in the certificate. Equality on \(e_0\), followed
by commutativity and cyclicity, also proves
\(T_i=h_i(T_1)\) for i=2,3.

In characteristic zero the irreducible polynomial f is separable:
its nonzero derivative has smaller degree and cannot share its
irreducible factor. If its roots are \(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_{20}\),
scalar extension gives
\[
 \mathcal A\otimes_{\mathrm Q}\mathrm C
 \simeq \mathrm C[z]/(f)\simeq\mathrm C^{20}.
\]
The last isomorphism is evaluation at the roots; its inverse is
Lagrange interpolation. This proves reducedness and gives exactly the
points
\((1,\lambda_j,h_2(\lambda_j),h_3(\lambda_j))\), without approximating
any root.

There are no additional points at infinity. At \(x_0=0\), the vector of
homogeneous minors is \(Hw(t)\); its vanishing forces every quartic,
including \(t_1^4,t_2^4,t_3^4\), to vanish. Then t=0, which is not a
projective point.

### Universal quadratic evaluations, including complex coefficients

The only non-elementary external proposition used here is the integer
Frobenius factorization theorem: Milne, *Fields and Galois Theory*,
v5.10 (September 2022), Theorem 4.28, pp. 55-56. It is recorded as
`L_QUADRATIC / A_DEDEKIND_INTEGER`; the actual source and proposition
excerpt are retained in `research/literature/`. Its independent
attribution is for the Host's Reviewer, not self-certified here.

Apply this integer theorem to F, not directly to a rational polynomial
with unspecified denominators. Since d is a unit at all three primes,
scaling roots by d preserves all irreducible factor degrees and the
rational splitting field. Thus the Galois group of f contains cycles
of types (1,2,17) and (1,19); irreducibility makes it transitive.
A nineteen-cycle fixes one point and is transitive on its complement.
Transitivity then makes every point stabilizer transitive on its
complement, so the action is two-transitive. The seventeenth power of
the first permutation is a transposition. Two-transitivity conjugates
this to every transposition, and these generate S20.

Over the splitting field, evaluation of the ten monomials of degree
at most two has column rank ten: they are part of the proved basis v
of the split coordinate algebra. Thus one ten-row determinant is
nonzero. Every ordered set of ten distinct roots is the image of
this set under some element of S20. Because the \(h_i\) have rational
coefficients, the corresponding field automorphism carries each
evaluation entry, and hence its determinant, to the selected one.
All these determinants are nonzero in the splitting field and remain
nonzero in C.

The last inference is crucial: a determinant nonzero in a subfield
stays nonzero under its embedding in C, so its matrix has trivial
kernel even on **arbitrary complex** coefficient vectors. No
automorphism of arbitrary transcendental coefficients is being
assumed. This is the bridge formalized in
`full_spark_over_extension` and `zero_bound_of_full_spark`.

Homogenizing degree-at-most-two monomials identifies them, up to column
ordering, with the ten coordinates
\(q(x)=(x_ix_j)_{i\leq j}\). Hence every nonzero homogeneous quadratic
vanishes at at most nine of the twenty directions.

### Rational construction, transpose conventions and positivity

All sixteen Gram blocks \(M_i^TM_j=M_j^TM_i\) are checked. Define the six
real Kraus matrices \(Q_r\) by \((Q_r)_{ai}=(M_i)_{ra}\).
The script checks their action on every matrix unit:
\(L(E_{ij})=\sum_r Q_rE_{ij}Q_r^T=M_i^TM_j\).
Thus L is CP, and its symmetric output blocks give \(T_4L=L\), so
it is PPT. For complex x the block symmetry gives
\[
 L(P_x)=M(x)^T\overline{M(x)}=M(x)^*M(x);
\]
the Gram kernel is exactly \(\ker M(x)\). Also
\(L(P_{e_0})=36I_4\), checked exactly.

Let \(W_s\) have columns \(e_i\otimes e_i\) and
\(e_i\otimes e_j+e_j\otimes e_i\), and let \(W_a\) have columns
\(e_i\otimes e_j-e_j\otimes e_i\). Put
\[
 D=\operatorname{diag}(1\text{ on }ii,\sqrt2\text{ on }ij,\ i<j).
\]
Then \(W_s=UD\), \(W_a=\sqrt2 V\), with exactly the manuscript's
isometries U,V and the same ordered complementary-pair involution K.
The normalized isometries and \(K^T=K,\ K^2=I\) are checked.

Use the integer-coordinate maps
\[
 S_0(B)=W_a^T(L\otimes L)(W_sBW_s^T)W_a,\qquad
 R_0(B)=K S_0(B)^T K.
\]
The thirty-six integer Kraus matrices of \(S_0\) are
\(B_{rs}=W_a^T(Q_r\otimes Q_s)W_s\). Comparing complete
superoperators proves, without restricting B to real or Hermitian
inputs,
\[
 S_0T_{10}=S_0,\quad T_6S_0=S_0,\quad
 R_0T_{10}=R_0,\quad T_6R_0=R_0.
\]
In particular \(R_0=\operatorname{Ad}_K S_0\) is CP with Kraus
\(KB_{rs}\). The transpose-fixed identities also prove their PPT
properties. The adjoint of \(R_0\) has real transposed Kraus matrices;
its output transpose is itself because \(R_0T_{10}=R_0\).
Embedding and compression by the real first-six inclusion E therefore
give two PPT maps
\[
 \Phi_{1,0}(B)=E S_0(B^T)E^T,\qquad
 \Phi_{2,0}(B)=R_0^\dagger(E^TBE).
\]
This proves PPT of the **pair**, not merely of its composite.

Normalization relates them to the specified manuscript pair:
\[
 S_0(B)=2S(DBD),\quad R_0(B)=2R(DBD),\quad
 \Phi_{1,0}=2\Phi_1\operatorname{Ad}_D,\quad
 \Phi_{2,0}=2\operatorname{Ad}_D\Phi_2.
\]
Consequently, for the manuscript's input-reference Choi convention,
\[
 Z_0=J(R_0^\dagger S_0T_{10})
     =4(D\otimes D)Z(D\otimes D).
 \tag{1}
\]
Conversely the original maps are positive multiples and CP
conjugations of these PPT maps, so are PPT themselves. Invertible
local congruence preserves rank, nonzero positivity, and whether the
range contains a nonzero product vector.

The script forms the superoperator with its input transpose, reshuffles
it using
\((Z_0)_{(i,a),(j,b)}=F_0(E_{ij})_{ab}\), and checks exact equality
with the Gram matrix of all 1296 vectors obtained from the composite
Kraus matrices \(B_{rs}^T K B_{uv}\). This verifies positivity and the
transpose/Choi indexing, rather than relying on sampled eigenvalues.
It also checks \(Z_0^T=Z_0\) and invariance under output partial
transpose.

The exact nonzero value is
\[
 (Z_0)_{(00,00),(00,00)}=40310784,\qquad
 Z_{(00,00),(00,00)}=10077696=6\cdot36^4.
\]

### A polynomial kernel identity replaces a delicate complex pairing

The strongest direct convention check is the following identity of
homogeneous vector-valued quartics:
\[
 Z_0\bigl(q(x)\otimes q(x)\bigr)=B\,(\Delta_\rho(x))_\rho,
 \qquad B\in\mathrm Z^{100\times15}.
 \tag{2}
\]
Every entry of B is reconstructed and serialized as
`tensor.kernel_minor_multipliers`. To check (2), let A be the
100-by-35 coefficient matrix with
\(q(1,t)\otimes q(1,t)=A(v,w)^T\). The script proves
\[
 Z_0A=B[D\mid H]
\]
coefficient by coefficient, including that B is integral. Equality
in the affine chart gives (2) by degree-four homogenization. Thus
every complex singular direction, not just a numerical root or a
finite-field sample, gives a kernel vector.

This identity is an alternative to assuming the exterior-dual pairing
correct. It incorporates the exact signed K, the output transpose,
and the Choi input transpose through the actual constructed matrix.
The full matrix identities already establish the intended normalization.

If \(u\otimes v=Z_0w\), symmetry and (2) imply
\[
 0=(q(x_j)\otimes q(x_j))^T Z_0w
   =(q(x_j)^Tu)(q(x_j)^Tv)
\]
at all twenty points. If u and v are nonzero, both factors are nonzero
homogeneous quadratics, each with at most nine zeros. Their zero sets
cannot cover twenty points. This proves product exclusion.
The pairing here is bilinear **transpose**, not Hermitian adjoint;
it is valid because \(Z_0\) is symmetric. No conjugation of the
complex roots, or mistaken equality of transpose and adjoint, occurs.
Equation (1) transfers the conclusion to Z.

The final implication, including its quantification over all extension
field coefficients, is the zero-hole Lean theorem
`DimensionTenCertificate.dimensionTen_from_galois_equivariance`.
The separate fidelity document states its exact assumptions and what
is not compiled.

### Exact rank and full kernel

The certificate constructs
\[
 X=A\begin{pmatrix}I_{20}\\ C\end{pmatrix},\qquad Z_0X=0.
\]
Twenty explicitly selected rows of X are exactly \(I_{20}\);
therefore these twenty rational kernel columns are independent and
\(\operatorname{rank}Z_0\leq80\). Independently, the certificate gives
row and column indices for an 80-by-80 minor of \(Z_0\) whose determinant
is 23 modulo 41. As \(Z_0\) is integral this proves
\(\operatorname{rank}_{\mathrm Q}Z_0\geq80\), hence its rank is
exactly eighty over Q and C.

At each of the twenty points,
\(q(x_j)\otimes q(x_j)=Xv(t^{(j)})\).
The twenty-by-twenty evaluation matrix of the basis v is invertible
by the split-algebra isomorphism. These twenty kernel products
therefore span \(\operatorname{im}X=\ker Z_0\).
Equation (1) transforms them to
\(\widehat x_j\otimes\widehat x_j\) and proves the same rank and
kernel-span assertions for Z.
