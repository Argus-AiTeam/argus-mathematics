# Primary-source reading

Eric Ricard and Quanhua Xu, *Khintchine type inequalities for reduced free
products and Applications*, arXiv:math/0505302v2, 27 February 2016.
Read at <https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/math/0505302>, Section 1 and
Section 2, especially Theorem 2.5 and Fact 2.6.
Version confirmed at <https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0505302>.

The hypotheses preceding Fact 2.6 are centered letters
\(a_k\in\mathring A_{i_k}\) with successively different component indices.
Here \(P_i\) is the projection onto reduced words beginning in component \(i\).
Fact 2.6 states the operator identity
\[
 a_1\cdots a_l
 =\sum_{k=0}^l C_1\cdots C_k A_{k+1}\cdots A_l
  +\sum_{k=0}^{l-1} C_1\cdots C_k K_{k+1} A_{k+2}\cdots A_l ,
\]
where \(C_k=P_{i_k}a_kP_{i_k}^{\perp}\),
\(A_k=P_{i_k}^{\perp}a_kP_{i_k}\), and
\(K_k=P_{i_k}a_kP_{i_k}\).
The formula is transcribed with these three abbreviations; it is not a
verbatim prose quotation.

Theorem 2.5 separately bounds the reduced-word norm above by \(2l+1\)
times the maximum of its cut norms, and below by that maximum, also at
matrix levels. Fact 2.6 itself is an identity, not this inequality.
Neither statement at these locators claims the particular multiset
constant or through-label summation proved in the present report.

This is context and a prior-art boundary. The audit's exact domain-restricted
cut construction is proved directly in report.md, Section 1, so acceptance
of its theorem need not stand on an unchecked external citation.
