## Result and its status

The two target assertions have an all-parameter proof below. In fact, the
same argument proves a stronger statement for **every** label multiset,
without requiring a full matrix algebra in any component.

**Theorem (all-multiset strengthening).** Let \(r\geq1\), in a von Neumann
algebra with a faithful normal tracial state. Suppose \(r\) scalar-free
unital components contain orthogonal projection partitions
\((e_a^{(j)})_{a=1}^d\), with sum 1 and trace \(1/d\). Define \(B_W\) exactly
as in the manuscript. Set
\[
 H_r=1+(r+1)(2^r-2),\quad K_r=r!(H_r+r+1),\qquad
 D_r=\max\{8r,\;4r\,r!(H_r+r+1)^2\}.
\]
For every \(d\geq D_r\) and every multiset \(W\) of cardinality \(r\),
\[
 \|B_W\|\leq 5\sqrt{(r-1)!}\,d^{-(r-1)/2}.                 \tag{1}
\]
Put \(\widetilde\beta_W=5\sqrt{(r-1)!}d^{-(r-1)/2}\) for all multisets.
If also \(d\geq13(r-1)(r-2)\), then
\[
 \max_a\sum_{|W|=r,\ a\in W}\widetilde\beta_W^2\leq26.     \tag{2}
\]
For \(r=1,2\), the left side in (2) is exactly 25 and the additional
condition is empty. Taking the maximum of these explicit thresholds for
\(1\leq r\leq N\) gives one threshold for arbitrary finite \(N\).

This is a proof and a structural strengthening, not a counterexample or a
finite-exhaustion result. The central additional observation is that
**submultisets, not just subsets, give a weighted partial matching** in a
preservation cut. No flawed literal manuscript assertion has been established.
No `F_` refutation claim is therefore being asserted. Independent Reviewer
acceptance, novelty, and publishable-level significance are still pending;
neither this theorem nor its formal core certifies universal stability.

## 1. Faithful model and exact cuts

Work in the norm-closed C*-algebra generated by the components, which
does not change the norm of the polynomial \(B_W\). Write
\(H_j^\circ=L^2(A_j,\tau)\ominus\mathbb C1\). The algebraic reduced
words, including 1, have exactly the tensor inner products in
\[
 \mathcal F=\mathbb C\Omega\oplus
 \bigoplus_{i_t\ne i_{t+1}}
 H_{i_1}^\circ\otimes\cdots\otimes H_{i_l}^\circ .
                                                               \tag{3}
\]
Here the sum includes every finite length. To check the inner products,
multiply the adjoint of the first word by the second and start at their
junction. If the junction copies differ, freeness kills the entire centered
reduced word. If they agree, split that adjacent product into its trace and
centered part. The centered term is again a reduced centered word and has
trace zero. The trace term removes the two junction letters. Induction gives
zero unless the copy sequences and lengths agree, and otherwise gives the
product of the leg inner products. Scalar/centered expansion of arbitrary
algebraic words and norm density in the generated C*-algebra prove
\(L^2\)-density, since \(\|x\|_2\leq\|x\|\).
Thus the product map extends to a unitary from (3) onto its GNS space.
Left multiplication has norm at most the original norm. For the reverse
inequality, if \(0<t<\|x^*x\|\), choose a nonzero continuous spectral
function \(f(x^*x)\) supported above \(t\). Faithfulness makes its
\(L^2\) norm nonzero, and
\(\langle L_{x^*x}f(x^*x),f(x^*x)\rangle
\geq t\|f(x^*x)\|_2^2\). Letting \(t\) increase to \(\|x^*x\|\)
proves that this is the original operator norm, not a quotient or an
approximating norm.

Put \(t=1/d\), \(h_a^{(j)}=e_a^{(j)}-t1\), and
\(D_j\delta_a=h_a^{(j)}\). Orthogonality of the projections gives
\[
 D_j^*D_j=tI-t^2\mathbf1\mathbf1^*,\qquad \|D_j\|\leq\sqrt t. \tag{4}
\]
For example, the latter follows directly from
\(t\sum|z_a|^2-t^2|\sum z_a|^2\leq t\sum|z_a|^2\).
On a tensor not starting in \(j\), multiplication by \(h_a^{(j)}\)
creates \(h_a^{(j)}\). On \(b\otimes\xi\) starting in \(j\), it is
\[
 \tau(h_a^{(j)}b)\xi+
 (h_a^{(j)}b-\tau(h_a^{(j)}b)1)\otimes\xi .
                                                               \tag{5}
\]
With the inner product linear in its second entry, the first coefficient
is precisely the \(a\)-coordinate of \(D_j^*b\); there is no conjugation
or order change missing. The second map on \(H_j^\circ\) is
\[
 K_a=P^\circ L_{e_a}P^\circ-tI.                              \tag{6}
\]

In \(h_{a_r}^{(r)}\cdots h_{a_1}^{(1)}\), operations are read from copy
1 to copy \(r\). After the first creation or preservation the first copy
is that operation's index. All subsequent, different indices must create.
The only possibilities are therefore \(p\) annihilations and \(r-p\)
creations, or \(p\) annihilations, preservation at \(j=p+1\), and
\(r-p-1\) creations.

The following domain restrictions are essential. Let
\(\mathcal F_{\notin S}\) include the vacuum and exactly those tensors
whose first index is outside \(S\). A non-preservation cut has tail
\[
 E_p=\mathcal F_{\notin
   ((\{p\}\text{ if }p>0)\cup(\{p+1\}\text{ if }p<r))}.
\]
Its source is \(H_1^\circ\otimes\cdots\otimes H_p^\circ\otimes E_p\);
its target is
\(H_r^\circ\otimes\cdots\otimes H_{p+1}^\circ\otimes E_p\).
For a preservation cut, its source is
\(H_1^\circ\otimes\cdots\otimes H_p^\circ\otimes H_j^\circ\otimes
\mathcal F_{\notin\{j\}}\), and its target is
\(H_r^\circ\otimes\cdots\otimes H_{j+1}^\circ\otimes H_j^\circ
\otimes\mathcal F_{\notin\{j\}}\).
Each is an isometric embedded summand; the cut is zero on its source's
orthogonal complement. Excluding \(p\) makes the input reduced, and
excluding \(p+1\) permits the first creation. These are restrictions on
copy indices alone, not on labels.

Consequently, a summed cut is the compression of
\[
 \bigl(\text{creation maps}\bigr)\,
       \bigl(M\otimes I_{\rm tail}\bigr)\,
       \bigl(\text{annihilation maps}\bigr),                 \tag{7}
\]
with the identity on the preserved leg where appropriate. Tuple order
reversals are unitaries. Equation (4) gives the bound
\(d^{-(p+q)/2}\|M\|\). Tensoring with the identity on an arbitrary
Hilbert space does not change this norm. All \(2r+1\) terms are bounded,
and their equality with the word follows from (5) on the dense algebraic
space. It therefore holds on the completed space (3), with no tail
truncation or interchange of an infinite sum of cuts.

## 2. Tuple matrices for arbitrary multiplicities

Let \(m\) be the multiplicity function of \(W\). For a submultiset \(b\)
of size \(p\), write
\[
 n(b)=\frac{p!}{\prod_a b_a!}.
\]
Grouping raw ordered tuples by their submultisets is an orthogonal
decomposition of the label space, irrespective of the Gram matrix (4):
at this point the space is the raw \(\ell^2\) label space.
The normalized constant vector on the \(n(b)\) orders is a unit vector.
The map taking the abstract vector indexed by \(b\) to this vector is an
isometry \(V_p\). A matrix constant on order blocks vanishes on the
orthogonal complements of these vectors, so its exact factorization is
\(V_q S V_p^*\), with block entry multiplied by
\[
                 w(A,B)=\sqrt{n(A)n(B)}.                   \tag{8}
\]
This explains both square roots of the permutation counts. Multiplying
by their product, or by \(r!\), at this stage would be wrong.

Without preservation, an allowed block satisfies \(A+B=m\). Each \(B\)
has at most one \(A\) and conversely. Hence
\[
 \|M\|^2=\max_{A+B=m}n(A)n(B)\leq p!q!.                    \tag{9}
\]
When the labels are distinct, equality holds and gives the manuscript's
exact \(\sqrt{p!q!}\).

With preservation, \(p+q=r-1\), and the allowed blocks satisfy
\[
                      A+B+\{a\}=m.                        \tag{10}
\]
Cancellation of multisets shows that \((A,a)\) determines \(B\), that
\((B,a)\) determines \(A\), and that \((A,B)\) determines \(a\).
This remains true with arbitrary repeated labels. Replace \(K_a\) first
by the full projection \(P_a=L_{e_a}\), retaining weight (8), and call
this matrix \(T\). Write \(w_*=\max w(A,B)\leq\sqrt{p!q!}\).
For any input vector,
\[
 \begin{split}
 \|T\xi\|^2
 &=\sum_A\sum_{B:\ (10)}
       w(A,B)^2\|P_{a(A,B)}\xi_B\|^2\\
 &\leq w_*^2\sum_B\sum_{A:\ (10)}
       \|P_{a(A,B)}\xi_B\|^2
 \leq w_*^2\sum_B\|\xi_B\|^2 .                             \tag{11}
 \end{split}
\]
The first equality uses distinct missing labels in each row, and the
last inequality uses distinct missing labels in each column. Both
orthogonalities are necessary. In fact \(\|T\|=w_*\) whenever an edge
exists: use one input coordinate in the nonzero range of its \(P_a\).

Compressing all component legs to \(H_j^\circ\) costs no norm. By (6),
one must then subtract \(d^{-1}S\otimes I\), where \(S\) has scalar
entries (8) on the allowed pairs. A row has at most \(p+1\) nonzero
entries: its missing label must occur in the residual multiset \(m-A\),
of size \(p+1\). A column has at most \(q+1\).
Row Cauchy--Schwarz followed by the column bound, or the weighted Schur
estimate, gives
\[
 \|S\|\leq w_*\sqrt{(p+1)(q+1)},\qquad
 \|M\|\leq\sqrt{p!q!}
       \left(1+\frac{\sqrt{(p+1)(q+1)}}d\right)
 \leq\sqrt{p!q!}(1+r/d).                                  \tag{12}
\]
For distinct labels all the relevant \(n(A),n(B)\) are \(q!,p!\);
thus (11) reduces exactly to the unweighted contraction asserted in
the manuscript. No support condition or factorial has been discarded.

## 3. Uniform summation and scalar restoration

Summing (9) through the non-preservation cuts gives at most
\((r+1)\sqrt{r!}d^{-r/2}\). Summing (12) gives
\[
 (1+r/d)\sqrt{(r-1)!}d^{-(r-1)/2} S_{r-1},\qquad
 S_m=\sum_{p=0}^m\binom mp^{-1/2}.                         \tag{13}
\]
This estimate now holds for every multiset, not just sets.

Here is an elementary improvement of the manuscript's bound \(S_m\leq5\):
\[
                            S_m\leq4\quad(m\geq0).         \tag{14}
\]
For \(m=0,1,2,3,4\), the values are
\(1,2,2+1/\sqrt2,2+2/\sqrt3,3+1/\sqrt6\).
For \(m\geq5\), the ratio
\(\binom m{p+1}/\binom mp=(m-p)/(p+1)\), together with symmetry,
shows that every interior coefficient \(2\leq p\leq m-2\) is at
least \(m(m-1)/2\). Thus
\[
 S_m\leq2+u+v,\quad
 u=2/\sqrt m,\quad
 v=(m-3)\sqrt{2/[m(m-1)]}.
\]
The exact identity
\((m-1)(m-4)-(m-3)^2=m-5\) gives
\(u^2+v^2/2\leq1\). Cauchy--Schwarz, in the exact form
\[
 3(u^2+v^2/2)-(u+v)^2=(2u-v)^2/2,
\]
gives \(u+v\leq\sqrt3<2\), proving (14).

For scalar restoration one may use the manuscript's deliberately crude
estimate, so no unproved distinctness assumption enters. A single centered
word of length \(l\) in distinct copy positions has \(l\) preservation
cuts of norm at most \(d^{-(l-1)/2}\) and \(l+1\) other cuts of norm at
most \(d^{-l/2}\); indeed \(\|K_a\|\leq\|e_a-d^{-1}1\|\leq1\).
Its norm is at most \((2l+1)d^{-(l-1)/2}\), for all label choices.
After multiplication by \(d^{-(r-l)}\), each scalar-expansion term
with \(1\leq l<r\) is at most \((2l+1)d^{-r/2}\).
The all-scalar term is at most \(d^{-r/2}\). There are at most \(r!\)
ordered tuples for any \(W\). Therefore the restoration error is at most
\[
 r!H_r d^{-r/2},\qquad
 H_r=1+\sum_{l=1}^{r-1}\binom rl(2l+1)
     =1+(r+1)(2^r-2).                                    \tag{15}
\]
The last equality follows from the binomial theorem and
\(\sum_l l\binom rl=r2^{r-1}\), including the subtracted endpoints.

Combining (13)--(15), for all multisets,
\[
 \|B_W\|\leq4(1+r/d)\sqrt{(r-1)!}d^{-(r-1)/2}
             +J_r d^{-r/2},\quad
 J_r=(r+1)\sqrt{r!}+r!H_r\leq K_r.                        \tag{16}
\]
For \(d\geq8r\), the normalized correction \(4r/d\) is at most \(1/2\).
Since
\[
 \frac{K_r^2}{(r-1)!}=r\,r!(H_r+r+1)^2,
\]
the second correction is at most \(1/2\) for the stated \(D_r\).
This proves (1), with an explicit integer threshold for every length.

To recover the manuscript's particular proof choices as well, use
\(S_m\leq5\), and take
\[
 d_r^{\rm old}=\max\{5r,\ r\,r!(H_r+r+1)^2\},\qquad
 C_r^{\rm old}=r!(2r+1+H_r).
\]
The two normalized errors are at most 1 each, giving its distinct-label
constant 7. The single-word estimate plus (15) gives its stated repeated
constant \(C_r^{\rm old}\). These are valid choices for the actual
existential constants in `lem:free-words`. Also,
\(B_W=B_W\sum_{a\in\operatorname{supp}W}e_a^{(1)}\), directly from the
rightmost factor of each original word; the asserted right support is
therefore valid for repeated as well as distinct labels.

## 4. Through-label sums and both thresholds

For the original beta, the distinct-label contribution is
\[
 49(r-1)!d^{-(r-1)}\binom{d-1}{r-1}\leq49 .
\]
For \(r\geq2\), the exact number of repeated-label multisets containing
\(a\) is
\[
 \sum_{t=1}^{r-1}\binom{d-1}{t-1}\binom{r-1}{t-1}
 \leq A_r d^{r-2},\quad
 A_r=\sum_{u=0}^{r-2}\binom{r-1}{u}/u!\leq2^{r-1}.        \tag{17}
\]
Choose the integer maximum over \(1\leq r\leq N\) of \(d_r^{\rm old}\)
and, for \(r\geq2\), of
\(\lceil (C_r^{\rm old})^2 2^{r-1}/15\rceil\).
Then the repeated contribution is at most 15, and the sum at most 64.
For \(r=1\) it is exactly 49. This proves `cor:through-label` with
the manuscript's beta and its actual simultaneous quantifiers.

For the stronger common bound in (1), remove one occurrence of the fixed
label \(a\). This is a bijection from multisets containing \(a\) to
multisets of size \(r-1\) on the same \(d\) labels, regardless of the
multiplicity of \(a\). Hence the squared sum is exactly
\[
 25(r-1)!d^{-(r-1)}\binom{d+r-2}{r-1}
       =25\prod_{k=0}^{r-2}(1+k/d).                       \tag{18}
\]
This is a sum of the **bounds**, not an asserted equality for the
operator norms. For nonnegative \(x_i\) with sum \(s<1\),
\(\prod_i(1+x_i)\leq1/(1-s)\). One direct induction multiplies by
\(1+x\) and uses
\((1+x)(1-s-x)=1-s-sx-x^2\leq1-s\).
For \(r\geq3\), the additional threshold gives
\(s=(r-1)(r-2)/(2d)\leq1/26\); (18) is at most 26.
The cases \(r=1,2\) were handled separately. There is no hidden
dependence on the ambient algebra, a tail length, or the chosen label.
