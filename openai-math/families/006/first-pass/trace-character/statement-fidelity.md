# Statement fidelity: root-character algebra

## Principal formal statement

`RootCharacter.root_character_of_class` quantifies over an arbitrary group
`G`, an arbitrary commutative ring `A` of characteristic exactly two, a
group homomorphism `pi : G -> DihedralGroup 3`, and a function `t : G -> A`.
There is no finiteness, reducedness, domain, field, or faithful-representation
hypothesis on `A`. In particular nilpotents are not killed.

The residual group `DihedralGroup 3` is the group of the six permutations
of three objects. In the formal convention `r i` acts on `Z/3` as
`x -> x+i`, while `sr i` acts as `x -> -x-i`. Thus `r 0` is the identity,
`r 1,r 2` are the two 3-cycles, and `sr i` are the transpositions. The
standalone verifier also checks this six-permutation identification.

The hypotheses are:

1. `t(c*x*c^-1)=t(x)` for all `c,x` (class invariance).
2. `t(1)=0`.
3. `t(x)*t(y)=t(x*y)+t(x*y^-1)` for all `x,y`.
4. `t(x)=0` whenever `pi(x)=1` (split vanishing).
5. `t(x)^2=1` whenever `pi(x)` is `r 1` or `r 2` (root square).
6. `t(x)*t(y)=0` whenever both residual images are transpositions.
7. There is an element whose residual image is `r 1`.

The conclusion is existence of a genuine monoid/group homomorphism
`alpha : G -> A.units` such that `alpha(g)^2=1` for every `g` and
the underlying ring value of `alpha(g)` equals `t(g)` for every root `g`.
The existence of this character is the conclusion, never a hypothesis.

Condition 7 covers any surjection onto the cyclic rotation subgroup or
onto the full symmetric group. Conversely any subgroup of the residual
group containing `r 1` is one of these two groups. Thus the formal statement
also covers infinite original groups, a structural extension of the finite
quotient argument in the source.

Matching parity in manuscript Lemma `tc:root-character` assumes that every
nonroot trace vanishes; that implies hypotheses 4 and 6. Opposite parity is
exactly hypotheses 4 and 6. Root-square and trace-identity hypotheses are
the same in both. The theorem does not assume that simple traces vanish
in the opposite case: they may be nonzero nilpotents.

## Load-bearing declarations

`TraceData` packages hypotheses 1--6, replacing class invariance by the
equivalent cyclic identity `t(x*y)=t(y*x)`. `cyclic_of_class` proves the
implication used by the principal theorem. `root_character` proves the
same conclusion from `TraceData`.

`TraceData.square_invariant` proves `t(x*u^2)=t(x)` for every split `u`.
`kernelSquares` is the subgroup generated by these kernel squares, not the
subgroup of all squares in `G`. `kernelSquares_normal` proves normality
in `G`. `square_subgroup_factorization` proves that this subgroup lies
in `ker pi`, contains every kernel square, and acts trivially on every
right translate of the trace. Consequently the trace descends to its group
quotient, whose residual kernel has exponent two and is abelian.
`kernel_comm` proves the exponent-two commutativity assertion directly.

For the main construction, `invisible` is the possibly larger normal
subgroup of the residual kernel whose right translations leave every trace
unchanged. Its quotient is constructed explicitly in `root_character`;
the descended trace satisfies all six identities and its residual kernel
has exponent two. This does not assume that the original residual kernel
was elementary or abelian.

`rotation_lift` constructs `R=g^4` of order three after this quotient.
`normalizer_lift` constructs a transposition lift `s` satisfying
`s*R*s^-1=R^-1`, using only the elementary-kernel relations. It does not
assume an involutive transposition lift: `s^2` can be a nontrivial kernel
element. No Schur--Zassenhaus theorem is imported as an unproved assertion.

`trace_rotation`, `trace_two_cosets`, `beta_mul`, `beta_conj`, and
`beta_simple_square` prove the required trace character on the kernel.
In particular `beta_simple_square` uses the two-simple product rule to
prove that the kernel character kills `s^2`.
`assemble_character` and `elementary_character` construct the homomorphism
from explicit residual sections and check their cocycles; a putative
character is not provided to these constructions.

`character_quotient_exponent_two` proves that the quotient by the final
character's kernel has exponent two. This is the algebraic content of
factorization through a rational elementary extension.

`pullback_unramified` proves that if an input homomorphism `rho` kills an
element, so does the pulled-back character. Applied to inertia elements
outside the stated support and a continuous finite Galois quotient, it
gives precisely preservation of unramifiedness. No unramifiedness or
continuity of the starting arithmetic quotient is proved by this group
theorem. The finite-source case ensures the character has finite image.

## What this certification does not establish

It does not prove `tc:shell-bound`, the modular trace construction
`tc:trace-algebra`, the claimed parity vanishings or repeated-prime square
identity for those operators, or the existence of the finite unramified
Galois quotient. These are source-application obligations in `D_TRACE_INPUTS`,
not external axioms used inside the formal theorem.

It does not verify the pointwise 2-converse, analytic twist densities,
the independent analytic tail estimate, or the mean-rank theorem.
It does not assert novelty relative to all existing mathematical literature.
Independent Reviewer assessment of this translation and its significance
remains required. The Lean kernel checks the stated group theorem, not
these arithmetic applications or this interpretation.
