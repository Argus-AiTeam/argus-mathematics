# Quantitative full-cone tensor amplification

## Result and scope

The construction in `prop:cone-drop` is valid under its stated
hypotheses. The proof below also works for an arbitrary matrix size and
an arbitrary fixed cutoff below one. This is a mathematical verification
theorem with machine-checked supporting core lemmas, not a Lean
formalization of the entire C*-algebra argument.

**Theorem.** Let d>=2, let P be a nonzero unital complex C*-algebra, and
put

    C_d = {f in C([0,1],M_d): f(0) is scalar}.

Suppose eta:C_d->P is a unital *-homomorphism and b=eta(t e11) is
full. There are a number delta in (0,1), an integer N>=1, and an
N-entry column V over P such that, with c=(b-delta)+,

    V* diag(c,...,c) V = 1.

For **every** integer L>=1 with d^L>=N, set

    k=N^L,       m=k+L.

In the maximal tensor power B=P^{tensor_max m} there are positive
contractions b1,...,bd and elements vi such that

    bi bj=0 (i!=j),   vi*vi=b1,   vi vi*=bi,
    1-sum_i bi <=_Cu (b1-lambda)+

for every real lambda in (0,1). The tuple and its exponent can be
chosen independently of lambda. For d=2, choose v2 as the manuscript's
v_B and lambda=1/2. This is exactly its asserted pair and comparison.
The Rordam-Winter implication then gives its unital I(2,3) map.

There is no nuclearity, separability, stable-rank, cancellation, trace,
or comparison-regularity hypothesis on P. Existence of L is not an
asymptotic assumption: L=N is always valid, although the least positive
L with d^L>=N is usually much smaller.

The generalization in d and lambda is included to expose which parts
of the argument are genuinely uniform. Novelty and publishability are
not inferred from a bounded literature search. Independent Reviewer
acceptance of the proof and its statement fidelity is still required.

## 1. Comparison conventions and exact witnesses

All tensor products of ambient C*-algebras below are maximal. For
positive elements of finite matrix algebras, a<=_Cu b means that
x_n* b x_n converges in norm to a, after zero stabilization.
The following facts are direct consequences of that definition.

For a rectangular v, put h=v*v. With
x_eps=v(h+eps)^(-1/2) one has

    x_eps*(vv*)x_eps = h^2(h+eps)^(-1) -> h.

The error is at most eps, by scalar functional calculus. Applying the
same argument to v* proves [v*v]=[vv*]. For pairwise orthogonal positive
ai, the row of their square roots has products sum ai and diag(ai).
Consequently [sum ai]=sum[ai]. Finally, tensor comparison witnesses with
the unit in the other factor:

    ||(x_n* b x_n-a) tensor e|| <= ||x_n* b x_n-a|| ||e||.

This proves external tensor monotonicity for the maximal product
directly. Distributing finite direct sums proves bilinearity. None of
these arguments cancels a summand or divides an order inequality.
The exact witness sqrt(a) also gives [a]<=[1] for every positive a.
Transitivity is equally elementary: if x_n* b x_n->a and
y_j* c y_j->b, choose j(n) with the latter error below
1/(n(1+||x_n||^2)). Then the products y_j(n) x_n witness
a<=_Cu c. Finite block-diagonal witnesses give additivity of order.

## 2. A full cut gives an exact column comparison

Fullness provides a finite sum S0=sum h_i b ell_i with
||1-S0||<1/2. The sum cannot be empty, since P is nonzero. Since
||b-(b-delta)+||<=delta, choose 0<delta<1 so that

    S=sum h_i c ell_i,       c=(b-delta)+,
    ||1-S||<1.

Thus S is invertible. Let

    T=(h_i sqrt(c))_i,       Z=(sqrt(c) ell_i)_i,
    D=Z*Z=sum ell_i* c ell_i.

Here T is a row and Z is a column. The C*-inequality
S*S=Z*T*TZ<=||T||^2 D, together with
S*S>=||S^(-1)||^(-2)1, implies that D is invertible.
Also T!=0 because S is invertible. Therefore the column

    V=(ell_i D^(-1/2))_i

satisfies V*diag(c,...,c)V=1 exactly. This proves [1]<=N[c].
It also proves that c is full. This step uses no cancellation or
polar decomposition in P.

For i=1,...,d, put

    ci=eta((t-delta)+ eii),
    wi=eta(sqrt((t-delta)+) ei1).

Then w_i*w_i=c, w_i w_i*=ci, the ci are orthogonal contractions,
and sum ci<=1.

## 3. The cubical algebra, without assuming nuclearity of P

For each k>=1, the maximal tensor power C_d^{tensor k} is the algebra
of continuous functions on [0,1]^k with values in M_d^{tensor k}
whose i-th tensor factor is scalar whenever t_i=0.

Here is an independent way to see the isometry of the natural map.
The scalar coordinate functions are central in the maximal tensor
product. In an irreducible representation they have fixed values
t_1,...,t_k. The representation of the i-th cone factor annihilates
every section vanishing at t_i. Indeed, the latter ideal is the closed
ideal generated by the scalar function s-t_i: multiply such a section
f by

    (s-t_i)^2 / ((s-t_i)^2+eps)

and let eps decrease to zero. This converges uniformly to f, and the
approximating section belongs to the scalar-generated ideal. The
quotient is M_d if t_i>0 and the scalars if t_i=0. The commuting
factor representations thus factor through their finite-dimensional
tensor product, with its unique matrix C*-norm. Taking the supremum
over irreducible representations shows that the maximal norm is at
most the supremum of the evaluation norms. The reverse bound follows
from the evaluation homomorphisms.

For surjectivity, take a basis of M_d consisting of 1 and d^2-1 other
vectors. A coefficient f_alpha of a section in this basis vanishes
on every face belonging to a nonscalar tensor index. If p_alpha is
the product of those coordinates, then

    f_alpha p_alpha/(p_alpha+eps) -> f_alpha

uniformly. Scalar continuous functions on the cube belong to the
tensor algebra by finite sums of products of one-variable continuous
functions. The section p_alpha times its basis tensor is elementary.
This proves the reverse inclusion, including all intersections of
zero-coordinate faces. The argument does not require an assumption
about nuclearity of P; it does not even need to invoke nuclearity of
the cone.

## 4. A simplex of embeddings on every face

Let F be the flip on C^d tensor C^d. It is selfadjoint and F^2=1.
Write

    Pplus=(1+F)/2,    Pminus=(1-F)/2,
    U(s)=Pplus+exp(i pi s) Pminus.

The two spectral projections are complementary and orthogonal, so
U(s) is unitary for every real s. On [0,1], it is a continuous path
from 1 to F. Directly on elementary vectors,

    F(a tensor 1)F = 1 tensor a.

These are universal identities: `Main.lean` proves them for the
actual flip matrix on every finite index type and for the actual
exponential phase, not a rational approximation to that path.

Induct on k to construct theta_r:M_d->M_d^{tensor k} for
r in the (k-1)-simplex. At k=1 use the identity. If r_k<1, let
tau be the preceding map at
(r_1/(1-r_k),...,r_{k-1}/(1-r_k)), and set

    theta_r(a)=(tau tensor id)(U(r_k)(a tensor 1)U(r_k)*).

At r_k=1 set theta_r(a)=1^{tensor(k-1)} tensor a.
Each map is a unital *-homomorphism, and the formula is point-norm
continuous away from that vertex. At the vertex, contractivity gives

    ||theta_r(a)-1 tensor a|| <= 2 ||a|| ||U(r_k)-F||.

This bound is independent of tau. Its right side tends to zero even
when the normalized preceding weights have no limit. The universal
norm estimate is `uniform_conjugation_bound` in `Main.lean`.
This handles the terminal vertex, not just paths with a fixed
normalized direction.

Face compatibility is exact. If r_k=0, then U(0)=1 and the formula
reduces to tau(a) tensor 1, so that tensor factor is unused. If an
earlier coordinate vanishes, the induction hypothesis puts a scalar
in that factor in every coefficient of tau; applying tau tensor id
preserves the restriction. At r_k=1 all preceding factors are scalar
by definition. Induction therefore also shows that inserting any
collection of zero weights agrees with the lower-dimensional family
on the surviving factors, in their inherited order. In particular
every vertex gives its specified coordinate inclusion. This argument
covers arbitrary k and arbitrary intersections of faces.

## 5. The pair, the origin, and the axis supports

In the cubical algebra define scalar functions

    g(s)=max(1-2s/delta,0),
    H(t)=1-product_j g(t_j).

For t!=0 let d(t)=max_j t_j and

    w_i(t)=max(t_i/d(t)-1/2,0),
    r_i(t)=w_i(t)/sum_j w_j(t).

A maximum coordinate contributes exactly 1/2 to the denominator,
which is therefore at least 1/2. The functions r_i are continuous
off the origin, nonnegative, and sum to one. If t_i=0, then r_i=0.
These statements, including existence of an attained positive maximum
for every nonzero nonnegative finite tuple, are checked in Lean.

For t!=0 set

    ai(t)=H(t) theta_r(t)(eii),
    vi(t)=sqrt(H(t)) theta_r(t)(ei1),

and set them equal to zero at the origin. For numbers 0<=x_j<=1,

    0 <= 1-product_j x_j <= sum_j (1-x_j).

For example, the inductive step uses
(1-x)(1-product x_j)>=0. Since
1-g(t_j)<=2t_j/delta, this gives the dimension-independent formula

    0<=H(t)<=(2/delta) sum_j t_j.

Consequently ||ai(t)||<=H(t) and ||vi(t)||<=sqrt(H(t)).
Both tend to zero at the origin. An explicit sufficient neighborhood
for ||vi||<epsilon is sum t_j<delta epsilon^2/2; no directional
limit of r(t) at zero is needed. The finite-product estimate is
proved in Lean for every finite coordinate set.

The face property of theta gives exactly the scalar boundary fibers
required in Section 3, so all these sections belong to the tensor
algebra. Matrix-unit multiplication gives

    vi*vi=a1,      vi vi*=ai,
    ai aj=0 (i!=j),      sum_i ai=H1.

Thus the defect is X=g^{tensor k}.

Put q(s)=(s-delta)+ e11 and

    yj=g^{tensor(j-1)} tensor q tensor g^{tensor(k-j)},
    Y=sum_j yj.

The identity qg=0 is exact, including the endpoints delta and
delta/2. Hence the yj are positive and pairwise orthogonal.
If yj(t)!=0, then t_j>delta and t_i<delta/2 for all i!=j.
Thus d(t)=t_j, r(t) is exactly the j-th vertex, and H(t)=1.
At that vertex a1 acts as the identity on the q corner. Therefore

    a1 yj=yj,       a1 Y=Y.

At a point where yj=0 the identity is immediate; the argument does
not improperly replace a strict support inequality by a statement
on its closure. The normalized-vertex calculation is the arbitrary-
index theorem `axis_weights` in Lean. In particular k=1 is covered:
the other-coordinate conditions are vacuous and r_1=1.

## 6. Every comparison multiplicity

Apply eta^{tensor k}; use the same letters for the images.
Let G=eta(g). Since [G]<=[1]<=N[c], tensoring with the other k-1
copies of G gives

    [X]<=N[yj]       for j=1,...,k.

Adding all k inequalities and using orthogonality yields

    k[X]<=N[Y].                                      (1)

This does not imply [X]<=[Y] by cancellation, and we do not use
such an implication.

Choose any L>=1 with d^L>=N and set

    R=P^{tensor L},       e=c^{tensor L},       k=N^L.

For each of the d^L words sigma of length L in {1,...,d}, the
element e_sigma=csigma_1 tensor ... tensor csigma_L is exactly
equivalent to e via the tensor product of the wi. Distinct words
give orthogonal elements, and

    sum_sigma e_sigma=(sum_i ci)^{tensor L}<=1_R.

Thus d^L[e]<=[1_R]. Because N<=d^L, positivity of the Cuntz monoid
implies N[e]<=[1_R]. For the reverse comparison, tensor the exact
column V with itself L times. After the usual finite matrix
reindexing, the resulting N^L-entry column gives

    (V^{tensor L})* diag(e,...,e) (V^{tensor L})=1_R.

Consequently, with no unspecified multiplicity,

    N[e] <= [1_R] <= N^L[e] = k[e].                  (2)

All counts are finite positive integers. Lean proves d^N>=N for
d>=2, the existence of L>=1, positivity of k and m=k+L, and the
exact word count d^L.

Now work in B=P^{tensor k} tensor R=P^{tensor(k+L)}. Combining
(1) and (2) by external tensoring gives exactly

    [X tensor 1_R]
      <= k[X tensor e]
      <= N[Y tensor e]
      <= [Y tensor 1_R].                            (3)

The first inequality uses the upper bound in (2), the second uses
(1), and the third uses the lower bound in (2). The theorem
`tensor_absorption` checks this implication in arbitrary preordered
additive commutative monoids with an order-preserving bilinear
pairing. There is no cancellation or order division in that theorem.

The edge cases are included. N=1 gives k=1 for every L>=1; (3)
remains valid. Equality d^L=N is allowed. Robert-Rordam Lemma
6.1(i) is only a comparison pattern: its printed hypothesis m<n
is not being invoked at equality. All inequalities in (3) were
proved directly for maximal tensor products.

## 7. The fixed cutoff and the unstabilized conclusion

Set bi=ai tensor 1_R and use vi tensor 1_R for exact equivalence.
From a1Y=Y, taking adjoints gives Ya1=Y, so a1 and Y commute.
For every continuous f on [0,1], polynomial approximation gives
f(a1)Y=f(1)Y. In particular, for every lambda in (0,1),

    (a1-lambda)+ Y=(1-lambda)Y.

Therefore the actual element

    z=(1-lambda)^(-1/2) sqrt(Y) tensor 1_R

satisfies

    z* (b1-lambda)+ z=Y tensor 1_R.                  (4)

The same tuple works for all lambda; only this comparison witness
changes. At lambda=1/2 it is precisely sqrt(2Y) tensor 1_R.
Equations (3) and (4) prove the claimed defect comparison in finite
matrices over B.

It also holds in B itself. A stabilized witness W with b in its
only nonzero target corner has upper-left compression
(W_11)* b W_11. Compressing a sequence of stabilized witnesses
therefore gives norm-approximation witnesses in B. This is not a
stable-rank or cancellation argument.

For d=2, the final step to I(2,3) is Rordam-Winter,
arXiv:0801.2259v1, Proposition 5.1, (ii) implies (iv).
Its final sentence explicitly limits the stable-rank-one hypothesis
to (i) implies (ii). The exact-equivalence definition on page 8
agrees with v*v and vv*, not merely Cuntz equivalence.
The separate primary-source reader and the short quotation are
recorded in `literature/`. This external implication is not part
of the Lean formalization.
