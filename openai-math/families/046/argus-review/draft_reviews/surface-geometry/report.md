## Bridge 1: detect whole local groups, then descend every meridian

At a boundary point the local complement has group `Z^a`, `a=1,2`. Its image
`A_i` is finite because it is generated by at most two commuting finite-order
elements. There are finitely many subgroup types up to conjugacy: finitely many
components and crossings, and a connected smooth stratum on each irreducible
component. This uses the projective SNC boundary; it is not an assertion about
an arbitrary infinite boundary.

For each nonidentity element in finitely many representative local groups,
profinite separation gives an open normal subgroup excluding it. Intersect
these finitely many subgroups to obtain open normal `N` with

```
N intersect g A_i g^(-1) = {1}, for every i and g in Q.
```

The finite quotient `P=Q/N` is injective on **whole** local groups, including
mixed relations at crossings. Normality is what makes the assertion uniform
over access-path conjugation. Density makes `pi1(U)->P` surjective, so its
kernel defines a connected regular finite analytic cover. SGA 1 XII 5.1
algebraizes it without assuming `U` proper.

Normalize `Y` in the function field of the connected smooth cover. Connected
smooth algebraic varieties are irreducible, so the field is defined. Nagata
finiteness applies to the finite-type reduced morphism `U'->Y`; the finite
normalization is projective. Strong resolution leaves its regular locus
unchanged. On the resolved surface the boundary curves have finite
normalization; their singularities, tangencies and multiple-branch points
can be resolved by point blowups avoiding an already smooth SNC open locus.
Surface resolution alone would not establish this prescribed-open property
for the pair.

The inclusion `U'->X` surjects on fundamental groups, with kernel normally
generated by all boundary meridians. General position moves loops off the
real-codimension-two boundary; a transverse null-homotopy disk avoiding its
crossings expresses a kernel loop by meridian conjugates.

For **any** final boundary component, including an exceptional component
contracted to a crossing downstairs, project a sufficiently small transverse
meridian disk into a downstairs boundary polydisk. The projected loop has
`Q`-image in a conjugate of `A_i`. It also has image in `N`, because its lift
is a closed loop in the finite cover. The displayed intersection kills it.
Normal generation gives the descent. No exceptional meridian is assumed to
be a pure power of one original meridian. The descended image is contained in
`N`; it is not generally dense in all of `Q`.

The universal rank-two lattice statement in report section 3 is also correct.
For commuting `alpha,beta`, let `a=ord(alpha)`, let `c` be the order of
`beta` modulo `<alpha>`, and choose `0<=b<a` with
`alpha^b beta^c=1`. Subtracting a multiple of `(b,c)` from any relation leaves
an `alpha`-only relation. Thus the kernel is
`Z(a,0)+Z(b,c)`, of index `ac`, for all integer exponents. The finite
exhaustion is not the proof of this universal identity.

## Bridge 2: identify the actual normalization and rational fiber

Trivial vertical monodromy gives identity local extensions at unmarked vertical
points and at main-tail nodes. At a horizontal-vertical intersection, with
horizontal image of exact order `e`, the kernel is `Z x eZ` and the normal
extension is `(u,w)->(u,w^e)`. Its domain is smooth and its reduced boundary is
`uw=0`, also for `e=1`.

These are the **actual** local extensions: analytification preserves finiteness
and normality, and SGA 1 XII 5.3 makes restriction fully faithful over a normal
polydisk minus its closed boundary. Every cover component dominates the base,
as required in definition 5.0. The two inverse isomorphisms on the dense
complement therefore extend. Mere agreement of a formal equation or finite
lattice index would not suffice.

The entire preimage of the distinguished fiber is already in the good smooth
SNC locus; prescribed-open resolution adds no exceptional components there.
Main-component covers are unramified covers of an unmarked `P1`, hence degree
one. A connected tail cover branches only at its two markings and not at the
node. Removing the markings gives a connected finite cover of `C*`,
classified by `dZ`, whose compactification is `P1`. Equivalently
`2-2g=2d-2(d-1)=2`. A third branch point would invalidate this argument;
the actual collision geometry excludes it. Local identity models at nodes
give ordinary transverse intersections.

## Bridge 3: infinite image survives specialization

Write `H_s=rho(pi1(F_s))` for a punctured smooth fiber. The finite quotient
image is all of `P`, so `F'_s` is connected and its `Q`-image is exactly
`H_s intersect N`. Because `N` is **open**, this image is dense in `N`.
The open subgroup `N` is infinite, since finite `N` and finite index would
make `Q` finite. A finite subset is closed in a Hausdorff group; hence this
image, and consequently the image in `pi1(X)`, is infinite.

The specialization proof does not identify smooth and singular fiber groups.
A compatible finite triangulation of the compact real analytic pair `(X,Z)`
exists by the cited semianalytic triangulation theorem. Closedness makes `Z`
a subcomplex. After barycentric subdivision it is full. On
`V={sum of coordinates at Z-vertices >0}`, retain those coordinates and
renormalize. Fullness ensures the result is in `Z`; the simplexwise
straight-line homotopy is a strong deformation retraction.

Properness makes `h(X\V)` closed and excludes the distinguished value. Thus
**whole** nearby fibers lie in `V`. A connected nearby punctured lifted fiber
lies in one component of `V`; its inclusion in `X` factors up to homotopy
through a component `Z_0`. Its infinite group image forces the image of
`pi1(Z_0)` to be infinite. A positive finite graph rank alone would not do so.

## Bridge 4: actual incidence graph and the holomorphic hull

For tail `j`, let `a_j in P`, `C_j=<a_j>`, `e_j=|C_j|`, and
`H=<a_1,...,a_k>`. Degree-one main lifts give vertices `P`; tail components
give cosets `P/C_j`. The trivial attaching-node group gives an edge `(p,j)`
from `p` to `p C_j`. Paths among main lifts multiply labels by tail generators,
so components are `P/H`. Therefore

```
components = [P:H]
vertices = |P| + sum_j |P|/e_j
edges = k|P|
b1 = |P|(k-1) - sum_j |P|/e_j + [P:H].
```

This applies to arbitrary finite `P`, including repeated or trivial tail
images. For the actual quotient, killed pair products leave a free sphere
group on one meridian per pair; density makes their images generate `P`.
The blowup coordinate `w=u v` identifies these with the actual tail
monodromies; multiplication by nonzero `u` preserves complex orientation,
and access-path changes introduce only killed node meridians. Thus `H=P`,
the actual reduced fiber is connected, and the final term is 1. The finite
`C2 x C2` and `D8` examples are not manufactured projective fibers and do not
prove this geometric identification.

A connected pullback `W` of `Z_0` to the universal cover corresponds to the
kernel of `pi1(Z_0)->pi1(X)`; its number of sheets is the infinite image's
cardinality. A fiber is closed, discrete and infinite, so `W` is noncompact.
The inverse image of `Z_0` is closed and each connected component is closed.
Covering charts prove ambient local finiteness of the lifted rational
components. Each component lifts compactly because `P1` is simply connected.

Every holomorphic function is constant on each compact rational component.
Constants agree at nodes, and local path connectedness and local finiteness
propagate equality throughout connected `W`. Hence `W` lies in the hull of
every singleton it contains. A compact hull could not contain the **closed**
noncompact subset `W`. The conclusion is failure of holomorphic convexity,
not merely failure of Steinness.
