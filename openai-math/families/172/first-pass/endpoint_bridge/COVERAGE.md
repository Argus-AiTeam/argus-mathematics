## The theorem actually proved

Let `I` be any finite coordinate set of cardinality `s >= 2`, let `K` be
the free group on an arbitrary label set, and let `N` be its exponent-sum
kernel. Let `A` be a set of allowable unit-copy tuples. Expressions in the
monoid generated by `A` and all diagonals retain their chosen degrees.
Assume each pair projection is a group containing `N x N`.

Let a finite group `H` act on a finite label domain `X`, with labels giving
the free-group letters. Suppose `f,g : I -> X` have corresponding entries
in the same orbit and every `H`-reindexed `f` tuple is an allowable unit
copy. Give each reindexed `g` tuple a positive weight `q`.
For every positive real `delta`, there is a positive-degree permutation
expression `w`, of degree `m`, and actual positive-copy correction
expressions `u,v`, of degrees `a,b`, such that

```
(w at f) u = (w at g) v,
a + b <= s delta m.
```

The resulting permitted paths have weights

```
lambda = m + a > 0,
mu = q m + b >= 0,
mu/lambda <= q + s delta.
```

There is no bound on `s`, the finite group, the number or sizes of its
orbits, or the real tolerance, and no commutativity, Ore, or `q < 1`
hypothesis. Trivial group actions and singleton orbits are included.
The path constructors permit only diagonals and positive copy factors;
they never invert a copy factor.

The source also proves an abstract-group extension: the same construction
works for any group and normal subgroup when the initial seed differences
belong to that subgroup. The free-group theorem proves this initial
condition from the exponent homomorphism, rather than assuming it.
This is a universal algebraic verification, not a finite experiment and
not a verification of the full Ramsey classification.

## Why the difficult steps hold

For a supported fraction `g^[i]=ab^-1`, conjugation by a diagonal preserves
the degree sum. Given any fraction `uv^-1`, conjugate `g` by `v_i^-1`
before choosing its numerator and denominator. Then

```
uv^-1 g^[i] = u (v^-1 g^[i] v) v^-1 = (ua)(vb)^-1.
```

This proves subgroup closure, minimum-cost subadditivity, and the tuple
cost bound without asserting closure of all `MM^-1`.
For a pair-supported fraction `z=cd^-1`, the inverse in the corresponding
pair projection supplies `b` with `t=vb` trivial on that pair. Thus `t`
commutes with `z`, and the new fraction is `(ubc)(td)^-1`.
Its auxiliary cost is only asserted finite.

Induction on the arbitrary coordinate cardinality projects away two
different coordinates. Lifting the induction hypotheses gives fractions
supported on `{i,j}` and `{i,k}`. Their commutator is supported on `{i}`
and realizes the commutator of their prescribed `i` entries. The
pair-supported multiplication rule represents the whole commutator as a
right fraction. Subgroup closure gives `N^(s-2) <= L_i`, including `s=2`.
The maximum of the now-represented minimum coordinate costs is therefore a
proved conjugation-invariant correction cost.

After normalization at an orbit anchor, averaging over a fixed ordered
list of `H` gives identical images in the relevant subgroup's
abelianization. Repeating this, and preserving all previously obtained
normal-subgroup conditions, gives the required derived depth on every
orbit. Only the abelianized products are reordered.

For cost contraction, one factor of each target-orbit product is the
anchor value `a`. Writing that product as `P a Q`, the appended factor
`a^-1` gives `P(a Q a^-1)`. Conjugation invariance removes the cost of the
selected factor, so the value bound is multiplied by `|H|-1` while the
represented degree is multiplied by `|H|`. The formal proof establishes
that a suitable power exists for every positive tolerance. The separate
product-difference inequality shows that each previous orbit's
cost/degree ratio cannot increase. A finite induction over all anchors
makes the bounds simultaneous.

Finally the needed discrepancy is `D_plus^-1 D_minus`, not its opposite.
Each coordinate is a conjugate of the inverse of the averaged
within-orbit difference. The proved tuple fraction bound gives the actual
correction expressions and their summed degree budget. The weight
