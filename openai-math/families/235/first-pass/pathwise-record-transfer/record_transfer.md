# Synchronization-free transfer of pathwise continuation records

## Result and scope

The record-transfer assertions `ct:old-records` and `ct:new-record` are
valid in the represented hierarchy constructed immediately before them.
In particular, none of posterior changes of global law, weak convergence,
or partition-selected reindexing requires weakening "almost every complete
global path" to a globally averaged estimate. The proof below also gives
the stronger simultaneous estimate

\[
 \sum_{j=1}^J\int |A_j(z,u)-S_b(z_{\le b},u)(\phi_j)|^2\,du
       \le 2J\gamma.                                      \tag{1}
\]

Only the scalar averaged overlap is needed for this estimate.
Componentwise ordering of the vector overlaps (`ct:ordering`) is
unnecessary for record creation. All depths here are arbitrary finite
integers, all limiting arrays are countably infinite, and the functions
may be merely measurable except for the specified continuous type tests.
There is no finite-support hypothesis on an overlap or conditional law.

This is a universal verification and structural reduction of the record
bridge, not a counterexample and not a new proof of computability of the
SAT threshold. The separate Gaussian selection and endpoint-value
arguments of `ct:transition`, the pressure formula, and the other
manuscripts' variance bounds are not certified by the Hilbert-space
compiler run. No false literal manuscript assertion is identified here.

## 1. General setup

Let a label tree have arbitrary finite depth `D`, with countably many
children at each vertex. A site has depth one. At every label vertex
`v`, let `Z_v` be a global uniform, and let `U_v^i` be its site-`i`
uniform, all mutually independent. The root global may equivalently
have an arbitrary standard Borel law. A boundary decoration `S_c`
takes values in a compact metric space `K` and depends only on the
global and site paths through `c`. An ancestor decoration `S_b`
takes values in `P(K)` and depends only on the paths through `b<c`.

For a fixed complete global path `z` and a site prefix `u` through `b`,
define

\[
 A_j(z,u)=\int\phi_j(S_c(z,u,v))\,dv,\qquad
 E_j(z,u)=A_j(z,u)-S_b(z_{\le b},u)(\phi_j). \tag{2}
\]

Here only the fresh site coordinates `v=U_{b+1:c}` are integrated;
no continuation global is averaged in `A_j`. The tests
`phi_j:K -> [0,1]` are a fixed finite nonempty continuous family.
The Hilbert spaces `L2(du)` and all their finite weighted sums are
separable. Bounded joint measurability makes `z -> A_j(z,.)` and
`z -> E_j(z,.)` strongly measurable Hilbert-valued maps, by approximation
with product-simple functions. Thus their conditional laws given the
ancestor globals are legitimate standard Borel probability kernels.

## 2. Absolute continuity preserves the exact record

Condition on any root history for which the trial kernels are defined.
Write `Y_l` for the nested old-packet values. The level density is

\[
 \rho_l=\exp\{t_l(Y_l-Y_{l-1})\}.
\]

It has conditional integral one by the definition of `Y_{l-1}`.
The finite product of these densities defines an absolutely continuous
law of globals and old-site variables. It contains no fresh-site
coordinate. Conditional on the entire enlarged global path, the fresh
site law is therefore still its product Lebesgue law. An additional
positive normalized first-level Gaussian tilt has the same properties
whenever its normalizer is finite and positive.

Keep all old type functions unchanged, using the original global
projections after enlargement. The left side of every old record is
then the same measurable function of that path as before the change of
law. Its exceptional set remains null by absolute continuity. This also
holds after selecting old-packet base data outside the conditional null
sets, by disintegration. No conditional expectation defining a type is
recomputed under the posterior. Recomputing it would be a different
operation and is not justified by this argument.

## 3. Closed constraints, weak limits, and random indices

For two label paths sharing the prefix through `b` and branching
immediately afterwards, form at site `i`

\[
 Y_i=(\phi(S_c^{p,i})-S_b^{p,i}(\phi))
     (\phi(S_c^{p',i})-S_b^{p',i}(\phi)).
\]

Given the two complete global paths, the `Y_i` are iid in `[-1,1]`.
Their conditional mean is
`<E_z,E_z'>` in `L2(du_0:b)`, because the shared site coordinates are
exactly the prefix coordinates and the later coordinates on the two
branches are independent. If the original records hold with tolerance
`delta`, Cauchy--Schwarz gives this mean in
`F=[-delta^2,delta^2]`.

For `M_I=I^(-1) sum_{i<=I}Y_i`, projection onto the interval gives

\[
 {\mathbb E}\,\operatorname{dist}(M_I,F)^2
 \le {\mathbb E}|M_I-{\mathbb E}(Y_1\mid\text{globals})|^2
 \le I^{-1}.                                               \tag{3}
\]

For fixed `I` the integrand is a bounded continuous function of
finitely many retained decorations: evaluation of a probability
measure against a continuous bounded test is continuous. Hence (3)
passes to any weak limit of the compact decorated arrays. For every
`r>0`, Markov's inequality at `I=2^n` gives summable bounds
`r^(-2)2^(-n)`. Borel--Cantelli, first for positive rational `r`,
shows `dist(M_(2^n),F) -> 0` almost surely.

There are only countably many deterministic path pairs and finitely
many specified tests. Intersect their probability-one events **before**
choosing any partition-measurable indices. On that event all selected
pairs obey the same conclusion, regardless of how discontinuously
their indices depend on the full partition. This proves the required
random-index transfer; no continuous mapping theorem is applied to
ranking cluster weights.

The same proof applies to a weighted sum of test products or error
products: these still lie in `[-1,1]` when the nonnegative weights sum
to one. Consequently a preexisting weighted joint record is preserved
as well as each individual old record.

## 4. Why the reindexed array has the needed representation

This paragraph isolates the exact marked-identity input. Suppose the
scalar overlap is ultrametric and its nested cut relations have
parameters `x_l`. The marked Ghirlanda--Guerra identity is required
against every bounded test of the past decorations as well as past
overlaps. For a seen cluster `C` and an anchor in it, that identity
gives

\[
 {\mathbb P}\{\text{next sample belongs to }C\mid
                  \text{past partition and decorations}\}
       =(n_C-x_l)/n.                                     \tag{4}
\]

At each parent, differences of the probabilities for its already seen
children give the remaining new-child probability. Thus the whole
finite nested partition extension has a transition kernel depending
only on the past partition, not on the decorations.

For any deterministic ordered tuple `I` of distinct natural indices,
exchangeability permits exposing it first. Iterating (4) and
conditioning down from the intermediate decorations shows that the
law of the remaining partition given `D_I` depends only on the
internal cut pattern `Pi|I`. A monotone-class argument from finite
partition cylinders to the full countable partition gives

\[
 {\mathbb E}[f(D_I)\mid\Pi]=K_{\Pi|I}(f).                  \tag{5}
\]

The kernel does not depend on the numerical values of the original
indices. The inverse images of any finite tuple of ranked positions
form a countably valued, partition-measurable tuple. Sum (5) over its
possible values. Its internal pattern is fixed by the new positions,
so the resulting decoration law is independent of the full partition.
It is invariant under every finite ancestry-preserving permutation of
the hidden clusters and their sample positions. All old descendant
subtrees move with their first sample, so their independent
hierarchical permutation invariances survive; sites are never
reindexed. This is exactly the product-tree invariance in the cited
Austin--Panchenko theorem.

In that representation, any duplicated boundary decoration is
prefix-measurable. Indeed, take two leaves with the same prefix and
independent continuations. Their copies of this decoration are equal
almost surely. Conditional on the prefix variables their values are
iid, so the common conditional law is a point mass. For a compact
metric target this follows by testing a countable separating family
of bounded continuous functions and observing that each conditional
variance is zero. Its measurable atom defines the required version.
There are only countably many entries and boundaries.

Equation (4) is essential: overlap identities with no decoration
tests would not justify (5). The staged manuscript explicitly retains
the stronger marked identities; we do not replace them by the weaker
overlap-only ones. Only the **scalar**, not vector, marked identities
are used here.

## 5. Conditional support theorem and old records

Let `mu` be a probability law on a separable real Hilbert space `H`.
If a continuous function of two independent samples lies almost surely
in a closed set, the corresponding pair constraint holds on
`supp(mu) x supp(mu)`. One proof closes the second-variable section
for almost every first variable, then closes the first-variable section
for every second support point. This also proves the statement on the
diagonal, although independently sampled diffuse points need never
coincide.

In particular, if `|<V,V'>| <= delta^2` almost surely, then
`||v||^2 <= delta^2` for every support point `v`. If instead all
pair inner products lie in `[a,a+epsilon]`, then for all support
points `v,w`,

\[
 \|v-w\|^2
 =\|v\|^2+\|w\|^2-2\langle v,w\rangle
 \le 2\epsilon.                                         \tag{6}
\]

Every support point's closed ball of radius `sqrt(2 epsilon)` contains
the support and therefore its closed convex hull. In particular it
contains the barycenter of any bounded law. Equivalently, Jensen's
inequality gives
`||v - int w dmu||^2 <= int ||v-w||^2 dmu <= 2 epsilon`.
These statements hold separately for almost every conditional law
given the ancestor globals.

Now apply the represented site's conditional strong law to the
empirical products in section 3. Conditional on the two complete
global paths, the site columns are iid, so their empirical mean is the
inner product of the represented error vectors in `L2(du_0:b+h)`.
The dyadic conclusion puts that inner product in
`[-delta^2,delta^2]`. Given the globals through `b+h`, these error
vectors are independent with the same law, since their continuation
global generators lie on disjoint branches. The first support theorem
therefore gives error norm at most `delta` for almost every
continuation global path, for almost every ancestor global path.
Fubini gives exactly `ctr:record`, including its root and complete-path
quantifiers, with the original tolerance. This proves `ct:old-records`.

This proof does not identify a conditional expectation from its weak
limit, nor assume that such identification is continuous.

## 6. Scalar-band creation: a stronger joint record

Take positive weights `lambda_j` with sum one and use the scalar
kernel

\[
 Q_\lambda(p,p')=\sum_j\lambda_j
       \int H_j(p,u_0)H_j(p',u_0)\,du_0.                 \tag{7}
\]

The manuscript takes `lambda_j=1/J`. It is a Gram kernel in the
weighted direct sum Hilbert space. Averaging over `I` sites the
bounded scalar products
`sum_j lambda_j phi_j(S_1^{p,i}) phi_j(S_1^{p',i})`
approximates (7) in squared mean by at most `1/I`. This finite-column
identity passes to the natural-array weak limit by continuity.
The dyadic and countable-intersection argument then identifies the
scalar overlap for **every** subsequently selected pair from the
decorations alone.

For the newly processed block choose pairs that agree through its
start `b` and branch immediately below it. The cut convention gives
`q <= Q_lambda < r`; the support of that band has diameter at most
`gamma`. Replace it by its closed convex hull, a fixed interval of
length at most `gamma`. Endpoint atoms cause no difficulty. The
four cases (both cuts present, only parent, only child, neither) all
have the same property: a missing parent means `P(Q<q)=0`, and a
missing child means `P(Q<r)=1`.

In the representation, let
`A=(A_1,...,A_J)` with Hilbert norm

\[
 \|A\|_\lambda^2=\sum_j\lambda_j\int|A_j(u)|^2\,du.
\]

Given the ancestor globals, two branches provide iid copies of `A`,
and their inner product is precisely the scalar site-empirical limit
above. The support theorem with `epsilon=gamma` gives

\[
 \sum_j\lambda_j\int|A_j(z,u)-\bar A_j(u)|^2\,du
        \le2\gamma                                    \tag{8}
\]

for almost every continuation global path. No interval bound for an
individual `Q_j` is needed.

There is a **single probability-valued type** realizing every
`bar A_j` at once:

\[
 S_b(z_{\le b},u)=
       \operatorname{Law}_{Z_{b+1:c},U_{b+1:c}}(S_c).    \tag{9}
\]

All the displayed continuation coordinates are integrated with their
current independent uniform laws, not with a fresh-packet Gibbs tilt.
Because `K` is compact metric, (9) is a measurable `P(K)` kernel.
Fubini identifies its test means with the conditional-global
barycenter of `A`; boundedness ensures Bochner integrability. This
proves (8) with one ancestor-measurable type, rather than unrelated
testwise centers.

With equal weights, (8) is (1), and nonnegativity gives every
individual squared error at most `2 J gamma`. Choosing
`0 < gamma <= delta^2/(2J)` proves exactly `ct:new-record` for
arbitrary positive `delta` and any specified finite test family.
The type is then retained unchanged in later posterior operations.

The exponent calculation is independent of this improvement. At most
two cuts are inserted, the sample exponent is `m`, and an intermediate
child exponent is `m x_c`. Since `x_c >= 1-eta` and `m<=1`,
`m-m x_c <= m eta <= eta`. If a parent cut is present, its exponent
is `0 < m x_p <= (1-eta)m`. Otherwise the processed block starts at
the root. These include all cases `h=0,1,2` and coincide with the
advertised exponent band and progress inequality.

### Corollary: scalar powers suffice for probing the record step

The original perturbation probes every multivariate monomial in
`Q_1,...,Q_J`. For the record step it is enough to probe
`C_l = Q_lambda^l`, `l>=1`. Indeed, (7) is a Gram kernel with
diagonal at most one; tensor powers of its feature map make every
`C_l` a separable positive-semidefinite kernel with diagonal at most
one. Thus these kernels satisfy exactly the hypotheses of
`ct:gaussian`, with the same summable coefficients `2^(-l)`.
Polynomial approximation of the resulting identities gives all
bounded scalar-overlap tests in the selected limit.

The scalar unit-diagonal Gram bridge supplies ultrametricity directly,
without proving any ordering statement for the vector overlap
support. Equations (4)--(9) then supply the cut representation and
all the specified test records. The old-record argument uses no
overlap ordering. The endpoint empirical evaluations depend on the
retained types and scalar cut partition, not on separate vector
kernels. Consequently, keeping those other upstream and endpoint
ingredients as in the manuscript, the vector-synchronization
ingredient can be removed entirely from this record construction.
This is a reduction of the required identities, not an independent
certification of the Gaussian-selection or endpoint ingredients.

## 7. A quantitative consequence for finite-test collapse

If the test family is the net in `cc:net-bound`, (1) gives directly

\[
 \int W_1(\nu_{z,u},S_b(z_{\le b},u))\,du
 \le \sqrt{\sum_j\|E_j(z,\cdot)\|_2^2}+e_0/2
 \le \sqrt{2J\gamma}+e_0/2.                             \tag{10}
\]

The first inequality uses the pointwise bound
`max_j |E_j(u)| <= (sum_j |E_j(u)|^2)^(1/2)` and
Cauchy--Schwarz in `u`. Thus `gamma <= e_0^2/(8J)`
suffices for a new block's site-law error at most `e_0`.
The stronger joint record itself survives all subsequent old-record
transfers by the weighted version of section 3 and the first support
theorem on the direct sum.

Starting with the reservoir (which has no old records), every
subsequently created block may therefore carry this stronger joint
record. In comparison, the manuscript's separate-test choices
`J delta <= e_0/2` and `gamma <= delta^2/(2J)` give the sufficient
budget `gamma <= e_0^2/(8J^3)`. Equation (10) improves that sufficient
overlap-band budget by a factor `J^2`; it is not an algorithmic
running-time claim. For an arbitrary preexisting block known only to
have the manuscript's individual records, this stronger aggregate
budget is **not** asserted retroactively.
