# Steinberg source passages

Source: Robert Steinberg, *Lectures on Chevalley Groups*, original Yale
1967 notes, scan with June 1968 acknowledgement:
https://www.math.utah.edu/~ptrapa/math-library/steinberg/steinberg-yale-notes.pdf

The following is a mathematical transcription of the theorem statements
read in that scan, with notation normalized; the complete original and
its OCR are `steinberg_notes.pdf` and `steinberg_notes.txt`.

**Theorem 41.** Assume that \(G\) is universal (a simply connected
algebraic group), and \(\operatorname{char}k=p\ne0\).
Every irreducible rational representation is uniquely
\(\bigotimes_{j\ge0}\rho_j\circ\operatorname{Fr}^j\), with each
\(\rho_j\) having highest weight satisfying
\(0\le\langle\lambda,\alpha_i^\vee\rangle\le p-1\).

**Theorem 43.** Let \(G\) be a finite universal Chevalley group or a
twisted analogue constructed as the fixed points of
\(x_\alpha(t)\mapsto x_{\pi\alpha}(\pm t^{q(\alpha)})\).
The irreducible polynomial representations of the ambient algebraic
group with
\(0\le\langle\lambda,\alpha^\vee\rangle\le q(\alpha)-1\)
remain irreducible and distinct on restriction to \(G\), and form a
complete set.

**Theorem 30.** For a Chevalley group with indecomposable root system
over a perfect field, every automorphism is a product of inner,
diagonal, graph, and field automorphisms.

**Theorem 36.** For the nontrivially twisted group generated by the
corresponding positive and negative root subgroups in that section,
every automorphism is a product of inner, diagonal, and field
automorphisms. The following remark explicitly notes the absence of a
separate graph factor.

The application here excludes the exceptional bounded-rank graph-field
cases and triality. The rank, finite-cover, and ordinary group-parameter
hypotheses must be checked, not silently dropped.
