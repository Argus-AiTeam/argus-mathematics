                                                                                1. Introduction
                                           1.1. The Combinatorial Invariance Conjecture. The Kazhdan–Lusztig poly-
                                           nomials Pu,v (q) ∈ N[q], indexed by elements u, v in a Coxeter group W , underlie
                                           deep connections between the singularities of Schubert varieties, representations of
                                           Lie algebras, bases for Hecke algebras, and Soergel bimodules [KL79, EW14]. Yet
                                           the Combinatorial Invariance Conjecture, due to Lusztig (c. 1983) and to Dyer
                                           [Dye87], asserts that they depend only on the Bruhat interval [u, v] as an abstract
                                           poset (a property we will more generally refer to as combinatorial invariance).
                                           Conjecture 1.1 (Combinatorial Invariance Conjecture). Let [u, v] ⊂ W and [u′ , v ′ ] ⊂
                                           W ′ be Bruhat intervals such that [u, v] ∼
                                                                                    = [u′ , v ′ ] as posets. Then Pu,v (q) = Pu′ ,v′ (q).
                                           Conjecture 1.1 can equivalently be stated also for the R-polynomials Ru,v (q), which
                                           in crystallographic type are the Fq -point counts of the open Richardson varieties
                                           Ru,v , or for Brenti and Dyer’s R eu,v polynomials (see Section 2).
                                              Despite significant attention, progress on Conjecture 1.1 for general Coxeter
                                           groups has been limited. It follows from the definitions [KL79] that Pu,v (q) has
                                           constant term 1 and that Ru,v (q) and R    eu,v (q) are monic of degree ℓ(u, v). And it
                                           follows from Dyer’s interpretation [Dye87] of R     eu,v (q) that the coefficient of q 2 in
                                           Reu,v (q) is a combinatorial invariant; this implies Conjecture 1.1 when ℓ(u, v) ≤ 4.
                                           To date, the Combinatorial Invariance Conjecture is not known to hold for any other
                                           coefficients or interval lengths, the most recent result for general Coxeter groups be-
                                           ing the resolution of the conjecture when u = e and u′ = e′ are both the identity
                                           elements of their respective groups [BCM06].

      5. Connection to cluster structure on Richardson varieties
   In this section, we briefly describe the features of the cluster structure on a

Theorem 3.5 applies in arbitrary Coxeter groups, while Richardson varieties exist
only in crystallographic type, and are known to be cluster varieties only for finite
