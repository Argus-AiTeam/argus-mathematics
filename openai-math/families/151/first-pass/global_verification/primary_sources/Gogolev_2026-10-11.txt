  A COUNTEREXAMPLE TO THE SHUB ENTROPY
              CONJECTURE

       Abstract. This is an expository note. For every finite r ≥ 1, we
       construct a non-invertible C r self-map of S 1 × S2 × S2 × S2 with
       zero topological entropy and eigenvalue 2 for f∗ on the second
       homology group. Every orbit either converges to a fixed point or
       is asymptotic to an orbit of period 20. The idea is to start with
       z 7→ z 2 on the Riemann sphere (the first S2 factor) and then destroy
       the entropy of the doubling map on the equator by pushing all
       poinst either to the north pole or to the south pole in a non-uniform
       fashion via a computer program like algorithm. This algorithm
       is using the “clock” variable t ∈ S 1 which plans how to push z
       to the north and auxiliary “memory and amplification” variables
       (w1 , w2 ) ∈ S2 × S2 which execute this push. If the clock “stalls”
       then the push never happens and the orbits sinks to the south.
       This algorithm can be realized as a single explicit C r map. The
       low regularity of the map is due to roughness of perturbations
       which are used to set the pushing algorithm in motion. One can
       replace S2 × S2 by any 4-manifold as auxiliary variables have local
       nature. With some extra care one can reduce the dimension of
       auxiliary variable to 3 and obtain a similar map on S 1 × S2 × S3 .
          The example was constructed by undisclosed model by Ope-
       nAI [O26] and released on the historic slop-drop day, October 6,
       2026. I claim no credit for the construction, I used GPT Sol 6.1 to
       simplify and help me understand OpenAI proof. It is completely
       elementary and could be understood by anyone with basic calculus
       knowledge.
          Andrey Gogolev.

   From now on lets fix the target regularity r ≥ 1 and proceed to
construct the posited C r map f : M → M , where M = S 1 ×S2 ×S2 ×S2 .
We will use (t, z, w1 , w2 ) as coordinates on M and view t ∈ S 1 = R/20Z
as the clock coordinate. We identify S2 with the extended complex
plane C b via the standard stereographic projection, so that |z| = 1
is the equator, where the entropy is concentrated. We denote by N
for the north ∞ pole and by S the south pole 0 ∈ C. We also do
the same identification for remaining S2 factors and view w1 , w2 ∈ C,
these are auxiliary coordinates which will always settle at a fixed point
(and to avoid confusion we won’t call 0 the south pole when auxiliary
coordinates are considered).
   Once the map is defined, it will be clear that it is homotopic to
(t, z, w1 , w2 ) 7→ (t, z 2 , 0, 0) hence ρ(f∗ ) = 2.

      2. Asymptotic behaviour and vanishing entropy
  We will choose a sufficiently large number R > 2 (4.1) and construct
f so that every orbit either converges to a point of AS or is asymptotic
to a periodic orbit in AN , where
                     AN = S 1 × {N } × {0} × {0}
and
             AS = {(10, S, 0, 0)}
                    ∪ {(10, S, w1 , 0) : |w1 | = 2R}
                    ∪ {(10, S, 0, w2 ) : |w2 | = 2R}
                    ∪ {(10, S, w1 , w2 ) : |w1 | = |w2 | = 2R}.

We call these the north and south attractors. The north attractor AN
will be a periodic circle, with f (t, N, 0, 0) = (t + 1, N, 0, 0). The south
attractor AS will be fixed pointwise and consists of one isolated point,
two circles, and a torus. It is not an attractor in the usual sense —
points arbitrarily close to AS can escape to AN .
   It already implies vanishing entropy. Every f -invariant probability
measure µ is supported on AS ∪ AN . Otherwise Poincaré recurrence
gives a recurrent point x ∈ supp µ \ (AS ∪ AN ). The variational prin-
ciple [M76] therefore gives
                        htop (f ) = sup hµ (f ) = 0.
                                     µ

Hence our goal is to show that for any x ∈ M we have f n (x) → AS ∪AN ,
n → ∞.

                             3. Clockwork
   The clock t-coordinate will alway increase on S 1 = R/20Z unless
t = 10. For |z| ≥ 3/4, the clock dynamics on S 1 is unform rotation
t 7→ t + 1. For |z| ≤ 1/2, it is t 7→ t + b(t), where we choose a smooth
function b : S 1 → [0, 1] satisfying the following conditions,
          b(t) = 0 ⇐⇒ t = 10,            b(t) = 1 if |t − 10| ≥ 2,
(3.1)
                      t + b(t) ≤ 10 (8 ≤ t ≤ 10).
Here we idetify S 1 with the interval [0, 20]. The last condition means
that 10 is a semi-stable fixed point and the clock t 7→ t+b(t) will “stall”
at 10.
  Between these two regions we interpolate smoothly:
                     h(t, z) = b(t) + (1 − b(t))e(z),

Every orbit has one of the following two behaviors.
    Stalled clock. The sequence t̄n is bounded above. In this case t̄n →
t̄∗ = 20k + 10 for some k ∈ Z. We will see that in this case orbits
converge to AS
    Ticking clock. We have t̄n → +∞, so the clock keeps going around
the circle. A full circle takes at least 20 iterates and could take much
longer due to a slow down at 10 (mod 20). Now we roughly describe
the purpose of different time intervals on the clock which are used to
interact with the auxiliary variables (w1 , w2 ) to execute the push to N
after which the orbit converges to the periodic circle AN .
     (1) Reset: [0, 3]. Both auxiliary coordinates are set to zero, clearing
          the “memory.”
     (2) Preparation: [4, 7]. Tiny “signals” si are added to the auxil-
          iary variables wi , i = 1, 2. Each signal records the direction
          of the square of the z-coordinate at its predicted future push
          time. The signal is scaled down according to the predicted delay
          before activation. The signal function is merely C r due to its
          sensitive dependence on the initial point near the stalled clock
          regime.
     (3) Amplification: [4, 15]. Amplification of the signals occur in the
          auxiliary variables through the possibly long wait near 10. It
          increases a small signal without changing its direction. If the
          clock stalls, the signal is never used to correct the z-coordinate.
     (4) Activation and Correction: [15, 18]. Each auxiliary variable is
          read at most once and used to push the main variable z to
          N . The stored correction in the auxiliary variables has same
          direction as the square of z, so it increases the absolute value
          of z. On the subinterval [16, 17] at least one auxiliary variable
          is read and is strong enough to push z into the basin of N .
        A COUNTEREXAMPLE TO THE SHUB ENTROPY CONJECTURE                         5

   (5) Dynamics in the basin of AN . After the push the z-coordinate
       tends to the north pole, the clock advances by one at every
       step, and no new signals are prepared. The next reset sets both
       auxiliary variables permanently to 0, and the orbit becomes
       asymptotic to the periodic circle AN .

                        7. Asymptotic behavior
   Here we will use the notation (tn , zn , w1,n , w2,n ) for an orbit of f . We
also use the clock lift t̄n ∈ R defined by (3.2).

|zn | < 3/4 for all sufficiently large n, since otherwise h̄(t̄n , zn ) = 1. In
this region zn+1 = zn2 , so zn → S.

variable follows wi 7→ RP (wi ). According to the definition of P each

Remark 8.2. With a modified signal one can replace the two auxiliary

Remark 8.3. Lipschitz counterexample in the invertible setting can be
found in the report on the conversation among R. Bowen, M. Hirsch,
A. Manning, C. Pugh, B. Sanderson, M. Shub and R. Williams [P75].
Likely finite smoothness counterexamples also exist in diffeomorphism
setting.
Remark 8.4. Given the new example one can ask for examples with
finite collection of periodic points of any given period. I expect that it
should also be possible by replacing the periodic attractor by a quasi-
periodic one. The remaining unresolved question is the Entropy Con-
jecture for aspherical manifolds. One can still hope for it to be true
even in the C 0 setting.
