## Exact hypotheses and conclusion

Theorem 3.8 states:

> Let \(M\) be a hyperkähler manifold and \(L\in\operatorname{Pic}(M)\) a nef line bundle with \(q(c_1(L))=0\). Assume that the pair \((M,L)\) admits a deformation \((M',L')\) such that \(L'\) is semiample. Then \(L\) is semiample.

“Hyperkähler” here has the paper's §2.1 convention: compact, simply connected, of maximal holonomy, equivalently the irreducible holomorphic symplectic Kähler setting described there. Nef means membership of the first Chern class in the closure of the Kähler cone. The quadratic form is the Beauville–Bogomolov–Fujiki form. Semiampleness means that a positive tensor power is generated by global sections, not merely that some power has a nonzero section.

Most importantly, the deformation is of the **pair**, with a holomorphic line bundle. The proof of Theorem 3.8 explicitly takes a smooth family \(\varphi:\mathcal M\to T\) over a connected base, a line bundle \(\mathcal L\) on \(\mathcal M\), and two fibers realizing the two pairs (text lines 319–322). After passing to the universal cover of \(T\), it maps into the relevant fixed-class Teichmüller locus. A deformation of the underlying manifold with a semiample bundle in an unrelated class would not meet this hypothesis.

Theorem 3.7(v) has a more specific moduli setup. Section 2.4 fixes a connected component \(\mathcal T^\circ(M)\), a **primitive nonzero integral** class \(\ell\) with \(q(\ell)=0\), and the appropriate component \(\mathcal D_\ell\) of its period domain. The complex structures considered keep \(\ell\) of Hodge type \((1,1)\), so that it represents a holomorphic line bundle. Definitions 2.9 and 2.11 define \(\mathcal T_{\rm sa}(M,\ell)\) and \(\mathcal T_{\rm nef}(M,\ell)\) by semiampleness and nefness of that bundle, respectively. The theorem begins:

> Assume that \(\mathcal T_{\rm sa}^{\circ}(M)\) is non-empty.

Under this hypothesis, part (v) concludes
\[
\mathcal T_{\rm sa}^{\circ}(M)=\mathcal T_{\rm nef}^{\circ}(M).
\]
The hypothesis belongs to the whole theorem, not just to parts (i)–(iv). It cannot be dropped when quoting part (v). Theorem 3.8 itself does not state a primitivity or nonzero hypothesis on \(c_1(L)\); those are the fixed-class setup for Theorem 3.7 and should not be silently inserted into the published pair statement.

## Decisive logical check

The proof of Theorem 3.8 identifies the exact use of the seed: because \(L'\) is semiample, the semiample locus in the relevant component is nonempty; **then** Theorem 3.7 applies. Because \(L\) is nef, the original point lies in the nef locus, and part (v) puts it in the semiample locus.

The proof of part (v) is more than an assertion about density of good periods. For the period of a nef point it uses Proposition 3.6 to obtain a semiample point with a Kähler cone in the same \(\ell\)-stable chamber. Since \(\ell\) is nef at both points, it lies in the closures of both Kähler cones. Walls not orthogonal to \(\ell\) select the same half-space for both cones. The cones therefore agree, and global Torelli identifies the marked points. This is why the specified nef pair, rather than only a birationally related manifold or a very general deformation, is the conclusion.

None of these steps generates the first semiample point. Likewise, Proposition 2.10 says that the semiample locus is open; its proof explicitly allows the empty case. Openness of an empty locus supplies no seed. The introduction of the literature source also expressly describes surjectivity of the semiample period map as conditional on nonemptiness and discusses separate seed constructions for known deformation classes. Thus the old attribution correctly distinguishes the propagation theorem from those constructions.

There is no unresolved mathematical gap in the exact **attribution-and-limitation claim** reviewed here. An application seeking unconditional strong SYZ in a prescribed component still has the separate obligation to construct a deformation carrying a semiample line bundle in that very class, or equivalently to establish nonemptiness of the corresponding semiample locus. The strong-SYZ manuscript expressly recognizes that obligation and proposes its torus-and-rotation construction to meet it. Whether that construction does so is the separate `strong-syz` unit.

Failure to have an already verified seed construction is **not** evidence that a seed cannot exist, and does not refute SYZ.
