# Joins of six conjugates without ambient primitives

## Abstract

Let F=F(x,y) and H=<x^-1 y^2 x y^-3, x^-1 y x^2 y x^-2>.
Every subgroup generated by at most six arbitrary ambient conjugates of H
contains no element of a free basis of F. Consequently the
primitive-conjugate width of H is at least seven, improving the previously
accepted bound of six. Neither conjugator length nor primitive-word length
is bounded.

The proof first reduces arbitrary carriers to vertex-attached trees of
original subgroup cores. Every six-copy tree then has either a balanced
three-plus-three split or one of three centroid forms. Normalization
preserves actual carried subgroups: it uses based equality at a centroid
and original-phase reconstruction on split halves. An exact computation
covers all 540,961,135 normalized attachment choices. Sign-acyclic outputs
exclude primitives directly; all remaining outputs have complete negative
primitive-language certificates. This is a computer-assisted universal
group-theoretic theorem, not a bounded-word search.

The mathematical argument and its unchanged certificates were independently
accepted in mission `37bc0522f66c`. Fixed-input primitive decision and
automorphism-orbit algorithms are classical and are not claimed as new.
The theorem-specific originality and publication significance of the
six-copy improvement are submitted for independent assessment. Exact width,
a stronger Andrews-Curtis layer bound, and an AC counterexample are not
asserted.

## 1. Statement and comparison baseline

Capitals denote inverses. Fix the literal words

    R=XyyxYYY, S=XyxxyXX, H=<R,S> <= F(x,y).

An **ambient primitive** belongs to a free basis of F(x,y), not merely to
a free basis of a subgroup. Define

    w_prim(H) = min { q>=1 :
      <c_1 H c_1^-1,...,c_q H c_q^-1> contains an ambient primitive
      for some c_1,...,c_q in F },

with value infinity if the set is empty.

**Theorem 1 (six-conjugate exclusion).** For every integer 1<=q<=6 and
every c_1,...,c_q in F, the subgroup

    <c_1 H c_1^-1,...,c_q H c_q^-1>

contains no ambient primitive.

**Corollary 2.** w_prim(H)>=7, with infinity allowed.

Carriers may repeat. There is no restriction on their length, their
readability in a core, the rank of the resulting join, the number of
occurrences of its generators, primitive-word length, or Nielsen depth.
No identification with another author's presentation numbered P2 is used.
The theorem does not require an assertion that F/<<R,S>> is trivial.

The strongest relevant previously accepted result is [5H], Theorem 1:
the same statement for q<=5, giving w_prim(H)>=6. The improvement here is
the exclusion of every six-copy configuration, including those not
obtained by extending the previously retained coherent five-copy cores.
The internal comparison baseline is not described as a prior publication.

## 2. Arbitrary carriers and original-copy attachment trees

We use the all-carrier colored-component lemma of [4H], Section 2,
independently accepted in its companion `CONJUGATE_WIDTH.md`, Section 1.
Its proof applies to any finite number of original core copies.

**Lemma 3 (colored-component reduction).** Connect finite folded unbased
cores by a finite labelled tree, retaining their actual carried generating
loops. During folding, color the images of original core edges. The
quotient collapsing each connected colored component is a tree at every
stage. Each final colored component is the least fold of a tree of vertex
attachments of a subset of the original copies, each used at most once.

**Proof.** At an intermediate stage write Gamma for the graph and Q for
the quotient by colored components. The map pi_1(Gamma)->pi_1(Q) is
surjective: lifted successive edges can be joined inside collapsed
components. Graph folds are surjective on fundamental groups. Thus the
carried original loops still generate pi_1(Gamma); each has trivial image
in Q. The connected graph Q has trivial fundamental group and is a tree.
In particular every uncolored edge is separating.

A fold of two colored edges is internal to one colored component. With
one colored edge, the other target cannot already lie in that component:
Q would contain a loop. With two uncolored edges, their targets cannot
lie in the same colored component: Q would contain a cycle. An exterior
fold therefore never identifies two different vertices of one colored
component. It can merge distinct components only at a single vertex.

Record original-copy phase preimages on both sides of each merger. The
recorded attachments form a forest, since every merger joins distinct
components. Internal folds introduce no additional attachment. Folding
before or after the recorded attachments generates the same least
label-folding equivalence. Original core edges lie on cyclic reduced
walks, whose labels remain reduced after folding; core pruning loses
neither their images nor needed attachment vertices. This proves the
claim.

In ambient rank two every cyclically reduced primitive is sign-coherent:
it uses at most one sign of each generator. Section 4 gives the classical
input and its application. A sign-coherent circuit cannot cross a
separating uncolored edge, since it would traverse that edge in both
directions. Every possible primitive cyclic representative consequently
lies in one component covered by Lemma 3.

This reduction preserves the actual carried generating loops. Replacing
an original carrier by another word inducing the same partial endpoint
map is not a valid alternative: [4H], `PRIMITIVE_LANGUAGE.md`, Section 4,
gives an explicit counterexample to that replacement. That failed route
is not used here.

## 3. Complete six-tree coverage with faithful normalization

The literal H core has eleven vertices and twelve nonoriented edges. Its
three theta arcs are

    P:o->z=Xy, Q:z->o=yxYYY, Z:z->o=xxyXX;
    R=P Q, S=P Z.

A tree of six such copies has at most 6*11-5=61 vertices before folding.
This is a consequence of the attachment model, not a bound on original
carrier length.

**Lemma 4 (split and centroid forms).** Every tree on six copy vertices
has an edge with two three-vertex sides, or a centroid with branch sizes
2+2+1, 2+1+1+1, or 1+1+1+1+1.

**Proof.** Descend into any branch containing more than half the vertices
until none does. At the resulting vertex every branch has size at most
three. A branch of size three gives a balanced edge. Otherwise the branch
sizes are at most two and sum to five, giving exactly the three listed
partitions.

### Balanced split

Fold each three-copy side before making the remaining attachment.
These operations commute: either order gives the least equivalence
containing the original attachments and closed under equally labelled
outgoing edges. Use every pair of vertices of the two folded halves.

The catalogue of unbased labelled half cores through three copies is
complete by successive leaf deletion and all-vertex reattachment.
There are 1,001 types through three copies; 975 are absent from the
one- and two-copy catalogue. This last number is a catalogue difference,
not a claim about a new intrinsic minimal-copy invariant.

An unbased isomorphism need not preserve an original basepoint. Every
retained half therefore includes its original-copy attachment tree and
the image of every original phase in the canonical core. These maps
cover every canonical vertex and preserve labelled edges and missing
edges. Choose an original-phase preimage of each attaching vertex,
reroot the second original tree there, and join the two original trees.
This realizes the split by six literal H conjugates.

If a half is already represented by at most two copies, preserve the
attaching vertex when using that alternative representation. Explicitly,
if its based subgroup is generated by d_i H d_i^-1 at o, and t labels a
path from o to the attaching vertex, its subgroup based at that vertex
has actual carriers t^-1 d_i. The replacement joined to the other half
thus uses at most five copies and is excluded by [5H]. Unshifted carriers
or equal individual endpoint maps would not justify this step.

Only the 975 remaining half types need a new test. Unordered half pairs
suffice, because exchanging the sides preserves the unbased joined graph;
all vertex pairs for each pair of halves are still included.

### Centroid forms

Fix the central H basepoint. Let q_v label a path from it to original
phase v. A leaf attached at phases (a,b) contributes the based join

    J(a,b) = <H, q_a q_b^-1 H q_b q_a^-1>.

A two-copy branch attached at phases (a,b,c,d) contributes

    K(a,b,c,d) = <H, u H u^-1, v H v^-1>,
    u=q_a q_b^-1, v=u q_c q_d^-1.

Normalize all 121 leaf coordinates and all 14,641 branch coordinates
by based labelled graph equality, keeping the central basepoint fixed.
The resulting lists have 50 nontrivial leaf types and 2,075 branch
types unequal to H or any leaf join. They are lists of actual based
subgroups, not carrier endpoint maps. Since every listed subgroup
contains the same central H, replacing one by an equal based subgroup
preserves its join with every other branch.

A leaf equal to H, a repeated leaf, or a branch equal to H or a leaf
join saves a copy. Two equal branch types also save copies. All such
cases are covered by [5H]. Branch order does not affect the based join.
The remaining domains are therefore exactly five distinct nontrivial
leaves; one branch and three distinct nontrivial leaves; or two distinct
branches and one nontrivial leaf. No additional omission is made.

The split and centroid arguments together preserve the original carrier
budget. Lemma 4 by itself, or a scan of selected coherent parents, would
not establish this completeness.

## 4. Entire primitive languages on the resulting graphs

The classical input [P], Section 1, Lemma 3, states that a primitive
conjugacy class in F_2 is determined by its exponent-sum pair. Primitive
exponent pairs are coprime, since a basis abelianizes to a unimodular
matrix. Euclidean subtraction and its reverse construct positive
primitive representatives for positive coprime pairs, using the actual
Nielsen substitutions

    tau0: (a,b)->(ab,b), inverse (aB,b);
    tau1: (a,b)->(a,ab), inverse (a,Ab).

Signed generators cover the axes, and the four choices of ambient signs
cover all other exponent signs. The uniqueness in [P] makes these
representatives exhaustive up to cyclic conjugacy. In particular every
cyclically reduced primitive is sign-coherent. Separate primitivity of
two words and an exponent determinant are not substituted for the
displayed inverse basis markings.

On a folded inverse graph a word acts as a partial injection of vertices.
If f,g are the maps of u,v, the map of uv is g after f. For each sign
choice start with the two signed generator maps and close ordered pairs
under

    (f,g)->(g after f,g), (f,g)->(f,g after f).

Equality of ordered partial maps preserves both successors and every
later fixed-point test. There are finitely many such pairs: on v vertices
the number of partial injections is

    I(v)=sum(r=0..v) binomial(v,r)^2 r!,

giving at most I(v)^2 pairs per sign choice. A fixed point of either
coordinate reads a primitive representative on a circuit. Conversely,
the preceding signed positive construction represents every primitive
cyclic class. Testing all vertices accounts for conjugacy into the based
subgroup. Complete closed saturation without a fixed point therefore
excludes all ambient primitives.

This is a graph-specific finite action quotient of primitive labels,
not a finite automaton for the entire primitive-word language. It imposes
no word-length or Nielsen-depth cutoff. Such fixed-input decisions are
already known by [S], Corollary 2.4, and its stronger orbit algorithms;
no priority for primitive decision or finite abstractions is claimed.
The independently accepted language theorem and its confirmed [P]
attribution are reused without new proof assumptions.

## 5. Exact computation and proof of Theorem 1

The normalized domains and retained coherent bindings are:

| Domain | Indexed units | Attachment choices | Coherent bindings |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Five leaves | 46 | 2,118,760 | 2,212 |
| One branch and three leaves | 2,075 | 40,670,000 | 30,896 |
| Two branches and one leaf | 2,074 | 107,588,750 | 61,890 |
| Two three-copy halves | 475,800 | 390,583,625 | 205,237 |
| Total | 479,995 | 540,961,135 | 300,235 |

These are indexed inputs, not counts of distinct resulting subgroups.
The centroid choice counts are respectively binomial(50,5),
2075*binomial(50,3), and 50*binomial(2075,2). For the split halves,
sum n_i=27,935 and sum n_i^2=803,025; all unordered half pairs with
all vertex pairs give ((sum n_i)^2+sum n_i^2)/2=390,583,625.

For each assignment the enumeration computes the genuine least fold.
The production engine merges outgoing-edge tables. The reference engine
retains the original edge list and repeatedly scans the equal-source,
equal-label implication to a fixed point. Each union is forced; at
termination every vertex class has at most one outgoing edge per label.
Thus both compute precisely the least required equivalence.

An output omitted from the coherent records must have all four signed
directed quadrants acyclic. Such a graph has no primitive circuit.
Every retained output is bound to its exact canonical graph and a
complete negative language as in Section 4. The completed reference
replay recomputed every domain choice, including the omitted acyclic
cases, and checked all 300,235 graph-language bindings. All four
reference processes exited zero.

The same retained verification checks all 20,449 normalization indices
and all 1,001 half reconstruction models. The 9,941 saved canonical
negative-language graphs contain 2,085,750 checked states. Each language
is checked against its own graph, including all four initial sign pairs,
partial injections, both successor identities, reachability, and absence
of fixed points. Equality of aggregate counts is not the certificate.
Neither is 9,941 asserted to count all six-copy subgroup types.

Independent correctness review `37bc0522f66c` read the structural
argument, checked every normalization index and every original half
phase isomorphism, independently refolded 3,127 literal representative
subgroups and all 300,235 retained coherent attachments, and verified
all 2,085,750 language states. It inspected the completed full
reference replay of omitted cases and both exhaustive folding paths;
it did not repeat the unchanged 540,961,135-choice enumeration.

**Proof of Theorem 1.** Suppose a join of q<=6 arbitrary conjugates
contained an ambient primitive. By Lemma 3 a cyclic conjugate is carried
by a component obtained from an attachment tree of at most six original
H copies. Components using at most five copies are excluded by [5H].
A six-copy tree has one of the forms in Lemma 4. Section 3 either
replaces its component by an actual at-most-five-copy representation,
again excluded by [5H], or puts it in a retained indexed domain.
The exact replay of that domain gives either acyclicity in all four
signed quadrants or a complete negative primitive language. Both exclude the
proposed primitive. This contradiction proves the theorem.

Corollary 2 follows immediately from the definition of w_prim(H).
The argument is an unconditional computer-assisted proof using
classical theorems, not a Lean kernel formalization.

## 6. Consequences and limits

| Quantity | Prior accepted theorem [5H] | Theorem 1 |
|---|---|---|
| Arbitrary H conjugates with primitive-free joins | Every q<=5 | Every q<=6 |
| Primitive-conjugate width lower bound | Six | Seven |
| Nonterminal AC conjugation-layer lower bound | Three | Three, unchanged |
| Exact primitive-conjugate width | Unresolved | Unresolved |
| Strict AC trivialization or AC counterexample | Not established | Not established |

The new conclusion excludes arbitrary reuse of six placements, not just
products with six relator occurrences. Every finite product of elements
from the same six conjugate subgroups is covered, however long the
product and however the generators inside a placement are Nielsen
remarked. Equivalently, expressing an ambient primitive using conjugates
of elements of H requires at least seven distinct conjugate-subgroup
placements. This is a reformulation of Theorem 1, not a separate
complexity or asymptotic theorem.

For the AC comparison, an N block is an arbitrary intrinsic tuple
Nielsen sequence and a C layer independently conjugates both components
by arbitrary ambient words. The accepted exclusion of

    N0,C1,N1,C2,N2,Cterminal

uses the four-copy enclosure

    <H,hHh^-1,kHk^-1,khHh^-1k^-1>.

Theorem 1 retains that consequence. It does not cover the general
eight-copy enclosure after another nonterminal layer. Terminal
individual conjugation preserves primitivity. No additional layer
bound follows from the present six-copy result.

## 7. Primary-source comparison and publication assessment

The primary-source distinctions already made in [5H] are retained and
expanded to credit the stronger nearby algorithms in [S]. The
comparison sources below are not additional hypotheses of Theorem 1.

| Primary proposition | Established conclusion | Specific distinction |
|---|---|---|
| [P], Section 1, Lemma 3 | Rank-two primitive conjugacy classes are determined by exponent pairs. | Classical input to Section 4, not a new primitive classification. |
| [S], Theorem 2.2; Corollary 2.4 | Decide intersection of an arbitrary fixed word's full automorphic orbit with a specified rational subset of F_2; in particular decide primitive containment. | Primitive length is already unrestricted. A specified join is a fixed subgroup input; the theorem does not itself enumerate all six-carrier realizations. |
| [S], Theorem 2.6(1)-(3) | Decide simultaneous rational orbit constraints, specified independently conjugated word-image constraints, and containment of an automorphic image of a fixed subgroup in another fixed subgroup. | These are stronger than primitive recognition. They do not, as stated, construct the variable subgroup generated by six independently placed copies of H. |
| [S], Corollary 4.11; Proposition 5.1; Theorem 5.2 | Effective finite truncated-automaton systems and rational solution sets for the stated automorphism membership, containment, equality and conjugacy problems. | Finite abstraction is also known. The contribution submitted here is the particular faithful carrier-budget realization and exclusion, not finite-state methodology in general. |
| [M], Theorem 2.6 | A finitely generated subgroup has a finite computable family of overgroups such that every overgroup is a free multiple of a family member. | Finiteness alone does not impose realization by six conjugated copies of H. |
| [M], Theorem 3.16 | For H<=K there is a unique algebraic extension of H that is a free factor of K, the intersection of free factors of K containing H. | H is algebraic in primitive-bearing F itself; unconstrained algebraic extensions cannot all be excluded. |
| [I], Section 2; Theorem 4.1 | Reduced-rank inequalities for generalized joins <H,K,S1> and nontrivial-intersection double-coset representatives. | The representatives themselves generate that generalized join. This is not primitive exclusion for joins generated only by arbitrary conjugated copies, including trivial-intersection cases. |
| [E], Section 3, Theorem 2 | Decide finite existential formulas of equations with specified rational constraints in free groups; the decision problem is PSPACE-complete. | Membership in a join whose carriers are variables needs an additional finite encoding. Six carriers do not bound how often the same carriers recur in a membership product. |

The algebraic-extension distinction is concrete. The exponent vectors
of R and S are (0,-1),(-1,2), with determinant -1. No proper free factor
of F, of rank at most one, can contain H. By [M], Theorem 3.16,
H is algebraic in F. Since F contains primitives, excluding primitives
from every algebraic extension cannot prove Theorem 1. A method using
that finite list would still have to enforce the six-carrier realization
constraint. This does not rule out such an adaptation of classical
extension methods.

Likewise, invoking [E] requires an actual finite permitted formula,
not the notation p in <c_i H c_i^-1> with variable c_i. Arbitrary reuse
within the join is precisely what must be handled. No impossibility
of a finite encoding is claimed. Our proof handles that reuse by
faithful core realization and complete primitive languages instead.

The graph-specific saturation in Section 4 is consistent with [S],
Section 3.3, Theorem 3.8 and Proposition 3.9: the full reduced primitive
language is context-sensitive but not context-free. We do not claim a
regular language of all primitive words or the first use of positive
substitutions; [S], Proposition 3.2 and Corollary 3.3, already give
positive-substitution, sign and conjugation descriptions.

The theorem-specific originality claim submitted for assessment is the
universal six-conjugate exclusion and improved width bound, supported by
the complete original-carrier split/centroid realization argument and
its exact coherent-language exclusions. Lemma 3 was already accepted
in earlier campaign work. Folding, fixed-input primitive decision,
automorphism-orbit algorithms, finite abstractions, and the elementary
width deduction are not claimed as original contributions.

The field-level significance case is that a primitive obstruction
survives every six-placement adjunction and arbitrary subsequent
multiplication, even when coherent circuits occur. The complete
classification resolves a placement-realization problem not supplied
by the particular prior propositions above. The numerical increment
alone is smaller than a new AC layer bound, an exact width theorem,
or an asymptotic result; none of those stronger consequences is supplied.
Whether this structural realization and uniform robustness constitute
a publication-level improved-bound theorem remains an independent
judgment, not a consequence of successful computation.

A bounded primary-source search found no theorem directly establishing
or refuting the literal six-copy conclusion. That retrieval outcome is
not evidence of global originality. Full text of Clifford-Goldstein,
*Subgroups of free groups and primitive elements*,
DOI:10.1515/JGT.2010.010, was not obtained; no theorem from it is used.
[S]'s introduction credits prior all-rank fixed-subgroup decision, so
even that broader algorithmic priority is expressly not claimed.

**Remaining publication obligation.** Correctness and statement fidelity
of the underlying theorem are accepted; verified-new originality and
publishable significance are not yet certified. If the faithful
six-copy realization and bound are judged insufficiently substantial,
the missing ingredient is a substantive structural generalization or
stronger consequence, not another replay or a larger negative sample
of excluded six-carrier constructions.
