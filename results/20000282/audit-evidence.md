The literal AIM question places no local-equidimensional hypothesis on \(R\).
Over \(k=\mathbf F_p\), the reduced finite-type ring
\[
R=k[a,b,c,d]/(ab,acd)
\]
has primes
\(\mathfrak p=(b,c,d)\subset\mathfrak m=(a,b,c,d)\) for which
\[
e_{HK}(R_{\mathfrak p})=2>1=e_{HK}(R_{\mathfrak m}).
\]
The strict \(3/2\)-sublevel set contains \(\mathfrak m\) but not its
generalization \(\mathfrak p\), so it is not Zariski open. This is an exact
counterexample to ordinary upper semicontinuity, not the previously rejected
weak-sublevel or family-fiber variant.

## Exact counterexample

Let
\[
A=k[a,b,c,d],\qquad I=(ab,acd),\qquad R=A/I,
\]
where \(k=\mathbf F_p\). Ideals of \(A\) are also used for their images in
\(R\) when no confusion can result.

### Radical decomposition and the two primes

For monomial ideals, intersection is generated by least common multiples of
the generators. Thus
\[
(b,c)\cap(b,d)=(b,cd)
\]
and
\[
(a)\cap(b,cd)=(ab,acd)=I.
\]
Consequently,
\[
I=(a)\cap(b,c)\cap(b,d).
\]
Each component is prime, and none contains another, so this is an
irredundant prime decomposition. In particular, \(I\) is radical and \(R\)
is reduced. The three components have dimensions \(3,2,2\), respectively,
so \(R_{\mathfrak m}\) is not equidimensional and \(R\) is not locally
equidimensional at \(\mathfrak m\).

Both relevant ideals contain \(I\), and
\[
A/(b,c,d)\cong k[a],\qquad A/(a,b,c,d)\cong k.
\]
Hence \(\mathfrak p=(b,c,d)\) is prime,
\(\mathfrak m=(a,b,c,d)\) is maximal, and
\(\mathfrak p\subsetneq\mathfrak m\).

### Length at \(\mathfrak p\)

Since \(a\notin\mathfrak p\), it is a unit in \(A_{\mathfrak p}\). The
relations \(ab=0\) and \(acd=0\) then become \(b=0\) and \(cd=0\). Therefore
\[
R_{\mathfrak p}\cong
k(a)[c,d]_{(c,d)}/(cd).
\]
This local ring has dimension \(1\). For every Frobenius power
\(q=p^e\),
\[
R_{\mathfrak p}/\mathfrak p^{[q]}R_{\mathfrak p}
\cong k(a)[c,d]_{(c,d)}/(cd,c^q,d^q).
\]
Its residue-field basis is
\[
1,c,c^2,\ldots,c^{q-1},d,d^2,\ldots,d^{q-1}.
\]
Every mixed monomial vanishes because \(cd=0\), and the displayed monomials
are distinct standard monomials. Hence, for every \(q=p^e\),
\[
\lambda\!\left(
R_{\mathfrak p}/\mathfrak p^{[q]}R_{\mathfrak p}
\right)=2q-1
\]
and, using \(\dim R_{\mathfrak p}=1\),
\[
e_{HK}(R_{\mathfrak p})
=\lim_{q\to\infty}\frac{2q-1}{q}=2.
\]

### Length at \(\mathfrak m\)

The component \(A/(a)\) has dimension \(3\), so
\(\dim R_{\mathfrak m}=3\). The quotient by
\(\mathfrak m^{[q]}\) is Artinian and supported only at \(\mathfrak m\);
therefore its local length equals the \(k\)-dimension of
\[
A/(ab,acd,a^q,b^q,c^q,d^q).
\]
Write a standard monomial as \(a^i b^j c^r d^s\) with all four exponents
between \(0\) and \(q-1\).

- If \(i=0\), the relations \(ab\) and \(acd\) impose no condition, giving
  \(q^3\) choices for \((j,r,s)\).
- If \(1\le i<q\), avoiding divisibility by \(ab\) forces \(j=0\).
  Avoiding divisibility by \(acd\) then requires \(r=0\) or \(s=0\).
  There are \(q+q-1=2q-1\) such pairs \((r,s)\), for each of the
  \(q-1\) choices of \(i\).

These cases are disjoint and exhaust the standard monomials. Thus, for every
\(q=p^e\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\lambda\!\left(
R_{\mathfrak m}/\mathfrak m^{[q]}R_{\mathfrak m}
\right)
&=q^3+(q-1)(2q-1)\\
&=q^3+2q^2-3q+1.
\end{aligned}
\]
After the required dimension-\(3\) normalization,
\[
e_{HK}(R_{\mathfrak m})
=\lim_{q\to\infty}
\frac{q^3+2q^2-3q+1}{q^3}
=1.
\]

As an exact all-\(q\) consistency identity, for every \(q>1\),
\[
\frac{2q-1}{q}
-\frac{q^3+2q^2-3q+1}{q^3}
=\left(1-\frac1q\right)^3>0.
\]
This identity is corroboration only; the two standard-monomial bases above
are the proof of the length formulas.

### Topological failure

Smirnov’s Definition 1 uses the standard convention: a real-valued function
\(f\) is upper semicontinuous when every strict sublevel
\(\{x:f(x)<\alpha\}\) is open. Set
\[
U=\left\{\mathfrak q\in\operatorname{Spec}R:
e_{HK}(R_{\mathfrak q})<\frac32\right\}.
\]
The computed values give \(\mathfrak m\in U\) and
\(\mathfrak p\notin U\). But Zariski-open sets are stable under
generalization: if \(\mathfrak p\subset\mathfrak m\) and an open set
contains \(\mathfrak m\), it must contain \(\mathfrak p\). Therefore \(U\)
is not open, and the exact localization function is not upper
semicontinuous on \(\operatorname{Spec}R\).

## Relation to the published theorems

Smirnov, arXiv:1407.6476v1, pp. 2–3, supplies all relevant conventions:
commutative Noetherian rings of characteristic \(p>0\), strict-sublevel upper
semicontinuity, \(q=p^e\), and dimension-normalized Hilbert-Kunz
multiplicity. Theorem 2 states the fixed-\(q\) result for locally
equidimensional rings. Immediately afterward Smirnov writes that
Shepherd-Barron showed it is false when \(R\) is not locally
equidimensional. Conjecture 4 then poses multiplicity upper
semicontinuity only for locally equidimensional excellent or \(F\)-finite
rings. Corollary 23 proves the exact localization theorem under the stated
locally-equidimensional plus finiteness hypotheses.

Lyu, arXiv:2503.13846v4, Theorems 1.2.3 and 6.1.3, removes the
\(F\)-finiteness restriction for excellent \(\mathbf F_p\)-algebras but
retains local equidimensionality. The present ring is finite type over a
finite field, hence excellent and \(F\)-finite, but it is not locally
equidimensional. The counterexample therefore does not contradict either
positive theorem; it shows that their structural hypothesis is essential
for the literal unrestricted AIM wording.
