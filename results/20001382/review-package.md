# Result 20001382 — a topological obstruction to the literal inverse arboreal problem

## Claim decided

The literal wording of AIM Problem 14.1 quantifies over an unrestricted
subgroup of a regular rooted-tree automorphism group. That formulation is
false.

Every arboreal Galois representation is continuous from a profinite absolute
Galois group to a Hausdorff profinite tree automorphism group. Its image is
therefore compact and closed.

The finitary automorphism group of the infinite rooted binary tree is a dense
proper subgroup and is therefore nonclosed. It cannot be an arboreal Galois
image.

## Scope

This is a correction to the unrestricted literal wording. It does not decide
the inverse problem for prescribed closed subgroups.

## Proof outline

1. Every automorphism of a finite truncation extends by acting trivially below
   that level.
2. The finitary subgroup therefore surjects onto every finite quotient and is
   dense in the inverse-limit topology.
3. It is countable because it is a countable union of finite groups.
4. The full automorphism group is uncountable because independent swaps on
   disjoint descendant subtrees encode every binary sequence.
5. Hence the finitary subgroup is dense, proper, and nonclosed.
6. A continuous image of the compact absolute Galois group in the Hausdorff
   tree automorphism group is closed, giving the contradiction.

## Review status

Separate independent reviews accepted the mathematical argument and the
atomic audit integration. The result is presented as a scope correction, not
as an original solution of the natural closed-subgroup inverse problem.
