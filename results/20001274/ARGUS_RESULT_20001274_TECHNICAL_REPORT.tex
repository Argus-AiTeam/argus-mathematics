\title[Congruent Plane Projections of Convex Bodies in $\mathbb{R}^3$]
{Congruent Plane Projections Do Not Determine a Convex Body Up to Translation or Central Inversion}
\author{Argus Open Research Project}
\date{September 5, 2026}
\subjclass[2020]{Primary 52A20; Secondary 52A15}
\begin{document}
\begin{abstract}
We give an explicit, analytically and rationally certified reconstruction of a pair of compact full-dimensional $C^2$ convex bodies $K,L\subset\mathbb{R}^3$ at perturbation parameter $\varepsilon=1/1000$ whose orthogonal projections $K|S$ and $L|S$ onto every two-dimensional subspace $S\in G(3,2)$ are congruent (allowing planar reflections in $O(2)$), while $K$ is neither a translate of $L$ nor a translate of $-L$.
\end{abstract}
\maketitle
\section{Construction and Verification}
Following the two-cap support perturbation mechanism of Zhang (2017), let $B^2\subset S^2$ be two disjoint spherical caps centered at non-antipodal, orthogonal unit vectors $u_1=e_1$ and $u_2=e_2$, and perturb the unit ball support function $h_{B^3}(u)=1$ on the two caps and their antipodes with opposite parity signs at amplitude $\varepsilon=1/1000$.
\begin{theorem}
At $\varepsilon=1/1000$, the support functions $h_K, h_L : S^2\to\mathbb{R}$ satisfy:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\arabic*)}]
  \item \emph{Strict convexity:} the spherical Hessian $h+\nabla^2_{S^2}h$ is bounded below by $111/8000 > 0$ on $S^2$, so $K$ and $L$ are compact convex bodies with nonempty interior.
  \item \emph{Universal planar projection congruence:} for every unit normal $n\in S^2$, the great circle $S_n=n^\perp\cap S^2$ intersects at most one perturbation cap pair at a time, and an explicit orthogonal transformation in $O(S_n)$ carries $h_K|_{S_n}$ to $h_L|_{S_n}$.
  \item \emph{Exclusion of translations of $\pm L$:} evaluating $h_K-h_{\pm L}$ along the three spanning directions $e_1,e_2,e_3$ and their negatives forces any candidate translation vector $t\in\mathbb{R}^3$ to vanish, leaving an exact support gap of $1/8000 > 0$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
