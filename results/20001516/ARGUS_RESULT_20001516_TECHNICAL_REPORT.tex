\title[A $\mathrm{CAT}(0)$ Free-by-Cyclic Group That Is Not Ordinary RFRS]
{A $\mathrm{CAT}(0)$ Free-by-Cyclic Group That Is Not Ordinary RFRS}
\author{Argus Open Research Project}
\date{September 5, 2026}
\subjclass[2020]{Primary 20F65; Secondary 20F67, 57M07}
\begin{document}
\begin{abstract}
In connection with AIM Problem~5.3 on $\mathrm{CAT}(0)$ and RFRS properties for free-by-cyclic groups, we provide a self-contained proof and exact verification that the mapping torus $G = F(a,b)\rtimes_\phi\mathbb{Z}$ with $\phi(a)=ab$ and $\phi(b)=ab^2$ (Button--Kropholler and Wu--Ye Example~7.2) is $\mathrm{CAT}(0)$ and virtually RFRS, but is not ordinary RFRS without passing to a finite-index subgroup.
\end{abstract}
\maketitle
\section{Main Theorem}
Let $F_2=F(a,b)$ be the free group of rank two, and let $\phi:F_2\to F_2$ be the automorphism $\phi(a)=ab,\ \phi(b)=ab^2$, with explicit two-sided inverse $\phi^{-1}(a)=a^{-1}b^2a^{-1}b^{-1}a$ and $\phi^{-1}(b)=b^{-1}ab$.
\begin{theorem}
The free-by-cyclic group $G = F_2\rtimes_\phi\mathbb{Z} = \langle a,b,t \mid tat^{-1}=ab,\ tbt^{-1}=ab^2\rangle$ satisfies:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\arabic*)}]
  \item $G$ acts properly and cocompactly by isometries on a $\mathrm{CAT}(0)$ square complex (by Button--Kropholler Theorem~2.1).
  \item The induced abelianization matrix $M=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}1 & 1\\ 1 & 2\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)\in\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})$ has characteristic polynomial $\lambda^2-3\lambda+1$, trace $\operatorname{tr}(M^n)\ge 3$ for all $n\ge 1$, and determinant
  \[
    \det(M^n - I) \;=\; 2 - \operatorname{tr}(M^n) \;\le\; -1 \;<\; 0 \qquad (\forall\, n\ge 1).
  \]
  \item Every finite-index normal subgroup $G_1\trianglelefteq G$ whose quotient $G/G_1$ is factored through $G^{\mathrm{ab}}_{\mathrm{tf}}\cong\mathbb{Z}$ satisfies $F_2\subseteq G_1$ because $(M^n-I)\mathbb{Q}^2=\mathbb{Q}^2$ forces $\operatorname{Hom}(G_1,\mathbb{Q})\cong\mathbb{Q}$. By induction along any RFRS chain $G=G_0\trianglerighteq G_1\trianglerighteq\cdots$, the nonabelian free kernel $F_2$ is contained in $\bigcap_{i\ge 0} G_i$, so $G$ is not ordinary RFRS.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
