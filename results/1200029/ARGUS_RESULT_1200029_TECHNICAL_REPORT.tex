\title[Dead Ends Can Have Positive Ball Density in an Infinite Cayley Graph]
{Positive Asymptotic Ball Density of Dead Ends in an Infinite Hyperbolic Cayley Graph}
\author{Argus Open Research Project}
\date{August 30, 2026}
\subjclass[2020]{Primary 20F65; Secondary 20F67, 05C25}

\begin{abstract}
Question~29 of Ab\'ert's problem list asks whether the density of dead ends in the Cayley graph of an infinite finitely generated group must tend to zero. Under the closed word-ball density convention, we document the counterexample of Calegari and Fujiwara (2015) on the hyperbolic $(2,3,7)$ triangle group.
\end{abstract}
\maketitle
\section{Main Theorem}
Let $\Gamma = \langle a,b \mid a^2 = b^3 = (ab)^7 = 1\rangle$ be the $(2,3,7)$ hyperbolic triangle group equipped with the symmetric generating set $S=\{a,b,b^{-1}\}$. An element $g\in\Gamma$ is a \emph{dead end} with respect to $S$ if $|gs|_S \le |g|_S$ for all $s\in S$.
\begin{theorem}[Calegari--Fujiwara]
In the Cayley graph $\mathrm{Cay}(\Gamma,S)$, let $B_S(n)=\{g\in\Gamma : |g|_S\le n\}$ denote the closed ball of radius $n$, and let $D_S(n)$ denote the subset of dead ends in $B_S(n)$. Then the asymptotic density exists and satisfies
\[
  \lim_{n\to\infty} \frac{|D_S(n)|}{|B_S(n)|} \;\ge\; \frac{1}{64} \;>\; 0.
\]
\end{theorem}
