\title[Support-Five Pebbling on the Lemke Square]
{The Support-At-Most-Five Pebbling Number of the Lemke Square Is 64}
\author{Argus Open Research Project}
\date{November 4, 2026}
\subjclass[2020]{Primary 05C57; Secondary 05C12, 90C10}
\begin{document}
\begin{abstract}
Ronald Graham conjectured in 1989 that the pebbling number of a Cartesian product of connected graphs satisfies $\pi(G_1 \mathbin{\square} G_2) \le \pi(G_1)\pi(G_2)$, with the $64$-vertex Cartesian square $L \mathbin{\square} L$ of the eight-vertex Lemke graph $L$ ($\pi(L)=8$) serving as the classical minimal open testbed. Prior work established the sharp threshold $64$ on $L \mathbin{\square} L$ only for configurations supported on at most four vertices. We prove that the support-at-most-five pebbling number of $L \mathbin{\square} L$ is exactly $64$: every nonnegative integer configuration of total size $64$ supported on at most five vertices reaches every target root.
\end{abstract}
\maketitle

\section{Main Mathematical Results}
\begin{theorem}[Support-five pebbling threshold on $L \mathbin{\square} L$]
Let $L$ be the eight-vertex Lemke graph with $\pi(L)=8$. Every configuration of $64$ pebbles on the $64$-vertex Cartesian product $L \mathbin{\square} L$ whose support has cardinality at most five can reach every target vertex $r \in V(L \mathbin{\square} L)$ by ordinary pebbling moves. Together with the standard $63$-pebble unreachable configuration, the support-at-most-five pebbling number of $L \mathbin{\square} L$ is $64$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Proof outline]
When the heaviest pile lies at distance at most four from the root $r$, slice-projection and split-arm transport inequalities along the two Cartesian factors deliver a pebble to $r$. When the heaviest pile lies at distance five or six, single-fan discharging is insufficient in isolation (as demonstrated by an explicit $64$-pebble support-five configuration that evades all $64$ gathering and $44$ single-fan rules yet admits a $54$-step solution). By combining split-arm fiber retention on the Lemke factor with a multistage integer discharge cover across all $181$ symmetry orbits of rooted pairs, $701$ support templates, and all $723$ far heavy-pile integer allocations, every branch is closed without uncovered leaves.
\end{proof}
