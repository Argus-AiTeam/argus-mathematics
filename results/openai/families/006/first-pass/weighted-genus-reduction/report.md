# Integral weighted genus reduction

## Result and boundary

Equation `cs:weighted-trace-identity` has the asserted normalization, provided
the upstream CM reduction and parametrization data have the meanings stated
below. In particular, there is no class-number denominator, no missing factor
at a split prime power (including two), and no further factor at the inert
prime. The proof is integral: it does not invert two in a finite-precision
coefficient ring.

This is a general mathematical verification of the **weighted comparison**,
not a proof of the whole odd-coefficient proposition. It uses the standard
oriented CM reduction theorem and the declared transformation laws of the
reduced parametrization. It does not certify the existence of the detecting
sequence, modularity of the theta series, the genus-index bound, analytic-rank
detection, or either Goldfeld theorem. No false assertion in the target
passage has been established.

The previously reviewed `V_TRACE_CHARACTER` is preserved without recompiling
or changing its evidence. Unlike that algebraic theorem, the present result
identifies an actual reduced genus sum. It does not use the trace-character
theorem as an assumption: the two verifications meet later in the manuscript.
No claim of literature novelty or publishable significance is made by the
Engineer; those remain questions for independent review.

Write `h = sigma D`, `d = |h_star|`, `k = h h_star = -dD`, and

* `h,h_star` are coprime odd fundamental discriminants of opposite real sign;
  `k < -4`; hence `O_K^times = {1,-1}`.
* They have the same unit squareclasses at every prime dividing `2N`.
  Consequently `K` splits at those primes, including two.
* The odd prime `ell` is prime to `2N h h_star`, is inert in `K`, satisfies
  `ell = 3 mod 4`, and `chi_h_star(ell) = -sigma`.

  audited.

## Verification theorem

Let the data and input laws above hold. Let the coefficient values lie in
any module over the ring generated by the local character values; in
particular they may lie in its quotient modulo `2^M`. Choose a positive
integer `c` divisible by every `|R_z^times|`. Define

    A(D) = sum_[z] (c / |R_z^times|) P(z) chi_h_star(Nrd z)
                      sum_{x^2=k, Trd(x)=0} f(z^-1 x z).

The quotients `c / |R_z^times|` are integers. There is a phase `zeta`,
a root of unity of two-power order, such that

    A(D) = c 2^omega(N) zeta
                 sum_[a] chi_h_star(Norm a) P(a z_0),                 (1)

where `z_0` has the orientation of the chosen conductor-one Heegner point.
The sum is over `Pic(O_K)`, once each, without division by its order.
The oriented CM input identifies that sum with
`lambda_M(red_ell(L P_D))`. Thus (1) is precisely the requested identity
with `A(D)=a_M(D)` and `c=c_0`.

The theorem allows arbitrary coefficient modules, including two-torsion
and nonreduced rings. The arithmetic geometry in the input is not thereby
formalized or removed.

### 1. Descent of the weights

At `q | h_star`, the reduction of `x` is a nonzero nilpotent matrix. It is
nonzero because its determinant has valuation one. Put
`X_0 = [[0,1],[0,0]]`. Its centralizer in `GL_2(F_q)` is

    [[a,b],[0,a]],  a != 0,

whose determinant is `a^2`. Therefore
`epsilon(g X_0 g^-1) = (det(g)/q)` is well defined.
Conjugating by `u` multiplies this sign by `(det(u)/q)`.
Also `diag(v,1) X_0 diag(v,1)^-1 = v X_0`, proving the stated
unit-scaling rule. These statements require neither a choice of lifts nor
a canonical sign for the whole orbit.

At level primes, conjugation by an order unit preserves the cyclic level
line and its selected root. At two the line is an eigensummand for the
**integral generator** `(1+x)/2`, not merely for `x mod 2`.
At `ell`, order units act trivially on the residue field and hence on the
trace-zero residue line. At all other places the weight is invariant.
It follows that, for `u in Rhat^times`,

    f(u^-1 y u) = chi_h_star(Nrd u)^-1 f(y).

This cancels the change of `chi_h_star(Nrd z)` under `z -> z u`.
For `z -> b z`, `b in B^times`, reindex `x -> b x b^-1`.
The finite quadratic idele character is trivial on the positive rational
number `Nrd b`. The whole summand is therefore representative independent.
Individual noninvariant local signs must not be discarded.

The character `a -> chi_h_star(Norm a)` is trivial on `K^times` because
its norms are positive principal rational ideles. It is trivial on
`O_Khat^times`: away from `h_star` the character is unramified; at
`q | h_star` the extension `K_q/Q_q` is ramified and the norm of a unit
has square residue. Thus it descends to `Pic(O_K)`.

This is the genus character used in `P_1(h_star)`. One can check its field
interpretation without assuming an additional counting formula:
`K(sqrt(h_star))/K` is unramified. At a prime dividing `h_star` it is
obtained by adjoining the square root of the other, unit discriminant;
the same argument applies at primes dividing `h`. At two both quadratic
discriminants are odd, so the local extensions are unramified or split.
At the remaining primes unramifiedness is immediate. Its value at a
prime ideal away from the discriminants is
`(h_star / Norm(prime ideal))`, the same norm character. Quadratic
reciprocity in the CM class-field action therefore identifies the
characters, also when the genus extension is trivial.

### 2. Local orientation types, including prime powers and two

At a split level prime, identify `O_K,p` with `Z_p x Z_p`.
An `O_K,p`-stable lattice is `I_1 + I_2` on its two idempotent summands.
A stable sublattice with cyclic quotient of order `p^e` has exponents
`(e,0)` or `(0,e)`: in general the quotient is
`Z/p^a + Z/p^b`, and cyclicity forces `min(a,b)=0` and `a+b=e`.
Multiplication by `K_p^times` is transitive on lattice pairs of either
type. Thus there are exactly two local orbits for every `e >= 1`.

This argument includes `p=2` because the two eigenvalues
`(1+r)/2,(1-r)/2` of the integral generator differ by the odd unit `r`.
For an odd unit `u`,

    (1+u x)/2 = u (1+x)/2 + (1-u)/2.

Consequently the selected summand is preserved even if the parities of
its eigenvalues interchange. Its selected root for `x` scales from `r`
to `u r`. Replacing this generator by `x mod 2` would give extra
spurious invariant lines; that replacement is not made in the source.

At `ell`, the maximal division order is unique. The unramified quadratic
subfield has valuation subgroup `2Z` inside the division-algebra
valuation group `Z`. Hence

    K_ell^times \ B_ell^times / R_ell^times = Z / 2Z.

The two classes give the two residue embeddings. A uniformizer switches
them; order units cannot. In particular `x` and `-x` are not conjugate
by an order unit.

Elsewhere `R_p` is maximal and an `O_K,p`-stable lattice is a fractional
ideal of the maximal quadratic order. That local order is a DVR or a
product of two DVRs, so these lattices form a single `K_p^times` orbit.
This includes the ramified primes of `h` and `h_star`. There is no
additional local orbit or multiplier at those primes.

### 3. The global correspondence, not an unweighted count

Fix a rational embedding `i:K -> B`, with `x_0=i(sqrt(k))`, and an
orientation tuple `epsilon`. Let `Z_epsilon` be the set of adeles `z`
for which `z^-1 i(O_Khat) z` lies in `Rhat` with that tuple.

The local classification just proved gives, for any one `z_epsilon`,

    Z_epsilon = Khat^times z_epsilon Rhat^times.

There is a representative for every tuple: starting with the CM
reduction representative, apply the independent local level normalizers
and the local uniformizer at `ell`. No global selectivity assertion about
one fixed order is needed; the theta sum ranges over **all** `[z]`.

The intersection of the local centralizer with the order units is
`O_Khat^times`, since the order embedded is maximal. Therefore

    K^times \ Z_epsilon / Rhat^times
             = K^times \ Khat^times / O_Khat^times
             = Pic(O_K).                                           (2)

All rational embeddings are conjugate. For trace-zero generators
`x,y` with square `k`, the nonzero quaternion `y+x` intertwines them
unless `y=-x`; in that case use any nonzero rational quaternion
anticommuting with `x`. Since `B` is a division algebra, these
intertwiners are invertible. This also proves that (2) accounts for
all the pairs `(z,x)` in the coefficient, not just a selected subset.

For `z_a = a z_epsilon`, commutation with `x_0` gives

    f(z_a^-1 x_0 z_a) = f(z_epsilon^-1 x_0 z_epsilon),
    chi_h_star(Nrd z_a) =
            chi_h_star(Nrd z_epsilon) chi_h_star(Norm a).             (3)

Thus (2) and (3) retain the genus weights term by term. They do not
infer a character sum from an equality of its cardinalities.

The cited oriented CM theorem identifies the tuple selected by the
original Heegner point with its ideal-action orbit. At conductor one
both source and target stabilizers of that action are
`K^times O_Khat^times`, so its orbit map has multiplicity one. The
reciprocal convention for Artin maps causes no change: this character
is quadratic, so its value on an inverse ideal class is identical.

### 4. Exact stabilizers before reduction

For fixed `z`, the stabilizer of an optimal embedding in `R_z^times`
is precisely

    R_z^times intersect i(K)^times = i(O_K^times) = {1,-1}.

Its orbit has `|R_z^times|/2` elements. The global unit has reduced norm
one, so its conjugation preserves the complete weight. The contribution
of this orbit to the integral coefficient is consequently

    (c / |R_z^times|) (|R_z^times| / 2)
                  P(z) chi_h_star(Nrd z) f(z^-1 x z)
      = (c / 2) P(z) chi_h_star(Nrd z) f(z^-1 x z).                    (4)

Here `c/2` is an integer; it is **not** the inverse of two modulo
`2^M`. Changing an integer lift of `P(z)` changes the original
coefficient by an integer multiple of `2^M`, since
`c / |R_z^times|` is already integral.

### 5. All orientation contributions have the same sign

At `p^e || N` choose the local normalizer interchanging the summands,
with reduced norm `p^e` times a unit. Put
`s_p = w_(p^e) chi_h_star(p)^e`. Right translation changes the three
factors as follows:

| Place | Schwartz factor | `P(z)` | Norm character | Product |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `p^e || N` | `s_p` | `w_(p^e)` | `chi_h_star(p)^e` | `1` |
| `ell` | `-1` | `sigma` | `chi_h_star(ell)=-sigma` | `1` |

The first entry follows from `r -> -r` and
`eta_p(-1)=s_p`, also at two by the integral-generator argument.
The second row uses the trace-zero residue line, whose negation has
Legendre symbol `(-1/ell)=-1`.

The normalizers may be chosen with their other finite components equal
to one. They commute at different places. They also commute with the
left ideal action on `z`. Taking
`z_epsilon = z_0 product(normalizers)` therefore transports each
class-group term with exactly the factors in the table; it introduces
no ideal-class translation factor.

Let `zeta = chi_h_star(Nrd z_0) f(z_0^-1 x_0 z_0)`.
Every factor is a root of unity of two-power order. Combining
(3)--(4) over the `2^(omega(N)+1)` tuples gives

    (c/2) 2^(omega(N)+1) zeta sum_[a] chi_h_star(Norm a) P(a z_0),

which is (1). Filter projections leave an admitted squarefree
coefficient unchanged, and the index extraction is `n=dD`, exactly
as in the source.

The local normalizer at `ell` is a right translation, not a second
choice of reduction of the original Heegner point. Only the initial
tuple is identified with that point by oriented CM reduction; the
remaining tuples are transported by the exact covariance table. This
avoids assuming that a reduction map with a fixed residue orientation
is surjective onto both orientations.

### 6. Uniformity

The integer `c` can be chosen independently even of the rational
definite quaternion algebra. Here is an elementary, deliberately
nonoptimal bound requiring no classification of finite unit groups.

A quaternion order is a rank-four integral lattice, and its unit group
is finite: every unit has positive integral norm with positive integral
inverse norm, hence norm one. Reduction
`R^times -> (R/3R)^times` is injective. Indeed, if a nontrivial torsion
element lies in `1+3R`, take a nontrivial power of prime order `q` and
write it `1+3^a v`, with `a>=1` and `v notin 3R`. If `q != 3`, the
first term of its binomial expansion has strictly smallest
3-adic valuation. If `q=3`, after division by `3^(a+1)` the expansion
is `v + 3^a v^2 + 3^(2a-1) v^3`, nonzero modulo three. Either case
contradicts finite order.

Since `|R/3R|=81`, every unit-group order is at most 81. Thus

    c = lcm(1,2,...,81)

is a valid common multiplier for every `D,ell,M,N`. Smaller customary
choices are unnecessary here. The argument for `L` involves only
fixed cuspidal constants of the fixed curve and parametrization;
neither regrouping the embeddings nor CM reduction enlarges it.
The displayed multiplier is exactly `c 2^omega(N)`, with `N` fixed.
