# Weighted local multiplicity

**Assessment:** the written argument supports the original uniform bound, conditional on the stated isolated-zero Bezout input. The factor improvement is a reproduction/sharpening already anticipated in the original argument, not an established new result.

Current assertion: `C_GEOM_MULTIPLICITY`.

On an open subset of complex affine space, let the coordinate degree weights `rho_a` and the costs `kappa_b` of a commuting analytic frame be positive rational numbers. Let `epsilon>0`, `N>0`, and let `Z` be an irreducible component of codimension `k>=1` of the common zero locus of polynomials `f_l` of weighted degree at most `N`. Suppose

    D^gamma f_l|Z = 0 whenever sum_b kappa_b gamma_b < epsilon N.

For every coordinate normal basis `A` and frame normal basis `B` at a general smooth point of `Z`, the original conclusion is

    product_A rho_a <= 2 k! epsilon^(-k) product_B kappa_b.

The old report claims the stronger coefficient `k!`. Both conclusions are uniform in `N`, the polynomials, their coefficients, and the component. The normal-basis ranks must hold on a common smooth open set; no assertion is made at a singular point or for a noncommuting collection of fields.

## Decisive argument

Choose a smooth point outside the other components where both selected bases are transverse. Flowing the `k` commuting fields of `B` from a parametrization of `Z` gives coordinates `(s,t)` with those fields equal to the transverse partial derivatives. The persistence hypothesis kills every coefficient of `t^alpha` with `kappa_B.alpha < epsilon N`, as an analytic function of `s`.

On the transverse coordinate `A`-slice, `t` remains a coordinate system and `s` becomes an analytic function of `t`. This particular substitution cannot decrease the exponents of a surviving `t^alpha`: its coefficient becomes a power series with nonnegative exponents. The argument does **not** require an arbitrary analytic coordinate change to preserve a prescribed weighted filtration. The restricted ideal is therefore contained in the monomial ideal generated by terms of cost at least `epsilon N`. Its quotient length is at least the number of nonnegative lattice points below the strict cutoff.

The floor of any point of the open positive simplex still satisfies that strict cutoff. The disjoint unit cubes with these lattice indices cover the simplex, so their number is at least

    (epsilon N)^k / (k! product_B kappa_b).

This is an exact volume argument for every positive `N`, not an asymptotic estimate.

The slice ideal has an isolated local zero: locally `Z` is the only zero-set component and the slice is transverse. Choose `k` generic constant linear combinations of its generators that still isolate this zero. Their smaller ideal has at least the original quotient length, without increasing weighted degree.

Choose an integer `M` with `e_a=M rho_a` positive integers and substitute `x_a=lambda_a+z_a^e_a`, with each `lambda_a` different from the coordinate of the selected point. There are `product_A e_a` preimages of that point, all unramified and with identical local length. Each equation after substitution has ordinary degree at most `MN`. The isolated-solution Bezout bound yields

    (product_A e_a) length <= (MN)^k,
    length <= N^k / product_A rho_a.

Combining the two inequalities gives the reported `k!` bound, hence the original `2k!` bound. Unramifiedness is why choosing the shifts off the point matters; a global proper-intersection assumption is neither asserted nor needed.
