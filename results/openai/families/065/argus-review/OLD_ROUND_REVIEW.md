## Decisive mathematical distinctions

The common-R assertion is not merely a formal algebra identity. Its
prose argument identifies the root-gerbe family of covered orbit lines,
uses universal-curve normalization to obtain a compatible POT mapping
to the diagonal constraint, and retains full joint endpoint moments.
Scalar two-point identities then determine the upper factor, translation
and bridge from **one** \(R\); vertex conversion and stable-graph
assembly supply the all-genus step. Each point and its geometric
hypotheses are assessed in the localization report. No claim is made
that an equality of virtual ranks, a finite matrix identity or a
genus-zero decomposition alone proves that geometry.

The uniqueness strengthening has its own decisive argument: at the
first differing ample degree the fixed solution changes by
\((\delta u\cup)/z\), which an upper regular factor cannot cancel.
Applying to the unit kills \(\delta u\), then \(\delta R\).
The arbitrary-E strengthening separately checks that the geometry uses
no positivity and that ample degree, not tautological degree alone,
controls finiteness. Twisting preserves \(\mathbb P(E)\) but not the
original \(\mathbb P(E\oplus\mathcal O)\) master space.

The failed product-of-marginals attack has a concrete exact diagnosis:
the shared-\(\mathbb P^2\) correspondence gives joint value \(18\), not
the product \(6\). The actual manuscript explicitly retains joint
moments and independent dummy scalars. This refutes only the attack,
not the manuscript. The current ledger records that retired route and
the failed verbatim-toric and twist-identification shortcuts with
specific reasons and source locations.

Downstream shift polynomiality, spectral projectors/logarithms,
