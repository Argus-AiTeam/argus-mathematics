\paragraph{Result and scope.}
This note proves the band identification and product-complement assertion
used in the staged manuscript's Lemmas \texttt{band-is-stabilization} and
\texttt{product-complement}. It then proves the stated detection of the
original family class, using the explicitly identified external connectivity
theorems. In particular, this audit finds no refutation of that deduction.
The proof is over the entire parameter space; it does not infer a product
family from a collection of fiberwise product diffeomorphisms.

The previously supplied findings established the availability of the tube
fibration and connectivity inputs. The additional result here is the
geometric, transition-compatible identification to which those estimates
apply. The argument also works for the compact parameter manifolds and
dimension bounds in Theorem~\ref{thm:vanishing} below.
No novelty or publishability verdict is claimed. Independent review is
outstanding. Neither the kernel-class calculation, the embedded Lagrangian
realization, nor Hamiltonian transport is certified here.

\paragraph{External inputs and source boundaries.}
We use the following results about the \emph{lower} product stabilization
of smooth h-cobordisms:
\begin{enumerate}
\item The operation is $(D,X)\mapsto(D\times J,X\times J)$.
The outgoing face in the bent model is
$(\partial_+D\times J)\cup(D\times\partial J)$, not just
$\partial_+D\times J$. Codimension-zero extension adjoins the
trivial target cylinder and commutes with stabilization.
See \cite[Definition 2.7, Remark 2.13, Definition 2.15]{CP}.
\item If $\dim X\geq\max(2r+8,3r+5)$, the stabilization
$H(X)\to H(X\times J)$ is $r$-connected
\cite[Theorem 2.16]{CP}. An $(r+1)$-connected map of underlying
manifolds induces an $r$-connected map of stable h-cobordism spaces
\cite[Theorem 2.17]{CP}. Here $r$-connected means an isomorphism
on $\pi_i$ for $i<r$ and a surjection on $\pi_r$.
\item For a compact parameter manifold $B$, smooth h-cobordism bundles
on the fixed incoming $X$, up to incoming-relative bundle isomorphism,
are classified by $[B,H(X)]$ \cite[Proposition 2.11]{CP}.
\item We use integral homological Whitehead for simply connected CW
spaces and Poincare--Lefschetz duality for a compact oriented manifold
whose boundary is split into its incoming and outgoing parts
\cite[Corollary 4.33 and Theorem 3.43]{Hatcher}.
\end{enumerate}

\section{A geometric assertion not requiring stable topology}

Let $B$ be a compact smooth manifold and $Y$ a closed smooth manifold.
Suppose $s:B\times Y\times[0,1]\to\R$ is smooth, equals a fixed $h^0$
on a collar of $t=0$, and equals $h_b^1$ on a collar of $t=1$.
Both end functions have zero as a regular value. Let $r(t)>0$, $r'(t)>0$,
and assume
\[
 F_b(u,t)=r(t)^2s_b(u,t),\qquad d_{(u,t)}F_b\ne0
 \quad\hbox{on }Y\times[0,1].
\]
All differentials and fields in the proof are vertical for projection to
$B$. Put $\Sigma=(h^0)^{-1}(0)$ and $C_b=s_b^{-1}(0)$.

\begin{lemma}[Parametric band and signs]\label{lem:band}
For sufficiently small uniform $\epsilon>0$ there is a smooth band
identification
\[
 \Phi:C\times[-\epsilon,\epsilon]\longrightarrow
 \mathcal R=\{|s|\leq\epsilon\}
\]
over $B$, with $s(\Phi(c,z))=z$, which preserves the end collar coordinates
and is fixed in $b$ at the incoming end. On
$P_{\delta,b}=\{\delta s_b\geq\epsilon\}$, for $\delta\in\{-1,1\}$,
there are smooth parameter-dependent fields $V_\delta$ with
\[
 \delta\,dF(V_\delta)>0,\quad
 dt(V_\delta)>0\text{ near the ends},\quad
 \delta\,ds(V_\delta)>0\text{ on }\delta s=\epsilon.
\]
At each end the two fields are restrictions of one whole-sphere
prescription.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The differential identity
\[
 dF=r^2ds+2rr's\,dt                                      \tag{1}
\]
implies $ds\ne0$ on $C$. The end regularity makes $C$ transverse to
the end faces. By compactness, $ds$ stays nonzero in a uniform band.
In its interior use $\nabla s/|\nabla s|^2$.
On an end collar use $\nabla_Yh/|\nabla_Yh|^2$, which is angular
and independent of $t$. Patching these choices preserves the affine
equation $ds(U)=1$. At the incoming collar the choice is fixed for
all $b$, since $h^0$ is fixed. The flow of $U$, for a uniform small
positive and negative time, gives $\Phi$; its inverse returns a point
to level zero in time $-s$. This is a global construction over $B$,
not a selection of trivializations of $C$.

Here is a quantitative local sign check. Write $a=\min r>0$,
$M=\max r'>0$ and choose a positive lower bound $m_0$ for $|ds|$
in the band. With the product metric, $V=\delta\nabla s$ satisfies
\[
 \delta ds(V)=|ds|^2,\qquad
 \delta dF(V)\geq r^2|ds|^2-2rr'|s||ds|.
\]
Taking $\epsilon\leq a m_0/(4M)$ gives a lower bound
$r^2|ds|^2/2>0$ on the sides.
On either end collar the common prescription is
\[
 V_0=\partial_t+h\nabla_Yh,\qquad
 dt(V_0)=1,\quad ds(V_0)=h|\nabla_Yh|^2,\quad
 dF(V_0)=h(2rr'+r^2|\nabla_Yh|^2).                       \tag{2}
\]
These have the required signs in the relevant sign region; on its side
$dh\ne0$ gives strictness for $ds$ as well.
Away from sides and prescribed end collars use $\delta\nabla F$.
All applicable inequalities are strict linear inequalities in the field.
A partition of unity subordinate to the corresponding neighborhoods
therefore patches the fields, retaining $V_0$ on smaller end collars.
The constructions and choices are smooth over $B$ and have uniform
margins by compactness.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Product complement over its varying entry bundle]
\label{lem:complement}
Let
\[
 W_b=(Y\times[-1,0])\cup\mathcal R_b
 \ \subset\ A=Y\times[-1,2].
\]
After compatible rounding of the interior interface, its complement
$K_b=\overline{A\setminus W_b}$ is a smooth product cobordism
over $E_b=\partial_+W_b$, smoothly in $b$. Moreover $W$ itself
is an incoming-relative product family on $Y$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Before rounding, $K_b$ consists of $P_{-,b}$, $P_{+,b}$ and
$Y\times[1,2]$. Use Lemma~\ref{lem:band} in the sign regions.
The common end prescription (2) extends to the upper cylinder over
the entire $Y$, with $dt(V)=1$. Thus the field points into $K_b$
from all the faces of its entry interface and has just one exit,
$Y\times\{2\}$.

We give the rounding with its orientation. At a lower corner use
\[
 v=t,\qquad w=\delta s-\epsilon.
\]
The complementary region is the quadrant $v,w\geq0$. At an upper
corner use $v=t-1$ and the same $w$; the complementary region is
the union $\{v\geq0\}\cup\{w\geq0\}$.
In both cases $dv(V),dw(V)>0$ in a uniform small neighborhood.
Round the lower interface in the first quadrant and the upper one
in the third quadrant, with conormal into the complement
\[
 A\,dv+B\,dw,\qquad A,B\geq0,\quad A+B>0.               \tag{3}
\]
Then (3) evaluates positively on $V$.

For completeness a genuinely smooth profile, rather than a circular arc
joined only with matching tangent, can be obtained as follows.
Choose a smooth nondecreasing $\alpha:[0,1]\to[0,\pi/2]$, equal to
zero near zero and $\pi/2$ near one, with
$\alpha(1-u)=\pi/2-\alpha(u)$. Up to a common small positive scale set
\[
 v(u)=\int_0^u\sin\alpha(x)\,dx,\qquad
 w(u)=\int_u^1\cos\alpha(x)\,dx.
\]
This joins the two positive axes with straight segments near its ends;
its conormal is $\cos\alpha\,dv+\sin\alpha\,dw$.
Negate both coordinates for the upper profile, retaining the conormal
pointing into the complement. It has the same positive evaluation on
$V$. The rational circular profiles in \texttt{verify.py} check these
quadrant and conormal signs, not the smooth gluing of a circular arc.

The coordinates $t,s$ are preserved by the collar transition maps:
at the lower end all transitions are identity, and at the upper end
they act only on the remaining level-set variable. Consequently the
same two-dimensional profile works over the whole family. If the
extended stabilized piece and the target collar are kept separately
labelled, choose their new separating wall in this fixed planar model.
It is a fixed adjustment at an identity transition seam. This keeps
the original collared models of both pieces; no parameter-dependent
choice of a tangential framing is needed.

Every trajectory reaches the top in finite time, uniformly on the
compact family. Indeed, take two thresholds inside the prescribed
lower and upper end collars. There $dt(V)$ has a uniform positive
lower bound, so the times spent in these collars are bounded, and
their thresholds cannot be crossed backwards in forward time.
Between the thresholds a trajectory stays in one sign region and
$\delta F$ increases at a uniform positive rate while remaining in
a bounded range. Its time in the middle is at most that range divided
by the positive lower bound. It cannot leave across the entry interface.
The same argument backwards reaches $E_b$. This also covers a trajectory
in the upper band: it meets the band-end entry without entering a
middle sign region.

ODE uniqueness and transversality give a smooth positive top hitting
time $\tau(b,e)$ for every $e\in E_b$, and smooth dependence of the
reverse hitting time. Hence
\[
 (b,e,u)\longmapsto
       (b,\operatorname{Fl}^{u\tau(b,e)}_V(e)),
 \qquad 0\leq u\leq1,
                                                               \tag{4}
\]
is a bundle diffeomorphism $E\times[0,1]\to K$ relative to $E$.
It does not presume a trivialization of $E\to B$.

Extend the coordinate of (4) a small uniform distance into $W$ by
extending its transverse field; this gives a collar $E\times[-\eta,0]$.
Choose one increasing smooth diffeomorphism
$\rho:[-\eta,1]\to[-\eta,0]$, equal to the identity near $-\eta$.
On the collar plus added complement apply $(e,u)\mapsto(e,\rho(u))$,
and apply the identity on the rest of $W$.
This identifies $W\cup_E K$ with $W$ over $B$, relative to the incoming
$Y$. The left side is the fixed product $B\times A$.
Thus $W$ is a product family. All roundings are internal interfaces
in the same ambient cylinder; they do not change this total product.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Which stabilization the band represents]\label{lem:which}
If $C\to B$ is an h-cobordism family with its incoming $\Sigma$ fixed,
then
\[
 [W]=e_!\sigma([C])\quad\hbox{in }[B,H(Y)],
\]
where $\sigma$ is precisely the lower interval-product stabilization
and $e:\Sigma\times J\to Y$ is the fixed incoming band embedding.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Adjoin the fixed collar to get
$C^-=(\Sigma\times[-1,0])\cup_\Sigma C$.
In the product $C^-\times J$ the faces are
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\text{incoming}&\Sigma\times\{-1\}\times J,\\
\text{fixed vertical collar}&\Sigma\times[-1,0]\times\partial J,\\
\text{movable outgoing}&(\partial_+C\times J)\cup(C\times\partial J).
\end{array}                                                     \tag{5}
\]
Bending the fixed vertical collar into the outgoing face converts
(5) into the product model of the external input. In particular,
both copies of $C$ on $\partial J$ are movable outgoing faces.
The construction is neither an upper stabilization nor a double.

Extension is the collar pushout
\[
 (Y\times[-1,0])\cup_{\Sigma\times[-1,0]\times J}(C^-\times J).
                                                               \tag{6}
\]
Cancelling the common collar in (6) leaves $Y\times[-1,0]$ with
$C\times J$ attached. The band map $\Phi$ identifies this with $W$.
Near its incoming seam $\Phi(x,t,z)=(e(x,z),t)$, because the band
field is angular and independent of $t$ there. Thus the identification
also preserves the prescribed smooth gluing data.

Here is the transition-map check. The projection $C\to B$ is a proper
submersion with its boundary faces also submersive. To construct local
trivializations, lift base coordinate fields, correcting them vertically
by $ds(U)=1$ so that they are tangent to $C$. At the incoming end the
lift can be the fixed product lift. At the outgoing end choose lifts
independent of the collar coordinate. Partitions of unity retain these
prescriptions, and their local flows give collar-preserving trivializations.
Consequently their transitions $g_{ij}$ fix the incoming collar and,
on outgoing collars, have the form $(x,t)\mapsto(g^+_{ij}(x),t)$.
The band transitions are $g_{ij}\times\operatorname{id}_J$.
Extend them by identity over the added collar and over its complement
in $Y$. At the lower seam they are identity; at the upper seam they
preserve both planar coordinates. Every fixed planar bending or rounding
used above therefore commutes with the transitions. This proves equality
of the entire families represented by (6) and $W$, not merely equality
of their fiberwise diffeomorphism types.
\end{proof}

\section{Why the actual zero levels are h-cobordisms}

We check the prerequisite rather than hiding it in the notation $[C]$.
Suppose $k,l\geq3$, $n=k+l$, $Y=S^{n-1}$, and
$f:B\times\R^n\to\R$ is smooth, has its only fiber critical point at
zero, equals the fixed $q=-|x|^2+|y|^2$ near zero, and is exactly
degree-two homogeneous outside a common ball, with angular data in
$T_{k,l}$. Choose the inner and outer radii in those fixed collars,
and take $F=f|_{\text{annulus}}$, $s=F/r^2$; decrease the band width
further so that $\epsilon<1/2$.

Here $T_{k,l}$ means the path component of $q|_{S^{n-1}}$ in the
$C^1$ angular functions with zero a regular value. For each smooth
member $h$, its negative domain is diffeomorphic to
$S^{k-1}\times D^l$ and its zero set to
$S^{k-1}\times S^{l-1}$. Indeed a compact path of regular functions
has a positive regularity margin, so it can be smoothed relative to
its endpoints. Near its zero sets solve
$\partial_a h_a+dh_a(X_a)=0$ using the nonzero angular gradient and
extend $X_a$ by a cutoff. Its flow is an ambient isotopy of the
domains. This is only a fiberwise fact: no coherent selection of
these paths over $B$ is made or used.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:hcob}
The zero-level family $C$ is an incoming-identified smooth h-cobordism
family on $\Sigma=S^{k-1}\times S^{l-1}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For a small uniform $\delta>0$ let $M_\pm=\{f\leq\pm\delta\}$.
The negative sublevel $M_-$ retracts to its portion outside the outer
sphere. On its compact inner portion choose a strictly descending field,
radial outward near that sphere, where $df(\partial_r)=2f/r<0$.
It cannot escape through $f=-\delta$, and its bounded function range
and strict descent force finite-time transverse exit through the sphere.
Stopped flow gives the continuous retraction, including at the boundary.
Angular projection of the remaining portion has the contractible radial
fibers
\[
 \left[\max\{R_0,\sqrt{\delta/(-h_b^1(u))}\},\infty\right),
 \qquad h_b^1(u)<0.
\]
Contract to their continuous lower endpoints. Thus $M_-\simeq S^{k-1}$.
On $f\geq\delta$, normalized negative gradient has derivative $-1$
on $f$ and bounded speed: the gradient is nonzero on the compact
part and grows linearly at infinity. Stopped flow retracts $\R^n$
onto $M_+$, so $M_+$ is contractible.

The local negative sphere is a homology generator of $M_-$.
Here is the relative local-handle argument needed for that assertion.
Choose $\mu$ with $-1<\mu'\leq0$, $\mu(0)>\delta$, and $\mu(s)=0$
for $s\geq2\delta$, and use
\[
 \widetilde f=f-\mu(|x|^2+2|y|^2)
\]
inside the fixed quadratic neighborhood. Its support is contained
in $f<\delta$, and its component derivatives are
\[
 -2(1+\mu')x,\qquad 2(1-2\mu')y.                       \tag{7}
\]
Thus its only critical point has value below $-\delta$.
Normalized descent across its regular slab retracts $M_+$, relative
to $M_-$, to $\{\widetilde f\leq-\delta\}$.
Outside $M_-$ shrink $y$ to zero when $|x|^2\leq\delta$, and to norm
$\sqrt{|x|^2-\delta}$ otherwise, leaving points of $M_-$ fixed.
The derivative in $|y|^2$ is $1-2\mu'>0$, so this is a relative
deformation onto $M_-\cup D$, where
$D=\{y=0,\ |x|^2\leq\delta\}$.
The disk is included because
$u+\mu(u)\geq\mu(0)>\delta$ for $0\leq u\leq\delta$.
The deformation is continuous at the fixed set and at $y=0$.
The pair therefore attaches one $k$-disk. Its long exact sequence,
using $M_+$ contractible, shows that $\partial D$ generates
$H_{k-1}(M_-;\Z)$. Expand this sphere within the local negative cone
to the core on the inner sphere.

Apply the negative field of Lemma~\ref{lem:band} to
$P_-=\{s\leq-\epsilon\}$. Its entry faces are the inner truncated
tube $T_-^0$ and the band side; its sole exit is the outer truncated
tube $T_-^1$. The strict decrease of $f$ and compactness force
finite exit in both time directions. Flow to the exit embeds
\[
 j_b:T_-^0\longrightarrow\Int T_{-,b}^1.
\]
The band side maps diffeomorphically to the residual region
$T_{-,b}^1\setminus\Int j_b(T_-^0)$.
Uniqueness of trajectories makes the two images disjoint except on
their common edge; backward exit shows that together they cover
$T_{-,b}^1$. Smooth exit times and the transverse side condition give
full rank, including at the corner edges and at the side's zero-time
outer edge. The band map identifies the side with $C_b$, with its
prescribed incoming parametrization.

Choose $\delta$ also small enough to contain the inner core in $M_-$.
Its negative-flow path stays in $M_-$. The generator just established
therefore maps to a generator in the outer negative angular domain.
Truncating this domain at a small regular negative level does not
change its homotopy type. Since both tubes have the homotopy type of
the simply connected $S^{k-1}$, $j_b$ is a homotopy equivalence.

Excision, using collars, gives
\[
 H_*(C_b,\partial_-C_b;\Z)
   =H_*(T_{-,b}^1,j_b(T_-^0);\Z)=0.
\]
This implies connectedness. The two tubes and their common boundary
$\Sigma$ are simply connected, so van Kampen gives $\pi_1(C_b)=0$.
The coorientation $ds$ makes $C_b$ orientable in the oriented annulus.
Integral duality and the universal coefficient theorem give
$H_*(C_b,\partial_+C_b;\Z)=0$. Both boundaries are diffeomorphic to
$S^{k-1}\times S^{l-1}$ and are simply connected.
Homological Whitehead now makes both boundary inclusions homotopy
equivalences. The local bundle construction in Lemma~\ref{lem:which}
includes the boundary collars and the fixed incoming identification.
\end{proof}

\section{Detection and the manuscript implication}

\begin{theorem}[Parametric zero-band vanishing]\label{thm:vanishing}
Let $B$ be a compact smooth manifold of dimension $d$, and let $f$ have
the normalized one-critical properties in the preceding section.
If
\[
 \min(k,l)\geq d+3,\qquad
 k+l-2\geq\max(2d+10,3d+8),                            \tag{8}
\]
then $C$ is a product h-cobordism family relative to its fixed incoming
$\Sigma$. The implication uses the external connectivity and
classification results stated at the start of this note.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The previous lemmas give $e_!\sigma([C])=[W]=0$.
Set $r=d+1$ and $m=k+l-2$. Every subsequent stabilization stage
has dimension at least $m$. The stability estimate makes
\[
 H(\Sigma)\longrightarrow\Hs(\Sigma\times J)
\]
$r$-connected. One can see the passage to the telescope directly:
maps and homotopies from finite spheres factor through a finite
initial telescope, which retracts to its last stage.

The space $\Sigma\times J$ is $(\min(k,l)-2)$-connected, whereas
$Y=S^{k+l-1}$ has zero homotopy in all degrees up to
$\min(k,l)-1$. Thus the fixed collar inclusion $e$ is
$(\min(k,l)-1)$-connected, independently of any knotting.
Its stable extension is $(\min(k,l)-2)$-connected, at least
$r$ by (8). Naturality with stabilization identifies the resulting
$r$-connected composite with
\[
 H(\Sigma)\xrightarrow{\sigma}H(\Sigma\times J)
 \xrightarrow{e_!}H(Y)\longrightarrow\Hs(Y).           \tag{9}
\]

An $r$-connected map induces an injection on homotopy classes out of
a finite CW complex of dimension at most $r-1$.
To justify the needed version, replace it by a fibration with
$(r-1)$-connected fibers. A nullhomotopy of the image, with the
specified original lift at time zero and the product lift at time
one, lifts over the relative CW pair
$(B\times I,B\times\partial I)$: its cells have dimension at most
$d+1=r$, and all the corresponding fiber obstruction groups vanish.
The lift nullhomotopes $[C]$. This argument also detects components
and does not require $H(\Sigma)$ to be connected in advance.
Finally the family classification identifies the null class with an
actual smooth incoming-relative product family.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[The requested deduction]
For $B=S^9$ and $k,l\geq20$, the deduction
$e_!\sigma(c_B)=[W]=0\Longrightarrow c_B=0$
in the staged manuscript is valid, under its normalized one-critical
hypotheses and the stated external inputs.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Take $r=10$. The stability bound is
$\max(28,35)=35$, while $\dim\Sigma=k+l-2\geq38$.
The inclusion $e$ is at least $19$-connected, so its stable map is
at least $18$-connected. Consequently (9) is an isomorphism on
$\pi_9$ in the product component.
For a direct sphere formulation, a free null class is the orbit of
zero under the fundamental-group action, and that orbit contains
only zero. Thus free nullity is detected as well. No choice of a
globally based trivialization of the varying outgoing boundary is used.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Relation to the original hypotheses.}
Proposition \texttt{one-critical} begins with a smooth family on
$\R^{2p}$, $p\geq20$, with one nondegenerate critical section and
trivial stable spherical infinity class. The preceding manuscript
normalization uses its negative-eigensphere comparison and the stated
injectivity of $J_8$ to make the local quadratic form fixed.
Its smoothing and positive stabilization then give the normalized family
used here, with $k=p$ and $l>k+9$.
Those are upstream claims, recorded separately in the proof graph;
this note does not assume the main theorem, the existence of its
nonzero kernel class, or a family product trivialization in order to
verify the zero-band step.
The extra inequality $l>k+9$ is needed later for straightening tube
cores, not for the argument just proved. In its actual integer range
$k=20+u$, $l=30+u+v$, the stability margin here is $13+2u+v$.
The verification of this central implication therefore does not close
the other dependencies of \texttt{one-critical} or of the main theorem.

\paragraph{Computational boundary.}
The companion \texttt{verify.py} recomputes differential and local-handle
identities, the quadratic sphere-gradient identity, both corner-conormal
signs, product-boundary corner cancellation, the exact connectivity
arithmetic, and an independently replayed rational enclosure returned
by one bounded Jacobian operation. It reads \texttt{exact\_certificate.json}.
The Jacobian result is a sign check on a specified rational box, not a
search over functions or a certificate of a bundle isomorphism.
The universal geometric assertions, smooth dependence, transition
compatibility and use of external theorems are the prose proof above.
No script assertion stands in for those topological steps, and no Lean
or other kernel verification is claimed.
