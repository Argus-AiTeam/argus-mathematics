# Primary-source comparison

This comparison concerns the arbitrary-chart, nonsymmetric, averaged-holonomy
mechanism, not the validity of the companion near-Kerr PDE constructions.
A separate research worker accessed the full-text passages listed below.
Independent Reviewer attribution and broader priority remain outstanding.
None of these results is used as an unproved substitute for the analytic
argument in report.md.

1. Jan Sbierski, *On holonomy singularities in general relativity and the
   C^(0,1)_loc-inextendibility of spacetimes*, arXiv:2007.12049v2,
   doi:10.1215/00127094-2022-0040. Proposition 2.6 (v2, pp. 9-10)
   supplies a Lipschitz first-exit graph for a globally hyperbolic original
   spacetime in a continuous extension. Lemma 2.12 (p. 11) is a
   fixed-endpoint causal-homotopy localization statement. Lemma 2.13
   (pp. 11-12), not Lemma 2.12, is the parallel-transport ODE estimate.
   The latter uses bounded coordinate Christoffel symbols and bounded
   coordinate velocity, giving an exponential transport bound. These are
   Lipschitz/bounded-connection results, not position-averaged Lp estimates.
   Primary text: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2007.12049v2

2. Jan Sbierski, *Lipschitz inextendibility of weak null singularities from
   curvature blow-up*, doi:10.1007/s00222-025-01387-0,
   arXiv:2409.18838v2. Theorem 3.14 (arXiv pp. 10-11; published p. 973)
   is nonsymmetric and allows arbitrary Lipschitz extension charts, but
   assumes Proposition 3.6's double-null reference geometry, specified
   bounded and integrable Ricci coefficients, angular-shift derivative
   integrability (3.15), and signed integrated curvature divergence
   robust under small continuous perturbations of the testing fields.
   Its proof uses boundary C1 comparison, smoothing, and Stokes.
   Published Remark 3.37 (p. 985), numbered Remark 3.38 in arXiv v2
   (p. 20), sketches an L2 route but states that the analogue of its
   Step 2 is not known to hold. This is a known failed/unfinished route
   below Lipschitz regularity, not an available L2 theorem.
   Primary texts:
   https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.18838v2
   https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s00222-025-01387-0.pdf

3. Peter Cameron and Jan Sbierski, *Spherically symmetric inextendibility
   of weak null singularities with Christoffel symbols in L^s_loc*,
   arXiv:2609.05167v1. Section 1.1 and Theorem 1.1 (PDF pp. 3-4)
   require a strongly spherically symmetric embedding, positive continuous
   extensions of the area radius and conformal factor, s>1, the stated
   divergence of the integral of the infimum of |partial_v r|^s,
   and local C0 equivalence of the competing quotient extension with
   the reference boundary at the exit. Example 3.3 shows that this last
   condition cannot generally be dropped. Their charged-model
   applications use additional global rigidity to exclude corner
   extensions. Their symbol p>1 in the mass-tail application is a decay
   exponent, distinct from s. This does not establish a nonsymmetric
   p>4/3 averaged-holonomy theorem.
   Primary text: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.05167v1

4. Philippe G. LeFloch and Cristinel Mardare, *Definition and stability
   of Lorentzian manifolds with distributional curvature*, Portugaliae
   Mathematica 64(4) (2007), 535-573, doi:10.4171/PM/1794,
   arXiv:0712.0122v1. Proposition 4.4 (published p. 553; arXiv p. 14)
   assumes a uniformly nondegenerate H1_loc cap Linfinity_loc metric
   and obtains a torsion-free metric-compatible L2_loc connection
   and distributional curvature. The bounded inverse hypothesis matters.
   Continuity and nondegeneracy on compact chart subdomains in the
   staged manuscript supply it. The proposition supplies no curve trace.
   Primary texts:
   https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/44574
   https://arxiv.org/pdf/0712.0122v1

Two bounded citation-trail comparisons were also made. Proposition 5.11
of arXiv:2208.07752v2 (pp. 22-23) uses a Lipschitz competing extension,
a causal congruence, bounded coordinate velocities, a continuously
extending parallel frame, and a common anchor to obtain C1 boundary
identification. Theorem 4.3 of arXiv:2511.13422v2 (p. 27) is a
1+1-dimensional anchored-extension classification into reference-equivalent
extensions and extensions collapsing a null boundary to a point.
Neither supplies the finite-Lp rectangle argument developed here.

No matching theorem was identified in this bounded primary-source comparison.

and a publishability judgement are not inferred from a search miss.
