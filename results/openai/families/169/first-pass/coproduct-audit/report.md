## Exact diagnostic: the nilpotent does not obstruct the coproduct

Use the three zero-parameter elementary occurrences
`p1=(1,0)`, `p2=(1,1)`, `p3=(0,1)`, with determinant pairing,
charge `x+y`, and height `y`. The proposed split is
`a=(0,1,0)`, `b=(1,0,1)`. The points are correctly ray-ordered,
the finite list is admissible, and both split exponents are `(1,1)`.
The arrow multiplicities are `a21=a31=a32=1`, all others zero.
Consequently
`chi(a,a)=chi(b,b)=1`, `chi(a,b)=chi(b,a)=-1`, and `chi(d,d)=0`
for `d=(1,1,1)`.

There are exactly two destabilizing first dimensions for `d`:
`(0,0,1)` and `(0,1,1)`. All packs have at most one variable, so
there is no nontrivial pack shuffle in these products. Each image is
the principal ordinary ideal generated by its kernel. Thus

    I_d = ((x1-x3)(x2-x3), (x1-x2)(x1-x3)).

Set `h=x1-x3`, `t=x2-x3`, and `z=x3`. Exactly, not just up to radical,

    B_d = Q[z,t,h]/(h^2, h*t)
        = Q[z,t] direct_sum h*Q[z]       (as a vector space).

The reduced Groebner basis is `(h^2,h*t)`. In particular `h != 0`
although `h^2=h*t=0`. Independently, the specialization
`h -> epsilon`, `t,z -> 0` into `Q[epsilon]/(epsilon^2)` detects
`h` and kills the ideal. Jacobian normal-form computation and the
standalone replay both confirm this nonzero class. No reducedness
assumption is being imposed.

For the two factors, `B_a=Q[u]` and `B_b=Q[v]`, the latter because its
only destabilizing kernel is `x1-x3`. Each has only the one-block
same-slope test, so every nonzero polynomial has filtration weight
`1/2`. Their tensor product is concentrated in weight `1`.
For `d`, the only same-slope lists are `(d)`, `(a,b)`, `(b,a)`.
Every source class uniquely has the form

    F(z,0) + t*H(z,t) + h*G(z).

The source grade `0` is `Q[z]`; grade `1` is
`t*Q[z,t] direct_sum h*Q[z]`; all other grades vanish. To see the
last assertion without a degree cutoff, the proper restrictions
kill `h*G` and send `t*H` to a nonzero polynomial in the center
difference whenever `H != 0`. Their weight-1 leading test is then
nonzero. If `H=0` but `G!=0`, the one-block test has nonzero
internal-degree-1 term `h*G(Z)`. The one-block internal-degree-0
test detects exactly `F(z,0)`.

For the ordered split `(a,b)`, put `U=u-v+Z`. The kernel is `-U`.
Restriction kills `h` and sends `t` to `U`, `z` to `v`. Hence, for
**every** polynomial `H`,

    K^(-1) R(t*H+h*G) = -H(v,U),
    Delta_ab([t*H+h*G]) = -H(v,u-v).

In the reverse split the constant coefficient is `H(u,v-u)`.
Swapping the factors changes the answer by `-1`, exactly
`(-1)^chi(a,b)`. For arbitrary `m,n>=0`, the first formula sends
`z^m*t^(n+1)` to `-v^m*(u-v)^n`. Its shifted degree is `m+n+1`
on both sides, and its symbol is
`-zb^m*(za-zb)^n`, the normalized concatenated restriction.
The nilpotent vectors `h*z^m` map to zero.

On source grade `0`, `F(z)` gives the genuinely negative Laurent
tail `-F(v)/(Z+u-v)`. Its coefficients need not vanish in
`B_a tensor B_b`; they vanish in its **weight-0 associated grade**,
which is zero. Confusing these two assertions would produce a false
counterexample. This diagnostic is an exact all-degree calculation,
not evidence by exhaustion up to a degree bound.

## Structural verification

**Theorem.** Under the literal hypotheses and definitions preceding
Lemma `alg:coproduct`, its map exists in the unlocalized associated
graded tensor product for every same-slope split. It is unique with
the stated symbol identities and preserves both stated gradings.
No quotient algebra needs to be a domain or reduced.

Here is an independent proof, including the dependencies relevant to
the possible image-membership obstruction. Work throughout over
`Q`. All ideals and tensor products below are the ordinary ones,
not saturated ideals or localized quotient rings.

First, the additive charge extends to a real linear functional on
the span of the lattice generated by `C`: additivity respects every
integer relation, and that lattice has rank at most two. If the
input spans two rays, the chosen height and charge are independent
linear functionals; equality of their ratios therefore makes the
two exponents positively collinear. In rank one collinearity is
automatic. Directly from the arrow definition,
`chi(a,b)-chi(b,a)=Omega(p(a),p(b))`, so the Euler form is symmetric
on the selected slope. This justifies every use below of
`s(a+b)=s(a)+s(b)+chi(a,b)`.

### 1. Quotient restrictions and refinement

The shuffle kernel is separately symmetric in each input pack.
Every destabilizing shuffle image is an ordinary ideal: restrict an
output symmetric polynomial to the input packs, decompose it as a
finite sum of separately symmetric polynomial tensors, and move each
factor inside the shuffle. The kernels are homogeneous, so these
ideals are homogeneous.

Consider a destabilizing product with first dimension `e`, restricted
to same-slope targets `r_1,...,r_m`. Split its two source packs among
the target packs. If every nonempty piece `e_j` of the first source
had slope at most `theta`, their charge-weighted average would give
`mu(e)<=theta`, a contradiction. Some target has
`mu(e_j)>theta`; its internal shuffle belongs to `I_(r_j)`.

This argument can legitimately be applied term by term. At independent
centers, expand intertarget kernels in homogeneous internal degree
over the rational-function field of the centers. Each coefficient is
a finite sum of polynomial tensors, symmetric in each source
subpack. The target's internal kernel is not expanded. Each term in
the destabilized target is therefore a defining shuffle image.
The whole expansion vanishes modulo the target ideals. A
nonnegatively graded vector space injects into the product of its
homogeneous pieces, and extension from `Q[centers]` to its fraction
field is injective on a vector-space-valued polynomial. It follows
that the original polynomial restriction vanishes. This proves
descent, without a geometric restriction theorem.

Common translation preserves the ideals because the kernels depend
on differences. Refinement of a restriction is composition of
substitutions with the centers added; the descent just proved makes
this an equality on the quotient spaces.

### 2. Polynomial normalized symbols

Write `S(P)=sum_i s(r_i)`. Merge any two entries `a,b` in a
same-slope list. The shift sum for the coarser list is
`S(P)+chi(a,b)`, because the Euler form is symmetric on this slope.
Refining this one merged group is homogeneous when its relative
center `delta=z_b-z_a` is assigned degree one.

A coarse restriction of a vector in `F_w` has internal degree at
least `w-S(P)-chi(a,b)`. A fine term of internal degree
`w-S(P)` must consequently have `delta`-degree at least
`-chi(a,b)`. When the latter is positive, it proves exactly the
required divisibility of the leading restriction by that power of
the diagonal difference. Expand the finite expression in a `Q`-basis
of the quotient tensor space. Each scalar polynomial coefficient
has this divisibility. Distinct unordered diagonal differences are
relatively prime irreducibles in the **center polynomial ring**,
so the product of the required powers divides each coefficient.
This proves polynomiality of `L_P`. It never cancels a zero divisor
inside a quotient algebra.

If every leading restriction of a class vanishes, its representative
belongs to `F_(w+1/2)` by definition. Thus the leading restrictions
jointly inject each associated grade. Multiplying by the nonzero
scalar center prefactors preserves this conclusion, since adjoining
the center field is injective on a vector space.

### 3. Tensor detection is detection in the actual tensor space

Fix a finite original-degree cutoff containing the tensor under
consideration. The filtration is homogeneous in original degree:
each restriction preserves total internal-plus-center degree, so
different original degrees cannot cancel in a restriction test.
Split each factor's finite filtration into
vector-space complements. In a tensor of lowest nonzero total
weight `w`, each factor-grade summand has weights `(u,v)` with
`u+v=w`. The tensor product of its two jointly injective families of
leading restrictions is injective over a field. In a fixed pair of
tests `(R,S)`, different values of `(u,v)` have distinct separate
internal degrees

    (u-S(R), v-S(S)).

They cannot cancel. Some pair of tests detects the lowest nonzero
summand. This proves both the characterization of the additive tensor
filtration by all unnormalized tensor tests and joint injectivity of
the normalized tests on every associated tensor grade. The proof
also works after adjoining independent centers. It is not a claim
that any prescribed polynomial family is in the image of the tests.

### 4. Actual Laurent coefficients, before any polynomiality argument

Let `f in F_w B_d` be homogeneous of polynomial degree `ell`.
Before quotienting, expand

    K_ab(x+Z,y)^(-1) f(x+Z,y) = sum_j C_j(f) Z^j

at infinity. Every kernel factor has form
`(-Z + y_j-x_i)^m` with integer `m`. Its leading coefficient is
`+1` or `-1`, a scalar unit over **any** of the quotient algebras
at issue. Binomial expansion therefore gives actual separately
symmetric polynomial coefficients. For fixed `j` only finitely many
choices occur, and homogeneity gives

    degree_pol C_j(f) = ell + chi(a,b) - j.

In particular `j <= ell+chi(a,b)` for a nonzero coefficient.
Quotient descent is already justified: the polynomial restriction
annihilates `I_d`, and each coefficient of the inverse kernel acts
by ordinary multiplication on the quotient tensor. Thus `C_j(f)`
is an element of `B_a tensor B_b`, not an element obtained only
after localizing this algebra.

### 5. Coefficientwise filtration preservation and symbol matching

Apply arbitrary same-slope refinements `R` of `a`, `S` of `b`.
The first list's centers become `z_R+Z`. In the completion by
internal degree, the refined inverse kernel has internal-degree-0
term

    product_(r in R,s in S) (z_s-z_r-Z)^chi(r,s);

all remaining terms have positive internal degree. The refined
restriction of `f` has internal degree plus `S(R)+S(S)` at least
`w`. Multiplication cannot lower this bound.

These expansions may be compared coefficientwise in `Z`. An
internal term of inverse-kernel degree `N` has upper `Z`-degree at
most `chi(a,b)-N`, while the substituted polynomial has upper
`Z`-degree at most `ell`. A contribution to `Z^j` therefore requires
`N<=ell+chi(a,b)-j`. There are only finitely many such nonnegative
internal degrees. This rules out an illicit interchange of infinite
expansions. Every refinement test of each actual `C_j(f)` has the
bound `w`; Step 3 gives

    C_j(f) in F_w(B_a tensor B_b).

On total weight `w` only the scalar term of the inverse kernel
survives. Multiply by the separate symbol prefactors for `R` and
`S`. Together with this scalar term they are exactly the prefactor
for the concatenation. Consequently, in the Laurent coefficient
space of actual associated tensor classes,

    (L_R tensor L_S)(sum_j [C_j(f)] Z^j)
       = L_(R,S)([f])(z_R+Z,z_S).                 (*)

This establishes image membership using actual lifts, not an
unproved surjectivity or saturation property of the symbol map.

### 6. Descent of polynomiality and evaluation at zero

Step 2 makes the right side of (*) a polynomial in `Z` and all
centers. Each negative coefficient on the left has zero image under
every tensor test. Step 3 forces `[C_j(f)]=0` for every `j<0` in
the actual weight-`w` tensor grade. Step 4 bounds the remaining
positive powers, so the left side is an actual finite tensor-valued
polynomial.

Define `Delta_ab([f])=[C_0(f)]`. Applying Step 5 with weight
`w+1/2` shows that changing the representative by that higher
filtration changes every coefficient by the higher tensor
filtration. Thus this definition descends to `gr_w B_d`.
Evaluating the now-polynomial identity (*) at `Z=0` proves
**all** the independent-center identities of the literal lemma.
Their joint injectivity also proves uniqueness.

The constant coefficient has polynomial degree
`ell+chi(a,b)`, hence target shifted degree

    ell+chi(a,b)+s(a)+s(b) = ell+s(d).

Total filtration weight was built into the construction. If one
dimension is zero, the kernel is `1` and the constant coefficient is
the usual identity tensor unit. This completes the stated-scope
proof.

The argument in Step 6 is separately formalized as
`polynomial_image_descent` in `Main.lean`. It even holds for
zero-preserving injections of pointed sets, so linearity is not a
hidden hypothesis of that final implication. The prerequisite
coefficient construction and the quotient/refinement arguments
remain mathematical proof, not claims of full Lean formalization.
