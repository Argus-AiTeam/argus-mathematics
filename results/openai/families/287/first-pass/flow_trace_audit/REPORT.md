# Scoped audit of the initial trace identity

## Question and hypotheses

The target is `lem:flow-trace-identity` and its bridge to
`lem:flow-moments` in the staged September 23, 2026 manuscript,
`build/sections/flow.tex`. The question is whether
`tau(ev_0(delta^r p)) = 0` for every formal polynomial `p`, every
positive integer `r`, every free group `F_n`, and every finitely
supported real coefficient tuple `h_1,...,h_n`.

The initial tuple `(A_1,...,A_n,C)` is a freely generating Haar
tuple in a finite von Neumann algebra with faithful normal tracial
state. The bounded self-adjoint logarithm is `S = arg(C)`.
The derivation fixes `z` and sends
`a_j` to `i sum_g h_j(g) z_g a_j`; inverse letters have the
manuscript's explicitly specified derivative. These are hypotheses,
not conclusions inferred from numerical experiments.

The normalized variable `T = S/pi` has uniform distribution on
`[-1,1]`, with moments `m_(2k) = 1/(2k+1)` and `m_(2k+1) = 0`.
Exact computations will take place in the original free product of
the group algebra and the polynomial algebra in `T`, not in an
assumed positive-time free tuple.

## Scope and dependencies recorded before computation

The trace identity separates into:

1. Nonidentity group labels have zero initial trace, and the formal
   derivation preserves the label.
2. The identity-label algebra is generated by the conjugates `z_g`;
   their evaluated component algebras form a free family.
3. Varying one component in a direction `i[Y_g,S_g]`, with `Y_g`
   in its free complement, has zero initial trace on every polynomial.
4. The first-derivative identity on the entire formal algebra implies
   every positive derivative order, because the derivation maps finite
   polynomials to finite polynomials.

The moment-preservation bridge additionally requires a global unitary
solution and an actual convergent Taylor expansion. Smoothness alone
and vanishing derivatives at one point are insufficient.

The staged preliminary lemmas are initially recorded as dependencies,
to be discharged only by independent proofs in this report. No
external exponentiation theorem is assumed. The wiki index contains
no topic on free-product trace-preserving flows; unrelated inherited
pages are not mathematical evidence for this audit.

Two approaches are excluded at scope: assuming freeness of the
evolving tuple is circular, and any bounded collection of passing
moments leaves the universal quantifiers unresolved. Exact finite
computations are adversarial diagnostics only.

## Result

The universal initial trace identity is **true** under the stated
hypotheses. The analytic bridge to actual moment invariance is also
valid. The proof below does not assume freeness at positive times.
No false assertion in the two targeted lemmas is identified.

In fact, the argument needs neither the uniform law nor the special
choice `S = arg(C)`: it applies to any bounded self-adjoint `S` free
from the Haar group tuple. This is a scope extension of the verified
statement, not a claim of a new publishable result.

### 1. Initial free product and labels

Let `G` be the algebra of the initial group tuple and `Q` the
polynomial algebra in `S`. Their freeness follows directly from the
Haar tuple. For Laurent polynomials in the original Haar `C`,
an alternating product of centered elements expands into nonidentity
reduced words of the original free group, each with trace zero.
To include the bounded logarithm, approximate its argument function
by its Fejer means on the circle. These Laurent polynomials are
uniformly bounded by `pi`, have the correct mean, and converge in
`L^2` to `S`. Explicitly, the convolution kernel is
`K_N(t) = |sum_(k=0)^N exp(ikt)|^2/(N+1)`, is nonnegative,
has normalized integral one, and has integral tending to zero
outside every fixed neighborhood of zero: there it is bounded
by `1/((N+1) sin(t/2)^2)`. Convolution therefore converges at
every continuity point of the argument function, and bounded
convergence gives the asserted `L^2` limit. Fixed powers also
converge in `L^2`, with uniform norm bounds. Telescoping products and
`|tau(xy)| <= ||x||_2 ||y||_2` pass the alternating centered
trace identities to polynomials in `S`. Thus no freeness assertion
about the flow is involved.

For any monomial, combine its maximal group blocks and powers of `S`.
In an alternating expression with both kinds of blocks, replace
each `S` block by its scalar trace plus a centered block. Group
blocks are already centered unless they are the identity, in which
case remove them and combine the neighboring `S` blocks. The trace
of the term with every block centered is zero by freeness. All
remaining terms have fewer blocks and the same total group label.
Induction therefore gives trace zero if that label is not the
identity: at the final one-group-block stage its trace is zero,
and a pure `S` block cannot have nonidentity label.

This argument is also the terminating exact recurrence implemented
by `original_trace` in `verify.py`. It uses only group reduction,
the marginal moments of `T`, and the definition of freeness. In
particular, that implementation does not assume conjugate-component
freeness or either target identity.

Each formal derivative replacement has the same group label as the
letter it replaces. For example, `a_g z a_g* a_j` has label `x_j`
and `a_j* a_g z a_g*` has label `x_j^-1`. Hence every derivative
term retains its original label.

Repeatedly using the formal identity `a_g z = z_g a_g` writes a
monomial as
`z_(g_1) ... z_(g_m) a_k`, where `k` is its label. In particular,
an identity-label monomial is a polynomial in the `z_g`. The
rewriting and its derivative take place in the formal algebra,
not in an assumed quotient by the initial evaluation kernel.

### 2. Initial conjugates and grouping

Write `A_g q(S) A_g*` for an element of component `g`.
Take centered factors of this form with consecutive labels
`g_1,...,g_m` distinct. For `m > 1`, cyclicity rewrites their
product trace as

```
tau(q_1(S) A_(g_1^-1 g_2) q_2(S) ... q_m(S) A_(g_m^-1 g_1)).
```

Every interior group block is nonidentity and centered. The final
group block is either centered or the identity, which can be
omitted. In both cases the remaining product is alternating and
centered in `G` and `Q`, so its trace is zero. For `m = 1` this is
just the chosen centering. This proves freeness of all polynomial
conjugate components.

Fix `g`. The algebra generated by all other components is spanned
by the scalar and nonempty reduced centered words in those
components. To obtain this span, center each factor, expand, and
combine adjacent factors from the same component, recentering their
product; combining lowers the number of factors, so induction
terminates. A centered element has no scalar term in such a
representation, since every nonempty reduced centered word has
trace zero. Alternating these nonempty complement words with
centered component-`g` factors gives reduced centered words in the
original family: the labels at the joining points differ because
none of the complement labels is `g`. This proves the required
grouped freeness for polynomial components.

The full von Neumann formulation recorded in the ledger follows
by separate normality, not by an unproved assertion about joint
continuity. For any fixed finite alternating pattern, the map

```
(v_1,...,v_m) -> tau(prod_j (v_j - tau(v_j) 1))
```

is separately ultraweak-continuous: all but one factor are fixed
bounded elements, and the remaining product trace is a normal
functional of that factor. Each polynomial component algebra is
ultraweakly dense in its generated von Neumann algebra. Extend
the zero identity in one position at a time, keeping the others
fixed; after finitely many extensions it holds on all of the
closures. Apply the same argument to the polynomial grouped
complement and its ultraweak closure. This proves both
`A_g W*(S) A_g*` freeness and the full grouped-complement
assertion. The actual flow proof needs only the already-proved
polynomial case.

### 3. Algebraic partial-inner-derivation theorem

**Theorem.** Let two unital algebras `A` and `B` be free in a
tracial noncommutative probability space. Fix any `Y` in `B`.
On formal polynomials in their components, vary every `A` factor
by `[Y,a]` and fix every `B` factor. The resulting first variation
has trace zero for every polynomial.

**Proof.** First take an alternating centered word `w = v_1...v_m`
in the two algebras. Let `V_A(w)` be the sum of insertions
`v_j -> [Y,v_j]` at its `A` positions and `V_B(w)` the analogous
sum at its `B` positions. The product rule for a commutator gives
the purely algebraic identity

```
V_A(w) + V_B(w) = [Y, v_1 ... v_m].
```

It holds for every length, including zero, in every
noncommutative ring; adjacent interior insertions telescope.
The Lean theorem `variation_split` proves it by induction on the
entire finite list, with arbitrary Boolean position markings.

At a `B` position, `[Y,v_j]` is still in `B` and has trace zero,
because the trace is cyclic. Thus every summand of `V_B(w)` is
again an alternating centered word, so freeness gives its trace
zero. The full commutator also has trace zero. Consequently
`tau(V_A(w)) = 0`. The Lean theorem `selected_trace_zero`
checks exactly this implication, with complementary zero trace
as an explicit hypothesis, not as the selected conclusion.

Finally the scalar and alternating centered words span every
polynomial in `A` and `B`, by the reduction in Section 2.
This reduction can be performed in the **formal** algebra:
subtract the fixed initial trace of a block as a scalar
coefficient, expand, and combine adjacent same-component blocks.
Variation kills those scalars and satisfies the product rule.
It is `[Y,q]` on a component-`A` polynomial `q`, and zero on a
component-`B` polynomial. No assertion that a derivation descends
through the initial evaluated relations is used. Linearity now
proves the theorem. QED.

No unitaries `exp(iuY)` or integrated auxiliary motions are needed
for this proof. Self-adjointness of `Y` is unnecessary for this
algebraic theorem. In the manuscript application it does hold
because the coefficient vectors are real.

### 4. Application to every coefficient support and derivative order

The cocycle exists with finite support at each group element.
For inverse letters its required value is
`D_(x_j^-1) = -lambda(x_j^-1) h_j`. In the shifted letter-sum
definition, the two contributions of an adjacent inverse pair
cancel. The definition therefore respects every group reduction,
and concatenation gives `D_(gb) = D_g + lambda(g)D_b`.
The same cancellation establishes the formal word derivative

```
delta(a_g) = i sigma(D_g) a_g,
delta(z_g) = i [sigma(D_g), z_g].
```

These are identities in the unitary formal algebra. They include
the inverse orientation and derivatives of all coefficient
polynomials, not just of the original generators.

For an identity-label polynomial `F(z_g : g in I)`, the set `I`
is finite. For each `g` remove the commuting term
`D_g(g) z_g` and set

```
Y_g = sum_(b != g) D_g(b) S_b.
```

It belongs to the free complement of component `g`, has finite
support, and is self-adjoint for real coefficients. Apply the
theorem in Section 3, with the selected positions being exactly
the occurrences of `z_g` in `F`. It gives zero trace for the
sum of insertions `i[Y_g,S_g]` at those positions. Summing these
zero traces over `g in I` is precisely the product rule for
`tau(ev_0(delta F))`. Section 1 covers all nonidentity-label
monomials. Thus

```
tau o ev_0 o delta = 0
```

on the **entire** formal polynomial algebra.

Every replacement has finite support and finite word length.
Thus `delta^(r-1) p` is again a finite polynomial for every
positive integer `r`, even though its degree and support may
grow. Applying the just-proved first-order identity to that
polynomial proves `tau(ev_0(delta^r p)) = 0`. The Lean theorem
`all_positive_iterates` checks this implication for every
endomorphism and every positive iterate. There is no degree,
support-size, or derivative-order cutoff in this argument.

### 5. Global actual solution

On the real Banach space `E^n`, the vector field is a finite
polynomial because all coefficient supports and group words
are finite. Adjunction is bounded real-linear. On any fixed
bounded ball the field has bounded norm and a Lipschitz bound.
On a sufficiently short interval, its integral operator maps
a closed ball of continuous paths into itself and has
contraction constant less than one. Successive approximations
are uniformly geometrically Cauchy, and completeness gives the
unique local solution. Differentiating the polynomial equation
repeatedly gives norm smoothness.

For real coefficients, every `H_j(X)` is self-adjoint even
before unitarity is established. Direct differentiation gives
`(X_j* X_j)' = 0`, so `X_j* X_j = 1`. In the given finite
tracial algebra, the projection `1-X_j X_j*` has trace zero.
Faithfulness gives `X_j X_j* = 1`. Thus all coordinates remain
unitary, and

```
||X_j'(t)|| <= ||S|| sum_g |h_j(g)|.
```

At any finite endpoint the solution is consequently
norm-Cauchy. Its limit is a unitary tuple in the closed algebra
`E`. Local existence at that tuple extends the solution,
contradicting maximality. This applies to both endpoints.
It proves existence and uniqueness on the full real line,
without compactness of a bounded ball or positive-time freeness.

### 6. Explicit analytic bridge

Let `l >= 1` bound the formal degree of a representative of `p`,
and let `b_0` be its absolute coefficient sum. There are constants
`J >= 1` and `B_1 >= 1` such that a letter replacement raises
degree by at most `J` and has absolute coefficient sum at most
`B_1`. For example, one can take

```
J = max(1, max_(j,g in supp h_j) (2|g|+1)),
B_1 = max(1, max_j sum_g |h_j(g)|).
```

Empty supports cause no problem. Induction on replacement count
gives degree at most `l+rJ` and absolute coefficient sum at most

```
b_0 B_1^r prod_(v=0)^(r-1) (l+vJ).
```

The product rule along the actual unitary solution yields, at
every real `t` and integer `r >= 0`,
`(d/dt)^r ev_t(p) = ev_t(delta^r p)`.
Set `R = max(1,||S||)`. Each evaluated formal letter has norm
at most `R`. Hence, uniformly for **all** real `t`,

```
||(d/dt)^r ev_t(p)|| / r!
 <= b_0 R^l (B_1 R^J)^r prod_(v<r)(l+vJ)/r!
 <= b_0 R^l [B_1 R^J (l+J)]^r.
```

The last step follows termwise from
`l+vJ <= (l+J)(v+1)`. Lean proves this for all natural
`l,J,v`, and `growth_bound` proves the product/factorial
inequality for every `r`.

For `f(t) = tau(ev_t(p))`, put `c = B_1 R^J (l+J) > 0`.
Repeated integration of the fundamental theorem of calculus
bounds the Taylor remainder after degree `r` at any center `t`
and real increment `u` by
`b_0 R^l (c |u|)^(r+1)`. It tends to zero when `|u| < 1/c`.
This is an actual convergent Taylor expansion, not an inference
from smoothness or a formal series.

Section 4 annihilates all positive derivatives of `f` at zero,
so `f` is constant for `|t| < 1/c`. To avoid an unrecorded
analytic-continuation theorem, use the same uniform Taylor
radius at successive centers `k/(2c)`, for integer `k`.
If `f` is constant in a neighborhood of one center, all its
positive derivatives vanish there, so its Taylor expansion
extends that constant to radius `1/c`; the next center has a
neighborhood inside this interval. Induction in both directions
covers the real line. Therefore

```
tau(ev_t(p)) = tau(ev_0(p))    for every real t.
```

The constants depend on the fixed polynomial and coefficient
tuple; no common radius for all polynomials or supports is
needed. This proves `lem:flow-moments` non-circularly.
