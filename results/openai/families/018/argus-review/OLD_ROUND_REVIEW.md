## Claims, dependencies, and concrete open obligations

Theorem A supplies exact block-supported shears for arbitrary finite disjoint
parameter tuples over a separable quadratic global extension. It does not need
the original conjugacy, odd block count, division-algebra realization, or
degree-six restriction. Characteristic two still needs
t_i bar(t_i)!=1. This is a proposed strengthening relative to the staged
manuscript lemmas, not a verified-new literature result.

Its phase ring has fraction field E. A finitely generated phase subring has
finite quotients by nonzero principal ideals, so powers of an infinite-order
phase supply the required congruence units. The affine commutator is an
actual subgroup element, and commutators of normal-kernel lifts belong to
their exact intersection. The bounds **40 letters** and **five exceptional
phases** concern one killed-block witness, not a uniformly bounded final
supported shear or additive-generation procedure.

Theorem D has a separate arithmetic proof. Its decisive issue is exactness:
local spanning/closure is followed by a full-lattice and CRT argument that
places the desired element in the abstract additive subgroup. The arithmetic
assessment reconstructs the compressed global step rather than accepting
density as equality. Its four external inputs remain explicit.

The conditional action argument uses these two theorems, with division
precisely when the nonzero increment y is inverted. Left unitary conjugation
gives increments vy, and finite sums give every delta through
delta y^-1 in Z U(k). No commutativity of D or limiting argument is used.

The two applications identify the actual manuscript blocks and generators,
including the standard Skolem--Noether input. Their ordered pair and
three-unitaries calculations, domain conditions, Sylvester invertibility,
and constant evaluation were inspected in prose. They are **not** Lean
consequences. The characteristic-two application separately checks why
commuting conjugations and d bar(d) not in k give the needed block restriction.
