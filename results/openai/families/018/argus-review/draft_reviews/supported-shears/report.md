Let E/F be a separable quadratic extension of global fields with involution

the tuple is disjoint from its conjugate tuple. In characteristic two also

nonidentity upper shear supported on each chosen block, and likewise a lower
shear. Disjointness implies t_i != 1, so the declared boosts are invertible.

## Decisive argument

### Triangular separation and characteristic two

At the chosen block write a=t_j and b=bar(t_j). Neither is 0 or 1 and a!=b.
The displayed branch-switch formulas are rational expressions in a,b,z.
The norm-one identities for z' and h', the equality of slope fibers, and the
lower-left entry of g_j were checked symbolically. Its lower-left entry has
the single parameter zero t=a; the coefficient-conjugate upper-right entry
has its single nonzero-parameter zero t=b. The disjointness hypothesis is
exactly what makes the unwanted blocks avoid those zeros.

The exceptional branch polynomial is

    N(z)=(ab-2a+1)z-ab+2b-1.

In odd characteristic its two coefficients sum to 2(b-a), which is nonzero.
In characteristic two it is (ab+1)(z+1), nonzero under ab!=1.
This separate argument is essential: a rational-function check over Q
alone would not prove generic distinctness of the two branches in
characteristic two.

At z=-1 all relevant denominators at the finite tuple are nonzero:
in particular D(-1)=-(a-1)(b-1), and 1-t_i!=0. The rational separator
specializes to the identity, so each diagonal entry is a nonzero rational
function. This is an algebraic specialization to establish nonvanishing,
not an appeal to a limit inside H.

The phase curve has infinitely many F-points:
(theta+v)/(bar(theta)+v), v in F, are distinct for theta not fixed by bar.
Consequently one can exclude all finitely many denominator, diagonal,
and branch zeros simultaneously. Using bar(t_j) instead gives the lower
triangular analogue. This argument also covers m=1.

### Phase ring and congruence units

For R=Z[E^1], multiplication closure of E^1 gives R=Z E^1 as additive
groups. For a nonfixed x, set v=x/bar(x) and
w=(x+1)/(bar(x)+1). Then bar(x)=(1-w)/(w-v), with nonzero denominator.
Subtracting a fixed nonfixed theta from theta+y handles fixed y. Thus
Frac(R)=E, without an additive-density claim.

A global field has only finitely many multiplicative torsion elements:
roots of unity have bounded algebraic degree in the number-field case,
and torsion lies in the finite constant field in positive characteristic.
The infinite phase set therefore supplies an infinite-order z.

Writing alpha=A/B and beta=C/D in Frac(R), c=AC has
c/alpha=BC and c/beta=AD in R. A finite subring T containing these elements,
z and z^-1, field generators as fractions, and r_0,r_1 with r=r_0/r_1
has Frac(T)=E and cT contained in both alpha R and beta R.

The finiteness of T/aT for nonzero a is justified, not assumed:
in characteristic zero each generator has a primitive integer polynomial,
whose reduction modulo each prime is nonzero. Thus T/pT is a finite
algebra over F_p (or the zero ring). The successive p-power quotients
and CRT give the same conclusion for T/nT. An algebraic equation for
a with nonzero constant term puts a nonzero integer n in aT.
In characteristic p, a nonzero constant is a unit. Otherwise a is
transcendental over F_p and E is finite over F_p(a). Each generator
has a polynomial relation P(a,X); canceling the common power of a makes
P(0,X) nonzero. All generator images modulo aT are algebraic over F_p,
so this finitely generated algebra is finite.

Applying this to a=c^2 r_1 gives u=z^q congruent to 1 modulo c^2 r_1 T.
It remains an infinite-order unit. The choices s=c and t=(u-1)/c give
s,t in I=cT, u=1+st, and (1-u^2)r in I. No unit theorem, divisor-class
finiteness, weak approximation, or normalization-finiteness theorem is
hidden in this step. The initially recorded weak-approximation dependency
on current claim R_SHEAR is therefore retired with this explicit reason;
it remains a dependency of the separate additive-generation argument.

### Normal extraction and exact intersections

For a normal subgroup P of H_0 containing p=[[a,b],[c,d]] with c!=0,
the four-shear identity constructs diag(u,u^-1) from parameters in I.
Because u is a unit of T, the divided parameters remain in I.
The translation (1-u^2)a/c is also in I. Therefore the affine h in
the draft is an actual element of H_0, not just of its closure.

Multiplication gives q=h^-1 p^-1 h p with diagonal (u^-2,u^2), and

    [q,U(x)] = U((u^-4-1)x) in P.

Normality proves membership. Infinite order of u and x!=0 prove
nonidentity, also in characteristic two. Conjugating the entire argument
by the axis swap obtains the opposite root; the swap itself need not
belong to H_0. These arithmetic-to-membership hypotheses are supplied by
the handwritten proof, not by the compiled matrix identity alone.

The explicit double commutator is exactly identity on block j. At a
surviving block, substituting y=x^2 in its entry formula leaves the
nonzero cubic ad x^3-ad x^2-ad x+bc and the possible exceptions x=1,-1.
Thus at most five phases are excluded (at most four in characteristic two).
The word has length at most 40 when boosts and diagonal phases each count
as one letter. This is a bound for one killed-block witness only.

Each full kernel K_j is normal in H; its projected image is normal in H_i
and has the required nonzero lower-left entry. To combine kernels, lift
U(v) from the current intersection and L(w) from the next kernel.
Their commutator belongs to the exact intersection, and its i-entry
has lower-left coefficient v w^2 != 0. Normal extraction reapplies.
This finite induction proves support at only i. No inference from
Zariski density to abstract membership occurs.
