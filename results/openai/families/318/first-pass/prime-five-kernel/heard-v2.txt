                                                                                   1. Introduction

                                           Let En be the n-th Morava E-theory (always considered at a prime p, which is suppressed
                                           from the notation) with coefficient ring
                                                                        π∗ (En ) = W(Fpn )[u1 , . . . , un−1 ][[u±1 ]]
                                           Here |ui | = 0, |u| = −2 and W(Fpn ) is the ring of Witt vectors over the finite field Fpn . By
                                           work of Goerss–Hopkins–Miller [Rez98, GH04], the spectrum En has an E∞ -action by the
                                           Morava stabilizer group, denoted Gn . These theories are complex oriented, and support
                                           formal group laws of height n.
                                           Work of Devinatz and Hopkins, Davis and others [DH04, Dav06a] shows that, for a closed
                                           subgroups K ⊂ Gn , it is possible to define a homotopy fixed point spectrum EnhK (or at
                                           least a spectrum that behaves like a homotopy fixed point spectrum), although we will only
                                           ever consider finite subgroups, for which the usual homotopy fixed point construction works
                                           (and agrees with the more complicated constructions of Devinatz–Hopkins and Davis).
                                           We will work at height n = p − 1 as here Gn has infinite cohomological dimension and
                                           has interesting maximal finite p-subgroups. Indeed, the maximal finite subgroups (of the
                                           closely related group Sn , as well as Gn ) have been classified completely by Hewett and
                                           Bujard [Hew99, Buj12]. At height n = p − 1, up to conjugation, there is a unique maximal
                                           finite subgroup G whose order is divisible by p. We note that if n is is not divisible by
                                           (p − 1), then all maximal finite subgroups have order coprime to p (see [Hew99, Theorem
                                           1.3] or [Buj12, Proposition 1.7]).
                                           Recall that for finite subgroups we can form the homotopy orbit spectrum (En )hK as

                                           norm map N : (En )hK → (En )hK relating them (see Section 3). Define EntK , the Tate
                                           spectrum, to be the cofiber of the norm map. Our main result concerns the Tate spectrum

                2. Finite subgroups of the Morava stabilizer group

Let Γn denote the height n Honda formal group law over Fpn with p-series [p](x) = xp . We
let Sn := Aut(Γn ) denote the n-th Morava stabilizer group. By Lubin-Tate theory [LT66]
there is a ring (En )0 = W(Fpn )[[u1 , . . . , un−1 ]] with an action of Sn , where W(Fpn ) denotes
the Witt vectors, whose associated formal group law is the universal deformation of the
Honda formal group law. By the Landweber exact functor theorem there is a cohomology
theory with coefficient ring (En )∗ = (En )0 [u±1 ], where |u| = −2, and the action of Sn
extends to an action on (En )∗ . We let En denote the representing spectrum. The Honda
formal group law is defined over the field Fpn , and so we can consider the group Gn =
Sn o Gal(Fpn /Fp ), known as the extended Morava stabilizer group. This also acts on
(En )∗ , and hence En , and furthermore the Goerss–Hopkins–Miller theorem [Rez98, GH04]
proves that this action can be lifted to a spectrum level action via E∞ -ring maps.
There is an alternative description of ring Sn . Write W for the Witt vectors over Fpn and
define a ring
                                   On = WhSi/(S n − p),
where the angled brackets refer to adjoining a non-commuting power series variable S
subject to the condition that
                                      Sa = aφ S,
for a ∈ W and aφ : W → W is the lift of Frobenius to the Witt vectors. The algebra On is
a free rank n module over W with generators 1, S, . . . , S n−1 , and is the ring of integers in a
division algebra Dn over Qp of dimension n2 and Hasse invariant 1/n. With this notation,
Sn = On× .
We will be interested in the maximal finite p-subgroups (up to conjugacy) of Gn at height
n = p−1; as mentioned previously, this is an important case due to the presence of p-torsion
in Gn , which implies that the cohomological dimension of Gn is infinite. These sometimes
go by the name of the higher real K-theories (although we refrain from using this), and are
sometimes denoted EOn , for reasons that the following simple example explains.
Example 2.1. Let n = 1 and p = 2. For n = 1, the Honda formal group law over F2 is
isomorphic to the multiplicative formal group law:

We start by reviewing some preliminaries on equivariant stable homotopy theory; for more
details we direct the reader to the usual references such as [LMSM86]. As usual, if V is
a representation of a group G, we let S V denote the one-point compactification of Rn as
a G-space. We then let ΣV (−) := S V ∧ − and Ωv (−) := F (S V , −) be the suspension and
loop space functors respectively.
Definition 3.1. A G-universe U is a countably infinite real inner product space with an
action of G through linear isometries such that:
    (1) U is the sum of countably many copies of a set of representations of G; and,
    (2) U contains the trivial representation.

If U contains a copy of every irreducible representation of G, then U is called complete;
conversely, it is called trivial if it only contains the trivial representations.
