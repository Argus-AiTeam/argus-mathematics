# Sharp lower-central supported-fraction corrections

## Theorem

Let s >= 2, let K be any group, let N be a normal subgroup of K, and
let M be a submonoid of K^s. Suppose that M contains every diagonal and
that every projection M_ij, i != j, is a group containing N x N.
Write e_i(g) for the tuple supported at i, and put

    L_i = {g in K : e_i(g) = uv^-1 for some actual u,v in M}.
    gamma_1(N) = N,   gamma_(k+1)(N) = [gamma_k(N),N].

Then gamma_(s-1)(N) <= L_i for every i. The index s-1 cannot be replaced
by s-2 for any s >= 3, even when N=K and all pair projections are K x K.

The inclusion also holds without ambient normality of N. Normality is
retained in the main statement for the endpoint-cost application.

This is a theorem about actual fractions, not just the subgroup generated
by M. The subgroup special case and optimal lower-central index are
known; see `literature/comparison.md`. No manuscript assertion is refuted.

The hypotheses really allow monoids that are not groups. For example,
in additive K=Q and any s>=3, take the half-space

    M = {x : x_0+...+x_(s-2)-(s-1)x_(s-1) >= 0}.

All coefficients are nonzero and sum to zero. Thus every diagonal is
in M, and any prescribed pair extends to the boundary hyperplane by
solving for one unused coordinate. All pair projections are Q^2, but M
is not closed under inversion. This elementary example is not claimed
as new or as a non-Ore example; it merely separates the hypotheses from
the subgroup hypothesis in the cited theorem.

## Proof of inclusion

We reuse the accepted supported-fraction rules in
`../endpoint_bridge/Main.lean`, without altering their statements or
source. In particular L_i is a normal subgroup, and a product of an
arbitrary right fraction with a pair-supported right fraction is again
a right fraction. The latter rule uses an inverse in the corresponding
pair projection to construct a denominator; it is not an Ore assumption.

One additional elementary operation is useful. If z=uv^-1 and x belongs
to M, then

    x z x^-1 = (xu)(xv)^-1.

Thus conjugation by an actual positive tuple preserves representability.
This operation is not conjugation by an arbitrary element of <M>.

Induct on s. At s=2 the pair projection is M itself, so N x N <= M.
For s>=3 fix i and choose j!=i. Projection away from j preserves all
the hypotheses. Given a in gamma_(s-2)(N), the inductive conclusion
in that projection supplies numerator and denominator lifts in M whose
quotient z is supported on {i,j}, with z_i=a. Given b in N, the pair
containment hypothesis supplies x in M with x_i=b and x_j=1.

Both z and x z^-1 x^-1 are pair-supported actual fractions. Their product
is therefore an actual fraction by the accepted denominator rule.
Coordinate by coordinate,

    z (x z^-1 x^-1) = e_i([a,b]).

At coordinate j this uses x_j=1; off {i,j} it uses z=1. No factors are
commuted at coordinate i. Consequently [a,b] belongs to L_i for every
a in gamma_(s-2)(N) and b in N. Since L_i is a subgroup, it contains
the subgroup generated by these commutators, gamma_(s-1)(N).
This proves the universal inclusion.

The difference from the manuscript's symmetric induction is that the
second factor only needs b in N: it is an actual positive tuple killing
coordinate j, not a second inductively produced fraction.

## Sharpness for every s>=3

Set c=s-2 and n=c+1=s-1. Let K=UT_n(Q), the group of upper unitriangular
n by n rational matrices, and take N=K. Let J be the rational algebra of
strictly upper triangular polynomial matrices X(t) such that

    degree X_pq(t) <= q-p       (0 <= p < q < n).

Zero entries satisfy every degree bound. This algebra is closed under
multiplication: every term X_pk Y_kq has degree at most
(k-p)+(q-k)=q-p. Any product of n strictly upper triangular matrices is
zero, since a nonzero matrix-unit product requires an increasing chain
of n+1 indices. Hence 1+J is a group, with

    (1+X)^-1 = 1-X+X^2-...+(-X)^(n-1).

Let t_j=j for j=0,...,s-1, and define the subgroup

    M = {(U(t_0),...,U(t_(s-1))) : U in 1+J} <= K^s.

The following arguments hold for arbitrary c, not only tested values.

**Diagonals.** Every constant unitriangular matrix belongs to 1+J.
Its evaluation is the desired diagonal.

**All pair projections.** Given distinct nodes alpha,beta and arbitrary
A,B in K, choose the matrix U(t) with diagonal 1 and entries

    U_pq(t) = ((beta-t) A_pq + (t-alpha) B_pq)/(beta-alpha).

Their degrees are at most 1<=q-p, so U belongs to 1+J and evaluates to
A and B at alpha and beta. Thus every pair projection is exactly K x K,
in particular a group containing N x N. This is entrywise interpolation;
it does not assert that a matrix-valued affine combination is a
homomorphism in A or B.

**Exact supported-fraction membership.** Since M is a subgroup,
MM^-1=M. Suppose e_i(g) is in M. Every off-diagonal entry of its
polynomial preimage vanishes at the s-1=c+1 other nodes, but has degree
at most q-p<=n-1=c. A nonzero polynomial of degree at most c has at most
c distinct roots: successive division by t-t_j proves this elementary
fact by induction on its degree. Thus every entry is zero, g=1, and
L_i={1}. Conversely identity is the actual fraction 1*1^-1. This checks
both directions of supported-fraction membership.

**The preceding lower-central term is nontrivial.** Put
x_k=1+E_(k-1,k), k=1,...,c. Set z_1=x_1 and
z_k=[z_(k-1),x_k] for k>=2. Matrix-unit multiplication gives

    [1+a E_pq, 1+b E_qr] = 1+ab E_pr       (p<q<r).

Indeed E_pq^2=E_qr^2=E_qr E_pq=0 and E_pq E_qr=E_pr, whose further
products with either matrix unit vanish. Multiplying the four factors
therefore proves the identity directly. Induction now gives
z_k=1+E_(0,k), so

    1 != z_c=1+E_(0,c) in gamma_c(K)=gamma_(s-2)(K).

For c=1 this is simply the nonidentity generator of UT_2(Q), and no
commutator step is needed. Its supported tuple is not a fraction at any
coordinate. For completeness, if F_r denotes strictly upper triangular
matrices vanishing on superdiagonals below r, then F_p F_q <= F_(p+q).
In the quotient by F_(p+q), elements 1+F_p and 1+F_q commute.
It follows inductively that gamma_r(K)<=1+F_r and gamma_(c+1)(K)=1.
The universal bound is therefore attained exactly.

This proves sharpness parametrically for every integer s>=3. It does
not claim a geometric copy-monoid realization or optimality of the
derived-depth bound in every particular group.

## Reduced averaging depth

The commutator weight identity

    [gamma_p(N),gamma_q(N)] <= gamma_(p+q)(N)

has a short proof, also formalized in `Main.lean`. Induct on q, for all p
at once. The case q=1 is the definition. For q+1, quotient by the normal
subgroup T=gamma_(p+q+1)(N). In this quotient the inductive hypothesis
makes both [[N,gamma_p],gamma_q] and [[gamma_p,gamma_q],N] trivial.
The three-subgroups lemma (Hall-Witt identity) then makes
[[gamma_q,N],gamma_p] trivial as well. Symmetry of the subgroup
commutator proves the desired bound.

Therefore the derived series satisfies

    N^(r) <= gamma_(2^r)(N)

by induction on r. The lower central series is descending. If
2^r>=s-1, then N^(r)<=gamma_(s-1)(N)<=L_i for every i.
The accepted minimum supported costs c_i are consequently finite on
N^(r); their maximum c is an invariant, subadditive cost there.

The accepted `simultaneous_averaging` theorem is already parametrized by
an arbitrary derived depth. Instantiate it at this r and at that maximum
cost. It yields an actual positive-degree permutation expression w of
degree m>0 whose differences on every orbit belong to N^(r), with cost
at most delta*m. The accepted tuple-fraction rule then constructs
positive corrections u,v of degrees a,b satisfying

    (w at f) u = (w at g) v,
    a+b <= s*delta*m.

The weights are exactly lambda=m+a>0 and mu=q*m+b>=0. For every q>0,

    mu/lambda <= q+s*delta.

The discrepancy used is (w at f)^-1 (w at g); conversion from the
opposite-orientation differences uses inversion and conjugation, not
commutativity. `endpoint_bridge_depth` carries the simultaneous
N^(r) membership and cost bound in its conclusion, in addition to the
positive-path and weight conclusions.

The least universally sufficient r from this argument is
ceil(log_2(s-1)), rather than the manuscript's s-2. The reduction is
strict for every s>=5. It is not a new verification of the geometric
pair-projection lemma, the two-scale construction, or the full Ramsey
classification.
