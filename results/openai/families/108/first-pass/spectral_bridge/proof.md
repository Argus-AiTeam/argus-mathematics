# A rectangular codimension bound for determinant coefficients

## Result and scope

**Theorem.** Let \(K\) be an algebraically closed field of any characteristic,
\(k\geq2\), and \(D=\operatorname{diag}(\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_k)\), with
pairwise distinct \(\theta_i\). For integers \(a,b,c\geq0\) with
\(a+b+c=k-1\), put
\[
 H_{a,b}(X)=[s^at^b]\det(X+sI+tD).
\]
Then
\[
 \boxed{\quad
 \dim V(\nabla H_{a,b})\leq k^2+k-(a+1)(b+1).
 \quad}                                                     \tag{1}
\]
If the right side is negative, the assertion means that the locus is empty.
The gradient locus does not include a separate equation \(H_{a,b}=0\).
For a finite scheme over a nonclosed field, "length" below means its
degree, equivalently the dimension of its coordinate algebra over that field.

In characteristic two, permanent and determinant coincide. Taking
\(a=b=q=\lfloor(k-1)/3\rfloor\) proves the live manuscript proposition and
strengthens its bound from
\[
 k^2+k-\binom{q+2}{2}\quad\hbox{to}\quad k^2+k-(q+1)^2.
                                                               \tag{2}
\]
The improvement is \(q(q+1)/2\), positive for \(q>0\). For \(k=128\), the
two bounds are respectively \(15566\) and \(14663\). No smoothness,
irreducibility, nodality, ordinary-ramification, or reduced-finite-scheme
hypothesis is added.

The proof below also gives an exact formula for the full-adjugate length
and a scheme-level, constructive row-scheme correspondence. No false
manuscript assertion has been established by this work. This is not a
verification of the manuscript's selector, characteristic-transfer, or
border-polar arguments, nor a refutation of its main theorem.

The argument for (1) is a mathematical proof, subject to independent review.
Lean checks the universal coefficient-recovery core, not the algebraic
geometry in this document. The rerunnable exact calculations are examples
and polynomial-identity certificates, not a proof by finite testing.

## 1. The distinguished boundary and the full adjugate

Write \(d=k-1\), \(M=sI+tD+uX\), \(F=\det M\), and \(C=V(F)\).
The restriction
\[
 F(s,t,0)=\prod_i(s+\theta_i t)                              \tag{3}
\]
has distinct roots. No component is contained in \(u=0\). A repeated
homogeneous factor of \(F\) would give a repeated positive-degree factor
of (3), so \(C\) is reduced. Each component meets \(u=0\). At
\(P_i=[-\theta_i:1:0]\), the matrix has rank \(k-1\), and the \(i\)-th
diagonal cofactor is nonzero.

The projection \(\pi:C\to\mathbf P^1_{t:u}\) is everywhere defined,
finite flat of degree \(k\), and generically etale. Indeed its two affine
algebras are monic degree-\(k\) polynomial algebras in the \(s\)-coordinate;
the distinct-root fiber proves generic separability. The monic bases
give
\[
 \pi_*\mathcal O_C=\bigoplus_{j=0}^{k-1}\mathcal O(-j),\qquad
 E:=\pi_*\mathcal O_C(d)=\bigoplus_{j=0}^{k-1}\mathcal O(d-j).
                                                               \tag{4}
\]
Also \(\pi^*\mathcal O(1)=\mathcal O_C(1)\).

Let \(J\subset\mathcal O_C\) be generated by all cofactors, with their
degree-\(d\) twists, and let \(Z=V_C(J)\). It is finite: no component
of \(C\) lies in its support. More importantly,
\[
                         Z\cap\{u=0\}=\varnothing.            \tag{5}
\]
This last fact, stronger than finiteness, is essential to the improvement.
It concerns the entire scheme, since a finite scheme with no support
on a divisor is contained in its complement.

Here is the uniform generation assertion, including its numerical form:
\[
 \begin{split}
 (I_Z)_d&=\operatorname{span}_K\{(\operatorname{adj}M)_{ij}\},\\
 H^1(\mathbf P^2,I_Z(d))&=0,\\
 \operatorname{length}Z
   &=\binom{k+1}{2}
        -\operatorname{rank}_K\bigl(\text{cofactor coefficient vectors}\bigr).
 \end{split}                                                   \tag{6}
\]
In particular (6) does not replace \(Z\) by its reduced support.

For completeness, use the maps on \(\mathbf P^1\)
\[
 \psi:\mathcal O^{k^2}\longrightarrow E,\quad
       Y\longmapsto\operatorname{tr}(Y\operatorname{adj}M),
 \qquad
 \chi:\mathcal O(-1)^{k^2}\longrightarrow\mathcal O^{k^2},\quad
       Y\longmapsto[tD+uX,Y].
 \]
On a monic affine chart, the base-ring span of the adjugate entries is
stable under multiplication by the affine \(s\)-coordinate, by
\(s\operatorname{adj}M=-(tD+uX)\operatorname{adj}M\).
It is therefore their ideal in the finite curve algebra. Consequently
\(\operatorname{im}\psi=\pi_*J(d)\).

Put \(A=\operatorname{im}\chi\), \(B=\ker\psi\).
Trace cyclicity gives \(A\subset B\). At \([t:u]=[1:0]\), the commutator
has rank \(k^2-k\); the diagonal cofactors
\[
 h_i(y)=\prod_{j\ne i}(y+\theta_j)
 \]
form a basis in \(K[y]/\prod_j(y+\theta_j)\), by evaluation at the roots.
Thus \(\psi\) has generic rank \(k\) and \(B/A\) is torsion.
From \(\mathcal O(-1)^{k^2}\twoheadrightarrow A\) one gets \(H^1(A)=0\):
\(H^1(\mathcal O(-1))=0\) and coherent \(H^2\) on \(\mathbf P^1\)
vanishes. Torsion has no \(H^1\), so \(H^1(B)=0\).
It follows that constants surject onto \(H^0(\pi_*J(d))\).
Two degree-\(d\) forms with the same restriction to \(C\) are equal,
since \(d<\deg F\). This proves the first assertion of (6).

Also \(H^1(\operatorname{im}\psi)=0\), using its presentation as a
quotient of \(\mathcal O^{k^2}\) and the vanishing of \(H^2(B)\).
In the plane sequence
\[
 0\longrightarrow\mathcal O_{\mathbf P^2}(-1)
  \longrightarrow I_Z(d)\longrightarrow J(d)\longrightarrow0
 \]
both \(H^1\) and \(H^2\) of \(\mathcal O(-1)\) vanish. This proves the
second assertion of (6), and the restriction sequence to \(Z\) proves
the rank formula. The cohomology vanishings used here are the elementary
two-chart computation on \(\mathbf P^1\) and the usual three-chart
computation for \(\mathcal O_{\mathbf P^2}(-1)\).

## 2. Rectangles, not just a single homogeneous triangle

**Affine coefficient lemma.** Let \(W\subset\mathbf P^2_K\) be a finite
scheme contained in \(u\ne0\), over any field \(K\).
Suppose \(\Lambda=[s^at^bu^c]\), \(a,b,c\geq0\), vanishes on
\((I_W)_{a+b+c}\). Then the functions
\[
 \{(s/u)^i(t/u)^j:0\leq i\leq a,\ 0\leq j\leq b\}             \tag{7}
\]
are linearly independent in \(H^0(W,\mathcal O_W)\).
In particular \(\operatorname{length}W\geq(a+1)(b+1)\).

**Proof.** Suppose a nonzero polynomial
\(p(x,y)=\sum p_{ij}x^iy^j\), supported in that rectangle, restricts to
zero on \(W\). Let \(m=\deg p\), and choose a nonzero coefficient
\(p_{ij}\) with \(i+j=m\). Homogenize to its actual degree:
\[
 P(s,t,u)=\sum p_{vw}s^vt^wu^{m-v-w}\in(I_W)_m.
 \]
This vanishing is valid scheme-theoretically: \(u\) is a unit on \(W\).
The exponents in \(s^{a-i}t^{b-j}u^c\) are nonnegative, and its product
with \(P\) has degree \(a+b+c\). Exactly one term contributes:
\[
 \Lambda\bigl(s^{a-i}t^{b-j}u^cP\bigr)=p_{ij}\ne0.
 \]
This is the required contradiction. No differentiation, factorial,
division by a degree, or reducedness of \(W\) was used. \(\square\)

The bound is sharp as a statement about finite schemes: the homogeneous
ideal \((s^{a+1},t^{b+1})\) defines a scheme supported at \([0:0:1]\)
of length \((a+1)(b+1)\), and its degree-\((a+b+c)\) part is killed by
\(\Lambda\). This does **not** assert that a spectral adjugate scheme
attaining equality exists for every triple.

This lemma is related to the monomial cactus-rank bound of Ranestad and
Schreyer, *On the rank of a symmetric form*, arXiv:1104.3648v1,
Proposition 1 and Corollary 2. That result and its technique are prior
art, not claimed as new. Its ordinary-differential interpretation is
not imported into characteristic two. The proof just given is a direct
coefficient-functional proof and additionally uses the distinguished
affine chart, so it requires no ordering of \(a,b,c\).

At a critical matrix, determinant differentiation gives
\[
 \partial F/\partial X_{ij}=u(\operatorname{adj}M)_{ji},\qquad
 \nabla H_{a,b}(X)=0
 \ \Longleftrightarrow\
 [s^at^bu^c](\operatorname{adj}M)=0.                            \tag{8}
\]
By (6), the functional kills \((I_Z)_d\). By (5) and the lemma,
\[
                    \operatorname{length}Z\geq(a+1)(b+1).    \tag{9}
\]

## 3. Conductor comparison, including wild ramification

We give the local justification rather than importing a characteristic-zero
spectral-divisor statement. At a base point let \(S\) be its discrete
valuation ring, \(Q=\operatorname{Frac}S\), \(B=S[y]/f\), and
\(f=\det(yI+A_0)\), generically separable. Its normalization \(R\)
in \(B_Q\) is finite. One elementary proof of finiteness embeds it in
the trace-dual lattice of \(B\): an integral element times \(y^j\) is
integral, so its trace is in \(S\), and separability makes the trace
pairing nondegenerate. A submodule of that finite \(S\)-lattice is finite.

Let \(L=\operatorname{coker}(yI+A_0)\). Division by the monic matrix
identifies it with \(S^k\). Generically it has rank one on every
separable field factor. Its evaluation ideal
\(\operatorname{im}(\operatorname{Hom}_B(L,B)\otimes L\to B)\)
is exactly the cofactor ideal: a lifted annihilating row \(b\) satisfies
\(bM=fb'\), whence \(fb=fb'\operatorname{adj}M\), and cancellation
is valid in the domain \(S[y]\).

Embed \(L\) as a lattice in \(B_Q\). The fractional ideal \(RL\) is
invertible over the semilocal product of normal one-dimensional rings
\(R\). A general constant \(K\)-linear combination of generators of
\(L\) generates \(RL\) at every maximal ideal: there are finitely many
proper linear subspaces of forbidden coefficients and \(K\) is infinite.
Rescaling gives an isomorphic lattice \(B\subset L'\subset R\).
Every conductor element multiplies \(L'\) into \(B\), and its evaluation
at \(1\) is itself. Hence the conductor \(\mathfrak c\) is contained in
the cofactor evaluation ideal \(J_0\).

Finally \(B\) is self-dual over \(S\) for the pairing
\(\lambda(xy)\), where \(\lambda\) selects the \(y^{k-1}\) coefficient
of the monic remainder. Reversing the columns of its pairing matrix
makes it triangular with diagonal \(1\), in every characteristic.
Under this self-duality the restriction \(R^\vee\to B^\vee\) has image
\(\mathfrak c\): by adjunction it consists exactly of multipliers
taking \(R\) into \(B\). The inclusion \(B\subset R\) and the restriction
on dual lattices have identical elementary divisors. Thus
\[
 \operatorname{length}_S(B/J_0)\leq
 \operatorname{length}_S(B/\mathfrak c)
 =\operatorname{length}_S(R/B).
 \]
Summing on the base gives
\[
                         \operatorname{length}Z\leq\delta(C). \tag{10}
\]
No assertion about reduced closed fibers or tame ramification entered
this argument. Length over the base DVR equals residue-field dimension,
not a ramification-index-weighted length.

## 4. The row construction on the entire Hilbert functor

Fix \(F\) satisfying (3), and set \(N=k(k-1)/2\). This section works over
any field containing the distinct \(\theta_i\).
Let \(\mathcal F_F\) be the affine scheme defined by equality of all
coefficients of \(\det(sI+tD+uX)\) and \(F\).
Inside \(\operatorname{Hilb}^N(C)\) let \(U_F\) be the open locus
\[
 Z'\cap\{u=0\}=\varnothing,\qquad
 H^0(\mathbf P^1,\pi_*I_{Z'}(d)(-1))
 =H^1(\mathbf P^1,\pi_*I_{Z'}(d)(-1))=0.                       \tag{11}
\]
The second condition is equivalent to \(\pi_*I_{Z'}(d)\simeq\mathcal O^k\).
Indeed this bundle has degree zero; the exact sequence at
\(b_0=[1:0]\) shows evaluation is an isomorphism on global sections,
and its determinant is a nonzero constant section of a degree-zero line
bundle. Openness follows from semicontinuity and the degree-zero
Euler characteristic. Disjointness is open because the universal
finite subscheme is proper.

There is a natural scheme isomorphism
\[
             \boxed{\ \mathcal F_F \simeq U_F.\ }             \tag{12}
\]
In particular, this is more than injectivity on geometric points.

**Forward map.** For \(X\) take the ideal generated by the row
\(r=(1,\ldots,1)\operatorname{adj}M\).
Its base span \(\rho:\mathcal O^k\to E\) is stable under \(s\), since
\(sr=-r(tD+uX)\), so it is precisely \(\pi_*J'(d)\).
At \(b_0\), its columns are the fixed basis \(h_i\).
It is injective and its torsion cokernel has length
\(\deg E=N\); hence its row scheme has length \(N\) and satisfies (11).

This construction also works for a locally Noetherian parameter scheme
\(S_0\), including a nonreduced one. The two bundles in \(\rho\) are
flat over \(S_0\), and the map is injective on each geometric curve
fiber. The local flatness criterion gives an injective map with
\(S_0\)-flat cokernel. Its support is proper and fiberwise finite,
hence finite over \(S_0\); its degree is constantly \(N\).
Stability under \(s\) identifies it with
\(\pi_*\mathcal O_{Z'}(d)\) even over this parameter scheme.
Thus the row scheme is a Hilbert family, not just a pointwise assignment.

**Inverse map.** For a family in (11), write
\(B'=\pi_*I_{Z'}(d)\subset E\). Fiberwise local freeness and flatness
give a vector bundle; cohomology and base change make the evaluation
map \(p^*p_*B'\to B'\) an isomorphism. The disjoint boundary identifies
its fiber at \(b_0\) with that of \(E\). Choose its **unique** global
frame \(p_i\) whose boundary values are \(h_i\).
Each section lifts uniquely to a homogeneous degree-\(d\) polynomial:
the kernel and higher obstruction are the zero cohomology groups of
\(\mathcal O_{\mathbf P^2}(-1)\), also after base change.

Multiplication by \(s\) preserves the ideal, giving
\[
                       sp=p(-tD-uX)                         \tag{13}
\]
on \(C\), for a unique constant matrix \(X\) over the parameter ring.
The \(t\)-coefficient is \(-D\), by the prescribed boundary frame.
To check the determinant **over a nonreduced parameter ring**, not
just at its geometric points, let \(P:B'\hookrightarrow E\) be the
inclusion in these frames. On \(t=1\), its determinant has invertible
constant coefficient at \(u=0\), so it is a non-zero-divisor in the
polynomial ring over the parameter ring (use the least nonzero
coefficient of a hypothetical annihilator). The two multiplication
matrices are intertwined by \(P\); taking determinants and cancelling
\(\det P\) proves
\(\det(sI+tD+uX)=F\). This cancellation does not appeal to reducedness
of the parameter scheme.

The polynomial row \(pM\) vanishes on \(C\), so \(pM=Fw\) for a
constant row \(w\). Each \(p_i\) has leading \(s^d\)-coefficient \(1\),
by its boundary value. Hence \(w=(1,\ldots,1)\).
Multiply by \(\operatorname{adj}M\) and cancel the monic polynomial
\(F\); this gives \(p=(1,\ldots,1)\operatorname{adj}M\).
The frame, the ideal, and \(X\) are therefore recovered exactly.
All operations commute with base change. These natural inverses prove
(12), including infinitesimal families; finite presentation extends
the description from locally Noetherian tests if desired.

Spectral sheaves, the boundary-normalized basis, and multiplication
reconstruction are classical, not newly invented here. Relevant prior
statements are Beauville, *Jacobiennes des courbes spectrales et systemes
hamiltoniens completement integrables* (1990), Theorem 1.4 and
Remark 1.10 (smooth, and integral singular complex curves), and
Adams--Harnad--Hurtubise, arXiv:hep-th/9210089v1, Section 1b,
(1.29), and Section 2a, (2.5)--(2.7), Theorem 2.3 (generic complex
spectral data). The bounded source check did not locate precisely
(12) with all its uniform hypotheses. This is not a claim that no
such theorem exists elsewhere.

## 5. Dimension count

For a plane curve \(C\), \(\dim\operatorname{Hilb}^N(C)\leq N\).
Here is a characteristic-independent upper bound. A length-\(\ell\)
punctual plane quotient, together with a frame, gives two commuting
nilpotent \(\ell\)-by-\(\ell\) multiplication matrices and a cyclic
unit vector. For a fixed Jordan type of the first matrix, let its
centralizer dimension be \(j\). Its orbit has dimension \(\ell^2-j\).
The nilpotent second matrices in that centralizer have dimension at
most \(j-1\): determinant vanishes there, but not at the identity of
the same linear centralizer. Adding the vector and subtracting the
frame dimension \(\ell^2\) gives the punctual bound \(\ell-1\).
The incidence with \(r\) distinct moving support points on \(C\) has
dimension at most \(r+\sum(\ell_i-1)=N\).
Finitely many length types cover the Hilbert scheme on geometric points.
This argument works at singular points as well.
By (12), every spectral matrix fiber has dimension at most \(N\).

The one substantive external dimension theorem used is Christ--He--Tyomkin,
doi:10.1007/s10240-022-00135-x, Definition 2.1, Lemma 2.6,
Proposition 2.7 and Remark 2.8: integral degree-\(b\) plane curves of
normalization genus \(g\) form a locally closed locus of dimension
\(3b+g-1\), over any algebraically closed field. Nodality is not a
hypothesis. The exact hypotheses and primary-source excerpt are in
`literature/CHT_excerpt.txt`; the complete retrieved source is retained.

For a reduced degree-\(k\) curve with \(r\) integral components of
degrees \(b_i\) and normalization genera \(g_i\),
\[
 \delta(C)=\frac{(k-1)(k-2)}2-\sum_i g_i+r-1.
 \]
For each fixed component type, multiplication of distinct component
equations gives a constructible image of dimension at most
\[
 \sum_i(3b_i+g_i-1)=\frac{k(k+3)}2-\delta(C).
 \]
There are finitely many component degree/genus types. Thus reduced curves
with \(\delta(C)\geq L\) form a constructible locus of dimension at most
\(k(k+3)/2-L\), including disconnected normalizations.

The polynomial \(F_X\) is fixed, not merely up to scale, by its curve
since its \(s^k\)-coefficient is \(1\). Equations (9) and (10) place
the image of the critical matrix locus among the curves with
\(\delta\geq(a+1)(b+1)\).
The fiber dimension bound therefore gives
\[
 \dim V(\nabla H_{a,b})
 \leq \frac{k(k+3)}2-(a+1)(b+1)+\frac{k(k-1)}2,
 \]
which is (1).
