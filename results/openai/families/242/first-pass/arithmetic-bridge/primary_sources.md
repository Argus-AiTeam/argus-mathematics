## Uniform canonical height

Joseph H. Silverman, *Canonical Heights on Abelian Varieties*, Arizona
Winter School 2024, author-primary PDF:
<https://swc-math.github.io/aws/2024/2024SilvermanNotes.pdf>.

The source reader opened Theorem 5.2(a)--(c), page 23 (existence of the
limit, uniform bounded difference, multiplication), Theorem 5.4(a) and
(b-1), pages 25--26 (quadraticity and vanishing exactly at torsion over a
number field for an ample symmetric divisor), and Theorem 4.8(b)--(c),
page 15, with Definitions 4.4--4.5, page 13 (height normalization).
The relevant short passage says "There is a constant C(A,D)" and
"for all P in A(bar K)".

For the elliptic x-map, the pullback of infinity is the divisor 2(O).
Its height is therefore h_x+O(1). The canonical height for this divisor
is lim 4^(-N) h_x([2^N]Q), twice the manuscript's canonical height.
Consequently one constant c_E, independent of Q, bounds
|h_x(Q)-2 hhat(Q)|. Quadraticity and positivity for nontorsion rational
points have the needed hypotheses. No normalization or uniformity
counterexample was found.

The reader opened the publisher's second-edition Chapter VIII page
<https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-0-387-09494-6_8>,
but the actual theorem pages were not accessible. The literal book
locators VIII Proposition 9.1 and Theorem 9.3 remain unverified. The
author-primary alternative above supports the mathematical proposition,
not the uninspected numbering.

## Earlier paired-Pell construction

Cantone, Cuzziol, Omodeo, *On Diophantine Singlefold Specifications*,
Le Matematiche 79(2) (2024), DOI 10.4418/2024.79.2.18:
<https://lematematiche.dmi.unict.it/index.php/lematematiche/article/download/2703/1218>.

The source reader opened Lemma 6.1, page 600, Theorem 4 and its following
warning, page 601, Theorem 5, pages 601--602, Section 7, page 602, and
the growth condition on page 588. Lemma 6.1 fixes particular Pell
witnesses. Theorem 4 allows a general growth relation but says:
"But, regrettably, univocity is not ensured." Theorem 5 instead uses
the specific cutoff v=2^u and a prescribed polynomial choice of u.
Section 7 asks: "Is there a finitefold (or better a singlefold)
Diophantine definition of a=b^c?"

Thus this paper is prior art for the method, not an assumption
establishing the manuscript's universal growth-to-power transfer.
Its open problem does not refute the new argument.
