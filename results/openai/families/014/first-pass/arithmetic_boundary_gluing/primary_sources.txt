Primary mathematical inputs and exact scope

1. David Rydh, Compactification of stacks and extending stackiness
   across the boundary, dated 16 September 2014.
   https://davidrydh.se/papers/compactification-survey20140916.pdf
   Theorem (4.5), printed pp.11-12.
   Read by the primary-gluing-citations source researcher.
   Short exact hypothesis:
   "Let S be the spectrum of a henselian local ring with closed point s.
   Let X be an algebraic stack that is proper over S with finite diagonal."
   Conclusion, in mathematical notation: Fet(X) -> Fet(X_s) is an
   equivalence. Covers here are representable finite etale morphisms.
   This is not an extension theorem for arbitrary covers of an open.
   The dated author preprint supplies no DOI.

2. Max Lieblich and Martin Olsson, Generators and relations for the
   etale fundamental group, Pure Appl. Math. Q. 6(1) (2010), 209-243,
   final published version, doi:10.4310/PAMQ.2010.v6.n1.a6.
   https://www.intlpress.com/site/pub/files/_fulltext/journals/pamq/2010/0006/0001/PAMQ-2010-0006-0001-a006.pdf
   Read by the primary-gluing-citations source researcher.
   Appendix A.2 (p.234): complete DVR, separably closed residue field
   of characteristic p; proper pair with integral geometric fibers and
   relative normal-crossings divisor. The A.7 setup further assumes
   smoothness. The root orders in A.10 are invertible in this DVR.
   Proposition A.10 (pp.238-239): the filtered root-stack restriction
   functors to tame covers of the complement, also on the indicated
   geometric generic and special fibers, "are equivalences of categories."
   This is the proposition used for extending finite tame boundary.
   Theorem A.11 (pp.239-240): for a proper smooth DM stack, closed-fiber
   restriction on all Fet is an equivalence; geometric generic
   restriction on all Fet is fully faithful and on Fet' an equivalence.
   Fet' restricts the degree of the Galois closure; all Fet does not.
   A.12 gives a surjection on full tame groups, not an isomorphism.
   A.13 (p.240) identifies the tame fundamental group with the inverse
   limit of the root-stack fundamental groups.
   In our mixed-characteristic lift only l-power root indices are
   used. Torsor-group orders themselves may be divisible by p.

3. Martin Olsson, (Log) twisted curves, Compositio Math.143 (2007),
   476-494, final published version, doi:10.1112/S0010437X06002442.
   https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/1CD4FDAEF1B71EFE102AC184372255A2/S0010437X06002442a.pdf/div-class-title-log-twisted-curves-div.pdf
   Read by the primary-gluing-citations source researcher.
   Definitions 1.2-1.6 require proper flat tame nodal DM curves and
   invertible stabilizer orders. "Twisted" is balanced here.
   Theorem 1.9 (p.477): the twisted/log-twisted equivalence is
   "compatible with base change S' -> S."
   Theorem 1.10 (p.478): the stack of twisted curves "is a smooth
   Artin stack"; this does not assert that the whole moduli stack
   is proper.
   Remark 1.11: if coarse node smoothing parameters are t_i and
   stabilizer orders r_i, the twisted versal ring is
   R[z_1,...,z_m]/(z_1^{r_1}-t_1,...,z_m^{r_m}-t_m).
   Proposition 2.2(ii) and Definition 2.3 (pp.480-481) give the
   local cyclic quotient node chart; balancing makes the two
   primitive branch characters inverse.
   These statements supply geometry, not arithmetic equivariance.

4. The Stacks Project, curves.tex, lemma-rr (Riemann-Roch) and
   lemma-genus-gorenstein; primary source inspected 5 November 2026:
   https://raw.githubusercontent.com/stacks/stacks-project/master/curves.tex
   For a proper Gorenstein pure-dimensional curve X/k, omega_X is
   invertible, deg(omega_X)=-2 chi(O_X), and
   dim H^i(X,E)=dim H^{1-i}(X,E^vee tensor omega_X) for locally
   free E. On a smooth geometrically connected genus-g curve this
   gives deg omega=2g-2 and H^1(L)=0 for deg L>2g-2.
   This last consequence and the elementary jet exact sequence
   are the precise inputs to Section 4; no guessed tag is cited.

5. The Stacks Project, examples-defos.tex, lemma-glueing-smooth,
   lemma-curve-isolated-lci, and
   lemma-smoothing-proper-curve-isolated-lci; primary source
   inspected 5 November 2026:
   https://raw.githubusercontent.com/stacks/stacks-project/master/examples-defos.tex
   In the gluing situation a smooth intersection makes the map to
   the two affine deformation categories smooth. An lci curve
   smooth away from finitely many points is unobstructed.
   The last named lemma assumes a proper lci scheme over a field,
   dimension at most one, smooth except at finitely many points.
   It gives a flat projective k[[t]]-family with smooth generic
   fiber and the specified special fiber. Its proof explicitly
   uses smoothness of the map from the global deformation category
   to the product of the singular local deformation categories,
   and algebraizes the resulting formal object to a projective
   scheme. We prescribe the local nodal smoothings ab=s in that
   argument. The proof does not assert equality of algebraic local
   rings with the chosen local models before completion.

6. The Stacks Project, coherent.tex,
   lemma-proper-over-affine-cohomology-finite and
   lemma-flat-base-change-cohomology; primary source inspected
   5 November 2026:
   https://raw.githubusercontent.com/stacks/stacks-project/master/coherent.tex
   The former assumes a proper scheme over a noetherian affine
   base and a coherent sheaf, and gives finite cohomology modules.
   The latter assumes a quasi-coherent sheaf, a quasi-compact
   quasi-separated morphism, and flat base change; it gives
   H^i(X,F) tensor_A B = H^i(X_B,F_B) over affine bases.
   These statements justify the finite-module and generic-field
   extension steps in the component-partition proof.
