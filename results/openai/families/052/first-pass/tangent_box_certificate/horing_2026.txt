                                                    FANO VARIETIES WITH SPLIT TANGENT SHEAF


                                                                                ANDREAS HÖRING
arXiv:2602.15427v1 [math.AG] 17 Feb 2026




                                                   Abstract. Let X be a mildly singular Fano variety such that the tangent
                                                   sheaf is a direct sum. We show that the direct factors are algebraically in-
                                                   tegrable, so the infinitesimal decomposition induces a product structure on a
                                                   quasi-étale cover of X.




                                                                               1. Introduction

                                           1.A. Main result. We say that a normal projective variety X has a split tan-
                                           gent sheaf if there are non-zero subsheaves Fi ⊂ TX such that TX = F1 ⊕ F2 .
                                           Varieties with this property appear naturally whenever one aims to prove a decom-
                                           position theorem, e.g. for varieties with nef anticanonical class [CH19, MW21]. In
                                           the study of singular varieties of trivial canonical class [GKP16] and Q-Fanos ad-
                                           mitting a Kähler-Einstein metric [DGP24] the semistability of the tangent bundle
                                           yields decompositions TX ≃ ⊕i∈I Fi where the direct factors are integrable. For an
                                           arbitrary Fano manifold the tangent bundle is not semistable and there are many
                                           examples of distributions that are not integrable. The main result of this paper
                                           shows that the existence of a splitting always guarantees the integrability:
                                           1.1. Theorem. Let X be a normal Q-factorial projective variety with klt singu-
                                           larities such that TX = V1 ⊕ V2 . If X is of Fano type (cf. Definition 2.14), the
                                           distributions Vi are integrable with algebraic leaves.
                                           This result is new even if X is a smooth Fano variety, cp. [CP02, Cor.3.7], however
                                           we need the greater generality of varieties of Fano type for the inductive strategy
                                           of our proof. Applying [DGP24, Thm.C] we deduce:
                                           1.2. Corollary. Let X be a normal projective variety of Fano type such that
                                           TX = V1 ⊕ V2 . Then there exists a finite quasi-étale cover ν : X ′ → X such that
                                           there is a decomposition
                                                                             X ′ ≃ X1 × X2
                                           into a product of normal varieties and the decomposition TX = V1 ⊕ V2 lifts to the
                                           canonical decomposition p∗X1 TX1 ⊕ p∗X2 TX2 .

                                           A Fano manifold is simply connected [Cam92, KMM92], so every quasi-étale cover
                                           is trivial. Thus we obtain:
                                           1.3. Corollary. Let X be a Fano manifold such that TX = V1 ⊕ V2 . Then X is
                                           isomorphic to a product X1 × X2 and the decomposition TX = V1 ⊕ V2 corresponds
                                           to the canonical decomposition p∗X1 TX1 ⊕ p∗X2 TX2 .

1.B. Background and strategy of proof. A well-known conjecture of Beauville
predicts that a splitting of TX , i.e. an infinitesimal decomposition of X, should
always correspond to a product structure on the universal cover:
1.4. Conjecture. [Bea00] Let X be a compact Kähler manifold such that TX =
V1 ⊕V2 , where V1 and V2 are integrable subbundles. Let µ : X̃ → X be the universal
covering of X. Then there is a decomposition
                                   X̃ ≃ X1 × X2
and the decomposition TX = V1 ⊕ V2 lifts to the canonical decomposition p∗X1 TX1 ⊕
p∗X2 TX2 .

This conjecture has been studied by many authors, e.g. [Dru00, BPT06, PT13,
DPPT22]. If X is simply connected (e.g. if it is Fano) it predicts that an inte-
grable splitting always corresponds to a product structure on X. For non-uniruled
manifolds the existence of a splitting implies that the direct factors are integrable
[Hör07, Thm.1.3] while for uniruled manifolds Beauville gave a counter-example
[Bea00, Sect.2.2]. His example is based on the observation that given an isomor-
phism TX ≃ V1 ⊕ V2 the embedding
                                      Vi ,→ TX
is not necessarily unique and for a sufficiently general choice of the embedding the
image is not always integrable. We expect that such counterexamples are never
rationally connected:
1.5. Conjecture. Let X be a projective manifold such that TX = V1 ⊕ V2 . Assume
that X is rationally connected. Then V1 and V2 are integrable.

In fact [Hör07, Thm.1.4] shows that the integrability of one direct factor implies
that both distributions are integrable with algebraic leaves. In a similar spirit a
conjecture of Touzet [Dru17, Conj.1.2] claims that a regular foliation on a rationally
connected manifold always has algebraic leaves.
A natural strategy for Conjecture 1.5 is to run a MMP X 99K X ′ so that we get a
Mori fibre space
                                   φ : X ′ → Y.
We have an induced decomposition TX ′ = V1′ ⊕ V2′ and we show in Proposition 3.7
there is an induced decomposition
                                  TY = W1 ⊕ W2 .
Since Y is also rationally connected it seems natural to proceed by induction on the
dimension by showing an analogue of Conjecture 1.5 for varieties with klt singular-
ities. Unfortunately such an analogue can’t be true as shown by Example 4.3. This
difference can be explained as follows: for a singular rationally connected variety
X and a quotient sheaf
                                       TX → Q
the determinant of the dual det Q∗ can be pseudoeffective, this is impossible in
the smooth case. In the case of a splitting TX = V1 ⊕ V2 the pseudoeffectivity of
det Vi∗ implies the integrability of V3−i [CH24, Thm.2], but we learn nothing about
Vi itself.
                                          2
If we assume X to be of Fano type, the situation is much better: by work of Peternell
[Pet12] and Ou [Ou23] we know that det Vi∗ can’t be pseudoeffective. Moreover Q-
factorial varieties of Fano type are Mori dream spaces so we can run a MMP with
respect to det Vi∗ to construct a suitable Mori fibre space structure on a birational
model. The presence of singularities creates technical difficulties but in the end the
naive strategy of an induction on the dimension works.

                          2. Notation and basic facts

We work over C, for general definitions we refer to [Har77], We use the terminology
of [Deb01, KM98] for birational geometry and notions from the minimal model
program.
Manifolds and varieties will always be supposed to be irreducible. We say that
a Zariski open subset X0 ⊂ X is big if the complement X \ X0 has codimension
at least two in X. A fibration on a normal variety X is a surjective projective
morphism f : X → Y such that f∗ OX ≃ OY .

2.A. Reflexive sheaves and distributions. Let us recall some basic facts. By
definition a coherent sheaf F on a normal variety X is reflexive if the natural
morphism F → F ∗∗ is an isomorphism. If two reflexive sheaves F and G are
isomorphic on a big open subset of X, they are isomorphic on X [Har80, Prop.1.6].
2.1. Definition. Let F be a reflexive sheaf on a normal variety X. We say that a
subsheaf G ⊂ F is saturated if the quotient F/G is torsion-free.
2.2. Remarks. A satured subsheaf G ⊂ F of a reflexive sheaf F is reflexive (this
follows from [Har80, Prop.1.1]).
A torsion-free sheaf on a normal variety X is locally free in codimension one, i.e.
there exists a big open subset of X where the sheaf is locally free. Applying this
remark to G, F and G/F in the definition above we obtain that there exists a big
open subset X0 ⊂ X over which all three sheaves are locally free and the inclusion
G ⊗ OX0 → F ⊗ OX0 is an injection of vector bundles.
If G ⊂ F is a non-saturated subsheaf of a reflexive sheaf F the saturation G sat ⊂ F
is the kernel of the surjective morphism
                           F → F/G → (F/G)/Torsion.

Given a normal variety X we denote by
                                     TX := Ω∗X
its tangent sheaf. In particular TX is reflexive [Har80, Cor.1.2].

We start with an introductory remark:
4.1. Remark. Let X be a normal projective variety, and let

Proof of Theorem 1.1. Since X is klt there exists a small modification µ : X ′ → X
that is Q-factorial [Kol13, Cor.1.37] and X ′ is of Fano type [PS09, Lemma 2.8(iii)].
Since µ is small we can use Remark 4.1 to see that it suffices to prove the statement
for X ′ . In order to simplify the notation we will assume without loss of generality
that X is Q-factorial. Since X is of Fano type we know by [BCHM10, Cor.1.3.2]
that X is a Mori dream space.
We will prove the statement by induction on the dimension, the first case being a
klt surface such that TX = L1 ⊕ L2 where Li is a reflexive sheaf of rank one. In
this case the integrability of Li is clear, moreover the canonical sheaf KLi = L∗i

is not pseudoeffective by Proposition 2.15. Thus the leaves are algebraic [McQ08,
Cor.II.4.2].
Fix now i ∈ {1, 2}, our goal is to show that Vi ⊂ TX is integrable. By Proposition
2.15 the determinant det Vi∗ is not pseudoeffective. Since X is a Mori dream space
we can run a terminating det Vi∗ -MMP [HK00, Prop.1.11(1)] X 99K X ′ that ends
