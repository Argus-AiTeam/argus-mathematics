# Marked monomial reduction and the relative log model

## Scope and present conclusion

The target is the **literal** Proposition `prop:log-model` in
`build/sections/06-model.tex`, including its use of
`prop:marked-monomial-reduction` in `15-local-numerical.tex` of the staged
September 24, 2026 manuscript. No Milnor-number hypothesis is imposed.

The monomial construction below gives an elementary proof of the marked
reduction in dimension three. The subsequent argument proves that a
terminating, divisor-nonextracting relative dlt MMP has all the required
preservation properties. **Existence of that MMP with the required hypotheses
has not been independently source-verified here. Consequently this is a
conditional derivation, not a verification or refutation of `prop:log-model`.**
No false manuscript assertion has been established.

The exact local calculation in `verify.py` is an illustration of the lattice
step, not a replacement for the universal proof and not an existence
certificate for the relative log model. There is no Lean formalization.

## The assertions being distinguished

The log-model proposition assumes a flat projective family of integral normal
Gorenstein surfaces over a smooth connected complex curve. Its total space is
normal Gorenstein. A section contains all fiber singularities, and the family
is smooth away from that section. Its conclusion, after shrinking and a finite
smooth-curve base change, simultaneously requires:

1. A projective birational map to the base-changed target, with normal
   Q-factorial source, an isomorphism off the section.
2. At **every** remaining parameter, a reduced Cartier scheme fiber and a dlt
   pair consisting of the source, the full reduced surviving horizontal
   exceptional divisor, and that fiber.
3. Relative nefness of the canonical divisor plus the horizontal boundary;
   all vertical exceptional divisors lie over the distinguished parameter.
4. Integral normal noncentral fibers with reduced horizontal restriction,
   and a unique component over the generic point of the central surface.
   The other central components are projective surfaces over its section point.

The marked reduction starts with a smooth quasi-projective threefold, a
monomial central fiber, and a horizontal marking which together have simple
normal crossings. Away from the distinguished point the family is smooth and
the marking is relatively simple normal crossing. The integer used in the
cover is any positive common multiple of the central multiplicities, not a
new unspecified sufficiently divisible integer.

## Elementary lattice theorem

Let \(1\leq k\leq3\), let \(a_i>0\), and let \(a_i\mid b\). Put
\[
 N=\{d\in\mathbb Z^k:\ \sum_i a_i d_i\equiv0\pmod b\},\qquad
 \ell(d)=\sum_i a_i d_i/b.
\]
The positive orthant is generated by the primitive lattice vectors
\(r_i=(b/a_i)e_i\). They all have height one. Its height-one section is
a compact simplex.

Insert every height-one lattice point as a ray by successive star
subdivisions. There are finitely many. Start with a simplicial fan. If a
point is not already a ray, subdivide its smallest containing cone.
This keeps the fan simplicial. Every new ray is primitive: a nontrivial
integral division would give a positive nonintegral value of the
integer-valued function \(\ell\).

Every final maximal cone has only height-one rays. Its height-one section
contains no other lattice point. Indeed, all such points were inserted,
and a ray of a fan contained in a cone is a face of that cone.

Here is the precise empty-simplex argument; it does not use a bounded search.
For a triangle, translate a vertex \(p\) to zero. Let \(u,v\) be its edge
vectors in the height-zero lattice \(L=\ker(\ell:N\to\mathbb Z)\).
If \(\mathbb Zu+\mathbb Zv\) were a proper sublattice of \(L\), a nonzero
coset would have a lattice representative
\[
 w=\alpha u+\gamma v,\qquad 0\leq\alpha,\gamma<1.
\]
When \(\alpha+\gamma\leq1\), \(p+w\) is a nonvertex lattice point of the
triangle. When \(\alpha+\gamma>1\), both coefficients are positive and
\(p+u+v-w\) is such a point instead. Both are impossible. Thus \(u,v\)
are a basis of \(L\). Since \(\ell(p)=1\), adjoining \(p\) gives a basis
of \(N\): for any \(n\), \(n-\ell(n)p\in L\).

For a segment, absence of intermediate lattice points says exactly that its
edge is primitive in the rank-one height-zero lattice. The same splitting
applies. The one-dimensional cone case is immediate. Therefore **all the
resulting cones are regular in their own lattices**. The dimension bound is
essential to this proof: an empty three-dimensional lattice simplex need
not be unimodular.

## From local cones to a global marked modification

At a point of the original central fiber there are etale local coordinates
with
\[
 \tau=u\prod_{i\in I}z_i^{a_i},\quad |I|\leq3,
\]
and all marking components present are other coordinate hyperplanes.
Adjoining a root of the unit \(u\) is an etale operation in characteristic
zero. The normalization of \(s^b=\prod z_i^{a_i}\), on each local connected
sheet, has precisely the cone and congruence lattice above. To see the
lattice condition, a valuation \(d\) extends with integral order of \(s\)
if and only if \(b\mid\sum a_i d_i\). If the binomial has several local
sheets, divide all its exponents by their common gcd; this gives the same
congruence condition. The remaining coordinates are unchanged product
factors, including the horizontal marking coordinates.

Compatibility is not an arbitrary independent choice of triangulation on
each chart. Label the original global components \(Z_i\). In the single
orthant on those labels, with its congruence lattice, order the finitely
many height-one points and make star subdivisions in that order. Restrict
to the faces that actually occur. A subdivision whose center is not on
such a face leaves that face unchanged. Thus every actual chart, of
vertical dimension at most three, receives the same subdivision as its
overlapping charts. The larger auxiliary orthant is only a way of fixing
compatible choices; no regularity statement in its larger dimension is
being used.

Here are actual global projective modifications, rather than only local
fans. A ray has an order vector \(v\) for the pullbacks of all the original
Cartier divisors \(Z_i\). Let \(G_v\) be the sum of the vertical prime
divisors with this vector. All current local cones are simplicial, so these
divisors are Q-Cartier. Suppose the next ray is in the relative interior
of the cone on \(v_1,\ldots,v_r\), and
\(v=\sum_j\lambda_jv_j\), with all \(\lambda_j>0\).
Choose positive integers \(c_j\) such that \(c_jG_{v_j}\) are Cartier and
the numbers \(c_j\lambda_j\) are equal. Such integers exist by clearing
the finitely many denominators and Cartier indices.

The normalized blowup of
\[
 \mathcal I=\sum_j\mathcal O(-c_jG_{v_j})
\]
realizes the required star subdivision. On a simplicial toric chart,
write a valuation as \(\sum d_jv_j\). The orders of the generators are
\(c_jd_j\). The regions where a specified one is minimal are exactly the
cones generated by \(v\) and all the old generators except that specified
generator. This is the star subdivision because
\(\lambda_j\) is proportional to \(1/c_j\). A chart missing one of the
required rays has a unit summand in the ideal, hence is unchanged.
These descriptions are etale local and use globally defined ideals,
so no algebraization or descent of an arbitrarily glued analytic space
is required. Blowups are projective and normalization is finite here.

After finitely many steps the lattice theorem makes every actual chart
smooth. Every vertical ray still has height one, so the divisor of \(s\)
is reduced. Regular toric coordinates make it simple normal crossing
together with the unchanged marking coordinates.

There is no self-intersection hidden by the order labels. On a normalized
local sheet, a fixed order vector is a single ray and has a single local
boundary branch. Distinct local sheets are disjoint. A cone cannot contain
two distinct rays with the same vector. Thus two branches of one global
component cannot occur in the same local chart as a consequence of this
construction. Its boundary is simple, not merely normal crossing.

Off the distinguished fiber there are no subdivision centers. At a smooth
point of the original morphism on that fiber there is just one vertical
coordinate, of multiplicity one, and its ray already is regular after
the base change. Hence this construction is an isomorphism on every
open set specified in the marked reduction proposition. No horizontal
exceptional divisor is introduced.

## Reduction of the log-model claim to the exact MMP input

The following are external inputs, not the output of the finite calculation:

* Characteristic-zero projective log resolution/principalization, supported
  on the nonsmooth locus of the pair and the section ideal.
* **MMP input still requiring primary-source confirmation:** for the
  projective birational morphism \(V\to X\) below, there is a finite
  \((K_V+H_V)\)-MMP over \(X\), starting with the Q-factorial dlt pair,
  made of divisorial contractions and flips. It preserves Q-factoriality
  and the usual dlt discrepancy inequalities for any boundary for which
  the same steps are negative, extracts no divisors, and ends with a
  relatively nef model or a Mori fiber space.

The second input is deliberately stated over the **birational target
\(X\)**, not just over the curve. It is not being inferred from a theorem
whose base is only a curve.

With the first input, resolve the section ideal and the central fiber
without changing the already smooth complement of the section. Shrink
the curve so the resulting smooth threefold and all dominating exceptional
strata are smooth over the punctured curve and all vertical exceptional
divisors lie over zero. This is possible by characteristic-zero generic
smoothness and the finiteness of the strata.

Take the cover with exponent a common multiple of the central
multiplicities. The pulled-back target is Gorenstein: locally it is the
quotient of \(A[s]\) by the monic nonzerodivisor \(s^b-\tau\), with \(A\)
Gorenstein. It is S2 and is regular off the codimension-two section,
so Serre's normality criterion applies. Flatness and integral fibers over
a connected smooth covering curve give an integral total space (there
are no vertical components, and the generic fiber has just one component).

Apply the global marked construction just proved. It produces a smooth
threefold \(V\), projective birational over the new \(X\), isomorphic off
the section. Its reduced horizontal exceptional divisor \(H_V\) satisfies
\((V,H_V+V_c)\) simple normal crossing at every remaining parameter.
All vertical exceptional primes are over zero.

## What the MMP would preserve, with quantifiers intact

Assume precisely the MMP input above. All its steps are over \(X\).
For any \(c\), the fiber divisor is pulled back from a point on the curve,
so has intersection zero with every curve contracted over \(X\).
Consequently every step is also negative for
\(K+H+V_c\). This is an argument for an **arbitrary** \(c\), not a
generic-parameter argument. The dlt preservation input can therefore
be applied separately for every \(c\) to the same fixed finite sequence.

It remains important to identify the pushed-forward boundary with the
scheme fiber. Every divisor on an intermediate model already occurred
on \(V\): neither a flip nor a divisorial contraction extracts a divisor.
Orders of a rational function at surviving divisorial valuations are
unchanged. Thus every component of the divisor of a local curve
parameter still has order one.

That divisor is Cartier because the parameter is nonzero in the integral
total space. It defines a reduced subscheme on a normal variety. Indeed,
if \(u^n\in(t)\), at each height-one prime with order of \(t\) equal to
one, integrality of valuations implies \(\operatorname{ord}(u)\geq1\).
At all other height-one primes \(\operatorname{ord}(u/t)\geq0\) as well.
The normal-domain intersection description then gives \(u/t\) regular.
Hence \((t)\) is radical. This argument also rules out a hidden
scheme-theoretic multiplicity, rather than merely computing a cycle.
The boundary used in dlt preservation is therefore exactly \(H+Y_c\).

All surviving horizontal exceptional primes retain coefficient one.
A prime which was nonexceptional over \(X\) cannot become exceptional
merely by a sequence over \(X\). The resulting \(H\) is consequently
the **full** reduced horizontal exceptional boundary, not a chosen subset.
All vertical exceptional primes still lie over zero.

Each step is an isomorphism off the section. There are no relative curves
there on the initial model; the contractions and their flips restrict to
the identity over that open subset.

The Mori fiber alternative is impossible over \(X\). Its final source
is still dominant and birational over the threefold \(X\), whereas a
Mori fiber contraction over \(X\) would factor that map through a
variety of dimension strictly less than three. Thus the final
\(K_Y+H\) is nef over \(X\).

For \(c\ne0\), an extra component of the fiber would be a vertical
exceptional prime, which has already been excluded. The unique component
is the strict transform of \(X_c\); it is normal as a coefficient-one
component of a dlt pair, and the fiber is integral because it is reduced.
At a generic curve of \(H\cap Y_c\), the two coefficient-one components
of the dlt boundary meet simply normally. The coefficient of the
restriction is consequently one. No two horizontal components can give
the same generic curve: that would violate the same codimension-two
simple-normal-crossing condition. Thus \(H|_{Y_c}\) is reduced as a Weil
divisor.

Finally a proper birational morphism onto a normal target is an
isomorphism at its codimension-one generic points. There is exactly one
prime on \(Y\) above the generic point of \(X_0\). All other central
components are exceptional, have image the section point, and are
projective surfaces since \(Y\to X\) is projective.

This proves every stated guarantee **conditional on the unconfirmed
external inputs**, and isolates the missing theorem application without
weakening any hypothesis or quantifier.
