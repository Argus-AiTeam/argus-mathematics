# An unlocalized cut-symbol theorem

## Statement and source boundary

The assertion audited here is Proposition `prop:filtration-bound`, especially
`eq:cut-leading-trace` and `eq:filtration-symbol-surjection`, in
`research/openai_manuscript/The-equivariant-cohomological-Hikita-conjecture-for-arbitrary-quivers-September-24-2026/build/sections/laurent.tex`.
Throughout, orbit sums use **distinct translates**, and subgroup traces are
**sums**, not averages. The maps below are maps of graded vector spaces (or
modules), not algebra presentations of the associated graded.

Here is an abstract version that also allows nonreduced coefficient bases.
Let \(K\) be any commutative \(\mathbb Q\)-algebra, let \(I\) be a finite
color set, and let \(\Omega_i\) be \(v_i\) gauge slots of color \(i\).
Put \(P=K[x_d:d\in\Omega]\) and \(W=\prod_i S_{\Omega_i}\). Choose arbitrary
nonnegative integers \(n_{ij},w_i\). For each remaining arrow \(i\to j\)
use the weights \(x_e-x_d\), for all \(d\in\Omega_i,e\in\Omega_j\);
there are \(n_{ij}\) such arrows. At \(d\in\Omega_i\) also use \(w_i\)
copies of \(-x_d\). Omit diagonal zero weights. For these weights define
\[
 E_\rho(\lambda)=(-\langle\rho,\lambda\rangle)_+,\qquad
 c(\alpha,\beta)=\prod_\rho
 \rho^{E_\rho(\alpha)+E_\rho(\beta)-E_\rho(\alpha+\beta)}.
\]
Define the **free** \(P\)-module algebra
\[
 L=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\mathbb Z^\Omega}P r_\lambda,\qquad
 r_\alpha r_\beta=c(\alpha,\beta)r_{\alpha+\beta}.
\]
For a field \(K\), this is exactly the Laurent model obtained by writing
\(r_\lambda=e^\lambda\prod_\rho\rho^{E_\rho(\lambda)}\).
The free-module definition avoids any use of fractions when \(K\) has zero
divisors. All excess exponents are nonnegative; associativity follows
weight by weight by telescoping the three \(E\)-terms. Thus this definition
works over every stated base, without a regularity assumption on a weight.
Let \(A=L^W\), and let
\[
 B=A/\langle A^{(d)}:d\ne0\rangle,\qquad
 \deg_{\rm top}r_\lambda=(\sum_{\Omega_i}\lambda_d)_i.
\]
This is the actual ideal quotient, never its reduction.

For each array \(m=(m_{i,s})_{s\ge1}\) with
\(\sum_s s m_{i,s}=v_i\), choose positive slots of those sizes and fill
the other gauge slots with size zero. Write \(s_d\) for the resulting
sizes, \(\lambda_m=s-1\), \(l_i=\sum_s m_{i,s}\), and \(l=\sum_i l_i\).
Let \(P_m=K[x_d:s_d>0]\), \(W_m=\prod_{i,s}S_{m_{i,s}}\).
For a nonempty subset \(S\) of positive slots put
\[
 q_S=\prod_{d\in S}x_d^{w_{i(d)}}
       \prod_{\substack{d\in S\\e\notin S,\ s_e>0}}
       (x_d-x_e)^{(n_{i(d)i(e)}+n_{i(e)i(d)})\min(s_d,s_e)},
 \qquad J_m=(q_S:S\ne\varnothing).
\]
Define \(F_L\) to be the sum in \(B\) of the images of the dressed
orbit types \(\lambda_m\) with \(l\le L\), and \(F_{-1}=0\).

**Theorem.** For all these data, \(F_{|\Omega|}=B\), and there are
surjections of graded \(K\)-modules
\[
 \bigoplus_{l(m)=L}(P_m/J_m)^{W_m}[\delta_m]
       \longrightarrow F_L/F_{L-1},                         \tag{1}
\]
where
\[
 \delta_m=\sum_{i,j}n_{ij}v_iv_j
 -\sum_{d,e:s_d,s_e>0}n_{i(d)i(e)}\min(s_d,s_e)
 +\sum_iw_i(v_i-l_i)\ \in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}.                  \tag{2}
\]
Over a characteristic-zero field this gives the manuscript's
coefficientwise Hilbert-series upper bound. For \(K=\mathbb C\), (1)
is exactly the selected manuscript assertion, **conditional on the BFN
identification `prop:laurent-model`**. That geometric identification is
an explicit external interface, not a conclusion of this note.

The proof below also proves coefficient generation for this abstract
algebra. Consequently it does not need a separate generation assumption
once the Laurent identification is supplied.

## 1. Polynomial orbit algebra and coefficient generation

The elementary inequality
\(u_++v_+-(u+v)_+\ge0\) proves polynomiality of every product coefficient.
The multiset of weights is \(W\)-stable, so the permutation action on \(L\)
is by algebra automorphisms. Because \(L\) is a direct sum on its displayed
basis, a \(W\)-invariant element has a unique finite decomposition into
\[
 {\mathsf S}_\lambda(p)=\sum_{w\in W/W_\lambda}w(p r_\lambda),
 \qquad p\in P^{W_\lambda}.
                                                               \tag{3}
\]
This assertion uses coefficients, not evaluations of Euler products.
It is therefore still true over a nonreduced \(K\).

We prove that \(A\) is generated by \(P^W\) and (3) for nonzero
positive or negative subset coweights. Induct on \(\|\lambda\|^2\).
The zero and subset cases are generators. If \(\lambda\) has a positive
entry, set
\[
 f(t)=1_{t>0},\quad g(t)=t-f(t),\quad
 \alpha=f(\lambda),\quad\gamma=g(\lambda).
\]
Otherwise use \(f(t)=-1_{t<0}\) and \(g(t)=t-f(t)\).
Outside the base cases both squared norms are strictly smaller.
Both \(f\) and \(g\) are nondecreasing. Their coordinatewise signs are
compatible, as are all their coordinate differences, even across colors.
Thus no weight has opposite-sign pairings on \(\alpha,\gamma\), and
\[
 c(\alpha,\gamma)=1.                                         \tag{4}
\]

For completeness the needed maximal-matching statement does not require
an unproved rearrangement result. In the positive case \(\alpha\) is an
indicator. At a color with \(k\) ones, the scalar product with a permutation
of \(\gamma\) is the sum of \(k\) chosen entries of \(\gamma\); its maximum
is the sum of its \(k\) largest entries. This is the ordered matching
because \(g\) is nondecreasing. Equality can only exchange tied entries
at the selection boundary, which does not change the multiset of sums.
For negative \(\alpha\), maximizing chooses its \(k\) smallest entries.
The same tie argument applies. Since
\(\|\alpha+w\gamma\|^2=\|\alpha\|^2+\|\gamma\|^2+
2\langle\alpha,w\gamma\rangle\), every other orbit in a product of the
two orbit types either is the orbit of \(\lambda\) or has strictly
smaller squared norm.

At the fixed representative \(\lambda\) the maximizing decomposition
is unique. In the positive case, an incorrect assignment must give
\(\alpha'_d=1\) at some \(\lambda_d\le0\) and
\(\alpha'_e=0\) at some \(\lambda_e>0\) of the same color. Then
\(\gamma'_d=\lambda_d-1<\lambda_e=\gamma'_e\), although the chosen entry
was supposed to be among the largest. Exchanging the two strictly
increases the scalar product, a contradiction. In the negative case,
an incorrect assignment has \(\alpha'_d=0\) at a negative coordinate
and \(\alpha'_e=-1\) at a nonnegative coordinate. It gives
\(\gamma'_d=\lambda_d<\lambda_e+1=\gamma'_e\), contradicting the choice
of smallest entries. Hence the coefficient of \(r_\lambda\) is exactly
\(p_\alpha p_\gamma\), not a nontrivial orbit multiple.

Order each color by increasing \(\lambda\). The level blocks of
\(\alpha,\gamma\) are intervals. Every cut of a \(\lambda\)-level block
is a cut of at least one of these interval partitions, since
\(f(t)+g(t)=t\). Prefix power sums at all such cuts are therefore sums of
block power sums for one of the two partitions. Differences of prefixes
give every power sum on every \(\lambda\)-block. It follows that
\[
 P^{W_\lambda}=
 \langle P^{W_\alpha},P^{W_\gamma}\rangle.                    \tag{5}
\]
Here is the algebra behind this assertion over the stated base. Symmetric
polynomials on a block are generated by its elementary symmetric
polynomials: subtract the product of elementary symmetric polynomials
with the same leading sorted monomial, and descend lexicographically in
each fixed degree. This algorithm works over any coefficient ring.
Newton's identities
\(j e_j=\sum_{k=1}^j(-1)^{k-1}e_{j-k}p_k\) then express those generators
in power sums over a \(\mathbb Q\)-algebra. Thus (5) uses no field
or algebraic-closure hypothesis.

A commutative ring generated by the two subrings in (5) is linearly
spanned over \(K\) by products \(p_\alpha p_\gamma\).
Their orbit products realize every desired leading coefficient.
Subtract the lower-norm orbit sums by induction; their dressings are
polynomial by the nonnegative excess inequality and invariant under
their stabilizers by invariance of the whole product. This proves
generation. Killing all nonzero charges kills each nontrivial subset
generator, so \(P^W\to B\) is surjective.

The array with all sizes one has \(\lambda_m=0\), and therefore its
image already contains all coefficients. This proves the claimed finite
exhaustion. No assertion that these multipartition orbits span the
neutral part of \(A\) before quotienting was used.

## 2. Zero-slot elimination

For each color \(i\) and \(k\ge1\) define the \(P\)-linear map
\[
 D_{i,k}(p r_\lambda)=
      p r_\lambda\sum_{d\in\Omega_i}\lambda_d x_d^k.
\]
The multiplier is additive in \(\lambda\), so the explicit multiplication
rule proves the Leibniz rule, including when a coefficient is a zero
divisor. The map commutes with \(W\), preserves charge, and consequently
preserves the ideal generated by all nonzero-charge summands.
It induces a derivation on \(B\) that kills \(P^W\).
Section 1 proves that those coefficients generate \(B\); hence the induced
derivation is zero.

Fix \(m\), let \(H=W_{\lambda_m}\), and let \(\Phi_m\) be its map to the
length-\(l\) symbol. For every \(H\)-invariant dressing \(p\),
\[
 {\mathsf S}_{\lambda_m}\left(
 p\left[\sum_{\substack{d\in\Omega_i\\s_d>0}}(s_d-1)x_d^k
       -\sum_{\substack{z\in\Omega_i\\s_z=0}}x_z^k\right]\right)=0
 \quad\hbox{in }B.                                          \tag{6}
\]
The bracket is itself \(H\)-invariant. This identity holds for **every
multiplier** \(p\), so it may be applied repeatedly inside a polynomial
in zero-block power sums. Newton's identities express the zero-block
invariant ring in its first \(v_i-l_i\) power sums. Replacing those
generators in any dressing using (6) leaves only positive-slot
invariants and preserves degree.

This is a spanning statement, not an assertion that the replacement
defines an isomorphism between the zero-slot and positive-slot polynomial
rings, nor that (6) lists every relation in the image. Any additional
relations in \(B\) can only shrink the target and do not obstruct the
surjection.

There is also a concrete description of the replacement: at color \(i\),
the number of zero slots equals
\(v_i-l_i=\sum_{s_d>0,\ i(d)=i}(s_d-1)\). Evaluate a zero-block symmetric
polynomial on the multiset consisting of \(s_d-1\) copies of each
positive variable \(x_d\). Its \(k\)-th power sum is exactly the
positive expression in (6). Thus all the replacement power sums are
compatible; no assertion of algebraic independence is needed.

## 3. Support, excess exponents, and the orientation sign

Fix nonempty \(S\subseteq\{d:s_d>0\}\). Put \(b_d=s_d\) on \(S\), zero
elsewhere, and \(a=s-b\). Set \(\alpha=a-1,\ \beta=b\).
The charge of \(\beta\) is a nonzero nonnegative vector, so
\({\mathsf S}_\alpha(p_a){\mathsf S}_\beta(p_b)=0\) in \(B\), for
arbitrary stabilizer-invariant polynomial dressings.

Consider any pair of translates, and write \(a',b'\) for the translated
size functions. It contributes at \(t-1\), where \(t=a'+b'\).
All entries of \(t\) are nonnegative and its color sums are \(v_i\);
it is another allowed multipartition. With \(A'=\operatorname{supp}a'\),
\(B'=\operatorname{supp}b'\),
\[
 |\operatorname{supp}t|
   =|A'|+|B'|-|A'\cap B'|=l-|A'\cap B'|.                    \tag{7}
\]
Thus an overlapping pair has strictly smaller length. Its excess
exponents are nonnegative and its full orbit coefficient is a polynomial
invariant under the new stabilizer; hence its orbit term really lies
in \(F_{l-1}\), not just on a smaller reduced support.
A disjoint pair instead has exactly the original multiset of positive
sizes at each color and thus exactly orbit type \(m\).

For the chosen disjoint split the contribution of an arrow \(d\to e\) is
\[
 (a_d-a_e)_++(b_d-b_e)_+
        -(a_d+b_d-a_e-b_e)_+
       =\min(a_d,b_e)+\min(b_d,a_e).                         \tag{8}
\]
This identity is valid for all nonnegative \(a_d,a_e,b_d,b_e\) with
\(a_db_d=a_eb_e=0\). Splitting the four zero alternatives proves it;
it is `root_excess_disjoint` in `lean/Main.lean`.
It is zero on same-side pairs and on pairs involving a zero-size slot,
and equals \(\min(s_d,s_e)\) on a cross-cut pair. The framing excess is
\[
 (a_d-1)_++(b_d)_+-(a_d+b_d-1)_+
       =1_{b_d>0},                                         \tag{9}
\]
also proved universally in Lean.

If \(d\in S,e\notin S\), an arrow from \(d\) to \(e\) contributes
\((x_e-x_d)^{n_{i(d)i(e)}\min(s_d,s_e)}\).
The reverse arrows contribute the opposite orientation of this same
root. The exact identity
\[
 (y-x)^u(x-y)^v=(-1)^u(x-y)^{u+v}                           \tag{10}
\]
shows that their combined contribution is the cut factor with sign
from the arrows whose **source is selected**. Each selected framing
weight contributes \(-x_d\). Consequently
\[
 c(\alpha,\beta)=(-1)^{\epsilon_S}q_S,\qquad
 \epsilon_S=\sum_{d\in S}w_{i(d)}
    +\sum_{\substack{d\in S\\e\notin S,\ s_e>0}}
        n_{i(d)i(e)}\min(s_d,s_e).                         \tag{11}
\]
The sign, root identity, their finite product, and (7)'s strict
inequality are machine checked for arbitrary finite sets and exponents.
Remaining loops are included: for each selected/unselected pair one
ordered weight occurs in each direction per loop, contributing exponent
\(2n_{ii}\min(s_d,s_e)\) and parity \(n_{ii}\min(s_d,s_e)\).
No loop diagonal is used.

## 4. The leading coefficient is exactly the stated trace

Fix the representative \(s-1\). Any pair of translates contributing to it
has length \(l\), so (7) forces disjointness. At each positive slot one
must choose either \(a'_d=s_d,b'_d=0\) or
\(a'_d=0,b'_d=s_d\). At zero slots both are zero. Within every type
\((i,s)\), the number of selected choices is fixed by the original cut.
Conversely, any choice with those counts is an allowed pair of translates.

The group \(H=W_{\lambda_m}\) permutes each positive type block and
each zero block. It acts transitively on these choices, and the stabilizer
of the original choice is precisely
\[
 H_S=W_\alpha\cap W_\beta.
\]
Its blocks are the selected and unselected copies of every positive
type and the zero blocks. In particular \(H_S\subset H\). This gives a
bijection between pairs of translates at the fixed representative and
\(H/H_S\). There is **one pair per coset**, not one per element of \(H\).
For example their number is
\(\prod_{i,s}\binom{m_{i,s}}{\#(S\text{ of type }(i,s))}\);
zero-slot permutations supply no extra factor.

Equivariance of the weight multiset gives
\(c(h\alpha,h\beta)=h(c(\alpha,\beta))\).
Dressings translate with their respective coweights. Equation (11)
therefore makes the coefficient of \(r_{\lambda_m}\) exactly
\[
 (-1)^{\epsilon_S}
       \sum_{h\in H/H_S}h(q_Sp_ap_b)
   =(-1)^{\epsilon_S}\operatorname{Tr}_{H_S}^H(q_Sp_ap_b).
                                                               \tag{12}
\]
The integer \(\epsilon_S\) depends only on the selected counts per type,
so no term has a different sign. A \(W\)-invariant sum on the orbit of
\(\lambda_m\) is uniquely determined by this representative coefficient,
by (3). Thus (12) is also the dressing of its leading orbit sum; no
factor \(|W/H|\) appears. Every other orbit has lower length.
As the whole product is zero in \(B\), (12) proves precisely
`eq:cut-leading-trace`. It is not a trace formula modulo a polynomial
localization.

## 5. All invariant cut relations, not merely undressed ones

Choose \(p_a\) only in the positive blocks of \(a\), and \(p_b\) only in
the positive blocks of \(b\). These are allowed dressings: their other
blocks are ignored, not distinguished artificially. Their products
linearly span the invariant ring \(P_m^{H_S}\), since this is the tensor
product of the symmetric-polynomial rings on the selected and unselected
blocks. The zero-block permutations act trivially on \(P_m\).
Also \(q_S\in P_m^{H_S}\).

For a finite group \(G\) write
\(\operatorname{Av}_G=|G|^{-1}\sum_{g\in G}g\).
These constants are units in \(K\). For any \(f\in P_m\),
\[
 \operatorname{Av}_H(q_Sf)
 =\operatorname{Av}_H\bigl(q_S\operatorname{Av}_{H_S}f\bigr)
 =\frac{1}{[H:H_S]}
       \operatorname{Tr}_{H_S}^H
          \bigl(q_S\operatorname{Av}_{H_S}f\bigr).            \tag{13}
\]
The first equality follows by summing \(hh'\) for each fixed \(h'\in H_S\);
it does not require \(H_S\) to be normal. The second follows because
its input is \(H_S\)-invariant. By (12) and the span just proved,
every expression in (13) has zero symbol.

Now let \(g\in J_m\cap P_m^{W_m}\). Write a finite ideal expression
\(g=\sum_S q_S f_S\) with arbitrary polynomial multipliers. Since \(g\)
is invariant, average this equality:
\[
 g=\sum_S\operatorname{Av}_{W_m}(q_Sf_S).
\]
The zero-block factor of \(H\) acts trivially here, so these are exactly
the averages in (13). Hence \(\Phi_m(g)=0\). Arbitrary multipliers are
essential in this argument; the undressed traces alone would not prove
the ideal assertion.

Finally invariants are exact here by a direct lift argument. If an
invariant class in \(P_m/J_m\) is represented by \(f\), its averaged
representative has the same class, because \(J_m\) is stable.
The kernel of \(P_m^{W_m}\to(P_m/J_m)^{W_m}\) is
\(J_m\cap P_m^{W_m}\). Thus the positive-dressing map factors through
this quotient. Section 2 gives the needed surjectivity on dressings,
and summing orbit types of length \(L\) proves (1).

## 6. Degree and boundary cases

For each remaining arrow \(i\to j\),
\[
 \sum_{d\in\Omega_i,e\in\Omega_j}(s_d-s_e)_+
 =\sum_{d,e}(s_d-\min(s_d,s_e))
 =v_iv_j-\sum_{d,e}\min(s_d,s_e).                           \tag{14}
\]
This uses both color size sums being their gauge dimensions, not the
number of positive slots. Zero sizes contribute zero to the last sum.
For framing, \(\sum_{\Omega_i}(s_d-1)_+=v_i-l_i\).
Adding gives (2). Every original summand is a nonnegative integer, proving
integrality and nonnegativity without inferring them from a difference
of two rational numbers. The symmetric \(c_{ij}=n_{ij}+n_{ji}\)
rewrites (2) exactly as \(C(\mathbf v)-D_m+\mathbf w\cdot(\mathbf v-\mathbf l)\).
Homogeneous dressings, (6), and cut factors preserve the indicated
half degrees.

There is a single empty array if \(\Omega=\varnothing\); its map is
\(K\to K\). Nonempty gauge data always have positive slots, so the full
cut is available. If all framing vanishes then that cut is \(1\), all
symbol sources vanish, and the finite exhaustive filtration gives \(B=0\).
Equivalently the all-ones coweight gives an invertible, nonzero-charge
element. The same argument applies to an isolated unframed component.
Full cuts, empty zero blocks, rank-one factors, repeated sizes, asymmetric
arrow multiplicities, and additional loops were not excluded anywhere.

No flatness, radical-ideal computation, passage to closed points, or
inversion of an \(x_d\) or \(x_d-x_e\) appears in the proof. Only integers
arising in Newton identities and finite averages are inverted.

`verify.py` checks the original exponent definition against the trace
formula by expanding distinct-translate orbit products on bounded,
explicit data, including asymmetric arrows, repeated sizes and zero
blocks. It independently enumerates the leading choices by selected
subsets of type blocks, checks overlap lengths and the degree formula,
checks zero-slot Newton elimination, and tests the generation induction
and its block-power-sum spans on a stated finite coweight grid. These computations are
**finite regressions**, not the proof of the all-data theorem.
