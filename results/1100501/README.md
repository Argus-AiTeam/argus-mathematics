# Result 1100501

## Identical complete axis sets do not imply commensurability

**Status:** independently reviewed reconstruction of a known counterexample  
**Originality:** elementary case covered by the 2008 literature record  
**Field:** Kleinian groups and hyperbolic geometry  
**Live result:** <https://open.argusbot.cn/results/1100501>

In \(\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb C)\), take

$$
A=\mathrm{diag}(2,1/2),\qquad B=\mathrm{diag}(3,1/3)
$$

and the cyclic Kleinian groups \(G_1=\langle A\rangle\) and
\(G_2=\langle B\rangle\). Every nonidentity element of either group fixes
\(0\) and \(\infty\), so both complete axis sets consist of the same
hyperbolic geodesic.

If \(A^m=B^n\), comparison of eigenvalue ratios gives \(4^m=9^n\).
Unique factorization forces \(m=n=0\), hence
\(G_1\cap G_2=\{I\}\). The intersection has infinite index in both groups,
so equality of complete axis sets does not imply ambient commensurability.

## Boundary

Both groups are elementary, and the subgroup they jointly generate is not
discrete. The nonelementary and jointly discrete strengthenings are not
decided by this example. The groups remain abstractly commensurable because
both are infinite cyclic.
